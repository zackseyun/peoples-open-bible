# Isaiah 7 14 pregnancy and birth timing

The bounded comparison adopts *is pregnant and will bear a son* over POB's
*is pregnant and is bearing a son*. Present pregnancy remains an interpretive
choice, not a grammatical proof that conception preceded the speech. The
prospective alternative, *will conceive and bear a son*, belongs in the reader
note. This is an English correction against unchanged selected WLC, not newly
recovered Hebrew or a decision about virginity, child identity or naming forms.

## Hebrew construction and competing interpretations

Isaiah has the pregnancy adjective הָרָה and feminine active participle יֹלֶדֶת.
The adjective names a state; English must supply its temporal framing. The
participle is not mechanically an English present progressive. Primary grammar
controls are [Gesenius 116a,d,m,n,p](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Gesenius%27_Hebrew_Grammar/116._The_Participles)
and [141b,f](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Gesenius%27_Hebrew_Grammar/141._The_Noun-clause).
These permit contextual present and prospective readings; they do not settle
this verse by morphology alone. No fresh HALOT consultation is claimed.

The strongest present case combines the state adjective, demonstrative
presentation and definite woman. Genesis 16:4 independently narrates conception
before the comparable announcement in 16:11; present pregnancy and subsequent
birth make sense there. Isaiah's child-development deadline also permits that
timeline. It does not prove a physically indicated or visibly pregnant woman.

The strongest prospective case combines the promised sign and future events
in Isaiah's context with Judges 13's conception/birth announcement. Judges
describes barrenness and promises conception before using the pregnancy/bearing
formula; conception is not narrated between those statements. Immediate
conception cannot simply be excluded, but present pregnancy is not obligatory
from the formula alone. Isaiah's deadline accommodates forthcoming conception
as well as existing pregnancy.

The parallel birth forms are not identically pointed to Isaiah's. An initial
digital-tag-based finite-verb analysis was corrected after
[Gesenius 80d](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Gesenius%27_Hebrew_Grammar/80._The_Indication_of_Gender_in_Nouns)
identified the contracted feminine participle in Genesis/Judges. Conflicting
digital tags must not manufacture a semantic contrast.

## Verified Greek control

Native Swete III, third edition 1905, printed page 113/PDF page 137, selects
ἐν γαστρὶ λήμψεται καὶ τέξεται υἱόν and reports ἕξει as a pregnancy-verb
alternative. These pregnancy verbs and the birth verb are future. The complete
page and apparatus were inspected, with adjacent printed page 112 for context.
The [primary scan](https://archive.org/download/theoldtestamenti03swetuoft_202003/theoldtestamenti03swetuoft.pdf)
and its exact hash are recorded in the contract. This is Swete's B-based selected
printed text, not a new Vaticanus ink reading or exhaustive Greek collation.

Our inference: this is evidence of an ancient prospective interpretation, not
proof of different Hebrew conjugations or a uniquely recoverable Hebrew source.
The Greek control is not silently substituted for the Hebrew main text.

## English choice and application boundary

One fresh reviewer received the source/context and three alternatives, without
the current/proposed identity mapping. The reviewer selected the present-state
plus future-birth candidate, preserving prospective pregnancy as a serious
alternative. Present progressive *is bearing* can suggest childbirth underway;
that implication is not required by the Hebrew or established by the inspected
context. Future *will conceive* is defensible but interprets a transition into
pregnancy rather than simply representing the adjective's state.
