## One object and several identifiers

The [British Library record](https://searcharchives.bl.uk/catalog/040-002104602)
identifies Papyrus 230, TM 62310, with the Psalms recto catalogued as
P.Lond.Lit. 207 and the Isocrates verso as P.Lond.Lit. 255. These are not two
independent biblical copies. Its bibliography identifies Rahlfs–Fraenkel's
number 2019; that number is not a publication year.

The record's overall date range is 250–350 CE; its content description assigns
the recto to the third century and verso to the fourth. Kenyon's historical
description favors the end of the third. Preserve those attributions without
claiming a newly established precise date or a pre-Pauline witness. The record
calls it a roll and marks its digital images currently unavailable. Root read
the actual complete record, not only an indexed search result. Catalogue text
is credited to the British Library Board under its displayed CC BY licence.

## The historical plate and transcription

plate I, description and transcription, appear at PDF pages 14–16 in the Getty
Research Institute scan. Root inspected all three complete pages and read the
two-column transcription, retaining its supplies and spelling. Kenyon identifies
the fragment's range as Greek Psalms 11:7–14:4, corresponding to Hebrew
Psalms 12:7–15:4. He describes two mutilated columns and distinguishes a later
syllable-dot hand. His other historical generalizations are not adopted as
current discoveries or dating criteria.

## Does this become an independent Romans fragment

collection. Her conclusion explicitly does not establish misidentification.
Her appendix reproduces Barker's transcription in transliteration; it is not
another physical witness. The archive dates the work 2010, deposited 2011;
the Library bibliography cites the 2011 collection. Root read all sixteen
PDF pages including notes and bibliography, and viewed complete pp. 37–38,

consulted here.
