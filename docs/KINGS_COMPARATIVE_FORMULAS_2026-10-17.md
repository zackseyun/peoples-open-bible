## Actual Greek attestation

The lawful historical fallback is Brooke, McLean and Thackeray, *The Old
Testament in Greek*, II.2, Kings (1930), acquired from the
[Palmer Seminary institutional collection](https://tmcdaniel.palmerseminary.edu/Brooke%26McLean/LXX_Brooke%26McLean_2-2.pdf).
The modern Antiochene II, *1–2 Reyes* (1992), remains unacquired; no unofficial
Scribd crop was adopted. The historical apparatus is not that modern edition
or a complete census of manuscripts known in 2026.

Kings' own prefatory note adopts the methods and symbols of Samuel II.1
(1927). That part's actual key and the inherited Genesis I.1 (1906) conventions
were therefore consulted. The governing note selects Vaticanus where extant,
not a reconstructed earliest Greek text. It requires checking the complete
variant list for a witness's precise wording and acknowledges reporting limits.
Swete and this selected B text are not two independent ancient witnesses.

The same-series key gives **b = 19 + 108**, o = 82, c₂ = 127, e₂ = 93,
r = 700 and g = 158. Thus the Hoshea report names the core five plus 700;
Ahaziah names the core five plus 158. This is a descriptive witness list,
not six independent votes. Additional agreements elsewhere in a verse are
not implied: at 17:2 the eyes-expression has its own, different support list.
Likewise, the extra patristic siglum at 21:11 is not another Greek manuscript.
No unanimity is inferred from an unprinted apparatus entry.

In the supplementary controls, i = 56, j = 243, v = 245 and z = 554.
The river addition is reported for 19, 108, 554, 127 and 93, not for 82.
The wisdom omission prevents treating the core five as a uniform wording
group across this sample. The absence of a reported παρά replacement in
these inspected units does not establish agreement of all Greek witnesses.

## The Latin control is positive, but incomplete

Fischer's actual OL115 edition has the same unbracketed severity comparator
at 1 Kings 16:25 and 2 Kings 17:2: **super omnes qui fuerunt ante eum**.
The new control is printed 79/PDF 37, fol. 20V, col. II, lines 15–18;
Hoshea is printed 86/PDF 41, fol. 32R. These are published readings of the
same physical witness, not newly read manuscript pixels.

The control's king is **Zambri** in the Latin body, not silently normalized to
WLC's Omri. The selected Greek also has Ζαμβρεί, while its name apparatus
reports Αμβρι for b, c₂ and e₂, among others. Comparator agreement therefore
does not establish whole-verse affiliation. An initial agent attribution of
Fischer's “intentional change” note to Zambri was corrected before use:
printed 79, II.3 actually concerns **asafi**, not Zambri.

The edition's coverage map and body boundaries do not provide the requested
negative/“like” controls at 2 Kings 3:2–3 or 14:3, or Ahaziah's target.
The other requested later targets are likewise not represented. These gaps
are not attested omissions. The map's “2 Rg 17–18” means Samuel, not 2 Kings.
Consequently this sample cannot establish how the Latin translator regularly
handled רק לא, or uniquely identify the Greek preposition behind Latin super.

## Hebrew retrieval and grammatical controls

The complete direct-written-word layers of the two pinned WLC XML books were
searched: **817 + 719 distinct verse elements**, with **13,140 + 12,281 direct
word elements** and no empty written units. These are digital-edition counts,
not ancient witness counts. The existing `word_text` function preserves inline
letters; note/readings and punctuation were excluded from the search stream.
Only the search layer removes points, accents and segmentation marks.

Three explicit queries produced:

- 45 broad co-occurrences of בעיני with הרע or הישר. These include direct
  speech and collective subjects, not 45 interchangeable royal evaluations.
- Eight co-occurrences of the annotated m/3605 lemma with an evil/wrath lemma.
  Five concern individual severity, one collective Judah, and two concern
  departure **from all sins**, not a comparative ranking (13:11; 14:24).
- Four adjacent רק לא sequences: 1 Kings 21:25; 2 Kings 3:2, 14:3 and 17:2.
  The first has intervening היה before כ; 14:3 qualifies right conduct.
  The shared words cannot by frequency alone date a redaction or determine
  a direction of moral comparison.

The broader checks also include wisdom, wealth and the rivers in 2 Kings
5:12. They are relevant to comparison grammar but are not all the same royal
evaluation formula. In that river verse the written river-name and qere are
separate; neither changes the comparative word under study.

The initial exploratory note counter incorrectly searched for type
`x-variant`. Its zero was corrected to the actual `variant` type: 45/73 notes
with 46/73 nested qere words. No absence inference or written-word query
depended on that erroneous counter. Supplies and reading branches were not
merged into the source text.

## What changes in the historical assessment

The concrete Greek evidence strengthens **attestation**, not a priority verdict.
The παρά readings at Hoshea and Ahaziah are not merely speculative Greek
retroversions of Latin. Their lexical similarity is consistent with an early
shared formula, but also with later formulaic assimilation. This test has not
dated either mechanism.

Richelle's footnote 33 calls for the wider comparative study; its 21:11
reference needs a local correction in our use of it. The footnote says B has
ὑπέρ there, while the article's own next-page table/discussion and the inspected
critical body have **ἀπό**. Its L discussion has **κατά**. We retain this
discrepancy rather than silently standardizing the quotation or discarding the
article's broader argument. Only printed 345–346 were reread for this follow-up.

The Manasseh control cannot be treated as a clean example of one translator
rendering the same comparative source: the Greek forms differ substantively
from the written Hebrew comparison. Nor should differing sections of Kings
be assumed to belong to one unchanged translator or revision layer. The new
sample does not independently establish a uniform παρά/ὑπέρ rule.

The connected Hebrew context at 23:25–26 also keeps distinct Josiah's unusually
wholehearted return and continuing wrath associated with Manasseh. A favorable
evaluation and later national judgment can coexist in the narrative. That
makes a countermodel intelligible; it does not date Hoshea's qualifier or
recover lost Hebrew letters.
