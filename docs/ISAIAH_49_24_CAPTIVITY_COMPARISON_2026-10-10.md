# Isaiah 49 24 and 25 captivity wording comparison

Retain WLC and current main English provisionally. A published Great Isaiah
Scroll reading changes the captor from righteous to ruthless; the selected
Greek instead describes taking someone captive unjustly. These are distinct
alternatives, not one versional consensus. The immediate contribution is a
precise reader disclosure and correctly placed note anchors, not recovered ink
or a demonstrated new original reading.

This bounded comparison began on October 10, 2026, local time (October 11 UTC).
Its baseline is `a52f2fe20b700e07f37a453d2249b222e91f7f87`. The older Isaiah
catalogue screen supplies identities, not passage adjudication.

## Evidence and its scope

Root reads WLC and POB context 49:22–26 and the complete 49:24–25 records.
The [publisher's Rahlfs and Hanhart chapter](https://www.die-bibel.de/bibel/LXX/ISA.49)
identifies its text as the 2006 second edition. This is a selected Greek text,
not a manuscript collation or the complete Göttingen apparatus.

The working [Qumran Digital 2022 transcription](https://lexicon.qumran-digital.org/transcriptions/1QIsa%5Ea%5E/2022-10-14/index.html)
provides column 41, lines 24–28 in context. Line 25 prints
`שבי עריץ ימלט` without supply brackets or uncertainty dots on the disputed
noun. The following response prints `מלקוח גבור` and
`ש^{ו}בי עריץ`. The raised vav in the response remains distinct.
This is attributed published transcription, not new manuscript-image reading.
The release date is not the scroll's physical date.

The linked noun pattern matters:

| Location | WLC pattern | Published scroll pattern |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Question 49:24 | plunder from mighty; captives of righteous | plunder from mighty; captives of ruthless |
| Answer 49:25 | captives of mighty; prey of ruthless | plunder of mighty; captives of ruthless |

The scroll also differs in the question's verb and particle. Substituting only
ruthless into the POB sentence is therefore a diagnostic noun comparison, not
a complete diplomatic translation of the scroll.

The [NET translators' own note](https://classic.net.bible.org/verse.php?book=Isa&chapter=49&verse=24)
reports and favors the scroll noun. Its rejection of the Masoretic phrase as
contextually meaningless is an editorial judgment, not another manuscript.
POB does not adopt that categorical conclusion.

Root obtains the official [NETS Esaias PDF](https://ccat.sas.upenn.edu/nets/edition/33-esaias-nets.pdf)
and reads complete native pages 1 and 41, printed 823 and 863, visually and
textually. Its introduction identifies Ziegler's 1967 Greek edition as its
basis. At the target it renders active capture followed by salvation; the
next verse explicitly describes capturing the mighty figure. NETS is a modern
English interpretation of a selected Greek text, not another ancient witness
or a newly consulted critical apparatus.

In Greek, `ἀδίκως` qualifies the capture action. The active
`αἰχμαλωτεύσῃ` in 49:25 has explicit accusative `γίγαντα`; this supports
understanding an object in 49:24. No affirmative evidence here establishes an
intransitive interpretation, but it is not declared lexically impossible.
The Greek is neither the straightforward noun tyrant nor uniquely recoverable
Hebrew עריץ. Its divergent response construction further cautions against
using it as proof of every scroll word.

## Competing histories and English consequences

The scroll noun fits the surrounding oppressor imagery. It could preserve
earlier wording subsequently changed in the Masoretic line. Against that
choice, its answer also rearranges the paired nouns to closely repeat the
question, supplying a concrete harmonization explanation. Neither history
is observed. The older physical tradition and smoother sense do not settle
direction by themselves.

The retained Hebrew adjective is difficult, not automatically meaningless.
A captor's legal entitlement, or an innocent captive, are interpretations
requiring grammatical and contextual argument. They are not different
attested consonants and are not approved merely because they reduce the
moral difficulty.

Full-sentence diagnostic alternatives, without adopting any:

- Current interpretation: Can plunder be taken from a mighty man, or can the
  captives of a righteous one be rescued?
- Scroll noun contrast: Can plunder be taken from a mighty man, or can the
  captives of a ruthless one be rescued?
- Legal-entitlement interpretation: Can plunder be taken from a mighty man,
  or can rightfully held captives be rescued?

The second tests the noun alone, not the scroll's entire differently worded
sentence. The third interprets the retained phrase, not a restored source.
Neither alternative establishes whole-verse optimality. Root and one
independent same-model assessment support provisional retention and explicit
disclosure, with historical priority held.
