website metadata. Allen and Fowler's [2025 appendix, pp. 192, 194-195](https://eprints.gla.ac.uk/342036/2/342036.pdf),

The root investigator inspected the paired published presentations without
attempting a word-level transcription. The proposed content ranges were known,
so this was not a blind reading. No additional agent or human expert was used.
In 44r, host-letter outlines, holes and mottling dominate. In 71r, the conspicuous
writing follows the mirrored chapter list. In 77v, mirrored host letters,
holes, repair bands and a speckled background dominate. None supplied a clearly
distinguishable continuous additional sequence to select for native inspection.

This is a resource-allocation decision at preview resolution. No registration
measurement, native-resolution target reading or new spectral separation was
performed for these three pairs. It does **not** show that no transferred ink
survives or that another observer could never decipher a portion. Published
processed RGB presentations cannot be treated as independent wavelength bands.

All six previews were assembled from 72 zero-overlap JPEG tiles totaling
994,856 bytes. Every preserved tile hash remained unchanged; all six assemblies
were rebuilt pixel for pixel. The receipts give each tile URL, pyramid level
and integer assembly rectangle. This verifies acquisition, not letter identity.

## Omont's historical plates

The public [1889 Omont volume](https://archive.org/details/noticesuruntrsa00omongoog)
was acquired as a 76-page PDF. OCR served only to locate discussion; the actual
plate captions were checked in rendered page images. The plates are split
across scan pages, so a single rendered portion must not be described as a
complete manuscript photograph.

| Plate | PDF pages, counted from one | Checked caption | Current direct view |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| I | 63-64 | Suppl. gr. 1074, fol. 9v | 74v |
| II | 67-68 | Coislin 202, fol. 14r | 96r |

The captioned portions on PDF pages 64 and 68 were rendered at a maximum
dimension of 2200 pixels and rotated 90 degrees clockwise by exact pixel
permutation. Both rotations were verified against their unchanged inputs.
No letter was completed, sharpened or generated.

These are digitized printed heliographic reproductions, not acquired camera
negatives. Printing, paper, scanning and rendering all intervene. Their color
does not constitute manuscript reflectance or an additional spectral channel.
They could support a future historical-state comparison of their own leaves,
but not an earlier-state comparison of Coislin 202 folio 1v, our Paris gap.
No newly lost stroke, deterioration measurement or new reading was established.
This does not rule out the existence of some other useful historical photograph.
