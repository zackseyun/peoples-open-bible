## What Fox actually argues

Fox selects both weary clauses and an exhaustion ending, וָאֵכֶל (with pausal
accent/meteg in the printed line), and moves the major pause to הגבר.
Repointing, segmentation, morphology and accents make this a source
interpretation, not merely an English preference. His argument uses the
awkward repeated recipient, expected instructional designation, the following
כי clause and the surrounding ignorance/death language. These are literary
and grammatical arguments, not newly observed Hebrew manuscript ink.

Fox explicitly calls the proposal uncertain. The genuine Ithiel name at
Nehemiah11:7 and a coherent superscription remain counterarguments. The
references to death and ignorance need not compel a terminally exhausted
speaker; that last qualification is our inference, not Fox's statement.

Greek completion/ceasing is suggestive of Fox's proposed vocalization, but he
expressly denies that it establishes those Hebrew vowels. Versional
retroversions and proposals about non-Solomonic attribution remain hypotheses.

Two published discrepancies must stay visible:

1. Printed378 apparatus labels the Greek/Syriac omission of the second clause
   haplography; printed380 prose instead calls the second Masoretic occurrence
   probably dittography. The selected Hebrew nevertheless keeps both.
   This is an unresolved apparatus/prose/selection tension, not a verified
   erratum or permission to remove the clause.
2. Fox379 reports Aquila's καὶ τέλεσον, a second-person singular aorist
   imperative. CTAT774 reports καὶ τελέσω, a first-person form read there as future.
   These are different reported forms. Neither underlying Greek attribution
   has been directly checked here; silently normalizing them would erase
   the next useful discriminator.

[CTAT's frozen report](PROVERBS_30_1_CTAT_REVIEW_2026-10-10.md) instead offers
repeated weariness with an ability/success ending, and separately an
Ithiel-based accepted-text interpretation. Fox's exhaustion proposal cannot
be merged with those into one scholarly consensus. The earlier report's
statement that the available preview cannot supply target commentary remains
true of that preview, but no longer defines the project's access ceiling.
