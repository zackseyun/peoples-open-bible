# Joshua passage order and the duplication alternative

## Judgment for POB

The [Joshua ending comparison](JOSHUA_24_ENDING_COMPARISON_2026-10-17.md)
established meaningful literary alternatives without deciding their priority.
A directly consulted statement by Michaël N. van der Meer now identifies a
specific competing explanation for the connected Qumran passage order:
duplication rather than relocation. This improves the test we should perform;
it does not establish that his explanation is correct. Hebrew, English, order,
reader notes and canon remain unchanged.

## What the author actually argues

In [“Septuagint Research in the Netherlands,” JSCS 51 (2018), 21–40](https://septuaginta.uni-goettingen.de/static/ioscs/pdf/volumes/jscs51.pdf),
van der Meer summarizes his own Joshua research at 34–35. He regards the Greek
translator as an interpreter and reviser of an already layered Hebrew text.
He also proposes that the 4QJoshuaᵃ scribe duplicated parts of Joshua 8:30–35
between 5:1 and 5:2, analogously to expansions in pre-Samaritan texts. This is
his explanation of the early law-reading passage, not an additional manuscript
witness to it.

Note 70 distinguishes his 2001 Leiden dissertation from its revised 2004
publication, *Formation and Reformulation*. The consulted retrospective is not
that book's detailed demonstration. Root read the title page and complete
printed/PDF pages 31–36, including notes, and visually checked 34–35; no
full-article or full-book reading is claimed. Those pages do not provide an
independently measured fragment layout or settle the specific Joshua 24 ending.
The [follow-up packet](../sources/textual_restoration/comparisons/joshua_countermodel.2026-10-17.v1.json)
binds the source and inspected derivatives.

## Tests that distinguish the explanations

The following are our proposed tests, not results supplied by the retrospective.

| Question | Discriminating evidence | What cannot decide it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Did the Qumran copy relocate or duplicate the law reading? | Establish the early placement independently, then check whether the same copy preserves the corresponding later location, with enough continuity to establish inclusion or omission | An early occurrence alone; no fragment covering the later location |
| Did a Greek difference originate in Hebrew or in translation/revision? | Compare the exact clauses with direct Hebrew evidence and demonstrated local translation behavior; test both accounts against the same wording | Fluent retroversion or a general description of the translator |
| Does this settle the knives, death order or final continuation? | Separate target readings and transmission arguments for each ending unit, while retaining their literary connections | Extending a Joshua 5/8 explanation to every Joshua 24 difference |

If the later location is physically lost, duplication and relocation may remain
indistinguishable in this copy. A reconstructed column must not supply its own
proof: joins, margins and line lengths require independent constraints, and
competing restorations must be tested under the same ranges. Likewise, agreement
with a familiar sequence may reflect inheritance or harmonization. Neither
model wins merely by naming a plausible scribal activity.
