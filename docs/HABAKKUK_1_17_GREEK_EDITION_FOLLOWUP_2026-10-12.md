The original DJD VIII transcription now provides directly inspected published
support for **μάχαιραν αὐτοῦ**, “his sword,” in 8ḤevXIIgr XVII.18. This upgrades
the [earlier source comparison](HABAKKUK_1_17_SOURCE_COMPARISON_2026-10-12.md)
from a scholarly report of Greek sword to consultation of the edition itself.
It does not establish two independent Hebrew exemplars or settle which noun
is earliest. Source priority remains **HOLD**, with POB's net provisionally
retained. No source, main English or reader note changes in this follow-up.

## The Hebrew beside the Greek is a reconstruction

Section 15.1 begins on [p. 151](https://books.google.com/books?id=lLanEaS5u1IC&pg=PA151)
and expressly identifies the Hebrew in the right column as a reconstructed
Vorlage of R. Its [p. 152](https://books.google.com/books?id=lLanEaS5u1IC&pg=PA152)
Habakkuk 1:17 entry places MT/selected-LXX net against R's μάχαιραν and an
inferred חרב/ו. That Hebrew is the editor's back-translation, not letters of a
second Hebrew manuscript. The entry stands above the subsection for wholly
reconstructed comparisons, consistent with the noun's unbracketed presentation;
this placement still cannot resolve individual doubtful-letter dots.

Page 153 introduces comparison with Greek manuscripts and Hexaplaric sources
using Ziegler's apparatus. It defines agreement, similarity and correction
notation, then begins Jonah entries. It does not contain the target Habakkuk
entry. No complete modern Greek locus apparatus has been consulted here.

## Reconsidering source priority

Sword has stronger evidential standing than the earlier packet could establish:
the Hebrew pesher's published חרבו now has directly inspected Greek-edition
corroboration. Sword also fits the usual Hebrew drawing/emptying-verb pairing.
Its earlier attestation receives the method's modest chronological preference,
without counting a translated reading as directly surviving Hebrew.

The strongest contrary evidence remains the fishing sequence in Habakkuk
1:14–16 and net's wider transmitted support. Sword could be inherited wording
later assimilated to that sequence, or a military clarification of an unusual
net expression. Agreement could also involve dependence between traditions;
the existing Lim/Hartog alternatives have not been resolved by reading a
transcription. The Greek opening differs from the pesher, which weakens any
claim of verbatim clause identity but does not prove independence. Neither a
conjectural copying mechanism nor an ancient editor's motive has been observed.

Without the chronological preference, both transmission directions remain
plausible. With it, sword merits heightened scrutiny but the presently inspected
evidence does not discriminate sufficiently to replace the source. HOLD is
provisional retention, not a finding that net is historically best. No new
English preference assessment is justified before selecting or interpreting the
complete source clause.
