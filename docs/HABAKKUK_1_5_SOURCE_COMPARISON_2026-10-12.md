# Habakkuk 1 5 nations and despisers source comparison

Habakkuk 1:5 has a consequential address difference: the retained Hebrew asks
the audience to look among the nations; the consulted Greek addresses
despisers. The published Hebrew preservation supports neither a simple
two-against-one witness count nor an automatic replacement. Mur88 preserves
the noun **גוים**, nations. In 1QpHab the proposed **בוגדים**, traitors, is
entirely supplied in the biblical quotation, although the following commentary
does concern traitors.

**Decision:** retain the current WLC source and main English provisionally;
earliest-wording priority remains HOLD. The Greek address merits reader
disclosure, but it does not uniquely recover a Hebrew replacement. This is a
published-reading comparison under
[method 2.0.2](TEXTUAL_ADJUDICATION_METHOD.md), not a new decipherment, full
Habakkuk witness census, canonical application or canon decision.

## Hebrew preservation and interpretation

The versioned [Mur88 transcription](https://lexicon.qumran-digital.org/transcriptions/Mur._88/2026-05-21/index.html?v=2026-05-21),
column XVIII line 3, publishes **[ב]גוים**. The noun is outside supply brackets
and has no uncertainty marks; the preposition is supplied. Lines XVIII.1–5
were checked, including the preceding line's closing bracket. This is positive
direct-Hebrew attestation of nations, not a WLC parallel displayed beneath the
physical row. It does not establish the complete prefixed form as surviving.
The verse ending in XVIII.4 is supplied.

In [1QpHab](https://lexicon.qumran-digital.org/transcriptions/1QpHab/2026-05-21/index.html),
I.16 reads **[-- ראו בוגדים וה]ב֯י֯[טו]**. None of the traitors noun survives;
the uncertain surviving letters belong to the following looking verb. I.17
is supplied throughout. II.1 preserves the final reporting verb before the
separator and the subsequent commentary. The inspected I.10–17 and II.1–17
keep quotation, commentary and supplies distinct.

Commentary at II.1, II.3 and II.5 refers repeatedly to traitors, supporting the
editor's reconstruction indirectly. That reconstruction and its supporting
commentary are not independent attestations. An interpreter can apply a
prophecy to sectarian traitors without quoting that noun in the lost address.
Conversely, the commentary is relevant evidence and must not be discarded
merely because it is not a continuous biblical copy.

These are edition readings, not independently read manuscript pixels. The
May 21, 2026 release date is not a physical-copy date. No precise chronological
advantage is assigned from these entry displays; provisional base retention
must not masquerade as an older-witness victory.

## Greek wording and the local translation control

The publisher's [Rahlfs Hanhart 2006 selected text](https://www.die-bibel.de/bibel/LXX/HAB.1)
at 1:5 has **οἱ καταφρονηταί**, despisers. It also adds a disappearance or
destruction imperative and explicitly expresses the first-person subject.
It is not simply the Hebrew verse with one noun replaced. The selected text
does not display the target apparatus or identify each manuscript's reading.

Within the same chapter, Greek 1:13 uses **ἐπὶ καταφρονοῦντας** where the
stored WLC has **בוגדים**, treacherous people. This local translation control
makes a traitors-family Hebrew source at 1:5 plausible. It does not establish
its exact spelling, article or morphology, or prove that it stood in the
translator's exemplar. Translator behavior is a control, not another witness.

Two serious explanations remain: a different Hebrew address behind the Greek,
or an interpretive address using contempt language already present in the
book. A third possibility, later Greek influence from Acts, requires actual
apparatus evidence and is not asserted as an observed transmission event.
No missing Hebrew letters are selected from these possibilities. In particular,
the plene form בוגדים must not be described as differing from בגוים by only
one letter; that proposed copying comparison would require a defective form.

## Acts reception and the implicit subject

Stored SBLGNT Acts 13:40 frames 13:41 as a warning from the prophets. The
quotation retains despisers, but differs from the consulted Habakkuk Greek:
it omits the intervening looking command and the companion astonishment
expression, changes the conjunction and pronoun order, repeats work, and adds
a final explicit to you. Greek reception and contextual reuse therefore do
not supply an independent Hebrew manuscript or an identical Habakkuk copy.
No Acts source or reader record is changed by this comparison.

The retained Hebrew work clause lacks an explicit first-person pronoun.
Habakkuk 1:6 supplies useful speaker context for POB's English I, but does not
restore a missing pronoun in 1:5. [NET's own notes](https://classic.net.bible.org/verse.php?book=Hab&chapter=1&verse=5),
also checked in the [licensed current presentation](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Habakkuk%201%3A4-6&version=NET),
discuss rare pronoun ellipsis and hypothetical loss of אני or אנכי. Those
grammatical and copying proposals are not attested replacement letters.
Neither consulted note set makes a Qumran/despisers claim at this verse.
An impersonal English rendering remains possible, as POB's existing note
already recognizes; supplying I is not grammatically mandatory in English.

## Reader consequences and application boundary

Looking among the nations directs attention toward the surprising Chaldean
action explained in 1:6. Addressing despisers instead makes contemptuous
hearers the explicit target of the warning. This difference warrants a note
even while source priority is held. A future note should distinguish Greek
wording from a reconstructed Hebrew address, and should not imply that the
Greek's additional imperative also survives in Hebrew.

The current record has a separate demonstrable anchor defect: note b explains
the doing-work clause but follows astounded. Its lexical and theological
astonishment choices also retain wording absent from the actual main English.
The rationales claiming I is required or necessary are too absolute. Correcting
those issues needs a frozen full-record candidate, archived prior review fields
and actual reader-export verification; it does not require choosing despisers
as earliest Hebrew. Current high-agreement metadata does not certify a new
candidate. Neighboring Habakkuk and Acts note defects are separate scope.

No source, main English, note, generation prompt or historical review field is
changed here. The next discriminating source acquisition is the actual target
Hebrew and Greek critical apparatus, with sigla, correction states and local
versional evidence. Another vote on this same selected Greek and missing lemma
would not advance priority. No ImageGen output supplies textual evidence.
