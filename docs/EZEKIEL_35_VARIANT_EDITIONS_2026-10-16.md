# Ezekiel 35: test the variant-edition argument against its witnesses

## Result

The Greek and Hebrew forms differ in more than isolated vocabulary: the
accusation in 35:10, the extent of the final judgment in 35:14-15, and the
addressee of its recognition formula must be compared together. Direct
Papyrus 967 inspection confirms consequential Greek differences. It does not
recover their exact Hebrew antecedent or establish which edition is earlier.

A specific evidential qualification changes the next research step:
**Masada's supposed corroborating noun at 35:14 has its distinguishing tav
restored, not preserved, in the consulted digital edition.** The same witness
preserves substantial wording of the longer 35:15a. Do not use it as an
unqualified Hebrew witness to the shorter Greek unit.

No canonical source, English, notes, chapter order or canon is changed here.
Source priority remains unresolved. The existing English of 35:14 needs a
separate temporal/subject comparison; a different English interpretation is
possible without reconstructing a different Hebrew text.

## Study acquired and read

Karin Finsterbusch, ["Traditional Textual Criticism Reconsidered"](https://doi.org/10.1628/hebai-2020-0020),
*Hebrew Bible and Ancient Israel* 9 (2020), 334-347, was acquired through the
[University of Tuebingen deposit](https://bibliographie.uni-tuebingen.de/xmlui/bitstream/handle/10900/130061/Finsterbusch_061.pdf?sequence=1).
Root read all fourteen pages, including all 23 notes and both translation
appendices. Printed 336-347 were additionally inspected as complete rendered
pages to check Hebrew/Greek forms, emphasis and reference boundaries. This is
not a newly consulted Zimmerli commentary, Talmon edition or modern apparatus.

The study argues that whole-passage comparison reveals patterned differences
which verse-by-verse selection can miss. It proposes a different Hebrew base
for the Greek and regards the two forms as literary editions. Its broader
argument rejects recovery of an original complete Ezekiel from the available
evidence, while explicitly allowing correction of demonstrable copying errors
and some relative chronological judgments (344-345).

We accept the need for passage-level comparison, not an automatic inference
that every coherent Greek difference originated in Hebrew. Nor does uncertainty
about an entire original book make every local source judgment impossible.
The historical target and confidence must remain explicit in our corpus.

and a native-coordinate region `1800,3400,3200,3900`, exported at width 1800.
Canvas numbers are viewer locators, not biblical or ancient page numbers.
These are context-informed checks of published readings, not blinded fresh
decipherment. No generated image or generated letter supplied evidence.

| Unit | Direct comparison | Consequence and remaining uncertainty |

| 35:10 | P967 visibly has `ΔΙΑΤΟΥΤΟΕΙΠΕΝϹΟΙ`, conventionally divided as "therefore he said to you." POB's retained Hebrew has יען אמרך, rendered "Because you said." | A real change in causal structure and grammatical person, not an English synonym. | 35:14-15 | The joy expression leads to `ΕΡΗΜΟΝΠΟΙΗϹΩϹΕΕΡΗΜΟΝ` and then `ΕϹΗΟΡΟϹϹΗΕΙΡ`: making the addressee desolate proceeds directly to Mount Seir's desolation. | The longer Hebrew comparison about Edom's rejoicing over Israel is absent at this physically continuous point. The article also discusses number alternations at 35:4, 9, 12 and 13, and
shorter wording in 11 and 13. Its paired chapter translations were read in
full. This pass does not claim an independently transcribed complete chapter
or an exhaustive collation of all Greek manuscripts. In particular, its
report of Alexandrinus's different 35:10 wording remains a reported reading,
not an inspected Alexandrinus photograph.

## Masada: the decisive character is supplied

Finsterbusch 343 cites Talmon 1997, 58*, for MasEz כשמחת, partially supporting
her retroversion בשמחת behind Greek ἐν τῇ εὐφροσύνῃ. The actual consulted
[Mas1d transcription, version 2026-05-21, column 1 line 5](https://lexicon.qumran-digital.org/transcriptions/Mas1d/2026-05-21/index.html?v=2026-05-21#c394328-i394359)
prints **כ֯שמח[ת]**. Stable word ID `394364` (display span `395029`)
marks the kaf uncertain and the tav restored. The [project's sigla](https://lexicon.qumran-digital.org/faq/v1/en/index.html)
explicitly distinguish both from securely legible letters. Root and a second
reader independently checked the primary row, not its parallel MT row.

Even if the restored tav is justified, that supplies a noun proposal, not the
different initial bet of the Greek retroversion. Its justification requires
Talmon's actual restoration argument and the manuscript image. We have not
read that page or inspected the Masada plate in this pass and do not declare
his restoration mistaken.

Moreover, the same column preserves the longer clause absent from P967:
line 6 includes כשמחתך לנ֯[ח]לת בית֯ (word IDs `394370`-`394372`), and
line 7 has unbracketed כן אעשה לך (IDs `394377`-`394379`). Intervening
damage prevents calling every word intact, but cannot turn the clause into
a wholly reconstructed presence. The final plural וידעו also appears in line 8.
This is a local witness profile, not an automatic vote for either whole edition.

## Competing explanations and the current POB English

At 35:10 the study argues that the translator's normal rendering of Hebrew
יען ... לכן makes the Greek's repeated "therefore" evidence for another
Hebrew arrangement. This is a useful testable argument, stronger than merely
preferring smoother Greek. It still needs an actual local translation-pattern
inventory and Greek transmission controls before the inferred Hebrew לכן
is promoted into the corpus. General literalness does not exclude a local
Greek change, and literary coherence alone does not establish priority.

At 35:14 the proposed contrast is less exclusive than the study suggests.
The retained WLC form is כ/שמח, annotated Qal infinitive construct in the
vendored XML (word `26in1`, `HR/Vqc`), not a finite Hebrew past tense.
[GKC 114e and 114q](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Gesenius%27_Hebrew_Grammar/114._The_Infinitive_Construct)
allow temporal כ with an infinitive and contextual determination of time.
This grammatical control does not identify the translator's actual Vorlage,
but keeps a temporal interpretation of the retained Hebrew available.

At this report's input baseline, POB says **"As the whole earth rejoiced, I will make you desolate."**
Its lexical fields instead select "rejoices," and its note offers
"When the whole earth rejoices." These are not equivalent accounts of
whether the joy precedes or accompanies the announced devastation. The
article's MT appendix instead takes the whole land as the object of rejoicing;
that interpretation, too, requires comparison rather than automatic adoption.
The main-text/lexical mismatch is real; no prior model agreement resolves it.

Next compare 35:14-15 as a unit under the unchanged Hebrew: whose joy, over
what, and at what time? Keep three questions separate: English tense and
syntax, the noun/infinitive textual hypothesis, and the longer clause's
historical priority. Do not remove 35:15a simply because the Greek is shorter,
or add "their God" to a Hebrew-labelled text without a justified source decision.

The subsequent [joy-clause comparison and application record](EZEKIEL_35_14_JOY_ENGLISH_APPLICATION_2026-10-16.md)
addresses this follow-up. It distinguishes its English decision from this
report's unresolved literary-edition priority and records implementation status
separately. The quotations and hashes above remain historical baseline evidence.
