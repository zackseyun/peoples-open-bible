# Ecclesiastes 8:10 Hebrew and version comparison

The disputed word can change the verse from the wicked being forgotten to boasting or receiving praise. A Hebrew bet reading, וישתבחו, is reported in published manuscript collations; it is not merely a modern conjecture. The current POB record follows the Masoretic kaf reading, וישתכחו. The full alternative below remains unapplied pending independent review of the source preference and English.

This is a reassessment of a known variant, not a claim of newly discovered ink, an original autograph, or a reason to change the biblical canon.

## Hebrew manuscript reports need qualified interpretation

De Rossi's complete pages report Kennicott 178 and an earlier reading in 107; re-collated Kennicott 433, 471, 475 and 553; De Rossi 16, 249, 272, 331, 613, 699, 729, 780 and 892; qualified observations in 33, 380, 579 and the earlier state of 857; external 26, 60, 66 and 133; and a Soncino Mahzor assigned to the fifteenth century.

These are identifiers from different collections, not an established count of independent witnesses. The conventions distinguish earlier and corrected states and uncertain observations. Most importantly, Kennicott 553 and De Rossi 331 report bet in the text with kaf as the marginal qere. A tally that presents them as uniformly bet is wrong. Kennicott 109's וישכחו is a separate form.

The root researcher and Hebrew acquisition agent read both complete target pages and the relevant volume I conventions. This verifies the published report, not the underlying manuscripts' glyphs, modern shelfmarks, individual dates or correction dates. The source-selection question still needs a target-level modern critical apparatus and, where obtainable, identifiable manuscript observations. BHQ's volume scope was verified, but its Ecclesiastes 8:10 apparatus was not read. Kennicott's original target entry was not read.

De Rossi discusses the possibility that the Talmudic interpretation influenced later copies, while the Greek could support an older alternative. That is a historical hypothesis, not a demonstrated explanation for every bet copy. Gittin also interprets “buried” as “collected” in its Titus narrative; its alternate-word formula cannot be treated as a normal Hebrew text transcription.

## Versions are not interchangeable Hebrew votes

The Greek praise reading is substantive. The inspected Greek book distinguishes forgetting at Ecclesiastes 2:16 and 9:5 from praising at 4:2 and 8:15. This makes “praised” at 8:10 more than an ordinary translation of “forgotten,” although it does not establish the exact Hebrew Vorlage or rule out interpretation or assimilation to nearby praise language.

The Greek Ecclesiastes translation must not inherit the Pentateuch's translation date. The inspected NETS introduction describes an extremely formal translation and kai-ge affiliation while treating attribution to Aquila as unsettled. Fourth- and fifth-century Greek copies are dates of copies, not dates of an original Hebrew bet reading. Swete's selected text and Rahlfs/Hanhart are editions of related evidence, not two independent witnesses. The Gentry 2019 preview lacks the target apparatus; it is not evidence that the edition lacks one.

The Latin praise wording contains an additional interpretive clause. Neither its “righteous works” nor the Greek next clause's causal wording should silently enter a Hebrew draft. The consulted Latin text has no inspected target apparatus and is not an independently established Hebrew Vorlage.

CAL's Peshitta is an electronic selected edition with specified corrections; it is not a diplomatic transcription of manuscript 7a1. CAL's Targum incorporates Paris 110, later corrections and editorial work; it is not an unchanged diplomatic Paris 110 text. The catalogue dates the Qohelet section's colophon to 1455, not the Targum's composition or every word in CAL's edited text. The Targum adds annihilation, righteous inhabitants and Gehinnom burning, making wholesale retroversion unsafe.

## Grammar and context

The BDB transcriptions consulted distinguish Hebrew שכח “forget” from a related Aramaic “find.” Under שבח, the Hebrew Hitpael controls at Psalm 106:47 and 1 Chronicles 16:35 support boasting or glorying; the Piel examples in Ecclesiastes support praising. A reflexive Hebrew alternative cannot automatically be translated as Greek passive “were praised.” These are identified BDB transcriptions, not a newly consulted HALOT entry or verified BDB facsimile.

All 17 records in Ecclesiastes 8 and five lexical or thematic controls were read and pinned. Delayed punishment in 8:11, the contrast in 8:12–13, and reversed fortunes in 8:14 make praise of the wicked contextually intelligible. Nevertheless, forgetting after death is explicit elsewhere in this book at 2:16 and 9:5. Context does not make either reading impossible. Ecclesiastes 8:15's praise of enjoyment supplies a possible assimilation explanation, not proof of it.

The surrounding burial, coming, holy place and going syntax is difficult independently of the target consonant. The candidate keeps the baseline's wicked subject and “had come and gone” interpretation; it does not settle a proposed switch to upright people or a temple narrative. Adding “had” before boasting places it with earlier activity rather than suggesting that buried people boast, but that timing remains an English interpretation requiring review.

## Full unapplied alternative

The normalized candidate changes only וישתכחו to וישתבחו. Its full English is:

> And so I saw the wicked buried; they had come and gone from the holy place[a], and had boasted[b] in the city where they had done so. This too is vanity.[c]

The candidate preserves the holy-place and hebel notes, replaces the target note with a disclosure of the alternate Hebrew and passive Greek/Latin, preserves the old revision entries, and archives old source, translation and review metadata by reference to the immutable baseline Git object. Old review results do not certify this candidate. The new generation metadata identifies the exact model version as unavailable rather than inventing one.

## Review and stopping conditions

The independent reviewer must decide both which source wording is warranted and whether the complete candidate English follows it, with explicit attention to reflexive versus passive meaning, subject, time sequence and the competing forgetting context. Mechanical source composition and a simulated reader export cannot establish an earliest reading.

A HOLD is a valid substantive outcome. Do not repeatedly send unchanged inputs until a reviewer approves, and do not substitute a note-only canonical change for rejection of the full alternative. A further review is warranted only by materially new target apparatus evidence, identified manuscript observations, or a genuinely revised grammatical argument and candidate.
