# Ezekiel 40 49 width and source comparison

POB currently gives a width of **twelve cubits**, but its declared WLC source
has **עַשְׁתֵּי עֶשְׂרֵה**, eleven. Twelve is a serious Greek reading, not a
lexical translation of that Hebrew compound. The working recommendation is
eleven under provisionally retained WLC, with the Greek alternative disclosed.
This does not establish eleven as the earliest attainable reading, and this
research makes no canonical change.

consulted target apparatuses or three independent manuscript votes. In
particular, Latin agreement on width does not imply agreement on stairs.

The number compound is controlled by the retained Hebrew at Exodus 26:7–9:
eleven curtains are subsequently grouped five plus six. Numbers 29:13,17,20,23
provides the masculine counterpart in a thirteen, twelve, eleven, ten sequence.
Root read these complete local source clauses, not merely modern English
renderings. These shared-base controls clarify Hebrew usage; they do not add
manuscripts of Ezekiel. The research agent and root also consulted Sefaria's
actual digital BDB entry BDB07732 and Klein entry P01255 through its
[lexical API](https://www.sefaria.org/api/words/%D7%A2%D7%A9%D7%AA%D7%99?lexicon=Brown-Driver-Briggs%20Hebrew%20and%20English%20Lexicon).
The digital BDB citation
erroneously says Ezekiel 40:9; it must not be repeated as a correct locator or
as an independently inspected printed page. No fresh HALOT entry was read.

The retained stairs expression is the relative clause
`אֲשֶׁר יַעֲלוּ אֵלָיו`. Substituting number עשר for relative אשר is a separate
proposed Hebrew emendation. Greek ten and Latin eight cannot silently become
recovered Hebrew, and correcting the width need not supply either stair count.

## Why Greek twelve remains worth comparing

The [Cambridge commentary's target argument](https://biblehub.com/commentaries/ezekiel/40-49.htm)
uses twelve to obtain a hundred-cubit total:
`5 + 12 + 6 + 40 + 2 + 20 + 6 + 4 + 5 = 100`.
Root checked the complete retained source clauses at 40:48 and
41:1–5,9,13. Their dimensions support the listed components; assigning them
that orientation and sequence remains an architectural interpretation. The
digital commentary has incorrect chapter-40 links for several chapter-41
components; those links were not accepted as the source contexts.

The same page's Keil and Delitzsch discussion retains eleven using a different
orientation and wall-thickness reconstruction. Its charge that Greek altered
the measurements is an argument, not demonstrated copying history. Both
target discussions were read, not just search snippets. Architectural
coherence makes Greek twelve a consequential candidate, but does not decide
between a different Hebrew exemplar and Greek regularization. Target critical
apparatus and transmission evidence remain the reopening gate for historical
priority. Retained WLC is provisional, not automatically superior.
