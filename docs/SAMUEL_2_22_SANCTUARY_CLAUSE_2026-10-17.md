# 1 Samuel 2:22 and the women at the sanctuary

Date: 2026-10-17. Published-source comparison, not a new decipherment.
Baseline: `a82570338f0e729c6a7ec78fe5f290c1ec98baf3`.

## Judgment and potential English impact

The **shorter form is provisionally preferred at this clause boundary**.
The available Hebrew space argument and short Greek text, together with the
shift toward Pentateuchal sanctuary language, make expansion a better working
explanation than a Greek-translator-only omission. This is not an autograph
claim: early Hebrew abbreviation or deliberate mitigation remain possible,
and Josephus positively establishes early knowledge of the longer accusation.
No canonical source, English, note or approval is changed by this comparison.

The proposed difference is consequential: the short form reports the sons'
misconduct without this particular sexual accusation or reference to women
at the tent. It does not excuse the sacrificial abuses in 2:12–17 or remove
the condemnation in 2:23–36. Nor does a textual variant establish that the
accusation was historically true or false.

For a later explicit critical-text proposal, retain the current WLC opening
and omit only `ואת אשר ישכבון את הנשים הצבאות פתח אהל מועד`.
The resulting consonantal research candidate is:

> ועלי זקן מאד ושמע את כל אשר יעשון בניו לכל ישראל

Its corresponding main-English candidate is: “Eli was very old, and he heard
all that his sons were doing to all Israel.” This is an **eclectic candidate
based on WLC minus one unit**, not the diplomatic text of 4QSamᵃ or a claim that
all witnesses have this entire verse. Other opening variants, including the
scroll's added age and differing verbs, are separate questions. Any application
must identify the critical source honestly and disclose the longer tradition.

## Early longer reception and competing explanations

The actual edited Greek of Josephus, *Antiquities* **5.337–342**, supplies an
important positive control. At **5.339**, women come for worship; the sons
mistreat them sexually, using force with some and gifts with others. This is
an early longer **retelling**, not a quotation of the complete Hebrew clause
or an independent continuous-text Hebrew manuscript. Its added force/gifts
must not be inserted into POB's Hebrew-based English.

Driver printed 33–34 / PDF 165–166 argues for expansion from the tent wording
against the surrounding house/temple/doors setting. Direct current Hebrew
controls confirm בית/היכל and doors at 1:7,9,24; 3:3,15. Exodus 38:8 contains
the related women's-service wording and `פתח אהל מועד`. That supports a
possible adaptation, not proof of its direction or a dated author: an original
author could also deliberately link Shiloh with Pentateuchal sanctuary language.

Driver also reports a marginal Targum mitigation: delaying women's offerings.
The underlying Targum page was not consulted; this is a reported reception
example, not proof that the short Greek or Hebrew arose through censorship.
Conversely, a Hebrew short form supported by spatial reconstruction weakens
an explanation confined to the Greek translator. An older shared Hebrew
omission would remain possible. No obvious repeated-ending skip has been
established for this whole clause. Neither brevity, harmonization nor theological
usefulness is by itself decisive.

The old scroll's chronology gives a modest tilt **conditional on the spatial
reconstruction**. Removing that tilt still leaves the local expansion argument,
but weakens the preference; it does not erase the longer form's early reception.
The result is a provisional clause-level preference, not a certified complete
earliest verse or an estimate with invented numerical confidence.
