# Genesis 6:3: remaining or contending?

The bounded comparison prefers **“My Spirit will not remain in man forever”**
provisionally, with the competing contending sense disclosed. This changes the
English interpretation, not the saved Hebrew letters. Original Hebrew spelling,
spirit/breath semantics and the scope of the 120 years remain unsettled.

## Why this can affect understanding

Contending presents a divine dispute with humanity. Remaining permits a reading
about divine spirit or life-giving breath remaining in mortal humans. Neither
reading is merely a smoother synonym for the other. The flesh and days clauses
matter, as does the local reference to flesh containing the breath/spirit of life
in Genesis 6:17. Genesis 7:22 provides another relevant association. Genesis 2:7
uses a different breath noun; it is not an identical lexical occurrence.

The strongest case for contending is the judicial/disputational Hebrew root and
the wickedness/judgment context in 6:5–7. But the unusual verb form and its
construction with the preposition need explanation. The OSHB lemma assignment
does not prove that this English sense is correct. The actual
[BDB dictionary section](https://biblehub.com/hebrew/1777.htm), excluding the
page's generated topical material, acknowledges judicial uses but questions
contending here. The [NET editors' actual notes](https://classic.net.bible.org/verse.php?book=Gen&chapter=6&verse=3)
also explain the lexical difficulty. Their cited cognate studies were not
independently consulted, and no fresh HALOT consultation is claimed.

The strongest objection to remaining is that an ancient translator or commentator
could be interpreting different wording or clarifying the difficult Hebrew.
Nevertheless, local spirit/flesh context and the locative construction give it a
modest working advantage. This is an explicit, revisable interpretation of the
unchanged WLC base, not a claim to have solved its etymology.

## What the early evidence does and does not establish

The actual [published 4Q252 transcription](https://lexicon.qumran-digital.org/transcriptions/4Q252/2026-05-21/index.html)
has a preserved, unbracketed dwelling verb with resh where WLC has nun. This
reworked quotation also adapts surrounding wording and explicitly connects 120
years to the flood. It is relevant Hebrew evidence, not a continuous Genesis copy
or proof of original wording. Qumran-Digital's attribution is DFG465277421,
Qumran-Wörterbuch/Abegg predecessor, CC BY-SA4.0.

The selected [Rahlfs–Hanhart Greek](https://www.die-bibel.de/bibel/LXX/GEN.6)
and [Weber–Gryson Latin](https://www.die-bibel.de/bibel/VUL/GEN.6) use remaining.
That establishes translated meaning in these editions, not a uniquely recoverable
Hebrew spelling or two independent Hebrew manuscript votes. Modern edition dates
are not ancient copy dates; no complete Greek/Latin apparatus was collated.

[Brooke's authorial article](https://bibleproject.huji.ac.il/sites/default/files/he_bible_project/files/brooke_g.j.pdf),
*Some Remarks on 4Q252 and the Text of Genesis*, Textus19(1998), pp8–10,
weighs an inherited resh reading against exegetical replacement. He favors an
inherited secondary variant while allowing original nun. An early inherited
variant is therefore not automatically the original. Only the relevant rendered
pages and introductory pp1–3 were consulted, not the entire article or its cited
works. The scan corrects the search snippet's misleading title and spelling.

The pinned biblical discovery dataset has no exact 6:3 reference tag. Actual
6Q1 and 4Q6 bodies do not cover this verse; the published Genesis comparison
lists 4Q252 and a Samaritan column. That column retains nun but is not a new
independent Samaritan manuscript collation. This is a bounded coverage check,
not a census of all discovered sources. Missing coverage is not an omission.
No manuscript pixels, ImageGen reconstruction or fresh paleographic reading
were used. Nothing here is a newly discovered variant.

## Review and application boundaries

considers the reversed order with current/proposed identities withheld and no
canonical YAML/history access. It also prefers remaining provisionally. Biblical
familiarity prevents complete knowledge blinding; agreement is not statistical
proof or manuscript evidence. The later Brooke consultation is separately
attributed and not smuggled into that review's approval.

The full candidate separately removes “therefore.” This does not declare that
Hebrew coordination can never imply a result. Neutral clause connection here
avoids making a particular causal interpretation compulsory. The days clause
neither adds a countdown nor imposes a universal age cap. Noah and Shem's long
post-flood lives challenge an immediately universal ceiling; they do not refute
every possible lifespan interpretation. Both possibilities remain noted.

spirit/breath, competing verb sense, generic humanity, the possible also element,

## Efficient continuation and canon

This pass produces a consequential, source-grounded English proposal rather than
another inventory or a repeated preference loop. Reopen it for discriminating
lexical, contextual or witness evidence, not extra agreement votes. Higher token
use does not guarantee recovery of lost wording or resolution of real ambiguity.
