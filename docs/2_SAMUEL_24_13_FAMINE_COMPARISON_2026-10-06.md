# 2 Samuel 24 13 famine duration comparison

The selected Hebrew has seven years of famine; the consulted Greek editions
have three. This is a consequential source comparison, not merely a discrepancy
with Chronicles. Earliest-wording priority remains unresolved. POB's Hebrew,
English and notes are unchanged in this bounded pass.

## Competing explanations and decision

Three has a strong contextual case: the alternatives become three years,
three months and three days, and Chronicles also has three years. Greek Samuel
attestation means the proposal is not solely an import from Chronicles. A
possible Hebrew source with three remains a reconstruction; Greek does not
preserve that hypothetical Hebrew spelling.

The strongest objection is that the same symmetry and parallel could have
prompted assimilation during Hebrew or Greek transmission. Conversely, seven
could be secondary, but this pass establishes no copying mechanism or local
pattern explaining that direction. Harder or asymmetrical does not automatically
mean earlier. The age of a translation is not the date of its consulted copies;
Latin agreement with seven needs a dependence assessment before being counted
as independent source support.

The checked contexts are Samuel 24:10–17, Chronicles 21:9–15 and Samuel 21:1.
Chronicles differs in more than this numeral and remains a distinct literary
account. Samuel 21:1's earlier famine does not justify an invented chronological
calculation to reconcile seven with three.

Outcome: retain the Hebrew base provisionally and hold critical-source
replacement. Edition-reading attestation is secure; priority is not established.
Three would shorten the offered famine and align the numerical sequence, but
that difference alone establishes no new theology.

The current note mentions only Chronicles, and all three note anchors occur
before the quoted alternatives. These are disclosure leads, not proof that
three is earlier. A later application needs its exact candidate, archived old
review inputs and reader-export verification. Inherited agreement scores cannot
certify this newly investigated question. No source or English change is applied.

Reopen priority with a manuscript-specific Greek numeral apparatus, relevant
revision/quotation evidence and a published local transmission argument that can
distinguish preservation from assimilation. Identify copies and hands before
dating or grouping them. Do not repeat these negative Qumran checks without
changed data or obtain more model votes on the same symmetry argument.
