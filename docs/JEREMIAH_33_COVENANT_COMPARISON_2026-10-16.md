# Jeremiah 33 covenant block comparison

## Result and consequence for POB

Jeremiah 33:14–26 presents a consequential literary-form question, not merely
an alternative English gloss. Parts of 33:16–20 are attested in 4Q72, while the
consulted shorter Greek continuous text moves directly from the material of
33:13 to 34:1. Swete also reports a longer Greek rendering in the Marchalianus
margin, with revision attribution and critical signs. Neither “medieval Hebrew
invention” nor “absent everywhere in Greek” describes this evidence accurately.

The source and English remain provisionally retained; this comparison neither
deletes the thirteen verses nor certifies them as the earliest attainable form.
Its contribution is a passage-specific ancient Hebrew control, a checked Greek
junction and opposing Greek marginal evidence, plus a connected interpretation
test. The [evidence record](../sources/textual_restoration/comparisons/jeremiah33_covenant.2026-10-16.v1.json)
pins the inputs and separates observed published wording from supplied text.
No canonical verse or reader note changes in this pass.

## Ancient Hebrew inclusion and its exact limits

Root and a delegated source reader checked the actual
[Qumran-Digital 4Q72 transcription](https://lexicon.qumran-digital.org/transcriptions/4Q72/2026-05-21/index.html?v=2026-05-21),
fragment 55 ii, all five primary rows and their separate parallel references.
The diplomatic rows, not the pointed parallel text, provide the following
conservative controls.

| Line and alignment | Published surviving core | Limit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1, 33:16–17 | Divine name and start of the righteousness expression | The rest of that expression and the following David/no-cutoff clause are supplied in QDR |
| 2, 33:17–18 | Man, sitting and throne wording, followed by the start of “house” | The continuation, Levitical priests and no-lack clause are supplied |
| 3, 33:18 | Before me, offering a burnt offering and burning-language | Grain offering, sacrifice and the continuous-time ending are supplied in QDR |
| 4, 33:19–20 | Word-of-Yahweh-to-Jeremiah formula | The following speech/covenant opening is supplied |
| 5, 33:20 | Parts of the covenant/night expression | Partial supplies and an uncertain letter remain; not a complete covenant sentence |

This is positive inclusion evidence inside the longer block, not thirteen
preserved verses or direct ink for every priestly promise. DJD XV (1997),
printed p. 200 / PDF p. 216, identifies the same fragment's column XXV as
33:16–20. Its preceding column XXIV has only one surviving letter and cannot
certify 33:14–15. Its line-four comment allows an unidentified missing or
supralinear word within a lacuna because the reconstructed line is long; no
particular omission is observed.

DJD p. 182 / PDF p. 198 tentatively dates the semiformal hand to the early
Herodian period, approximately the latter first century BCE. This is the
edition's qualified physical-copy date, not a date for composing the block.
Root and the source reader visually checked those complete pages. The source
reader also visually checked conventions pp. 4–5; root read their extracted
text. Square-bracket supplies do not establish preserved wording. DJD and QDR
present some supply endpoints differently; their displays remain separate
rather than being flattened into a new diplomatic transcription. A physical
photograph/edition cross-check is needed to resolve those endpoints. They are
two presentations of one scroll, not independent witnesses.

## The short Greek junction and longer marginal rendering

In Swete III, third edition (1905), complete printed pp. 320–321 / PDF
pp. 344–345 show Greek 40:13 followed by 41:1, aligned by content with Hebrew
33:13 followed by 34:1. The selected continuous text lacks the intervening
block. The publisher's [RH chapter 40](https://www.die-bibel.de/bibel/LXX/JER.40)
likewise ends at the shepherd/flock unit corresponding to Hebrew 33:13.

The Swete apparatus after 40:13 prints the longer 33:14–26 rendering across
both pages. It introduces it with **θ′ and an asterisk** and concludes it
**Qmg**; at verse 20 it separately gives a **σ′** alternative. The same
volume's preface viii–ix / PDF pp. 12–13 and sigla PDF p. 24 identify Q as
Marchalianus, θ′ as Theodotion and σ′ as Symmachus, and distinguish marginal
Hexaplaric annotations from the continuous text. Both readers inspected the
target pages and this key.

These are specific published reports of Greek transmission, not a newly read
marginal hand, proof that the addition belonged to the original Greek
translation, or independent Hebrew attestation of a retroversion. A later
Hebrew-aligning Greek supplement can transmit the longer passage without
proving that the shorter continuous form lost it.

## Connected wording and competing histories

Root read the complete Erzberger article, *Old Testament Essays* 26/3 (2013),
pp. 663–683, including notes and bibliography, and visually checked its main
comparison tables. [The article](https://www.scielo.org.za/scielo.php?pid=S1010-99192013000300008&script=sci_arttext)
argues that the block develops linked traditions in 23:5–6 and 31:35–37,
rather than functioning as an isolated insertion. It favors expansion of the
long form while allowing changes in both traditions. Its proposed social
setting and devaluation of kingship are interpretive arguments, not manuscript
dates or recovered editorial intentions. Not every reference label in its
prose is reliable; the controls below use the actual passages.

Root independently compared WLC/current POB 33:10–26 and the connected units
23:5–6, 29:10, 31:14, 31:31–37, 33:9 and 34:1. The actual publisher's
[RH chapter 23](https://www.die-bibel.de/bibel/LXX/JER.23) and
[chapter 38](https://www.die-bibel.de/bibel/LXX/JER.38) supply the Greek controls.

| Connected feature | Direct comparison | Consequence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Royal language | Both WLC 23:5 and Greek 23:5 explicitly speak of reigning as king; WLC 33:15 lacks that expression, but 33:17 and 33:21 retain throne/reigning language | The difference is real; wholesale removal of kingship is not |
| Righteousness name | WLC 23:6 associates the name with the ruler; 33:16 uses feminine city reference; Greek 23:6 has the name Ιωσεδεκ with the Lord as naming subject | Do not silently import one passage's referent or Greek spelling into another |
| Cosmic guarantees | WLC 31:35–37 and Greek 38:35–37 arrange the corresponding units differently; Greek has elevation/lowering and explicit non-rejection where WLC has measurement/exploration and a rejection conditional | More than a verse-order change is involved |
| Law terminology | Greek 38:33 and 38:37 both use νόμοι; WLC 31:33 and 31:36 use תורה and חקים | A translation choice could create the verbal link; no distinct Hebrew source word is thereby recovered |
| Priestly control | WLC 31:14 has priests; Greek 38:14 explicitly has priests, sons of Levi | The shorter form cannot be explained by a blanket Greek rejection of Levitical service |

The shorter Greek form still contains the new-covenant promise, royal hope,
priestly abundance and Israel's enduring existence. Removing this particular
block does not remove all those themes. The longer Hebrew block adds their
explicit Davidic/priestly covenant coordination. A change in block selection
would therefore affect interpretation, not simply shorten the same message.

Expansion, loss in an ancestor or translation-stage omission remain competing
histories. Coherence and shared motifs can support a history but cannot
identify a scribe's motive or independently date a layer. Likewise a phrase
about a “name of joy” at 33:9 need not be a proper-name pointer whose only
possible completion is 33:16; that proposed dependency is not compulsory.
These are case-level objections, not a refutation of the article as a whole.
The separately located Jonathan F. Grothe article, “An Argument for the
Textual Genuineness of Jeremiah 33:14–26 (Massoretic Text),” *Concordia Journal*
7 (1981): 188–191, remains a bibliographic lead, not a consulted argument
([reported in Boda's bibliography/notes](https://jhsonline.org/index.php/jhs/article/download/5963/5016/12995)).
This is not Walter Groß's 1999 study cited by Erzberger. The modern full target
apparatus also remains unconsulted; bibliographic reports substitute for neither.

## A related excerpt is not a second Jeremiah copy

The source reader and root checked all seven primary rows of
[4Q252 section 5](https://lexicon.qumran-digital.org/transcriptions/4Q252/2026-05-21/index.html?v=2026-05-21).
It interprets Genesis 49's Judah promise using Davidic, throne, branch and
royal-covenant language. The cutoff expression includes supplied and uncertain
letters. It is not an explicit Jeremiah quotation, a continuous Jeremiah copy,
or preserved Levitical wording.

The source reader also checked WLC 1 Kings 2:4, 8:25, 9:5 and Psalm 132:11–12:
royal-promise wording is shared beyond Jeremiah. Attribution of 4Q252
exclusively to Jeremiah 33:17 is therefore unproved. This new attribution test
does not turn the previously consulted Genesis passage into a newly discovered
text or an independent vote for every word of the covenant block.

## Decision and discriminating next evidence

Attestation and priority have different outcomes. Ancient Hebrew inclusion of
material within the block is established at the published-edition level;
complete-word preservation, composition date and earliest-form priority are
not. Modest preference for an older physical witness does not automatically
defeat an earlier textual form transmitted in later copies.

Next check the actual Marchalianus marginal block, its attribution labels and
hands against a modern target apparatus, and reconcile the 4Q72 supply
endpoints against the physical evidence. These are specific unperformed tests.
Do not repeat the article reading, short junction, printed Greek supplement or
4Q252 attribution check as new evidence. No generated image, conjectural
Hebrew completion, source-corpus replacement or canon addition follows.
