# Habakkuk 2 16 Hebrew alternatives and English implications

The published ancient Hebrew quotation supports a real alternative to POB's
“show your uncircumcision”: **והרעל**, commonly understood as staggering or
reeling, rather than WLC **והערל**. Greek, Syriac and Latin controls also express
shaking, agitation or stupor. This is stronger than a conjecture-only lead,
but it does not yet establish the earliest Hebrew spelling. Retain the declared
source and main English provisionally; a reader disclosure is warranted.

This judgment follows the [full pesher quotation map](HABAKKUK_PESHER_QUOTATION_MAP_2026-10-12.md)
and [method 2.0.4](TEXTUAL_ADJUDICATION_METHOD.md). It is a published-reading
comparison, not fresh decipherment, an exhaustive apparatus or an application.
No canonical record changes in this packet.

## Lexical analysis is not letter restoration

The complete BDB verb entries were read in the reused 1907 Boston and New York
facsimile: printed 790/PDF 823 for ערל and printed 947/PDF 982 for רעל. The
former treats the retained Habakkuk form as a Niphal imperative concerning
uncircumcision/mockery, marks that interpretation doubtful, and proposes
**הֵרָעֵל**, “reel,” with a different consonant order. It cites Greek, Syriac
and named editors; those citations are not newly consulted individual witnesses.
The רעל entry directly discusses quivering in Nahum 2:4 and cross-refers to ערל.

Sefaria's complete BDB entries agree substantively with the facsimile. They
are controls on the same lexicon, not a second lexical authority. Its separately
labeled Klein Dictionary entry ערל II instead treats the written root through
lexicalized metathesis and a poisoning interpretation at Habakkuk 2:16. That
digital entry was read, but no printed Klein page or fresh HALOT entry was
verified. It keeps an unchanged-spelling interpretation in consideration; it
does not certify idiomatic “stagger” under our exact pointed form.

Root also checked the complete local WLC contexts at Habakkuk 2:15–17,
Nahum 2:4, Zechariah 12:2, Isaiah 51:17 and 22, and Psalm 60:5. Drinking,
reeling-cup language and shame support the reeling interpretation. Nakedness
in 2:15 also supports exposure/humiliation in 2:16. Related Hebrew uses establish
semantic plausibility, not this verse's manuscript letters. No ancient vowels
are recovered from the unpointed pesher.

## Greek readings and apparatus scope

Swete III, third edition 1905, printed 60/PDF 84, was read completely, with
adjacent pages 59–61. Its Vaticanus-oriented selected clause contains
**καρδίᾳ σαλεύθητι καὶ σείσθητι**, shaking in heart followed by another shaking
imperative. The target apparatus reports:

> καρδίᾳ σαλεύθητι] καὶ διασαλ. אᶜ·ᵇ vid (postea καρδ. σαλ. revoc.) om A

Alexandrinus's reported omission concerns the heart-plus-first-verb unit,
not the following **καὶ σείσθητι**. The Sinaiticus correction is qualified
**vid** and subsequently reverted. Swete's conventions distinguish that
correction from original-hand text; the historical c.b attribution is to a
seventh-century corrector, not automatically the age of the codex itself.
These are edition reports, not newly collated manuscript pixels.

The publisher's [Rahlfs–Hanhart 2006 text](https://www.die-bibel.de/bibel/LXX/HAB.2)
instead selects **καὶ διασαλεύθητι καὶ σείσθητι**. Its actual critical apparatus
was not acquired. Editions do not add manuscript votes. The uppercase sequences
ΚΑΙΔΙΑΣΑΛΕΥΘΗΤΙ and ΚΑΡΔΙΑΣΑΛΕΥΘΗΤΙ differ in one letter and word division;
internal Greek transmission is a possible explanation, not an observed scribal
event or established genealogy. The heart expression does not require a lost
Hebrew heart noun. Shaking supports a meaning comparison but does not uniquely
recover Hebrew **הרעל** rather than an interpretation of **הערל**.

Swete III's preface v–x and sigla, plus volume I's conventions xv–xvii and
Sinaiticus-hand discussion xxi, were consulted. The reused volume I is fourth
edition 1909, reprinted 1925, not an 1887 first-edition scan. No silence of
Cryptoferratensis is counted as agreement; its stated legibility limit remains.

fresh Latin manuscript/apparatus collation. Neither semantic convergence nor
the different language labels prove independent Hebrew exemplars.

Hartog's 2017 [Pesher and Hypomnema](https://www.pthu.nl/over-pthu/organisatie/medewerkers/p.b.hartog/downloads/hartog-pesher-and-hypomnema.pdf),

## Source judgment and reader implication

**Attestation:** the unqualified published Hebrew letter-order contrast is
established within this consultation. **Priority:** HOLD. The ancient quotation
and versional motion/stupor meanings make reeling a serious source/interpretation
alternative, not a modern stylistic preference. But mechanical metathesis can
run in either direction, contextual translation can converge without identical
source letters, and the commentary can exploit wordplay. Uncircumcision is
not ruled out merely because another expression fits drunkenness.

The earlier direct-language evidence gives the quoted form a modest
chronological advantage, not a verdict. Removing that preference still leaves
the original letter order unresolved. Treating every version as an independent
vote or every comment as another quoted reading would falsely resolve it.
The principal open questions are the actual locus apparatus, local copying and
translation behavior, and the grammar of the unchanged-spelling counteranalysis.

POB's current main English remains provisional, with no variant disclosure at
this phrase. A bounded later application should add a note after “show your
uncircumcision” while retaining source and marker-free English. Proposed scope:

> An ancient Hebrew quotation reads a different letter order, commonly understood
> as “stagger”; Greek and Syriac also express shaking or agitation. The quotation's
> commentary nevertheless invokes an uncircumcised heart. Which Hebrew wording
> is earliest remains unresolved.

This proposed disclosure is not an applied candidate or approval of the whole
verse. A full-record candidate must preserve the existing sparse provenance,
archive the old revision-pass claim without treating it as current approval,
and verify all 56 Habakkuk export units. Earlier 1:5 and 2:4 application records
must retain their historical hashes when this later verse changes. No corpus-wide
English improvement, canon addition or ImageGen restoration is inferred.
