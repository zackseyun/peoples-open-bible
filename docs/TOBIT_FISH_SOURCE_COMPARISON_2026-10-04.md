# Tobit fish episode source comparison

At main revision `17176d2b9c7e5b20e858753c005a70e88e01b335`, POB's Tobit
6:1–6 follows Swete's upper Vaticanus text, not the lower Sinaiticus text
claimed by the source guide. This comparison provisionally selects the complete
lower Greek attack, capture, preparation and meal unit at 6:3–6. Early published
Aramaic supports distinctive details distributed across that unit. It does not
establish every Greek word or prove earliest-original priority. The rest of
Tobit is not newly collated or converted into a continuous Sinaiticus edition.

## Working selection and strongest contrary account

Prefer the connected Sinaiticus Greek unit, not an isolated foot insertion into
Vaticanus. Foot, size and singular eater have early direct-language support;
the complete Greek supplies an intelligible connected form. This is more than
choosing a modern, less extraordinary story. Printed Greek I remains accurately
renderable and is retained as the alternative in the reader notes and archived
source records. Without the chronological tilt, the early local agreements
would remain evidence but the preference would be weaker.

The strongest objection is an early explanatory expansion or harmonization,
already present before the surviving Aramaic copies. Specifying the foot could
clarify the washing danger, size could heighten the scene, and a singular eater
could continue the youth's singular actions or regularize an angel's participation.
Greek I could preserve earlier wording. No established mechanical omission
proves that it lost foot, and narrative realism does not disqualify its reading.
Aramaic and Greek agreement may descend from related forms; these are not
independent votes automatically proving originality. Working preference is
moderate; earliest-original certainty remains lower and unresolved.

The local target covers 6:3–6 through the return to joint travel. The earlier
river-arrival unit remains Greek I; the following Greek I question about the
organs still follows coherently. POB's existing dog at 5:17 is not duplicated
from the longer journey unit. Both Greek forms contain the fish encounter;
same-numbered Neubauer Hebrew is not content-aligned and is not an ancient
source. Repair the misleading 6:1–2 notes rather than call numbering differences
lost Greek content. Media replaces Ecbatana at the selected unit's endpoint
because those are the actual differing Greek place names; later geography is
not thereby certified. This explicitly declared local form selection yields
an eclectic working book, not a claim that the complete POB Tobit existed in
one ancient manuscript. Source fields preserve the printed Greek with line
breaks joined; no Hebrew/Aramaic retroversion is substituted.

## English assessment and application scope

A separate candidate-identity-withheld English assessment compared two exact
candidate pairs against the selected Greek, then a second pair for the enlarged
preparation and meal scope. It preferred wanting over trying, the singular
threatened foot, both capture instructions, brought up over threw, and singular
eating with explicit partitives and a salted retained portion. It recommended
explicitly identifying the youth as the implicit subject of the cry. Root uses
young man consistently with the surrounding narrative; the assessment explicitly
allowed that contextual label. No distinction in age is invented between the
two Greek youth nouns. The English review does not decide source priority.

The separate source assessment revised its boundary from attack/capture to
3–6 after checking the meal preservation. This was a concrete scope correction,

## Limits and reopening evidence

This is known evidence applied to POB, not novel decipherment, an original
Semitic autograph, canon expansion, general translation superiority or public
deployment. The current exporter includes 13 eligible chapters and 244 IDs;
chapter 9 is already withheld at the baseline. Preserving that denominator does
not certify a complete Tobit or repair unrelated chapter coverage.

Reopen source priority for a discriminating local argument, revised fragment
reading or identification, or actual Greek/Latin apparatus and manuscript
evidence that changes the transmission explanation. Fresh disputed-letter or
join claims require the existing image gate. Do not reacquire the same consulted
pages merely to obtain agreement. Continue the wider OT Hebrew/Aramaic program
and other relevant witnesses; this additional-book comparison does not replace
the full goal or complete its unperformed calibration.
