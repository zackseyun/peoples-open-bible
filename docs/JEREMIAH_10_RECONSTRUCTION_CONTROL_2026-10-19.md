## An opposing argument accepts the shorter arrangement

[Adcock's original 2015 thesis](https://research-repository.st-andrews.ac.uk/handle/10023/11968),
printed 242–253 / PDF244–255, criticizes reconstruction flexibility but accepts
the shorter arrangement of 4Q71. His preference for the longer text also invokes
structure and the relative dates of copies. The section therefore does not
refute the shorter arrangement; it argues against its priority. A copy's earlier
date cannot by itself date every reading's origin.

## What the DJD table compares

DJD XV, complete printed 171–176, was rechecked, including visual inspection of
173 and 175–176. The line-length table's MT-based column is not a test of the
complete unchanged MT sequence. Its line 6 explicitly uses Greek-based wording;
the reconstruction on 176 follows Greek verse layout while retaining MT wording
in details. Reading its roughly 115-letter-space result as proof that the whole
longer MT form fits would be mistaken.

The 115 count is a reconstruction-supported estimate, not an independently
measured missing width. The edition itself notes that only about 10–15 percent
of line length survives. Reusing its supplied text to demonstrate an exact
physical capacity would be circular.

## Conditional unchanged-order control

DJD's transcription ends line 5 with the hammer word and line 6 with the purple
word. Their alignment to the WLC words `ובמקבות` at 10:4 and `וארגמן` at 10:9
is content-based; the first has a different conjunction prefix. Keeping WLC's
unchanged written order, the interval after the former through the latter has
66 tokens and 254 Hebrew letters. Joining those tokens with one ordinary space
gives 319 characters. Verses 6–8 contribute 31 tokens and 119 of those letters.

The calculation uses only direct written XML words, retains spelling, removes
vowels, accents, morpheme slashes and punctuation, and excludes qere and paragraph
segments. The [evidence record](../sources/textual_restoration/comparisons/jeremiah10_reconstruction.2026-10-19.v1.json)
contains the complete normalized interval and reproducible recipe.

This is substantially more text than the edition's reconstructed line loads,
even before spaces. It tests a strictly unchanged WLC interval, not every
possible longer Hebrew form. It supplies neither a measured glyph-width limit
nor a probability, excludes no untested compression or rearrangement, and does
not choose expansion over omission. Deleting verses 6–8 alone also does not
reproduce the Greek arrangement; exact wording and placement still matter.
