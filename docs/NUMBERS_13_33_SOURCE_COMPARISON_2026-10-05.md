# Numbers 13 33 and the longer complaint narrative

The longer Samaritan form materially changes the sequence of the spies narrative:
Israel accuses God of hatred and Moses urges courage before Numbers 14's crying
and renewed complaints. The complete added sequence parallels Deuteronomy
1:27–33, with adapted introductions. A working expansion explanation is plausible,
but the comparison does not establish the earliest narrative. POB provisionally
retains the shorter source and main English and adds qualified variant notes.

Baseline: main `c14ef7e301576128acd863042cdd667b27051dce`. This is a
published-source comparison, not fresh manuscript decipherment, a complete
witness census, or a canon recommendation. The [alignment record](../sources/textual_restoration/discovery/numbers13_33_alignment.2026-10-05.v1.json)
and [application receipt](../sources/textual_restoration/applications/numbers13_33.2026-10-05.v1.json)
separate attestation, source selection, English consequence and delivery.

## A second Samaritan manuscript control

Florentin and Tal's published Nablus 6 edition, printed pages 639–642 (PDF
656–659), preserves the seven-part passage as 33a–g and identifies its
Deuteronomy parallel. Complete pages and the apparatus at 640–641 were visually
checked. Its 33f contains an additional he compared with the pinned Chester
Beatty wording. The two editions must not be described as word-for-word
identical or independent early branches merely because they share the passage.

The introduction, printed pages 39–42, distinguishes consonantal transliteration
from Samaritan pronunciation; page 41 discusses this particular spelling and
its concessive interpretation. The symbols at pages 81–84 distinguish substantive
variants, pronunciation and manuscript/oral-tradition differences. The edition's
translation is not an ancient English witness or a fresh transcription of pixels.
Copyright and citation year are 2024; the publisher records release on January
31, 2025. Its CC BY-NC 4.0 restrictions remain attached to the private input.
No full text or PDF is imported into the repository.

## Ancient coverage and the Hexaplaric distinction

QDR's own 4Q27 fragment 3 ii+5, lines 1–16, ends with portions of Numbers
13:24. The next published fragment begins with 15:41 and 16:1. Neither 13:33
nor its transition into chapter 14 is preserved in these rows. The complete
physical-line context was read, including brackets and uncertain characters;
embedded MT/SP controls were not counted as ancient ink. **4Q27 is not an
inclusion or omission witness for this addition.**
[QDR version 2026-05-21](https://lexicon.qumran-digital.org/transcriptions/4Q27/2026-05-21/index.html).

Hjelm's native printed pages 19–22 (preview PDF 33–36) warn against assuming
Masoretic and Deuteronomic priority before literary analysis. Page 21 distinguishes
missing extant text in this group from the line-calculation reconstruction at
12:16. The preview does not include the full 13:33 discussion, so no unseen
passage-specific argument is credited to it. Its warning is part of the strongest
countercase to expansion, not proof that the longer text is earlier.
[Hjelm 2018 chapter](https://doi.org/10.1515/9783110617306-002),
[lawful preview](https://api.pageplace.de/preview/DT0400.9783110617306_A35435841/preview-9783110617306_A35435841.pdf).

Cambridge 1911, printed pages 456–458 (PDF 62–64), was read in complete native
context; pages 457–458 were visually inspected. Its selected Greek text goes
from the local report to 14:1 without the long passage. Its **separate bottom
Hexaplaric apparatus**, attached to Israel at 14:2, quotes a related long passage
in Latin with the Syro-Hexaplaric symbol S and a notice attributing it to Samaritan
material. This is not the same placement as the SP node after 13:33. Nor is an
apparatus quotation automatically an insertion into a continuous Greek or
Syriac text. Manuscript-specific placement and the underlying Syriac note still
need checking before claiming an independent long-form witness.

The Numbers preface retains the earlier apparatus method. Genesis 1906's
preface distinguishes this third apparatus of marginal Hexaplaric material from
continuous-text variants, explains Latin quotation of version-only readings,
and identifies S with Lagarde's Syro-Hexaplaric edition. No physical manuscript
date or complete Syriac collation is inferred from the symbol. This evidence
establishes reported reception of the longer form, not independent earlier
Hebrew authority. [Cambridge Numbers and Deuteronomy](https://tmcdaniel.palmerseminary.edu/Brooke%26McLean/LXX_Brooke%26McLean_1-3.pdf).

## Separate variants within the local verse

Cambridge labels the local report 13:34, corresponding by content to MT 13:33.
Its selected text lacks the Anak/Nephilim ancestry clause. The apparatus also
reports Greek ancestry wording, some qualified by Hexaplaric signs. Greek
absence is therefore not unanimous. The longer Hebrew clause could be an
explanatory gloss; alternatively, repeated Nephilim endings could cause a skip.
The repeated-word explanation and Hebrew-aligned Greek forms prevent treating
selected-text omission alone as proof of shorter original Hebrew.

The selected Greek also uses their perspective in both halves of the grasshopper
comparison, unlike WLC's our perspective followed by theirs. The inspected
apparatus units also vary the Greek preposition, not automatically its pronoun.
Assimilation to the final clause is a
plausible countercase to selecting the repeated perspective; it is not demonstrated
as the actual copying event. This separate source-interpretation question does
not require changing POB's present rhetorical contrast. No unique Hebrew
back-translation, unanimous Greek reading or new lexical meaning is claimed.

## Source decisions and English consequences

The declared target remains the shorter Numbers narrative with alternatives
disclosed, not a synthetic combination of literary forms. The complete long
passage, ordered parallels, distinctive repeated SP wording and adapted speech
introductions support a working expansion/harmonization explanation. The strongest
countercase is an inherited fuller narrative or shared older material, shortened
intentionally in another branch. Both transitions are narratively intelligible;
neither literary fit nor manuscript age establishes the direction. No single
obvious eye-skip explains loss of this whole paragraph. A different literary
edition remains possible rather than merely a copying error.

Source outcome: **retain WLC provisionally** for the long passage, ancestry
clause and two perspectives. Historical priority remains unresolved. English
outcome: **unchanged words**. Adopting the longer form would add explicit speeches
and change their timing, not just improve a gloss. Reopen for named surviving
manuscript/material evidence or a transmission argument that distinguishes the
live explanations; for the Hexaplaric claim, inspect the underlying Syriac
note and its relation to continuous text. No further model vote substitutes
for those inputs.
