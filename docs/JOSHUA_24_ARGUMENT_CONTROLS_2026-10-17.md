## The original connected argument

Samuel Holmes's [1914 study](https://archive.org/details/joshuahebrewand00holmgoog)
is available as an unrestricted historical scan. Its actual title page confirms
the author, Cambridge University Press and 1914. The consulted complete pages
are printed 8–10, 28–30, 74 and 80, not the entire book.

Holmes links the knives at 21:42d and 24:30a (RH's 24:31a unit) to the
circumcision narrative in chapter 5. His argument is more specific than the observation that the Greek
contains extra material: he proposes a Hebrew reviser's objection to the
Egypt-exiters' being uncircumcised. Yet his comparison table on p. 29 expressly
labels its Greek side **slightly emended**. On p. 30 he proposes a stop after
מהם and deletion of אשר before נשבע, and treats Greek δύο as corruption
connected with שנה. The table's forty-year narrative is therefore not an
unmodified transcription of the selected Greek form. The earlier comparison's
actual forty-two/forty distinction remains intact.

At p. 74, the claim that 21:42d stands or falls with 24:30a expresses connected
literary reasoning and the proposed deletion motive. It is not a second
physical witness or independent confirmation of that motive. His alternative
assessment of the Shiloh readings at pp. 8–9 also cautions against treating his
general preference for Greek as approval of every Greek difference.

At p. 80 Holmes introduces the final continuation's Hebrew as a possible
rendering into Hebrew. He favors originality because it can be rendered easily
and looks fragmentary, and proposes book division or objections to movement of
the ark as possible loss mechanisms. Those Hebrew lines are his retroversion,
not newly found Hebrew ink. Neither fluency nor a possible motive decides the
language, direction or date of the alteration. These controls qualify how the
argument may be used; they do not refute every earlier-Hebrew explanation.

## Exact apparatus located but not visually consulted

This access check reconfirms the boundary already recorded in the
[countermodel follow-up](JOSHUA_COUNTERMODEL_FOLLOWUP_2026-10-17.md); it is not
a new route or acquisition. That follow-up's direct reading of van der Meer's
2018 retrospective remains distinct from his unacquired detailed monograph.

The editor's [author profile](https://huji.academia.edu/EmanuelTov/Edited%20books)
links the actual [Margolis Part V upload](https://www.academia.edu/28952775/E11_Max_L_Margolis_The_Book_of_Joshua_in_Greek_Part_V_Joshua_19_39_24_33_Monograph_Series_Annenberg_Research_Institute_Philadelphia_1992_).
Its exposed prefatory prose describes a critical reconstruction, with recension
readings separated from manuscript support and internal variants. It also
explains that the publication reproduces a recovered photostat of the lost
manuscript and that some details were reinforced in preparing its plates.
Thus the apparatus is an editorial product, not another ancient witness.

The source agent's browser inspection found a 116-page upload, but the full-PDF
control required sign-in. No account was used and no access restriction was
bypassed. Root read the editor's listing and exposed prefatory prose; the
handwritten target apparatus's OCR is not reliable enough to assign its sigla.
No target-page images or trustworthy ending collation were obtained. The next
useful input remains readable target apparatus pages and their conventions,
not another attempt to reason from corrupted OCR. The separate original
O'Brien argument and van der Meer's 2004 monograph were not acquired in the
bounded search and are not represented as newly read. Do not repeat these
same access routes without a genuinely new input.
