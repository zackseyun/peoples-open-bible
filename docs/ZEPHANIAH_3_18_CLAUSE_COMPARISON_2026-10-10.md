# Zephaniah 3 18 clause interpretation comparison

Date: October 10, 2026. Baseline: `cfbc9d9b5fba46a3d08558ef35b38410b1244b0e`.

## Decision and meaning

Retain the complete pointed WLC provisionally and prefer a different interpretation
of its clauses. The proposed English is:

> I have gathered those grieving over the appointed festival—they were from you; reproach was a burden upon her.

Current POB attaches “from you” to gathering, then describes the grieving people
as a reproach and burden. The proposal joins “from you” to “they were” and makes
reproach the subject of a separate nominal clause. This can alter a reader's
understanding without replacing any Hebrew letters or vowels. It is a
source-interpretation change, not mere English polish, a newly discovered
reading, or a uniquely optimal translation.

One identity-masked comparison favors the proposal moderately. Reversing the
order with the same reviewer provides a diagnostic, not a second independent
vote. The first grief/festival gloss and “have gathered” remain common inputs,
not newly certified. The [frozen English contract](../sources/textual_restoration/comparisons/zephaniah3_18_english_contract.2026-10-10.v1.json)
and [result](../sources/textual_restoration/comparisons/zephaniah3_18_english_result.2026-10-10.v1.json)
separate this preference from exact-record review and historical priority.

## Hebrew preservation and grouping

The private published QDR Mur88 fragment21 lines14–15 reads
`נוגי מ מועד אספת[ י ] ממ׳ך# / [ הי ]ו? משאת עלי׳ה חרפה#`.
All available fragment lines were read to establish bracket context.
The final yod of “I gathered” and first two letters of “they were” are supplied;
the surviving waw is uncertain in the legacy notation. “From you” and
“reproach” are marked. The grief, preposition, festival, burden and “upon her”
readings are unbracketed and unmarked in that published transcription.
No complete preserved verse, pointing, or fresh manuscript-pixel reading is claimed.

The related [Qumran-Digital publication](https://lexicon.qumran-digital.org/transcriptions/Mur._88/2026-05-21/index.html?v=2026-05-21)
retains the relevant supply brackets but not every legacy uncertainty marker.
These are publication streams of one witness, not additional votes. Restricted
data remain private. The bounded Zephaniah-labelled QDR screen finds only Mur88
exact3:18 rows among4Q77,4Q78,4Q82,Mur88; it is not an exhaustive census.
Previously acquired [Ra943 catalogue coverage](https://septuaginta.uni-goettingen.de/catalogue/Ra_943/)
reports other Zephaniah passages, not3:18.

The actual received text has tiphcha on “I gathered,” munach on “from you” and
athnach on “they were.” This favors keeping the latter words together.
Mercha connects burden with “upon her,” whose tiphcha separates that group from
final reproach. The pointed burden noun is absolute, not a construct form.
A predicate-first nominal clause permits “reproach was a burden upon her.”
The final copula is supplied in English; past time is a contextual choice, not
required by the nominal Hebrew clause.

[GKC §15](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Gesenius%27_Hebrew_Grammar/15._The_Accents)
explains these groupings and expressly qualifies the logical force of accents.
[GKC §141](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Gesenius%27_Hebrew_Grammar/141._The_Noun-clause)
permits nominal and prepositional predicates, contextual copulas and
predicate-first order. These are general controls, not a Zephaniah-specific verdict.
The Hebrew acquisition agent also consulted §§119w,z and130a, Genesis17:16
and Exodus37:22. An adjacent-lemma screen yielded14 locations, several across

## Versions and an ancient competing explanation

The publisher's [Rahlfs–Hanhart2006 Greek](https://www.die-bibel.de/bibel/LXX/ZEP.3)
gathers the broken/crushed and then asks who took reproach upon her. Its
festival comparison belongs to the preceding segment. Compare complete3:17–19,
not modern verse numbers alone. Future gathering, the question and aorist verb
differ from the received Hebrew's qatal, plural “they were” and nominal burden.
An exact underlying Hebrew cannot be reconstructed from that difference alone.

SweteIII third1905 printed68–69 supplies the same selected Greek clause.
The limited apparatus adds dative σοι in AQ after the broken/crushed phrase;
it is not possessive σου. The reproach unit is abbreviated
`ου. επ αυτην ℵ (postea επ αυτ. ου.) AQ`; no visible asterisk qualifies
ℵ there. Preserve that limited report rather than inventing complete hand text.
Root inspected the complete passage/apparatus; the version agent also inspected
the title and preface. VolumeI apparatus conventions and the latest Göttingen
passage apparatus remain unacquired. This is edition-mediated evidence, not
fresh transcription or Greek unanimity. The compact private derivative and
original [recoverable scan](https://archive.org/download/theoldtestamenti03swetuoft_202003/theoldtestamenti03swetuoft.pdf)
are pinned in the [machine comparison](../sources/textual_restoration/comparisons/zephaniah3_18_clause.2026-10-10.v1.json).

The publisher's [Weber–Gryson2007 Latin](https://www.die-bibel.de/bibel/VUL/ZEP.3)
has “from you they were,” supporting an ancient interpretation of that
relationship. It also adds causal/purpose framing and has plural “over them”
where Hebrew has feminine singular “upon her.” It is not full-phrase agreement,
an independent Hebrew manuscript, or an apparatus adjudication.

[Jerome's commentary III §§44–55, especially54](https://monumenta.ch/latein/text.php?nf=1&rumpfid=Hieronymus%2C+Commentarii%2C+in+Sophoniam%2C+03&tabelle=Hieronymus)
reports Aquila and the Septuagint's “woe” where he renders “they were,”
explaining the Hebrew expression as past plural. This supplies an ancient
interpretive/resegmentation explanation that must compete with a different
Hebrew-source hypothesis. It does not explain every difference or establish
the exact Greek Vorlage. Aquila is an indirect report here, not an acquired
manuscript. Bracketed later editorial annotations are not Jerome's own text;
his linguistic-origin claims and theological polemic are not adopted.

## Strongest alternative and application limits

Current POB keeps the people as the grammatical thread, using burden/reproach
as abstract predicates or apposition across athnach. That is possible poetic
predication; singular abstract nouns do not automatically invalidate a plural
subject. It can describe their humiliating condition without morally condemning
them. The alternative is not rejected for pastoral unattractiveness.

The proposal's weaknesses are an extra supplied copula, contextual past tense,
an abstract subject switch and terse “from you.” “Belonged to you” would smooth
English but select a further relationship. The received grouping plus locally
complete clauses justify moderate preference, not certainty. The feminine
“you”/“her” switch remains; it does not prove different referents.
