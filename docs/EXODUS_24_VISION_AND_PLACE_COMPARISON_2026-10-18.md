# Exodus 24 divine vision and Greek place language

## Working judgment

Exodus 24:9–11 contains a consequential difference in the account of Israel's
leaders encountering God. The consulted Hebrew says they saw God twice; the
Greek selected text shifts the encounter toward God's place and changes the
survival clause. Retain POB's direct-vision Hebrew and main English provisionally.
The paired Greek changes are real, but do not establish a recovered Hebrew
place clause or prove where interpretation entered the transmission.

This comparison is a new passage study, not a new ancient discovery, a
whole-book collation or another vote on Psalm 40. The
[packet](../sources/textual_restoration/comparisons/exodus24_vision.2026-10-18.v1.json)
pins baseline `4a0b56b0260153006c7d85cb8071c6642873f723`, sources,
preservation limits and 26 actual WLC alignments. No source, main English,
reader note, implementation or canon change is applied.

## The connected differences

Root read the complete local Exodus 24 context and the 33:11, 18–23 and 34:5
controls. Swete I printed 151 / PDF 183 and Brooke–McLean's Exodus fascicle
(1909), printed 233–234 / PDF 91–92, were read as text and inspected as complete
page images, including the target apparatus.

| Unit | Hebrew control | Consulted Greek selected text |
|---|---|---|
| 24:10 sight | ויראו את אלהי ישראל | εἶδον τὸν τόπον οὗ ἱστήκει ὁ θεὸς τοῦ Ἰσραήλ: the place is the object of sight |
| 24:11 survival | לא שלח ידו: God does not extend his hand against them | οὐ διεφώνησεν οὐδὲ εἷς: none of the chosen was missing/lost |
| 24:11 encounter | ויחזו את האלהים: they behold God | ὤφθησαν ἐν τῷ τόπῳ τοῦ θεοῦ: they appear/are seen in God's place |

The scene still includes ascent, eating and drinking. The Greek still refers
to God's feet. These are connected interpretive differences, not a simple
one-word substitution. The passive Greek does not compel one reconstructed
Hebrew spelling or vocalization: interpreting an unpointed verb and rewriting
a clause remain distinguishable possibilities.

At 24:10 Swete's selected text lacks **ἐκεῖ**; its apparatus reports the extra
adverb in A/F. The broader historical apparatus also reports it. Do not erase
that difference when evaluating the more Hebrew-like “where … there” construction.
The consulted target apparatus does not report a direct-God substitute for the
place clause. That is a bounded edition observation, not a census of every
surviving Greek copy. Edition agreement is not another physical witness.
No new manuscript hand or original correction layer was read.

## Qumran coverage is not surviving vision wording

The hash-matching biblical QDR snapshot was queried with its actual **Ex 24:9,
Ex 24:10, Ex 24:11** labels. It returns 4Q22 display unit 26, lines 31–33.
“26” is retained as the export's unit identifier, not newly certified as a
physical fragment number. The diplomatic stream at line 32 is:

> ישר[ אל ׃ ו יראו את אלהי ישראל ו תחת רגלי׳ו ] כ מעש[ ה לבנת ה ספיר ]

The opening bracket occurs inside the preceding Israel token and closes after
the feet phrase. Thus **יראו את אלהי ישראל** is wholly supplied in this
transcription, even though its normalized tokens look complete. At line 33,
**ויחזו** also lies inside a supplied span. These cannot be counted as preserved
early Hebrew vision clauses. This is a published-transcription check, not a
fresh reading of papyrus or proof that the Hebrew variant never existed.

Longacre's 2015 thesis, printed 120 / PDF 135, lists 4Q364 coverage at 24:11–14.
The reacquired, hash-matching nonbiblical QDR snapshot has no usable verse
annotations; its 4Q364 record was inspected directly, not searched by empty
references. Display f14 contains the subsequent ascent/tablet instruction,
but the vision clause was not independently located and certified there.
Coverage lists and unresolved opening traces do not supply another witness
for either encounter reading. Target edition/plate verification remains open.

## Competing explanations and an internal control

Wyckoff's
[2012 article](https://www.academia.edu/8655302/When_Does_Translation_Become_Exegesis_Exodus_24_9_11_in_the_Masoretic_Text_and_the_Septuagint),
printed 675–693, was read through its complete public page text, including
notes and conclusion, not as an acquired PDF. He distinguishes ordinary
lexical/paraphrastic changes from theological ones and slightly favors a
translation-stage origin for the place expansions. He nevertheless allows
earlier Hebrew intervention and addresses a competing Hebrew-exemplar argument.
His proposed sanctuary emphasis is an interpretation, not a newly attested noun.

McClellan's
[2010 thesis](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/322315156_Anti-Anthropomorphism_and_the_Vorlage_of_LXX_Exodus)
was consulted only at the public text of printed 25–28 and the opening 29
control, not in full. He argues that the resumptive adverb favors a Hebrew
exemplar and proposes a Numbers-like Hebrew survival clause. He explicitly
acknowledges the adverb's omission in some Greek branches, interpreting that
as stylistic improvement. These are counterarguments and retroversions, not
newly discovered Hebrew wording.

The actual Swete 33:11–23 / printed 172–173 / PDF 204–205 control narrows a
blanket avoidance claim. At 33:18, the Greek request is **Ἐμφάνισόν μοι σεαυτόν**,
where POB's Hebrew has “show me your glory.” At 33:20 Greek specifies God's
**face** in both clauses; Hebrew's second clause has “me.” At 33:22–23 the
Greek still mentions God's hand; its **τὰ ὀπίσω μου** need not be forced into
one English anatomical interpretation. Therefore these witnesses do not
display a simple policy of deleting all bodily language or every possibility
of divine visibility.

Both an inherited Hebrew expansion and translation-level interpretation
remain viable. Easy retroversion alone cannot choose between them; neither
can the fact that the result has theological significance. The paired place
language supports a mediated-encounter reading of this Greek form, but does not
independently prove a Jerusalem-temple identification or the exact earliest
Hebrew clause. Direct vision remains the appropriate rendering of the retained
pointed Hebrew; it should not be harmonized away to resolve a theological tension.
