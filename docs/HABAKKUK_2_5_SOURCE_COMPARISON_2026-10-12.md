# Habakkuk 2 5 wine wealth and Greek editorial reconstruction

The ancient Hebrew quotation at Habakkuk 2:5 genuinely reads **הון**, usually
understood as wealth, rather than the retained WLC **היין**, wine. Its following
verb also differs. Newly consulted Greek evidence removes an apparent shortcut:
the modern edition's wine-related word is an editorial conjecture, not the
transmitted Greek word reported in the consulted scholarship. Neither this
correction nor the older Hebrew quotation establishes the earliest wording.
Source priority remains **HOLD**; no Hebrew, English or reader notes change.

This is a passage-level comparison under
[method 2.0.4](TEXTUAL_ADJUDICATION_METHOD.md), following the
[complete pesher quotation map](HABAKKUK_PESHER_QUOTATION_MAP_2026-10-12.md).
The contribution is newly collated evidence and a more accurate assessment of
support, not a newly discovered variant or recovered manuscript letters.

## Greek transmitted wording versus a conjectured ancestor

Swete III, third edition 1905, printed 59–60 (PDF 83–84), selects
**ὁ δὲ κατοιόμενος καταφρονητής**. The publisher's selected
[Rahlfs–Hanhart 2006](https://www.die-bibel.de/bibel/LXX/HAB.2) instead has
**ὁ δὲ κατοινωμένος καὶ καταφρονητὴς ἀνὴρ ἀλαζών**. The extra nu in the
opening word matters: the latter is wine-related. These editions cannot be
flattened into one supposedly uniform Greek witness.

[Reinhart Ceulemans, JSCS 47 (2014)](https://septuaginta.uni-goettingen.de/static/ioscs/pdf/volumes/jscs47.pdf),
printed 104–106, directly reproduces Ziegler's apparatus on 105, identifying
κατοινωμένος as **scripsi cum Rahlfs**, against the Greek manuscripts'
κατοιομενος or κατοιωμ., with a separate spelling in 534. He also reproduces
La Bible d'Alexandrie's explanation for retaining the transmitted word with
an arrogance sense and its patristic lexical argument. Root and the version
agent read and visually checked all three pages, including notes 40–46.
Notes 41–42 identify the major edition as Ziegler's third edition, 1984, and
explain what is being quoted. This is **mediated apparatus evidence**, not
independent consultation of that apparatus, original Cyril passages, or the
complete argument for reconstructing a wine-related Greek ancestor.

Swete's bounded uncial apparatus supplies καί and ἀνὴρ ἀλαζών through other
witnesses and a qualified marginal B hand. It does not attribute those additions
to unqualified original B. No opening-word variant is printed in that bounded
unit; silence is not a census of all Greek manuscripts. The older source
comparison's Swete edition/conventions controls remain applicable.

Consequently, the selected modern wine word supplies an editorial explanation,
not independent manuscript agreement with היין. Conversely, the transmitted
Greek can plausibly express arrogance without uniquely retroverting Hebrew
הון meaning wealth. A Greek spelling error from a wine-related ancestor and
an interpretive rendering of Hebrew wine remain possible explanations. Neither
is an observed copying event. No surviving decisive 8ḤevXIIgr wording has been
established in this packet; its catalogue range is not positive attestation.

## Other versions lexical controls and competing scholarship

The selected [Weber–Gryson Latin, fifth edition 2007](https://www.die-bibel.de/bibel/VUL/HAB.2)
explicitly has vinum in a comparison about wine deceiving its drinker. The
drinker and comparative framing are interpretive elaborations, not separately
attested Hebrew words. This is a wine interpretation in a Hebrew-based Latin
translation tradition, but not a newly collated ancient Latin manuscript or
proof of a particular independently established Hebrew exemplar.

Current CAL chapter and metadata requests returned the same HTTP200
anti-scraping notice, not Syriac text. No fresh Syriac reading or agreement is
claimed. Gert Prinsloo's reported Syriac and Targum remain reported evidence,
not independently inspected edition or manuscript wording in this packet.

[Prinsloo 2016](https://hts.org.za/index.php/hts/article/view/3576/9124)
argues for reading wine and wealth in their respective literary contexts.
The relevant complete argument sections, conclusion and decisive notes were
read in HTML; no independent verification of his codex delimiter census or
table images is claimed. His wine argument uses the drinking/shame material
at 2:15–16 and analogous biblical imagery. His wealth argument emphasizes the
Wicked Priest application. Note 26 reports a third proposal: an inferred rare
Hebrew arrogance noun, related to the verb in Deut 1:41, favored by cited
critical scholarship. Those original scholarly works and the BHQ target
apparatus were not consulted. His contextual cautions are persuasive; his
conclusion that wealth would be unacceptable in the MT context is stronger
than this packet accepts. Greed and conquest in 2:5–12 also make wealth coherent.

[Richard D. Patterson's own exposition](https://bible.org/seriespage/habakkuk2),
the 2:5 discussion and additional notes, offers a different working preference:
presumption, through a rare-noun proposal linking Deut 1:41 and Num 14:44.
Its description of wealth as mere repointing understates the actual consonant
change from היין to הון. Its account of Greek wine manuscript support must
also be qualified by the newly consulted apparatus report above. The rare-noun
proposal deserves consideration, but an English preference and the existence
of a cognate verb do not establish the unattested target noun or its vocalization.

The independently read Sefaria BDB entry BDB02382 defines הון as wealth and
sufficiency; it is a lexical control, not a Habakkuk manuscript. The same
response's separately identified Klein entry E00403 treats the Deut 1:41 verb
as daring or regarding something as easy. It does not establish the proposed
Habakkuk noun. Jastrow's Aramaic proper-conduct entry is not Biblical Hebrew
arrogance attestation. The current YAML's HALOT labels do not constitute fresh
HALOT consultation. Local WLC contexts in Habakkuk 2, Prov 20:1 and 21:24,
Jer 51:7, Deut 1:41 and Num 14:44 were inspected separately; literary parallels
are not extra witnesses to Habakkuk 2:5.

## Transmission judgment and English consequence

Wealth has two substantial strengths: early direct Hebrew attestation and a
natural link to insatiable conquest and the following indictment of plunder.
Its strongest objection is possible local adaptation, involving both the noun
and verb, for the commentary's application. Wine's contextual difficulty is
real but not fatal: personified wine can deceive, and the later drinking/cup
imagery makes it intelligible. That does not prove wine original; harmonization
to that imagery is itself a possible transmission explanation. The Latin wine
rendering shows reception of that interpretation, not certain early priority.

A double-yod/waw graphic mechanism is conceivable in either direction, but
היין has four consonants and הון three, with an article/root reanalysis as
well as the accompanying verb difference. Calling this only a vowel change
or one unproblematic letter substitution is inadequate. The arrogance proposal
can connect transmitted Greek and the preceding pride language, but requires
an inferred Hebrew lexical analysis; it is not a third newly discovered noun.

The ancient quotation gives wealth a modest chronological advantage in
attestation. It does not choose between inherited wording, deliberate
adaptation and copying change. Removing that advantage leaves priority
unresolved as well. **HOLD is a historical judgment, not automatic preference
for WLC.** Retaining the declared base provisionally is different from proving
its superiority. No uniquely optimal main English is approved here.

A separate reader application should disclose the wealth variant after
**wine**, qualify the lexical rationale's base-only assertion, and repair
the existing note anchors. Note a currently explains wine but attaches to
Moreover; b explains rest but attaches to satisfied; c explains Sheol but is
also delayed to satisfied. These are independently observable reader defects,
not reasons to select a Hebrew variant. Proposed textual note:

> An ancient Hebrew quotation reads 'wealth' rather than 'wine' and also uses
> a different form of the following verb. Which wording is earliest remains
> uncertain.

This is not a frozen full-record candidate or approval to apply it. Earlier
review fields must be archived and actual full-book reader output checked in

The next evidence that could change priority is the actual Hebrew/Greek locus

preservation/reconstruction of the early Greek revision. Repeat model votes
on this packet would add no independent textual evidence.
