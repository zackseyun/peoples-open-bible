## Competing source and English decisions

The negative reading has direct written-form support and an explicit Greek
negative clause. POB’s “we did not make ourselves” naturally supplies the
making verb and object from the preceding assertion. Those supplied English
words are an interpretation of ellipsis, not recovered Hebrew letters.

The belonging reading is also in the Hebrew source and agrees with the selected
Hebrew-based Latin. Its strongest contextual case is the adjacent his people
and his flock parallel. It should not be dismissed as merely a popular English
translation. Conversely, that context could encourage normalization toward
belonging; the negative Greek tradition remains a substantive countercase.
Homophonous Hebrew forms permit a transmission hypothesis, not observation of
which change happened. Neither awkwardness nor poetic smoothness decides age.

Retain both as serious alternatives. Source and English retention is provisional,
not an earliest-wording victory. Physical-copy dates, translation dates and
edition dates are different; this pass lacks a dated direct Hebrew collation
and complete hand/version apparatus. Removing chronological preference leaves
the same provisional outcome. The belonging clause changes the assertion,
but possession is already explicit in the verse; no new theology follows.
