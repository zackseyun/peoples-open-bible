The independently read Greek chapter has passive praise, not forgetting. That is substantive support for investigating a different Hebrew source, but not proof of its exact consonants or reflexive sense. Earlier Greek reporting gives this hypothesis a modest chronological advantage; it does not give medieval Hebrew copies an ancient physical date or make related Greek editions and Latin independent Hebrew votes. [Rahlfs and Hanhart chapter 8](https://www.die-bibel.de/bibel/LXX/ECC.8).

The countercase is real: later bet copies could reflect interpretation or correction; Gittin explicitly offers a homiletic alternate reading while quoting kaf, and also alters “buried” to “collected.” Neither that formula nor De Rossi's contamination hypothesis proves the history of every copy. Meanwhile, postmortem forgetting fits the actual Hebrew controls in Ecclesiastes 2:16 and 9:5. Injustice in 8:11–14 makes praise attractive but does not invalidate forgetting. [Gittin 56b.11](https://www.sefaria.org/Gittin.56b.11).

The resulting preference is provisional retention of the declared WLC base, not a demonstrated historical victory for kaf. Target-level modern apparatus, identified manuscript dates/correction states and a discriminating transmission argument could change that outcome.

## Complete English candidate

“Had boasted” is grammatically possible under the candidate's disclosed prior-activity interpretation, but not uniquely established. It places boasting before the burial observation and avoids making buried people boast. The surrounding perfect/imperfect sequence does not itself uniquely specify that pluperfect relation or prove that every plural verb retains the wicked as subject. The inherited burial, coming and going construction remains difficult.

The Psalm and Chronicles controls support reflexive glorying, but are closely related examples; they do not settle this target. Their ב introduces what is gloried in, whereas בעיר can identify where boasting occurs. There is no need to reject the candidate as “boasting about the city” or insist that an object must be expressed. The identified [BDB transcription](https://biblehub.com/hebrew/7623.htm) supports a possible interpretation, not priority or optimal English.

Reflexive boasting and passive praise differ materially: one describes self-commendation, the other public approval, potentially after death. The Greek praise reading cannot directly certify the candidate's changed agency. Conversely, Hitpael is not always reflexive; [GKC section 54g](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Gesenius%27_Hebrew_Grammar/54) explicitly allows passive uses, including the current kaf target. That general fact does not establish passive meaning for bet here.

The prior boasting interpretation could lead into delayed punishment in 8:11, but it gives up the public/postmortem approval effect of passive praise. “Where they had done so” remains compressed without determining what “so” refers to. A footnote admitting uncertainty is honest, but cannot replace a whole-clause argument for selecting this exact interpretation. Full-record application remains on HOLD rather than being rejected as demonstrably ungrammatical.
