# Deuteronomy 27 altar location and the chosen place

## Result

The arguments for Ebal and Gerizim depend on the altar's literary role and
the interpretation of the chosen-place formula, not simply on counting
witnesses. A proposed chronological bracket from two Qumran compositions does
not establish when Deuteronomy's verb changed. Keep POB's Ebal and “will
choose” provisionally, with the existing qualified mountain note. No source,
English, note, approval, layout or canon change is applied.

## Two competing historical explanations

Stefan Schorch's [2011 author text](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/265561069_The_Samaritan_Version_of_Deuteronomy_and_the_Origin_of_Deuteronomy),
pp. 26–29, connects Deuteronomy 27's crossing, offerings, eating and rejoicing
with chapters 11–12. He identifies the altar as the chosen sanctuary, prefers
Gerizim and rejects reconciliation models that leave Jerusalem equally
legitimate. At pp. 32–35, he favors earlier בחר, “has chosen,” and dates its
change to יבחר between 4QMMT and the Temple Scroll, around the middle of the
second century BCE. These are his historical judgments, not dated manuscript
corrections. His Greek and daughter-version claims depend on Schenker's work;
that original apparatus is not newly consulted here.

Antti Laato's [2014 original chapter](https://www.academia.edu/11077633/The_Cult_Site_on_Mount_Ebal_A_Biblical_Tradition_Rewritten_and_Reinterpreted),
pp. 74–84, instead favors Ebal. An abandoned northern sanctuary could be
received positively in Jerusalem-oriented history without endorsing a current
rival. Changing Ebal to Gerizim would then authorize the active Gerizim cult.
He rejects the inference that covenant curses make Ebal inherently unholy.
His scenario depends on historical reconstruction; p. 81 n. 81 explicitly
qualifies the temple's chronology and possible Jerusalemite introduction of
Mosaic legitimation. His conclusion does not identify the excavated structure
as Joshua's altar. His label for the Giessen text does not supersede our
earlier revision and preservation controls.

Our inference: both accounts can explain a positive northern altar tradition,
but assign it different temporal and institutional functions. Neither a
blessing association nor a curse association by itself selects the mountain.
Literary links between chapters 12 and 27 are real features to explain; they
do not alone establish one permanent sanctuary or the direction of a name
replacement. Moving Ebal to Gerizim, or the reverse, cannot be dated from a
model of later community interests without independent transmission evidence.

## A grammatical alternative to the binary tense argument

Innocent Himbaza's [2016 study](https://sonar.ch/documents/309325/files/se58006.pdf),
*« Le lieu que YHWH aura choisi ». Une perspective narrative, historique et
philologique*, argues that qatal can express a choice anterior to future
worship: “will have chosen,” not necessarily “had chosen before Moses spoke.”
At pp. 131–132 n. 30 he directly objects to using 4QMMT to date the change,
because that composition explicitly identifies Jerusalem. His conclusion
still favors qatal priority and regards exclusive readings on both sides as
late. It is not a defense of every Masoretic form or proof of Gerizim priority.

The local written WLC/OSHB controls were parsed directly with namespace-aware
OSIS verse IDs. Morphology labels describe this edition, not independent ink.

| Deuteronomy 30:1 | הדיחך, qatal with suffix | Future curses, dispersion and recollection in Moses' speech; relative anteriority need not precede the speech |
| Deuteronomy 23:17 Hebrew numbering | יבחר, yiqtol | A refugee's choice of residence, not a claim about God's sanctuary |

Our inference is limited: form alone cannot equate qatal with a sanctuary
already selected at the speech's present. These selected examples do not
measure which construction is most frequent, prove a date or require a single
English tense at every occurrence. They also do not, by themselves, authorize
replacing POB's current יבחר source at 12:5 with the competing בחר form.

## The actual Qumran contexts do not form a revision clock

The Qumran-Digital published transcription at
[4Q394 fragment 8 iv, lines 9–12](https://lexicon.qumran-digital.org/transcriptions/4Q394/2025-11-11/index.html?end_reading_id=113274&v=2025-11-11)
prints Jerusalem explicitly at line 10 and שבחר בו at line 11. The choosing
verb is outside reconstruction brackets. Surrounding letters and later words
have their own uncertainty/supply signs; the whole passage is not intact.
This is direct Hebrew reception evidence, not a continuous copy of
Deuteronomy 12. The published line also has םחנה; it is not silently corrected
to מחנה here.

The corresponding control at
[11QTemple Scroll a, column 52, lines 7–10](https://lexicon.qumran-digital.org/transcriptions/11QT%5Ea%5E/2025-11-11/index.html?end_reading_id=195510&v=2025-11-11)
prints במקום אשר אבחר at 52:9, in instructions about annual eating before
the divine speaker. The first-person verb is outside supply brackets. This
confirms the reported form, not the loss of a yod in an earlier Deuteronomy
copy. The other three Temple Scroll loci cited by Schorch are not newly
collated as separate controls.

Our inference: retrospective identification of Jerusalem and prospective divine
instruction can coexist. Even if the proposed composition dates were accepted,
two differently situated compositions would not bound the first revision of
a third text. Their physical copies, compositions and incorporated traditions
must also be dated separately. This test confirms the forms while rejecting
their use alone as a clock; it does not refute every wider historical argument.
