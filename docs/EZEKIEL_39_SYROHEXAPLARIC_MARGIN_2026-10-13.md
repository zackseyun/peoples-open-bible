# Ezekiel 39 Spirit and wrath in the Ambrosian Syrohexapla

The Ambrosian Syrohexaplaric facsimile supplies the manuscript context behind
Field's attributed Spirit reading at Ezekiel 39:29: **wrath in the main text,
Spirit in the margin**. Ceriani identifies an erasure and uncertain hand in
the marginal Spirit noun. The evidence establishes a transmitted main/marginal
distinction, not an uncomplicated first-hand reading or newly recovered Hebrew.
Retain POB's declared Hebrew and current English; historical priority remains
unresolved.

| Margin at 39:29 | `ܪܘܚܐ ܕܝܠܝ`, my spirit, within the pouring-upon-Israel phrase, with the Symmachus siglum. | Matches the attribution and Syriac-mediated reading in Field's actual entry. Main text and margin must not be flattened into one reading. |

| Field 1875, printed 873 / PDF 889, note 22 | Attributes the Spirit phrase to Symmachus and explicitly cites Syro-hexaplaric Syriac. | Field's Greek representation and this facsimile are linked evidence, not two independently inspected Greek copies or two independent Hebrew witnesses. |

The [earlier Field comparison](EZEKIEL_36_LANGUAGE_CONTROLS_2026-10-12.md#field-edition-and-the-transmitted-spirit-reading)
already established the printed attribution. The new facsimile/critical-note
check strengthens that provenance and adds the specific marginal erasure/hand
qualification. It neither invents a variant nor independently recovers the
original Greek of Symmachus. The main text also documents an anger
interpretation in this Greek-derived Syriac transmission, not a rule excluding
other meanings of the Greek word. The already consulted Peshitta is a different
Syriac transmission; language agreement alone cannot establish independence
or Hebrew priority.

Ceriani's preface describes supplementary and corrective work using other
sources, including distinctions between main text, margins and added material.
The facsimile, commentary and Field's retroversion therefore remain separate
in the comparison. This is context-informed consultation of a published
manuscript reproduction, not autopsy of the present original, a blinded
transcription or a calibrated restoration result. Deterministic PDF detail
