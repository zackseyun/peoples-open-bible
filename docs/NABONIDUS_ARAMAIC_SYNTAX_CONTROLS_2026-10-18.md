# Aramaic controls for forgiveness in the Prayer of Nabonidus

## Judgment

The three cited passages support the grammatical distinctions in Stadel's
argument, but they do not establish the diviner as the agent of forgiveness
in 4Q242. Retain the [Daniel comparison's judgment](DANIEL_4_NABONIDUS_AND_GREEK_FORMS_2026-10-18.md):
agency in the related prayer is disputed; neither a changed Daniel source nor
a changed POB translation follows. This is verification of published evidence,
not a newly deciphered reading or a discovery new to scholarship.

The useful result is narrower than a preferred translation. A separate pronoun
introduced by ל can express an object, but it can also identify the recipient
of forgiveness. Its role must be established from the construction rather than
from its spelling alone. All readings below are from edited transcriptions,
not a fresh examination of manuscript ink.

## The two object constructions

In [1Q20 XIII 16](https://lexicon.qumran-digital.org/transcriptions/1Q20/2026-10-06/index.html#c711754-i25261),
the sequence ומענפן לה ויתברן לה has no brackets or uncertainty marks.
The finite example is ויתברן לה, with the winds acting on the olive tree.
It follows ומענפן לה, a participial construction with the same object. This
supports the setting for Stadel's proposed attraction from a participle:
the later verb retains the preceding pattern. The adjacency is observable in
the edition; attraction as its cause is an interpretation. The plural winds
and singular tree are different referents, unlike a self-referential dative.

In [1Q20 XIX 19](https://lexicon.qumran-digital.org/transcriptions/1Q20/2026-10-06/index.html#c712250-i26047),
די יבעון למקטלני ולכי למשבק is likewise unbracketed and unmarked for uncertainty.
The infinitive למקטלני has an attached first-person object, while ולכי places
the second-person feminine object before למשבק. In the dream explanation to
Sarai, the contrast is between killing the speaker and sparing her. The
object is fronted within the second coordinated infinitival phrase; it is
not demonstrably at the beginning of the entire sentence. The earlier ל[ה]
after the speech verb is a distinct, partly supplied expression.

These examples make the separate object construction possible, not ordinary
in every context. They validate the particular comparisons; they do not
independently reproduce Stadel's corpus-wide frequency claim. The electronic
word spans contain no publisher morphological analysis, so the grammatical
descriptions here are analysis of the text, not imported annotations.

## Forgiveness with a recipient

[11Q10 XXXVIII 2–3](https://lexicon.qumran-digital.org/transcriptions/11Q10/2026-10-06/index.html#c184841-i184848)
reads, across the line break, ושבק / להון חטאיהון בדילה. These target words
are complete and have no uncertainty marks in the edition. The pronoun is
להון, third-person plural, not לכון. The clause identifies their sins as the
object and them as the recipients: God remits their sins for Job's sake.
The preceding divine designation is partly supplied, א[ל]הא, while אלהא
also survives within the following restoration clause on line 3.

This is a particularly useful control because the same verb can have a
recipient introduced by ל alongside an explicit object. It is not evidence
that the prayer's singular לה necessarily has that role: the prayer has a
different discourse, a damaged preceding line and competing clause divisions.
Nor does the Job passage independently establish an ethical dative, in which
the pronoun refers back to the grammatical subject.

## What changes in the assessment

Stadel's preferred division attaches וחטאי to the preceding clause, then takes
שבק לה גזר as a complete clause with the diviner as its subject. His subsequent
Jewish-identity clause no longer interrupts an unfinished predicate. That is
a serious alternative to the usual divine-agent interpretation, but it still
requires the unusual separate object pronoun. The two object controls do not
remove this cost; the Job control cautions against assigning the pronoun's
function automatically. These three passages do not test the whole argument
about circumstantial clauses or eliminate every competing division.

The controlling discussion is Stadel, *The Biblical Annals* 14/4 (2024),
pp. 641–642, including notes 15–27. The first case study was read in the
predecessor comparison; these two pages and their renders were checked again
here. Fassberg's 2018 article was located bibliographically but its full text
was not acquired. Stadel's exclusion of the ethical dative with this transitive
verb, and his correction of Fassberg, therefore remain attributed claims,
not independently reproduced results.
