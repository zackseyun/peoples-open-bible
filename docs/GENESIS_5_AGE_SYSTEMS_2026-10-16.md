# Genesis 5 age systems and their translation implications

## Decision

Genesis 5 contains consequential differences in the ages assigned to its
genealogical figures. POB accurately translates its declared Masoretic numbers.
A reader note at 5:12 now discloses the linked Kenan variant; replacing the
Hebrew numbers is not yet justified by this comparison. The wider chapter
patterns remain documented here rather than fully annotated in the reader.
These are established published variants newly compared for POB, not newly
deciphered manuscript text.

The comparison treats each person's three numbers as one linked unit: age at
the named son's birth, years afterward, and total years. It keeps the selected
Greek editions separate, because their Methuselah numbers differ. All 36
triads below balance. Arithmetic consistency helps detect a mistaken mixture
of readings; it cannot decide which internally consistent system is earlier.

## Compared readings

Each cell lists **before / after / total**, in years. References are to Genesis
5. Enoch's 365 denotes his total days in 5:23, not a stated age at death; 5:24
describes God taking him.

| Lamech | 28, 30, 31 | 182 / 595 / 777 | 53 / 600 / 653 | 188 / 565 / 753 | 188 / 565 / 753 |

For six figures, both selected Greek texts place 100 more years before the
named son's birth and 100 fewer afterward than WLC, preserving the total.
This is a relative comparison, not proof that Greek expanded an earlier
Hebrew number: movement in the opposite direction also produces the pattern.
Jared, Methuselah and Lamech prevent a single uniform adjustment from describing
the whole chapter. The Samaritan edition agrees with WLC for the first five
figures and Enoch, but differs in all three numbers for Jared, Methuselah and
Lamech.

The differences affect the chronology implied by reading the genealogy as a
continuous sequence. Adding the nine begetting ages gives 1,056 years to Noah's
birth for WLC, 707 for this Samaritan edition, 1,662 for Swete's selected Greek,
and 1,642 for Rahlfs–Hanhart. These are conditional sums of textual numbers,
not historical dates, Flood dates, or a finding about whether the genealogy
must be read as a continuous chronological record.

## Edition identity and preservation

**Masoretic control.** The repository's WLC/OSHB Genesis source and all 32 POB
chapter records were inspected. The displayed Hebrew numerals and their English
renderings agree at the 27 age loci. Other issues in the chapter's notes and
name spellings are outside this comparison. The baseline commit and chapter
manifest digest are preserved in the [numerical record](../sources/textual_restoration/comparisons/genesis5_age_systems.2026-10-16.v1.json).

**Samaritan control.** Moshe Florentin and Abraham Tal's 2024
[edition](https://doi.org/10.11647/OBP.0415), printed pp. 100–103 (PDF pp.
117–120), supplies the Hebrew readings above. Its introduction, pp. 39–41,
identifies MS no. 6 (C) as its basis and explicitly distinguishes its written
text from the community's full range of manuscripts and oral readings. It is
not the Chester Beatty 751-based digital dataset used in earlier POB cases.
Pages 62–63 explain the notation: paired double-angle marks indicate absence
relative to MT, not damaged or reconstructed manuscript letters. The red marks
at 5:18, 25 and 28 must not become claims of uncertain physical preservation.
The accompanying apparatus compares SP with MT; it is not a complete collation
of Samaritan manuscripts.

**Greek controls.** The complete [Rahlfs–Hanhart 2006 Genesis 5 text](https://www.die-bibel.de/bibel/LXX/GEN.5)
was read on the Bible Society's site. Swete, volume I, fourth edition (1909,
1925 reprint), printed pp. 7–9 (PDF pp. 39–41), was checked visually. Its local
base is Alexandrinus (A), not surviving Vaticanus text at this passage.
Brooke–McLean's 1906 Genesis, printed pp. 11–13 (PDF pp. 27–29), was also
inspected. Its prefatory note identifies the outer-margin siglum as the local
base and explains its reuse of Swete's text; Swete's fourth-edition preface in
turn acknowledges corrections from the larger Cambridge edition. They are not
independent manuscript votes.

At 5:25, Brooke–McLean reports 167 for A's apparent original reading, with
187 in its selected text and a correction over erasure. The qualification
*videtur* matters. At 5:26 its apparatus records the associated 802/782
variation. These are edition reports of manuscript states, not a fresh reading
of erased ink. Rahlfs–Hanhart selects 167/802/969; Swete selects 187/782/969.
Other numeral variants also occur in the Cambridge apparatus. Thus neither
column represents unanimous Greek testimony, and agreement between modern
editions cannot establish an additional ancient witness. A complete modern
Greek apparatus comparison remains open.

**Qumran control.** The actual
[4Q2 transcription, fragment 3 ii, line 1](https://lexicon.qumran-digital.org/transcriptions/4Q2/2026-05-21/index.html)
places the relevant Genesis 5:13 age entirely inside supplied-text brackets.
Only Kenan's name precedes that bracket, with its final letter marked doubtful.
Consequently this line does not attest surviving 840 rather than 740. Its MT
and SP comparison rows are not additional Qumran ink. This is a local
preservation finding, not a census proving every other ancient witness absent.

## Competing explanations and POB impact

The repeated hundred-year transfers are evidence of a coordinated relationship
among the selected texts. They are harder to explain as nine unrelated slips
than as transmission involving linked adjustments. Nevertheless, this pattern
does not identify the direction, stage or language of adjustment. Greek
differences could involve a Hebrew exemplar, Greek transmission, or both;
arithmetical coherence alone cannot supply the missing history. The Samaritan
pattern and the distinct Greek Methuselah readings must be explained rather
than averaged into one supposed original.

The strongest case for reconsidering POB is substantive: the alternative ages
change relationships between generations, not merely spelling or English
style. The strongest objection to changing its numbers now is also substantive:
this comparison establishes competing coherent readings, but has not traced
their full manuscript distribution or shown which explains the others best.
An older translation's conventional date cannot be assigned to every number
printed in a modern Greek edition. Removing a general preference for older
witnesses therefore does not change this provisional retention decision.

A concrete example protects against a false improvement: selecting Kenan's
Greek 170 at 5:12 while retaining WLC's 840 at 5:13 and 910 at 5:14 yields
170 + 840 = 1,010, not 910. The mismatch is a reason to investigate the whole
unit, not permission to silently repair an attested witness to fit an expected
sum.

## Reader application

The applied note at 5:12 reads:

> The Masoretic text, followed here, gives Kenan 70 years before Mahalalel's
> birth and 840 afterward (verse 13); the Greek text printed by Rahlfs–Hanhart
> gives 170 and 740. Both total 910 years (verse 14). The Samaritan text
> printed by Tal and Florentin agrees with the Masoretic numbers here.

The note names the selected editions rather than implying unanimity among
Greek or Samaritan manuscripts. It does not decide which age system is earlier.
The new marker **b** follows “seventy years”; the existing lexical note **a**
and its marker are unchanged. Hebrew, marker-free main English, lexical
decisions and original draft provenance are unchanged. Five prior fields are
archived exactly: status, revision pass, cross-check, main English and notes.
The current record is draft/needs-review rather than inheriting approval of
the older record.

One independent delegated reader checked the exact complete candidate,
verses 5:11–15, the comparison report and numerical record. The review passed
the edition attribution, age relationships, note placement and archival
boundaries. It is not a fresh manuscript collation or source-priority decision.
The [contract](../sources/textual_restoration/comparisons/genesis5_12_age_reader_contract.2026-10-16.v1.json)
pins the baseline and candidate; the [application receipt](../sources/textual_restoration/applications/genesis5_12_age_reader.2026-10-16.v1.json)
records the review and actual export check. All 1,533 Genesis verses were
exported: only 5:12 changed, its two notes are present, and the other 1,532
units remain identical. The applied export matches the preflight digest.
This verifies the export data, not deployment or mobile visual rendering.

All four application tests pass, alongside 47 existing numerical, shared
reader-export and Genesis 6:3 application tests. The WLC source and unrelated
working file retain their hashes; documentation links, workflow parsing and
the diff check pass. These checks validate implementation and preservation,
not which ancient age system is original.

The original numerical record remains a historical pre-application receipt;
its no-application flags are not rewritten. A chapter-level reader summary
remains a useful next step for disclosing the wider pattern without repeating
notes at all 27 age loci.

the four Samaritan text pages, its relevant introductory sections, and the
current POB records. A separate source reader independently checked all nine
Samaritan triads and the edition's notation and basis, finding no discrepancy.
That check does not certify Greek priority or the proposed English note.

hundred-year differences, the three Samaritan differences and the distinct
Greek Methuselah profiles. Its unit tests check these relationships and reject
the example hybrid; they do not prove the transcriptions or historical priority.
PDF hashes identify consulted files, which remain outside Git. The record
stores numerical facts and citations, not whole copyrighted source editions.

All four `tests.test_genesis5_age_comparison` tests pass. A separate validation
reproduced the WLC hash, all 32 chapter records against the pinned baseline,
