# Ecclesiastes 8 10 reviser evidence followup

Newly consulted published apparatus and a target-level critical argument strengthen consideration of וישתבחו, but do not approve the unchanged full “had boasted” candidate. This is a comparison of known readings, not a new manuscript decipherment. The [original independent HOLD](ECCLESIASTES_8_10_EXACT_REVIEW_2026-10-10.md) remains a historical receipt with its original inputs.

## What the reviser reports establish

Frederick Field, *Origenis Hexaplorum quae supersunt*, volume II (Oxford, 1875), printed page 396, presents Aquila/Theodotion with ἐκαυχήσαντο, “boasted,” and Symmachus with ἐπαινούμενοι, “being praised.” The Old Greek line has passive praise. The target page and notes were read in full, together with the title, volume II page 3 and volume I page xcv conventions. [Target page](https://archive.org/download/origenhexapla02unknuoft/origenhexapla02unknuoft_jp2.zip/origenhexapla02unknuoft_jp2%2Forigenhexapla02unknuoft_0412.jp2?ext=jpg).

Crucially, note 17 supplies a Syro-hexaplaric report, not surviving Greek Aquila/Theodotion letters. Field reconstructs Greek from that Syriac transmission. Note 18 assembles Symmachus partly from Nobilius and partly from the Syro-hexapla, and records uncertainty about the preceding clause. Combined interpreter sigla can identify shared sense rather than identical wording in separate Greek copies. They are not two new independent manuscript votes.

Field's introduction identifies the Ambrosian Syro-hexaplaric source through Norberg's 1778 apograph and Middeldorpf's 1835 edition, with Ceriani's manuscript rechecking of marginal readings for Field. These are different stages in one evidential chain. Neither the target codex pixels nor its physical date were newly verified here. Field's parenthetical Hebrew bet is an editorial comparison, not another Hebrew manuscript attestation.

## The modern target argument

Peter J. Gentry's 2013 *The Distinctive Aims of the Göttingen Apparatus: Examples from Ecclesiastes—An Edition in Preparation*, pages 94–96, supplies an actual target text, both apparatuses and an argument. Its second apparatus reports Aquila/Theodotion boasting through Syh and distinguishes Symmachus; Latin wording there represents the Syriac report, not a newly observed Latin witness. [Official repository record and article](https://rep.adw-goe.de/handle/11858/00-001S-0000-002D-B47A-F).

Gentry argues that the Old Greek presupposes Hebrew bet and possibly a different conjunction on the going verb, while otherwise largely matching MT. He rejects Goldman's larger reconstruction as insufficiently attested, particularly its “approaching” instead of “buried” and sanctuary-preposition changes. This supports testing the small bet correction without importing a temple narrative. It remains a scholarly inference from transmission and translation, not direct Hebrew glyph evidence. The 2013 sample does not certify the final 2019 apparatus or its errata.

Gentry quotes Goldman's BHQ proposal. That quotation was read; the BHQ target entry itself was not. Likewise, the earlier NET citation to preliminary HOTTP III 584–585 remains unread. It must not be confused with *Critique textuelle de l'Ancien Testament* volume III: the relevant CTAT wisdom volume is V.

## Source selection and English remain separate

The new reports make a simple “all praise is merely a late English preference” account untenable. They corroborate an old transmitted contrast between forgetting, boasting and praise. Together with De Rossi's qualified Hebrew collation, they strengthen the bet hypothesis. But related reviser reports, modern apparatuses and scholarly interpretations cannot be added as independent ancient Hebrew copies.

The countercase remains the MT forgetting tradition, forgetting in Ecclesiastes 2:16 and 9:5, uncertain relations of later bet copies to reception, and unresolved target chronology/correction states. No copying motive is proved. A reading can explain the Greek well without thereby being the earliest Hebrew wording. Provisional retention of WLC is still not a demonstrated historical victory for kaf.

Even if bet becomes preferred, “had boasted” requires separate justification for reflexive agency, prior time and the subjects of the surrounding verbs. Revisers strengthen the lexical plausibility of boasting but do not settle those whole-clause choices. Passive public praise and self-commendation are different meanings; general passive Hitpael examples do not alone select either here. No canonical YAML, reader English, note-only substitute or canon designation changes in this followup.
