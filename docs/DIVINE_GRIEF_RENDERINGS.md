# Divine grief wording — 2026-09-14

At the maintainer's explicit request, POB now reads Genesis 6:6:

> And Yahweh was grieved that he had made man on the earth, and he was pained in his heart.

Genesis 6:7 and 1 Samuel 15:11, 35 use corresponding grief language. This is a
translation-choice revision, not a claim that the former rendering was lexically
impossible. Hebrew נחם can express retrospective sorrow/regret; the selected
wording retains the prior action as the object of grief. Genesis 6:6 retains
“was pained” for the separate עצב verb. The rendering does not settle the
philosophical relationship between divine grief, foreknowledge, and constancy.

The [NET Genesis 6:6 translation notes](https://classic.net.bible.org/passage.php?passage=Gen+6%3A6)
recognize grief/sorrow and regret within the contextual range of נחם, and
distinguish this from relenting concerning a stated course of action. The
repository's WLC source text is unchanged. Previous choices remain in revision
history and regret remains visible as a lexical alternative in the notes.

A search of English POB reader text for regret, repentance, sorrow, and relenting
identified these four affirmative divine-regret renderings in the canonical
books. Judgment-withdrawal passages (including Exodus 32:14 and Jonah 3:10),
negative statements about relenting (including 1 Samuel 15:29), human repentance,
and human regret are not blanket-replaced. Extra-canonical characters and their
repentance are not equated automatically with the biblical divine subject.

Scope: English POB source records. Simplified POB and other languages are separate
translation records and are not revised by this change. A source commit is not
proof of website publication or a mobile release.

Validation: focused rendering and context-preservation tests, footnote audit tests,
and a four-record footnote audit. Original Hebrew and AI draft provenance are
preserved; current lexical/theological explanations and revision records document
the maintainer-selected alternative.
