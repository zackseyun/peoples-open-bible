# Zechariah 12 10 source comparison and English decision

At main `f8ad8874d89c8b3eaae0342f84df094559b3aa5c`, compare the pointed
WLC phrase `אלי את אשר דקרו` and the subsequent `עליו`, distinguishing
Hebrew wording, Greek transmission and Hebrew syntax. Provisionally retain
WLC. Prefer the compact English **to me, whom they pierced**, with the
substantive alternative **to me concerning the one they pierced** disclosed.
The first-person to third-person shift remains. This is a local rendering
refinement and evidence comparison, not new decipherment or recovered original.

## Hebrew syntax and competing explanations

Root and the source assessor read complete [GKC section 118](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Gesenius%27_Hebrew_Grammar/118._The_Looser_Subordination_of_the_Accusative_to_the_Verb)
and [section 138](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Gesenius%27_Hebrew_Grammar/138._The_Relative_Pronoun).
Section 118m describes circumstantial accusatives; the `את` body-part example
in 1 Kings 15:23 is **118q**, correcting the assessor's initial locator. This
makes a respect construction worth testing, not proved for this verse. Section
138e footnote 6 directly objects to the received construction and conjectures
`אל־אשר`. That deletion is a scholarly proposal, not attested new Hebrew or
GKC's endorsement of the respect interpretation.

The appositional interpretation identifies the pierced object with the speaker
and preserves the difficult following him. The respect/cause interpretation
relates the looking to the pierced person and makes him an easier antecedent;
its general grammatical permission is not an exact constructional parallel.
Neither context nor the person shift settles the relation. The supplied packet
contains 12:1, 9, 11–14 and 13:1–3: Yahweh's speech, communal mourning and the
later piercing action do not identify the pierced person in 12:10.

The strongest inspected transmission alternative is Hebrew metathesis or Greek
interpretation producing dancing, followed by Greek alignment with piercing.
The source assessor read Karrer/Kraus 2008 pp. 48–50 and Menken 2007 p. 286;
those are author/edition reports, not root manuscript collation. Published `רו`
fits `דקרו` rather than the proposed `רקדו` ending `דו`. This favors piercing
without certifying the complete ancient verb or the syntax. Removing a modest
chronological preference would not remove this letter distinction or settle
the syntax. Oldest copy, majority support and theological usefulness do not decide.

## Anonymous English comparison and root decision

The [frozen packet](../sources/textual_restoration/applications/zech12_10_english_comparison.2026-10-04.v1.json)
fixed the source, paragraph contexts, other English words and fidelity-first
rubric before assessment. Candidate identities were withheld and order was A,
B, C rather than current wording first. C was current POB; A was concerning
the one; B was whom. This was a same-model separate assessment, not cross-family
calibration, a blinded manuscript transcription or corpus-wide evaluation.

The assessor modestly preferred B: economical English preserving the abrupt
me to him shift. A remains substantive but resolves the relation differently.
C is defensible, yet its the one adds an explicit nominal bridge that partly
eases the person shift. Root accepts B provisionally, not as a uniquely optimal
translation. No other main-English words change; the a, b and c note anchors
move to the phrases they explain. Notes d and e disclose disputed syntax and
the limited Hebrew/Greek evidence. No source consonants or pointing change.
