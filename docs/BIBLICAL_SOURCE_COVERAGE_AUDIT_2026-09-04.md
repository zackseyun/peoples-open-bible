# OT and NT source coverage: audit and comparison plan

Initial audit: 2026-09-04; Latin and Leviticus follow-ups: 2026-09-05. Companion to [method 2.0](TEXTUAL_ADJUDICATION_METHOD.md)
and the [approach review](TEXTUAL_RESTORATION_APPROACH_REVIEW_2026-09-04.md).

## Verdict

We are using relevant sources, but we have not compared all known witnesses.
The current work is a useful pilot, not a comprehensive Hebrew/Aramaic or Greek
critical edition. The biggest improvement is to connect the pilot to systematic
catalogue and critical-apparatus coverage, then adjudicate specific readings.
Collecting more photographs alone will not establish completeness or priority.

“All discovered sources” is not a closed, fully accessible dataset. Our
operational target is all identifiable, relevant published/catalogued witnesses
as of a recorded cutoff, with inaccessible, unpublished, disputed, and
unidentified material explicitly listed. Unknown material cannot be certified
as covered. A newly digitized copy is not necessarily a newly discovered text.

Aramaic portions of the OT stay Aramaic; “Hebrew corpus” is shorthand, not

### Editions and discovery authorities to use

1. **BHQ with BHS fallback:** inspect the apparatus and book introduction, not
   only the printed base. The publisher describes a Leningrad-based diplomatic
   text with additional codex and pre-Tiberian witness comparison. Its current
   list has Genesis and Deuteronomy published, but Exodus and Samuel still in
   preparation; therefore those pilot books cannot all be assigned a completed
   BHQ apparatus. Pin the actual fascicle and edition used.
   [BHQ project and publication table](https://www.die-bibel.de/en/home/scholarly-editions/vulgate/scholarly-bible-editions/biblia-hebraica-quinta-bhq).
2. **Hebrew University Bible Project:** use available book apparatuses for
   versions, Judean Desert witnesses, medieval/Genizah manuscripts, and
   quotations. Its Aleppo-based edition is not a second independent ancient
   vote. **HBCE**, where published, supplies an explicit alternative critical
   argument; its proposed readings remain editorial judgments.
   [HUBP](https://en.bible.huji.ac.il/projects-and-publications),
   [HBCE editorial method](https://www.sbl-site.org/sbl-press/browse-books/the-hebrew-bible-a-critical-edition/).
3. **IAA, Israel Museum, published archaeological editions and versioned
   transcriptions:** reconcile object identifiers and passage survival. The
   IAA explicitly includes finds beyond Qumran in the Judean Desert corpus.
   QDR's 266 indexed biblical records are a discovery aid, not the global
   denominator. Newly published fragments and joins require dated updates.
   [IAA discovery/publication account](https://www.deadseascrolls.org.il/learn-about-the-scrolls/discovery-and-publication?locale=en_US).
4. **Göttingen LXX catalogue and critical editions:** use Rahlfs identifiers
   and the individual book's apparatus. Early papyri matter as well as
   Vaticanus, Sinaiticus and Alexandrinus. The IOSCS distinguishes diplomatic
   Swete/Cambridge from the critical Göttingen project and identifies other
   editions useful where coverage differs. Check current book availability
   rather than assuming one edition covers every book equally.
   [Catalogue](https://septuaginta.uni-goettingen.de/catalogue/),
   [edition guide](https://septuaginta.uni-goettingen.de/ioscs/editions/).
5. **Samaritan critical edition and manuscript catalogues:** add the
   Schorch-led project to the Torah dossier; record which volume was actually
   consulted. Do not infer a manuscript's text from a different reference
   edition. [Project](https://www.theologie.uni-halle.de/bw/samaritanerforschung/samaritanusedition/).
6. **NLI/Ktiv and holding-library catalogues:** discover surviving Hebrew
   codices/fragments, then use the object's catalogue and edition. Cambridge's
   Nash record illustrates why coverage and genre matter: it is a brief
   Decalogue/Shema witness, not an early complete Pentateuch.
   [NLI access guidance](https://www.nli.org.il/en/at-your-service/reference/online-access),
   [Nash Papyrus](https://exhibitions.lib.cam.ac.uk/linesofthought/artifacts/the-ten-commandments/).

## New Testament: required evidence map

| Source group | Role | Current gap / priority |
|---|---|---|
| Greek papyri | Early direct Greek evidence in the passages actually preserved | Catalogue all relevant coverage, not just famous P45/P46/P66/P75. Inspect consequential readings and correction layers. P0 |
| Greek majuscules | Direct evidence, including Sinaiticus, Vaticanus, Alexandrinus, Ephraemi, Bezae and other book-specific witnesses | Build GA-number/hand/passage maps; no codex covers every NT passage. P0 |
| Greek minuscules | Later physical copies can preserve earlier readings and important local relationships | Do not discard by date; use apparatus selection and local genealogy, including Byzantine evidence. P0 |
| Lectionaries and commentary manuscripts | Biblical text within liturgical or interpretive transmission | Record lection boundaries and distinguish biblical text, lemma, comment, and adapted quotation. P1, higher where decisive |

P1 |

## Which sources are worth comparing?

### Operational NT refinements — 2026-09-06

The required NT source classes above remain appropriate. This follow-up checked
official resource descriptions, not a new manuscript census or passage apparatus.
For every acquisition distinguish catalogue identity, image availability,
transcription availability, apparatus consultation and actual passage survival.
The web reader exposed navigation at the [NTVMR Liste](https://ntvmr.uni-muenster.de/liste)
and [ECM Digital](https://ntvmr.uni-muenster.de/ecm), not usable passage evidence;
these access attempts do not count as collations.

Pin ECM/CBGM book, edition, dataset and analytical phase. INTF describes a
reasoned manuscript selection, not a census of every extant reading, and
[Genealogical Queries 2.0](https://intf.uni-muenster.de/cbgm/index_en.html)
specifically uses the 2013 Catholic Letters edition. Do not extrapolate that
dataset's coverage to the whole NT. [ECM method](https://www.uni-muenster.de/INTF/en/forschung/ecm/index.html).

For ancient versions and commentary witnesses, use the specific GALaCSy,
CATENA and Vetus Latina acquisition routes within the
[ITSEE projects](https://www.birmingham.ac.uk/research/centres-institutes/textual-scholarship-and-electronic-editing/projects),
including Gothic where relevant. For patristic evidence require author,
work/section, consulted critical edition, original/translated language,
surviving transmission and quotation-versus-commentary-lemma status. An author's
date is not the date of the surviving copy or a guarantee of verbatim quotation.
[Beuron's editorial description](https://www.herder.de/vetus-latina/institut/)
provides a concrete route to individual Latin witness wording.

Track editorial inheritance explicitly: UBS6's adoption of ECM decisions does
not add independent attestations to them. Agreement among editions is useful
for discovery, not manuscript voting.
[Publisher's UBS6 description](https://www.die-bibel.de/en/en/the-sixth-edition-of-the-greek-new-testament-ubs6).
No restricted apparatus was imported and no NT source was changed by this review.

Discover broadly; prioritize detailed collation by the question being asked.
For every candidate, record:

1. Object/edition identity, provenance and authenticity status. Disputed
   identification or unverified commercial fragments stay in a separate lane.
2. Exact passage and disputed-letter survival, not just book-level coverage.
3. Direct language, translation, quotation, commentary, or modern edition.
4. Scribal hand, correction state, date range and known local relationships.
5. Whether it distinguishes the candidate readings. A translation compatible
   with both Hebrew alternatives cannot choose between them.
6. Likely English or literary-form impact, and unresolved material questions.
7. Access/rights, checked date, and why included, deferred, or excluded.

High priority means strong potential to resolve the current question, not a
predetermined historical winner. Never silently remove contrary evidence.
An uncertain source may be important without supporting either reading.

different numbering, passage order, omissions and literary forms. Then test
which reading best explains the others through copying, revision, translation
practice and context. Record the strongest counterargument and abstain when
the evidence does not decide.

For Greek OT evidence, establish the Greek textual form before proposing a
Hebrew/Aramaic retroversion. A back-translation is a hypothesis, not newly
discovered Hebrew. Keep larger literary editions parallel where combining them
would create an unsupported composite. NT Greek remains Greek; a Syriac or
Hebrew rendering is not automatically an earlier original.

The deliverable is a pinned base plus justified source patches and apparatus,
with English checked against that exact source. “Retain,” “propose change,”
and “unresolved” are all valid outcomes. Conjectures and supplied text remain
labelled. ImageGen never supplies manuscript evidence.
