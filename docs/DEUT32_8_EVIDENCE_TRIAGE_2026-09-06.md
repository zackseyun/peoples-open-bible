# Deuteronomy 32:8: component-specific evidence and stopping decision

Checked 2026-09-06. **Retain the canonical WLC/“sons of Israel” for now; keep
“sons of God” as the existing provisional source-change proposal.** This pass
does not approve or reject its eventual adoption, raise confidence, restore
letters, or rewrite frozen candidate/preflight records.

## The question that matters

The [earlier comparison](DEUT32_8_ADJUDICATION_FOLLOWUP_2026-09-05.md) and
[numbering check](DEUT32_8_NUMBERING_CHECK_2026-09-05.md) already distinguish
published Hebrew wording from physical preservation and manuscript identifiers.
The remaining issue is not how many modern publications repeat “sons of God.”
It is which ancient witness actually preserves each part of the phrase, how
those witnesses relate, and whether the Hebrew reading is inherited or adapted.

Four distinctions control the decision:

- **4Q37:** the published Hebrew reading בני אלוהים is the direct basis for
  the proposal. It is one fragmentary manuscript, not a complete independent
  attestation of every word in the proposed composite verse.
- **Fouad 848:** the completed [published-note and plate consultation](../sources/textual_restoration/discovery/deut32_8_fouad_review.v1.json)
  supports the sons noun, not an independently preserved God/Israel complement.
  Do not repeat the older “locate and inspect 848” acquisition instruction.
- **106 correction:** the [Cambridge consultation](../sources/textual_restoration/discovery/deut32_8_greek_review.v1.json)
  reports sons of Israel with its correcting-hand and uncertainty qualifications.
  A list supporting the sons noun cannot establish that the same hand reads God
  in the next word. The modern adjacent apparatus entries remain unconsulted.
- **Masoretic/Samaritan and El alternatives:** MT/SP preserve בני ישראל;
  the OHB sample proposes בני אל without claiming direct Hebrew attestation
  for that exact phrase. An attractive explanation of how the readings arose
  is not an observed copying event. Greek does not uniquely select אלוהים
  over אל, even when it supports a divine referent.

32:8–9 and the Inheritance of the Land of Israel,” *Tarbiz* 85/1, pp. 29–63,
[institutional PDF](https://jstudies.huji.ac.il/sites/default/files/jstudies/files/tarbiz_-_8501_-_archive_-_02_-_bar-on-paz.pdf).

dates it October–December 2017; some author bibliographies list 2018.

Actually inspected PDF pages 1–3, printed pp. 29–31, including the complete

Wevers, *Notes*, pp. 512–513, and *La Bible d’Alexandrie*, pp. 325–326. They
distinguish their reconstructed text from the quoted Hebrew evidence.

This is a directly consulted scholarly argument, **not a newly collated Greek
manuscript, inspected Armenian edition, or proof of exhaustive non-attestation**.
It corroborates the need to separate noun support from complete-phrase support;
it does not substitute for consulting the modern apparatus itself.

## Stop and reopen deliberately

No additional canonical edit follows from this pass. The divine-referent
working preference remains moderate, not disproved by loss of complete-phrase
support from individual Greek witnesses. Conversely, an editorially printed
Greek phrase is not itself a manuscript carrying that whole phrase. The
current POB note already discloses the competing Hebrew and Greek readings.

Reopen acquisition when at least one concrete new resource is available:

1. The complete modern adjacent noun/complement apparatus entries, ideally
   with their conventions or a legible 106 correction image. Determine which
   readings belong to which hand and which letters actually survive.
2. Dayfani's full material reconstruction or equivalent passage-specific
   evidence capable of testing adaptation in 4Q37. Do not infer adaptation
   merely from its excerpt character.

These are discriminating evidence targets, not a demand for certainty before
any provisional editorial decision. A future editor may explicitly adjudicate
from the available evidence while recording these limits; it must be a new
source/full-sentence decision, not a silent checkmark on an old acquisition gate.
Do not repeat failed preview searches or the completed image/Fouad work without
a new access lead. Other consequential cases can proceed in parallel.
