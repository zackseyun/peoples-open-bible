# Hosea 13:14 translation review

The opening Hebrew can express either a rescue promise or questions implying
refusal to rescue. The immediate judgment passage favors the questions as a
working interpretation; the promise remains a serious alternative. This is a
meaningful English decision against unchanged Hebrew, not a new manuscript
reading, recovered ink, or an argument about biblical canon.

## Hebrew grammar and passage context

The first-person imperfects identify the speaker and acts of ransom/redemption,
but do not determine whether these acts are asserted or questioned. The opening
has neither an explicit interrogative particle nor an explicit negative.
[GKC 150a](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Gesenius%27_Hebrew_Grammar/150._Interrogative_Sentences)
allows unmarked questions, although marking is usual and some examples are
disputed. [GKC 107r–t](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Gesenius%27_Hebrew_Grammar/107._Use_of_the_Imperfect)
allows potential/interrogative imperfects. These establish possibilities, not
this verse's particular rhetorical force.

The selected Hebrew controls in Isaiah 44:19, Zechariah 8:6 and Jeremiah 25:29
illustrate unmarked questions. Isaiah's fronted prepositional phrase and
first-person imperfect are particularly relevant. Conversely, Hosea 5:10 and
7:15 show assertions with fronted prepositional phrases. Neither word order nor
morphology settles the choice. Jeremiah's explicit following denial must not
be inserted into Hosea.

In Hosea 13, guilt remains stored, the unwise son fails to emerge at birth,
compassion is hidden, a destructive wind dries and plunders, and Samaria is
punished. POB follows Masoretic numbering here: the Samaria passage is 14:1,
not 13:16. Within that sequence, deliberative questions fit refusal to rescue.
The negative answer is inferred from context; it is not a newly found Hebrew
word. The later questions addressed to Death/Sheol can rhetorically summon
their destructive powers without being translated as unattested imperatives.
The same-chapter form in 13:10 supports retaining “where” independently of Paul.

The strongest contrary case is an abrupt rescue promise. Hosea 11:8–9 genuinely
reverses contemplated surrender into stirred compassion and refusal to destroy;
14:2–5 moves through return/confession to healing and love. A reversal in 13:14
therefore cannot be excluded merely because judgment surrounds it. On that
reading, the questions mock Death, and the final clause could express refusal
to relent from rescue. That interpretation remains possible, but fits the
shared “Compassion” wording and immediately continued destruction less easily.
This is contextual preference, not grammatical certainty.

## Greek wording and later reception

The private, pinned CATSS/Rahlfs-derived digital control has future rescue and
redemption forms, “where” questions, penalty/judgment addressed to Death and
sting addressed to Hades. It has no victory noun at this position. Its token
file has no punctuation, so the indicative forms alone do not prove that an
assertion rather than a question was intended. It is an edition-derived
control, not a manuscript collation or proof of Greek unanimity.

The selected SBLGNT wording in 1 Corinthians 15:55 has victory, then sting,
addressing Death twice. POB's former Hosea note blended these distinct forms.
The candidate separates them. Paul's resurrection use is meaningful reception,
but cannot by itself dictate Hosea's original speech act. No NT variant or
Hebrew back-translation is adjudicated here.

[Popko's 2019 argument](https://czasopisma.kul.pl/index.php/ba/article/view/4503)
on printed pages 499–501 distinguishes questions, past-habitual, future and
potential readings and recognizes both the negative context and ambiguity in
Death's role. This supports preserving interpretive range, not recovering a
different Hebrew source. The publisher's parsed PDF body was consulted;
native page layout could not be verified. No later Hexaplar apparatus or plate
inspection is claimed.

The pinned QDR index returns no word hit for Hos 13:14; neighboring hits locate
4Q82 and 4Q78 with supplied material. That result establishes neither target
ink nor absence from every known manuscript. Full QDR/Greek data remain private
consultation inputs. The bounded record gives hashes, locators, rights and
coverage limits. WLC/YAML controls are not counted as independent witnesses;
Greek reception is not a second direct Hebrew vote. The modest chronological
preference does not change this contextual outcome.

## English comparison and application boundary

Before the independent assessment, the source, 25 context units, three
candidates and ordered rubric were frozen. Meaning and unsupported additions
preceded ambiguity, literary force and readability. Candidate origins were
withheld, and candidates were displayed in S/N/R order. The assessor selected
N, retained R as serious, and found S weaker because “could” foregrounds a
particular modal possibility and weakens the deliberative confrontation.

The selected opening is “Shall I ransom them from the hand of Sheol? Shall I
redeem them from death?” It adds neither an explicit refusal nor an imperative.
The promise appears in a reader note. The following “where” questions and
compassion clause remain; their contextual interpretation is documented without
claiming ambiguity has disappeared. Alternate “I will be” and word/plague
analyses are not resolved by this opening-clause review.
