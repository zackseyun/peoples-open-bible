## Actual PTA text, not an automatically analysed derivative

PTA's [official data page](https://pta.bbaw.de/en/project/data) links its source
repository. The original `data/` XML was consulted, not the automatically
tokenized/analyzed `analyzed/` files. In the inspected
[2022 version](https://github.com/PatristicTextArchive/pta_data/blob/9d05b6882ed19fc620f42599d13b6cd2bbff423c/data/pta0003/pta020/pta0003.pta020.pta-grcBibex1.xml)
the Psalm 39 division is absent. It is present in the
[2026 version](https://github.com/PatristicTextArchive/pta_data/blob/ef861ab391ea35c5d85b41dd8d7ba2bf65530b21/data/pta0003/pta020/pta0003.pta020.pta-grcBibex1.xml),
whose commit date is May 13, 2026. This establishes coverage between inspected
versions, not the exact first-publication date.

Root read the whole current Psalm 39 division, all five fragments, and
re-read fragment 3 with its lemma, two paragraphs and header/preface.
The extended lemma has **ὠτία δὲ κατηρτίσω μοι**. It also has plural burnt
offerings, **οὐκ ἐξῄτησας**, **ἐβουλήθην**, and internal-law **κοιλίας**.
Do not silently substitute another selected edition's verb.

Inside the explanatory paragraph, the phrase is
**τὰ ὦτά μου καὶ τὴν ὑπακοὴν τῶν σῶν λογίων κατηρτίσω**.
The surrounding discussion is interpretive, not a continuous literal quotation.
Symmachus is explicitly named for nearby readings at Greek 39:6, 8b and 9b,
but not attached explicitly to this ear phrase. Nearby attribution does not
establish that this phrase is Symmachus's; neither does its presence establish
an independent pre-Hexaplaric Old Greek reading.

The edition names Barbara Villani, Sergey Kim, Benjamin Gleede and Magali
Coullet, under the Berlin-Brandenburg Academy of Sciences and Humanities.
Its header calls it a critical edition in progress, currently without variants;
the preface describes a preliminary reading text. The general source list,
including a Psalm 37 witness group, is not a target Psalm 39 apparatus.
A modern lemma is not another ancient continuous Psalter manuscript.
The file is CC BY-SA 4.0; this report attributes its bounded Greek excerpts,
and does not vendor the XML or relicense the project's corpus.

## New quotation controls from Gäbel — bounded, not full-chapter reading

Gäbel's 2024 chapter, DOI
[10.1515/9783111549682-001](https://doi.org/10.1515/9783111549682-001),
was extended to Eusebius, printed 10–13 / preview PDF 28–31, and Didymus,
printed 16–20 / PDF 34–38. All nine complete target pages were visually
inspected after text extraction; the intervening Athanasius section is not
adjudicated here. The full chapter, printed 3–47, remains unavailable.

Gäbel treats Eusebius's phrase as paraphrase and leaves its source open.
The chapter's then-missing PTA section can now be checked as above.
For Didymus, his collation reports body prepared, ears prepared, and ears dug.
“In others” need not identify separate Psalter copies; the terminology can
refer to interpreters or translators. Repeated quotations are not independent
manuscript votes. The dug form is Aquila-like and should not be counted as
proof of pre-Hexaplaric Old Greek ears. That qualification does not settle
the origin of ears prepared.

These are Gäbel's edition-based reports. Root has not consulted the actual
Didymus Tura target edition/image or Field's target page. Semantic fit of
“prepared” with body is an argument, not proof of historical priority.

## Bodmer: catalogue date established, target reading still not newly read

The [owner catalogue](https://collections.museumofthebible.org/artifacts/42463-pbodmer-xxiv-rahlfs-2110?tab=description)
identifies P.Bodmer XXIV / Rahlfs 2110 as MS.000170.1-.49 and dates it
approximately AD 225–325. This catalogue range does not securely place the
copy before Origen or Hexaplaric revision. The page describes 49 surviving
leaves, 98 pages, and two scribes. Its ten gallery labels were inventoried;
15R/15V were opened and 15V enlarged, but Greek Psalm 39:7 was not identified.
This is not a claim that the verse is absent or that all gallery images were read.

The [Göttingen entry](https://septuaginta.uni-goettingen.de/catalogue/Ra_2110/)
provides a bibliographic route, not a newly consulted target transcription.
Neither the Kasser–Testuz 1967 target nor Pietersma's 1980 target discussion
was acquired. The body reading remains a scholarly report in this dossier,
not a fresh pixel reading. Do not repeat the same unlocated gallery previews
as another evidence experiment.

## Consequences, efficiency and reader presentation

Greek priority remains unresolved. No Hebrew body wording has been recovered;
this follow-up supplies no basis for harmonizing Psalm and Hebrews, approving
an optimal main English rendering, or adding a book to the canon.
The next discriminating work is the actual Bodmer target edition/folio or a
full modern local apparatus, and the original Didymus passage. A separately
reviewed qualification of the existing blanket “the Septuagint” note remains
useful, without presupposing either transmission history.

the reading view clean and detailed evidence behind the existing selected-verse
**Check Provenance** route. No inline panel, reader/publisher implementation,
