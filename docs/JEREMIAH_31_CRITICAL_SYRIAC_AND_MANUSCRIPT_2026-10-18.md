# Jeremiah 31 critical Syriac and manuscript evidence

## Judgment

The negative Syriac wording at Jeremiah 31:32 is now checked in the modern
critical edition's electronic text and in the historical facsimile of its
identified base manuscript. This strengthens attestation and provenance beyond
the previously inspected 2020 edition. It does not establish an independent
Hebrew ancestor, the meaning of Hebrew בעלתי, or historical source priority.
Retain the current Hebrew and distinct Jeremiah and Hebrews main translations
provisionally. The [reader correction](JEREMIAH_31_READER_NOTE_CORRECTION_2026-10-18.md)
remains appropriate; this research applies no further canonical or reader edit.

## The critical edition is now directly available

The 2019 *Old Testament in Syriac*, III/2, gives Jeremiah prepared by D. M.
Walter with K. D. Jenner and J. G. Veldman. Its title and contents were checked
in the [two-page library copy](https://toc.library.ethz.ch/objects/pdf03/z01_978-90-04-32145-8_01.pdf).
The publisher's [Jeremiah 31 response](https://scholarlyeditions.brill.com/library/passage/urn:cts:syrLit:SyrBible.Jeremiah.peso-syr1:31/json/)
supplies actual text and embedded apparatus notes, not a search snippet.
The complete 31:31–34 units and [3:14 control](https://scholarlyeditions.brill.com/library/passage/urn:cts:syrLit:SyrBible.Jeremiah.peso-syr1:3/json/)
were read, including their embedded notes. The normalized target is
**ܒܣܝܬ ܒܗܘܢ**, despising them; 3:14 has **ܨܒܝܬ ܒܟܘܢ**, favorable
disposition toward you. This corroborates the earlier mixed profile, not a
mechanical negative interpretation wherever Hebrew has the following ב.

Four notes occur within 31:32, numbered 952–955, but none attaches to the
negative verb or its following recipient expression. That is not a manuscript
unanimity finding. The [actual preface p. x](https://scholarlyeditions.brill.com/library/passage/urn:cts:syrLit:SyrBible.Jeremiah.peso-eng2:x/json/)
explicitly warns against conclusions from silence. It uses an emended 7a1
base, rather than selecting each word as the reconstructed original Peshitta.
Pages xi–xv explain corrections, supplies, excluded variants and the need for
manuscript descriptions. The [Jeremiah remarks p. 6](https://scholarlyeditions.brill.com/library/passage/urn:cts:syrLit:SyrBible.Jeremiah.peso-eng1:6/json/)
give book-specific rules. The tokenized chapter response does not reproduce a
reliable printed-page summary of witness availability or legibility. Exact
support from other manuscripts still requires that missing coverage evidence
and their individual target states.

The public reader's collection and version responses advertised the chapter
URLs. Their responses retain `access: false`; no authentication, credentials,

Public endpoint availability is not a redistribution licence. The

## The identified manuscript agrees at the target

The edition's [manuscript description p. 10](https://scholarlyeditions.brill.com/library/passage/urn:cts:syrLit:SyrBible.Jeremiah.peso-eng1:10/json/)
identifies 7a1 as Milan, Ambrosian Library B 21 inf., Jeremiah folios
158a–174b, dated to the sixth or seventh century. That date is not a claim of
second-century physical attestation. Pages 7–14 discuss the old manuscripts,
including corrections and damage; choosing 7a1 as a practical base is not a
claim that it is the best witness throughout Jeremiah.

Ceriani's actual facsimile [scan n355](https://archive.org/download/CerianiTranslatioSyraPescittoVeterisTestamentiExCodiceAmbrosiano/page/n355.jpg),
printed 341 / folio 167r, carries the beginning of 31:32 at the bottom of its
left column. It continues at the top of the right column on
[scan n354](https://archive.org/download/CerianiTranslatioSyraPescittoVeterisTestamentiExCodiceAmbrosiano/page/n354.jpg),
printed 342 / folio 167v. The negative verb and recipient expression are visible
there. The reversed scan sequence was verified from the page labels and
continuous wording, not inferred from scan numbers alone. This is a
context-informed check of a known reading, not a new blinded decipherment.

The historical title dates volume I to 1876 and calls the manuscript roughly
sixth-century; that older estimate does not override the critical edition's
sixth/seventh-century description. Ceriani's preface, scanned n14–15 / printed
7–8, explains photolithographic treatment, including removal of perceived
extraneous spots and clarification of faint traces. These are reproductions,
not untouched modern photographs. No faint stroke, new correction layer or
previously unknown letter is accepted here. The facsimile and the critical
edition's base remain dependent presentations of one physical witness, not two
new ancient votes. Selective Greek influence still remains a possible
explanation for the Syriac's negative target.

reading preview pages 4 and 138–139 of the 2013 fourth edition, but the target
locator, printed 362, was explicitly unavailable. No retained local target
image or apparatus was obtained. A downloaded placeholder is not an edition

those exhausted routes without new access.

For Syriac, the next discriminating input is target-specific coverage and
readings of other old witnesses, not another selected text. The introduction
identifies 6h14 and 7h8; it reports damage involving 31:31–32 in the latter.
That report is a reason to inspect exact preservation, not to count its missing
or damaged words as agreement. A complete Greek target apparatus remains
unconsulted. No Hebrew source replacement, ImageGen restoration, manuscript
majority or canon decision follows from the new checks.
