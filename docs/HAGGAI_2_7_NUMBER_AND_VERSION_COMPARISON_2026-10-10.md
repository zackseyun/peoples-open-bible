## Hebrew grammar and the strongest objection

The local WLC/OSHB target reads `וּבָאוּ חֶמְדַּת כָּל־הַגּוֹיִם`.
`חֶמְדַּת` has morphology `HNcfsc`, a feminine singular construct noun;
`וּ/בָאוּ` has `HC/Vqp3cp`, conjunction plus Qal qatal third-person common
plural. The [publisher's BHS](https://www.die-bibel.de/bibel/BHS/HAG.2)
has the same target forms. These are edited controls on the Masoretic text,
not two independent manuscript attestations.

[Gesenius section 145b–e](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Gesenius%27_Hebrew_Grammar/145)
permits singular collectives with plural predicates, including collectives
denoting people. Its specific Haggai note instead recommends `חֲמֻדֹת`
following the Greek. That recommendation is an emendation, not the morphology
or a newly established Hebrew manuscript reading of the retained text.

[Section 146a](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Gesenius%27_Hebrew_Grammar/146)
also permits agreement with a plural genitive rather than a singular construct
head. The local parallels `קֶשֶׁת גִּבֹּרִים חַתִּים` (1 Samuel 2:4) and
`קוֹל־נְגִידִים נֶחְבָּאוּ` (Job 29:10) make a number-only exclusion too strong.
Applying that construction specifically to Haggai remains a proposal; these
parallels do not identify a messianic individual or settle this verse's syntax.

The [BDB entry as transcribed online](https://biblehub.com/bdb/2532.htm)
interprets this locus as desirable/precious things, while also recording
abstract desire and the disputed Daniel 11:37 referent. No printed facsimile
or HALOT target entry is checked in this pass. Haggai 2:8's silver and gold
give additional material-context support. Together these justify provisional
retention of "treasures," not an absolute grammar-based prohibition of every
personal reading or proof of the universally best English.
