# Isaiah 19 Egyptian cities and the blessing of nations

Isaiah 19:16–25 contains consequential alternatives, not one uniform Hebrew
and Greek text. Two published Dead Sea Scroll transcriptions attest **City of
the Sun** at19:18 against POB's **City of Destruction**. That alternative merits
a concise verse note. Retain the current Hebrew and marker-free English
provisionally: neither the earliest city name nor the history of the different
blessings at19:25 is established by this comparison.

This is a connected-unit comparison toward the wider OT program, not a complete
Greek apparatus or all-witness census. The
[evidence record](../sources/textual_restoration/comparisons/isaiah19_egypt_city.2026-10-20.v1.json)
pins the actual inputs and distinguishes manuscript transcription, selected
edition text and historical interpretation.

## Hebrew city name and actual preservation

The current [verse](../translation/ot/isaiah/019/018.yaml) follows the vendored
[WLC Isaiah](../sources/ot/wlc/Isa.xml): הַהֶרֶס, with he, gives Destruction.
The published Qumran Digital body rows instead print החרס, with heth, without
supply brackets, uncertainty dots or correction markup on that word:

| Witness | Physical location in the published transcription | Consequence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [1QIsaᵃ](https://lexicon.qumran-digital.org/transcriptions/1QIsa%5Ea%5E/2026-05-21/index.html?v=2026-05-21#c250211-i250461) | Column15, line25 | עיר החרס is an attested Hebrew sun spelling, not a Greek retroversion. |
| [4Q56 or 4QIsaᵇ](https://lexicon.qumran-digital.org/transcriptions/4Q56/2026-05-21/index.html?v=2026-05-21#c331788-i332064) | Fragments10–13, line26 | The city phrase is outside the neighboring supplied spans; the decisive heth is not reconstructed. |

These are two physical manuscript designations, not two independent ancestry
votes. Their published readings were checked in primary body cells, excluding
synoptic parallel cells. No manuscript photographs or original DJD plates were
read for this case. The museum route returned502; a textual column locator is
not a verified image crosswalk. Nothing here is newly recovered ink.

Sun is a lexical rendering of the alternative spelling. Identifying the city
specifically as Heliopolis, linking it to Onias's temple, or declaring that a
scribe deliberately attacked that temple requires additional argument. Those
claims are not part of the proposed note.

The Qumran witnesses also require word-span discrimination at the blessing:

- 1QIsaᵃ16:3 prints the complete three-part Hebrew blessing without supply or
  uncertainty marks. At15:32→16:1 its19:23 service clause lacks the second
  explicit Egypt noun found after the verb in WLC. Do not silently supply it.
- 4Q56 fragments10–13:36 preserves the Egypt/my-people expression, with the
  yod of עמי doubtful. Most of work-of-my-hands/Assyria is supplied; line37's
  inheritance/Israel expression is wholly supplied.
- [1Q8 or 1QIsaᵇ](https://lexicon.qumran-digital.org/transcriptions/1Q8/2026-05-21/index.html#c263564-i263639),
  fragment8a:7, supplies the Egypt/my-people expression and almost all work of
  my hands. The final yod of hands is doubtful; Assyria and inheritance/Israel
  are outside supply. It is not another preserved Egypt-my-people attestation.
  At8a:5, the second Egypt noun after the service verb survives.

**1QIsaᵇ is1Q8; 4QIsaᵇ is4Q56.** Their superscript letters do not make them
the same object. The physical line numbers above are not Bible verse numbers.

## Competing original arguments

Csaba Balogh's institutional [2009 dissertation](https://proteo.cj.edu.ro/sites/default/files/documents/publications/2009/1338913762.pdf),
printed288–292 and433–446, argues for retaining Destruction. He proposes
distributive each one rather than one of them, challenges the righteousness
retroversion, and questions whether Josephus's temple story establishes Sun.
His proposed mechanical developments and Qumran–Leontopolis relationship are
hypotheses, not newly observed copying events. His service discussion expressly
challenges the comitative interpretation; it must not be summarized as agreement
that the two construals are equally good.

Our assessment: his distributive examples warrant a construction-sensitive
test, not an automatic replacement of POB's one of them. An inferred temple
connection cannot establish which consonant preceded the other. Conversely,
retention of the inclusive Hebrew blessing at25 challenges a comprehensive
anti-Egyptian revision theory without excluding a selective change at18.

Knut Holter's complete [2018 article](https://doi.org/10.17159/2312-3621/2018/v31n3a14),
printed651–662, interprets the Greek blessing through a diaspora-centered plot
and discusses translation loyalty. He treats the service construal as possible
without Hebrew changes and suggests two added Hebrew bet prepositions could
explain the blessing. That is a possible reconstruction, not extant Hebrew
attestation. His shorter Greek quotation does not include Swete's γῆ.

Our assessment: the interpretive consequence is important, but a coherent plot
does not identify an earlier Hebrew exemplar or a translator's exact motive.
Greek21–22 also prevents reducing the entire passage to rejection of Egyptians.
The rival forms should remain visible rather than being combined into an
unsupported preferred theology. Neither form justifies a canon addition.
