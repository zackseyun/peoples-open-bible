# Deuteronomy 32:8: reviewed source and English integration

## Editorial result

The exact new [candidate](../sources/textual_restoration/applications/deut32_8_candidate.2026-10-15.v2.json)
has passed one independent full-record editorial review for a **provisional**
critical-source integration. The [source decision](DEUT32_8_PROVISIONAL_SOURCE_ADJUDICATION_2026-10-15.md)
selects attested בני אלוהים over retained MT/SP בני ישראל, with moderate
referent confidence, not certainty about the earliest exact form. It explicitly
retains base spelling and interpretation elsewhere, including בהנחל.

The reviewed English is:

> When the Most High gave the nations their inheritance, when he separated
> humankind, he fixed the boundaries of the peoples according to the number
> of the sons of God.

The meaningful source change is **Israel → God**. The separate English change
is **the sons of man → humankind**, with the repeated Hebrew sons construction
explained in a note. Neither change implies an explicit number of gods, a
biological relationship, or an identification/distinction between Most High
and Yahweh. Historical priority remains provisional; this is not new ink or
a discovery of a previously unknown variant.

## Actual English comparison

Before review, the [contract](../sources/textual_restoration/comparisons/deut32_8_english_contract.2026-10-15.v1.json)
froze the selected Hebrew, its retained interpretation, 32:7 and 32:9 context,
three complete English candidates, policy constraints and rubric. A new agent
received only this contract, with earlier candidate identity, previous votes
and root preference hidden. It verified the contract digest and read no other
materials. This is a masked English assessment under a stipulated source,
not a blind source-priority decision.

The [actual result](../sources/textual_restoration/comparisons/deut32_8_english_result.2026-10-15.v1.json)
preferred a minimal synthesis. “Divine beings” clarified the referent but
lost sons imagery; “sons of man” preserved the parallel but sounded unnatural
and could suggest male descendants. “Apportioned the nations” shifted the
inheritance construal toward nations as the things distributed, against the
stipulated causative interpretation. The accepted sentence retains inheritance
language linking verse 9, clear human reference and the sons-of-God wording.

The strongest objection is recorded: humankind removes the repeated sons
construction from the main text. Note b preserves it explicitly, without
claiming that a note and main-text parallel are equivalent literary effects.
The implied inheritance is made explicit in English. Reversing presentation
order did not change the assessment; this is not a second vote or measured
reader-comprehension study.
