# Proverbs 30:1: named recipients or a confession of weariness?

Provisionally retain the selected Hebrew and current POB English. The alternative
in the existing note is a serious interpretation, but the evidence examined here
does not establish it as preferable. This is a completed bounded comparison,
not an applied correction, new decipherment or finding about biblical canon.

The question concerns only the final Ithiel/Ucal clause. Agur, Jakeh, Massa,
the opening title and the identity of the son in verse 4 are outside this decision.

pins the inputs and separates grammatical possibility from source priority.

## What would change in the source interpretation?

The inspected OSHB/WLC 4.20 text has two lamed-prefixed occurrences of Ithiel,
followed by Ucal. The XML's modern proper-name tags describe an interpretation;
they are not additional manuscript evidence. The current POB English addresses
Ithiel twice and then Ucal. Its existing note discloses a repeated confession
of weariness addressed to God and a final statement of exhaustion.

| Selected unit | Alternative analysis | Operations required |
| --- | --- | --- |
| לְאִיתִיאֵל, twice | לָאִיתִי אֵל: a first-person perfect of לאה, followed by a vocative | Change vowels and word division; reassess accents and the prefix analysis. |
| וְאֻכָל | וָאֵכֶל: a proposed shortened first-person consecutive imperfect of כלה | Repoint and reanalyse; reassess accents. |
| וְאֻכָל | First-person ability verb from יכל | Reanalyse the word; defective spelling and pausal vocalization can accommodate a verbal reading. |

These proposals can preserve the consonantal sequence
**לאיתיאל לאיתיאל ואכל**. That does not make them the same selected Hebrew
interpretation. The weariness proposal changes both word divisions and vowels.
The shortened כלה proposal must not silently become an unshortened
וְאֶכְלֶה with an additional final ה. Nor is an ordinary passive of אכל
interchangeable with the shortened כלה derivation.

[GKC §75](https://bohuslav.me/gesenius/gesenius/75.html), paragraphs k, o–q and t,
describes apocope in consecutive imperfects, resulting vocalization and
first-person shortened/full comparisons. These rules provide a morphological
route for testing the shortened proposal without adding a consonant. They do
not attest that reading in a manuscript of Proverbs or decide its intention
here. The ability reading likewise supplies no explicit negative: an English
inability claim or an added object would need further justification.

## Strongest cases on both sides

Nehemiah 11:7 actually places the same pointed name inside a genealogy. This
establishes that Ithiel can be a personal name, not that Proverbs names the same
individual. The repeated recipient phrase can resume and expand the address
to include Ucal. Neither recipient is explicitly called a son or disciple.
This is a positive syntactic case for names, not merely reliance on the XML tags.

The verbal reading has a genuine contextual advantage: repeated weariness can
lead naturally into verse 2's explanation of limited human understanding and
verse 4's questions about knowledge beyond human capacity. Direct address to
God occurs later in verses 7–9. Nevertheless, verse 2 can also explain the
speaker's discourse after a superscription. Its introductory כִּי does not
force an assertion of weariness in the preceding clause. Repetition is awkward
enough to investigate, not enough by itself to prove textual corruption.

[McKenzie and Shelton's 2020 article](https://www.sebts.edu/wp-content/uploads/2023/06/McKenzie.pdf),
printed page 11 and notes 23–26, recognizes the name/verbal alternatives and
explicitly identifies changes to vowels and spacing. It argues from prophetic
utterance parallels toward an exhausted-speaker interpretation. That is a
literary argument, not attestation of reconstructed vowels. Its embedded
modern English quotation was encountered during consultation; no new English
draft was prepared from it. References to Fox and Krantz are citations in the
article, not works independently consulted here. The article's broader
Davidic/messianic thesis does not determine this local source decision.

## What the Greek does and does not support

Root inspected the actual CATSS/Rahlfs-derived digital target and adjacent
lines corresponding by content to Proverbs 30:1–9. The final clause addresses
people trusting God and then has a first-person statement of ceasing. It is
not a literal equivalent of repeated weariness addressed to God. It offers
evidence of a different ancient interpretation, without proving that the
proposed Hebrew redivision is its exact Vorlage.

One digital token labels the trusting form as a finite verb, although its
article and dative plural construction support participial syntax. Automated
morphology therefore cannot substitute for reading the construction. Neither
that tag nor a secondary characterization of the Greek is a witness vote.

This pinned digital control is edition-derived, not a fresh Greek manuscript
collation, verification against a printed critical apparatus or proof of Greek
unanimity. Its token stream does not establish punctuation. Full restricted
Greek data remain private; the Git record stores bounded observations and
provenance. WLC, its XML annotations and POB's YAML are not three independent
Hebrew witnesses.

The already acquired pinned QDR snapshot returned no indexed word hit for
Prov 30:1, 30:2 or 30:3. That limited query is not a comprehensive absence claim
and supplies no target Hebrew ink. The publisher's Latin route exposed no
usable body; no Latin reading or apparatus result is claimed. Hebrew target
images, Fox's critical apparatus and later Greek recensions were not consulted.

## Decision, next discriminator and efficiency

Retain the names provisionally and retain the existing alternate note without
rewriting it in this task. The compared sources do not compel a new selected
Hebrew analysis. Names have actual pointing/segmentation support and a real
name parallel; the verbal proposals have grammatical and contextual plausibility
but remain editorial interpretations in this investigation. Conservative
retention is not proof that the Masoretic interpretation is earliest.

Reopen for a precise critical-apparatus argument or independent version evidence
that distinguishes the Hebrew analyses and explains the Greek divergence.
Any verbal promotion must identify the final verb explicitly, justify syntax
and accentuation, then undergo a separate full-record English comparison and
application check. More agreement among models is not the missing evidence.

The adjacent verse 3's knowledge/negation question is a separate possible lead,
not a decision made here. No correction is inferred just from a difference
between Greek and POB. The Hebrew and Greek contexts were consulted for this
clause, not comprehensively adjudicated verse by verse.

The existing clear-image calibration still has a disputed reference label and
does not establish damaged-ink character accuracy. Its agreed writing-region
classifications must not be presented as a successful reading benchmark.
Following the existing pause, no repeat image exercise or model-generated
manuscript evidence was used for this comparison.
