# Exodus 18 and the longer appointment narrative

The compared Samaritan form explicitly includes consultation with the people,
qualified tribal leaders, and instructions for impartial judgment in Exodus's
appointment narrative. Parts of that longer sequence survive in the Hebrew
manuscript 4Q22. This is a consequential ancient literary alternative, not a
new decipherment or proof that the longer form is earlier. POB provisionally
retains its WLC source and main English wording, adds one connected draft
variant note at 18:24, and repairs three existing note markers across 18:24–25.

Moses's first-person recollection becomes third-person Exodus narration;
retrospective timing formulas disappear and tribal/judicial possessives change.
Some clauses match the Samaritan Deuteronomy wording but differ orthographically
from WLC. Exodus 18:24's added incapacity statement includes an explicit I;
its how form also differs. Crucially, the longer form does not retain WLC
18:25's distinct selection of capable men from all Israel: the adapted tribal
selection occurs at the end of 18:24 and the appointment continues in 18:25.
The combined 432-consonant length difference is therefore not a pure verbatim
insertion into unchanged WLC wording. Neighboring 18:26 also has large case
where WLC has difficult case; that neighboring verse is not changed here.

## Surviving Hebrew and supplied wording

Root and the source assessor read the manuscript-owned rows of the dated
[Qumran-Digital 4Q22 transcription](https://lexicon.qumran-digital.org/transcriptions/4Q22/2026-05-21/index.html#c289509-i289558),
column 19 lines 1–17 and 21–24, excluding embedded comparison rows. Line 6
contains damaged portions of the common conclusion; lines 7–17 positively
preserve parts of the longer narrative. Lines 7–9 have portions of the burden,
multiplication, stars and blessing material; 10–12 have fragmentary portions
of the burden, selection and response. This is not merely a manuscript-family
attribution: wording outside supply brackets establishes partial attestation.

Important limits remain. The entire selection line 13, including the beginning
of the appointment clause, is supplied; its continuation partly survives in
lines 14–15. Line 16
preserves parts of its surrounding instruction but supplies judges, hear and
judge righteously. Rows 18–20 are absent; they cannot certify the full concluding
impartiality instructions. The exact contemporary Samaritan form is not
preserved word for word. Uncertain letters, supplied brackets and missing
coverage remain distinct. No custodial pixels or direct DJD apparatus were
newly collated, so this is published-transcription evidence, not fresh ink.

The complete [4Q11 fragment 20](https://lexicon.qumran-digital.org/transcriptions/4Q11/2026-05-21/index.html#c281006-i281140)
ends with damaged 18:24 wording in line 13 and unidentified traces in line 14.
The next fragment resumes at 19:24. This does not attest either omission or
presence of the longer sequence. QDR's embedded Deuteronomy manuscript
parallels likewise do not attest placement in Exodus.

## Published explanation and strongest countercase

Anderson and Giles, *The Samaritan Pentateuch* (2012), printed page 62
(PDF 72), table row 13, links Exodus 18:25 with Deuteronomy 1:9b–18 and 4Q22.
Root checked the complete table context, printed pages 61–63, and the full
printed page 68 (PDF 78), including native page images. The latter explains
the additions as making Moses's retrospective Deuteronomy account explicitly
occur in Exodus and Numbers. The table's reference label is not identical to
the two-node boundary in the pinned control. Its broad attribution cannot
certify every ancient letter. [Publisher PDF](https://www.sbl-site.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/11/SamaritanPentateuch_SBL_Anderson-Giles.pdf).

Longacre's 2015 thesis, printed pages 180–184 (PDF 195–199), also explains
18:25's longer speech as corresponding to the later Deuteronomy report.
Its table 4.15 gives Deuteronomy 1:9–15, a narrower range than Anderson–Giles
and the complete control alignment. Preserve this difference; the table is
not a reason to discard the following mapped instructions. Root and the source
assessor read the full pages, with native images of pages 182 and 184 checked.
[Institutional thesis](https://etheses.bham.ac.uk/id/eprint/5780/).

Brooke and McLean (1909), complete printed pages 215–216 (PDF 73–74), have
the shorter Greek main narrative. The bottom apparatus under 18:24 explicitly
reports the Samaritan extension in Latin under ed. Sam.; it is not a Greek
manuscript addition. The Exodus prefatory note, printed page v (PDF 9), also
qualifies the edition's selection of readings and corrections. This is an
edition-level comparison, not fresh Vaticanus imagery or complete Greek
collation. [Edition PDF](https://tmcdaniel.palmerseminary.edu/Brooke%26McLean/LXX_Brooke%26McLean_1-2.pdf).

The working explanation is harmonizing expansion: the distinctive sequence
follows Deuteronomy in order while adapting its retrospective language to
Exodus's narrative. It explains both the extra details and recast appointment
summary. The strongest countercase is an inherited fuller Exodus subsequently
abridged in the shorter branch. Ancient attestation makes that possibility
serious; parallels alone cannot establish direction. No checked transition
proves such shortening, and a simple accidental omission would not account
for the recast summary as well. Deliberate abridgment remains possible.
Neither shorter length, specimen age nor a majority of editions settles this.
Retain WLC provisionally rather than construct an unattested hybrid.

## Reader contribution and stop conditions

The full candidates and baseline hashes are linked in the receipt. One note
summarizes the connected longer form and qualifies partial 4Q22 preservation.
The existing obedience marker moves to the father-in-law clause; capable men
and heads markers move from thousands and fifties to their actual referents.
Existing note bodies, lexical decisions and original drafting provenance are
unchanged. Prior review objects are archived verbatim, not transferred as
approval; both records are draft and need review.

The complete Exodus before/after export contains 40 chapters and 1,213 verses;
only 18:24 and 18:25 change. Source and marker-free English remain identical.
No blind rendering evaluation or general translation-superiority claim is
made. Commanders versus chiefs is not decided here merely for consistency:
that separate rendering question needs its own contextual evaluation.

This contributes a checked, meaningful alternative now. It does not establish
a new original source reading, publication approval or deployed-reader release.
It also does not establish a rule for adding books to a canon. Additional works
need their own surviving-wording and reception studies, with a named community
and authority criteria for any canonical recommendation.

Reopen historical priority only for direct DJD IX apparatus/plate comparison
of 4Q22 column 19 with a verified custodial image crosswalk, or another securely
preserved witness that distinguishes expansion from shortening. Stop this
case at provisional retention and disclosure; do not repeat model agreement
reviews or re-download the same controls as a substitute for that evidence.
