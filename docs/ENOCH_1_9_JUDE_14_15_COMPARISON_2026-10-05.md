# Enoch 1 9 and Jude 14 15 source comparison

The surviving evidence makes Enoch a worthwhile comparison text and supports
a close relationship between its judgment oracle and Jude's quotation.
It does not establish word-for-word identity, an intact Aramaic source verse,
historical authorship by the patriarch or universal canonical authority.
This comparison corrects POB's source-routing documentation and identifies
Jude 15's conviction object as a consequential next adjudication. No canonical
source, English, note or review metadata is changed here.

## Actual Aramaic coverage

The [Qumran-Digital transcription dated November 11, 2025](https://lexicon.qumran-digital.org/transcriptions/4Q204/2025-11-11/index.html)
locates the unit at 4Q204, fragment 1, column i, lines 15–17. Root inspected
the target and adjacent published lines; the bounded assessor inspected the
complete surviving published column, lines 15–30. Its
[notation guide](https://lexicon.qumran-digital.org/faq/v1/en/index.html)
distinguishes supplied text in square brackets from uncertain character remains
marked with circelli. These are published readings, not our own pixel readings.

| Line | Text outside reconstruction brackets | What it can establish |
|---|---|---|
| 15 | `את קדישו` | Part of the myriads expression and part of holy ones. Coming, the full number expression and the possessive ending are supplied. |
| 16 | `שרא על עו֯בד` | Parts of flesh and work/deed with a preposition; one letter uncertain. Conviction, quantifiers and the full wickedness clause are supplied. |
| 17 | `ר֯ב֯רבן וקשין` | Great/proud and hard adjectives, with two uncertain letters in the first; the speech and against-him clauses are supplied. |

This limited survival supports continuity with the oracle but cannot adjudicate
every verb, pronoun, quantifier or clause. In particular, partial flesh is not
a complete attestation of all flesh or a manuscript of Jude's Greek. Following
lines continue into Enoch's observational exhortations; editorially supplied
words at the end of line 17 already introduce the next section.
The assessor corroborated the locator in browser OCR of Milik and Black's
1976 edition, pages 184–186, not native page images; that OCR is not the basis
for new letter claims. No exhaustive exclusion of other fragments is claimed.

## Greek and Geʿez editions

The reacquired Flemming–Radermacher 1901 and Charles 1906 PDFs match the
repository manifest hashes. Root inspected complete native Flemming pages
34–35, printed 20–21, including apparatus and the facing German text. Page 34
contains the complete Greek 1:9, including the final sinners/ungodly words.
The local OCR file `flemming_1901_greek_p_p0034.txt` instead stops at
`κατ' αὐ`. That clipping is a transcription defect, not an ancient omission.
The raw OCR is preserved; this report does not repair the extraction pipeline.

Root also inspected complete Flemming native pages 15–16, 27–28 and 30,
printed 1–2, 13–14 and 16. The introduction identifies Akhmim material through
32:6, Syncellus quotations and a Vatican fragment, and discusses editorial
use of Ethiopic. The sigla distinguish the papyrus from editions and the
Ethiopic translation. Thus this is an edited Greek control, not a complete
Greek book or a freshly collated papyrus. The former ENOCH.md attribution of
Chester Beatty chapters 97–107 to the 1901 edition is removed; that requires
a separately verified edition of the later Chester Beatty–Michigan material.
Bouriant's corresponding Chester Beatty attribution is also removed. Its scan
was not separately reacquired or collated in this pass.

Charles native PDF page 42, printed 4, contains the complete Geʿez 1:9 and
apparatus. Root visually inspected the whole page and compared the current
source record and chapter OCR. Apparatus notes 19–25 explicitly compare Greek,
Jude and other quotations, identify defective wording and propose emendation
or restoration. The source's asterisks, daggers and square brackets are editorial
signals, not ancient letters or speech punctuation. No global OCR-fidelity
approval follows from this one page; no manuscript-by-manuscript collation
or Geʿez lexical retranslation was performed.

These dependencies matter: a Geʿez edition informed by Jude is not independent
confirmation of every Jude clause. Nor are Charles, Dillmann and a digital
control three independent ancient manuscripts. Compare actual manuscript
readings and apparatus-supported choices before promoting a reconstructed text.

## The oracle and Jude differ

Root read the complete current Enoch 1:9 and Jude 14–15 records, their source
and notes, the local SBLGNT apparatus and the official
[NA28 Jude chapter](https://www.die-bibel.de/bibel/NA28/JUD.1).
The following glosses describe the selected editions; they are not approved
replacement translations or assertions of manuscript unanimity.

| Feature | Inspected Greek Enoch 1:9 | Current SBLGNT Jude 14–15 |
|---|---|---|
| Coming | `ἔρχεται`, comes; no explicit Lord in this clause | `ἦλθεν κύριος`, the Lord came |
| Holy host | Myriads and his holy ones | His holy myriads |
| Punitive clause | `ἀπολέσει πάντας τοὺς ἀσεβεῖς`, will destroy all the ungodly | No separate destroy-the-ungodly clause in these verses |
| Conviction object | `ἐλέγξει πᾶσαν σάρκα`, will convict all flesh | `ἐλέγξαι πάντας τοὺς ἀσεβεῖς`, to convict all the ungodly |

Current POB's Geʿez-based English retains destroying the wicked and rebuking
all flesh; Jude's English should not be used to overwrite those differences.
Neither the coming tense contrast nor the holy-host phrasing alone identifies
the unique textual form available to Jude. Jude expressly attributes prophecy
to Enoch, and the sustained judgment/deeds/speech correspondence favors reuse
of this oracle. A shared circulating form rather than direct copying of one
surviving manuscript remains possible. No transmission direction is proved
by comparing only modern editions of later witnesses.

There is also an internal Jude variant: the inspected NA28 print chooses
`πᾶσαν ψυχὴν`, every soul, where SBLGNT/POB has all the ungodly. The pinned
local SBL apparatus already reports this edition difference and the RP addition
of their. This is not a new discovery. It changes the stated scope of conviction,
so it deserves a passage-specific Greek manuscript/hand and ECM comparison.
Neither Enoch's all flesh nor a count of edition supporters resolves Jude's
earliest wording; dependence or harmonization must be tested in both directions.
Retain current Jude source/English provisionally pending that comparison.

The current reader cross-reference remains useful but does not show the actual
differences. Misplaced note anchors and stale lexical metadata observed in the
three records are application debt, not silently repaired or newly approved
by this documentation-only pass.

## Jude 15 manuscript follow up

The bounded follow-up moves beyond edition votes. The
[institutional Sinaiticus page](https://www.codexsinaiticus.org/en/manuscript.aspx?book=58&chapter=1&verse=15)
locates Jude on British Library folio 325, quire 90, folio 1 recto.
Root retrieved its
[published transcription](https://www.codexsinaiticus.org/handler/transcription.ashx?q=90&f=1&s=r&type=1)
and checked Jude 15 words 6–8: `ελεγξαι παϲαν ψυχην`.
The two object words are plain spans, with no correction tooltip or supply
marker there. This establishes the project's reading of Sinaiticus, not our
own photograph collation, exhaustive hand analysis or independent verification
of P72 and Vaticanus. The response was 30,678 bytes, SHA-256
`6aabc4f234688b1a0e5dd637ec4bd9dba7f5920c1d799defefb46566c125c999`.

Timo Flink's original
[2007 discussion, page 117](https://www.bsw.org/filologia-neotestamentaria/vol-20-2007/reconsidering-the-text-of-jude-5-13-15-and-18/224/article-p117.html),
with [distribution on page 116](https://www.bsw.org/filologia-neotestamentaria/vol-20-2007/reconsidering-the-text-of-jude-5-13-15-and-18/224/article-p116.html)
and [conclusion on page 118](https://www.bsw.org/filologia-neotestamentaria/vol-20-2007/reconsidering-the-text-of-jude-5-13-15-and-18/224/article-p118.html),
tests both directions: harmonization toward Enoch or Jude 4 could produce
all the ungodly; influence from Romans 2:9 or avoidance of repeated ungodly
vocabulary could produce every soul. He ultimately favors all the ungodly,
also arguing from distribution and Jude's triadic style. These are competing
historical explanations, not additional manuscript witnesses. A read-only
agent located Gerd Mink's response in the SBL-hosted Wachtel–Holmes volume;
root has not independently inspected those pages or acquired the official
passage-specific ECM data, so that lead is not a completed adjudication here.

The difference changes the explicit conviction object; the verse already
speaks of judgment against all. Neither variant alone proves a new doctrine
of universal salvation or a broader canon. Retain POB provisionally until
the Greek attestations and transmission arguments are assessed together.
The follow-up adds actual attestation and a counterargument, not new ancient
letters or a completed source/English application.

## Canon and comparison policy

Jude gives this prophecy positive authority in its argument. That is more
specific than merely finding an old fragment, but it does not name or ratify
all literary sections of today's 108-chapter collection. Source survival,
literary dependence, reception and a community's canon decision are separate
questions. Enoch is already named in the
[Ethiopian Orthodox published canonical list](https://www.ethiopianorthodox.org/english/canonical/books.html?lang=en).
The comparison therefore supports a clearly labelled POB comparative edition
and links from Jude, not a newly discovered universal canon requirement.
