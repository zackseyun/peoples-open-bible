# Jeremiah release years and the connected debt laws

Jeremiah34:14's Hebrew seven-year opening and Greek six-year opening have
different intertextual effects, but neither alone establishes an earlier
Hebrew reading. The Hebrew verse itself specifies six years of service;
the selected Greek still presents a communal emancipation and condemns its
reversal. Retain POB's seven/six wording provisionally. No Hebrew, English,
note, approval, reader-layout or canon change is applied.

## The complete original article and its limits

[Terblanche2016](https://www.scielo.org.za/pdf/at/v36n2/07.pdf),
*Acta Theologica*36(2):148–161, argues that the seven-year formula links
Deuteronomy15:1's debt release with15:12's service-release law. He also
interprets the plural-to-singular shift as incorporation of the latter text.
His p.150 n.3 treats Greek six as a probable translator change, expressly
citing Lundbom2004:563. The article supplies a literary explanation, not a
local manuscript collation demonstrating that translation-stage cause.

Terblanche considers Leuchter's alternative link to public Torah reading in
Deuteronomy31:9–11 before preferring15:1+12. These cited originals, including
Allen2008:387, were not newly read here. His broader distributive-justice
interpretation is not itself a recovered law or textual variant. He expressly
acknowledges that the institution of debt bondage is not abolished (p.154).

## Historical judgment and English consequence

One plausible history retains Hebrew seven and explains Greek six as
harmonization or clarification toward the service period. Another starts with
a six-year form and regards seven as a later incorporation of a debt-release
or public-law formula. These are competing explanations, not observed editing
events. Shared legal language and internal coherence cannot by themselves
distinguish them. Nor do these texts supply individual service-start dates or
independently establish a sabbatical date for Zedekiah's action.
