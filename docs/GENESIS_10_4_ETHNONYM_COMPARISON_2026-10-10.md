The noncommercial corpus remains private; neither its full target verse nor corpus is redistributed or relicensed as POB data. Nearby Genesis10:3 has Riphath where Chronicles1:6 has Diphath. This demonstrates another divergence between these lists, not a rule deciding R/D direction. ## Competing explanations and exact proposal

R has directly consulted Hebrew support at the Samaritan Genesis locus and in the Chronicles parallel, plus a coherent Greek geographic interpretation. A change from R to D can explain the written Genesis contrast through similar-letter confusion. But the reverse direction is also possible, and neither copying event is observed. Harmonization to Chronicles or geographic normalization could produce R from an unfamiliar earlier D-name.

D’s strongest case is its actual Genesis base and Latin preservation, with an unfamiliar name potentially attracting later normalization. Its strongest objection is the need to explain the different R-controls without simply dismissing them. R’s strongest objection is that contextual coherence and agreement do not prove independence or priority. Removing chronological preference changes no conclusion because no discriminating early direct-Hebrew target was acquired.

The tentative R-proposal does not translate the name as “Rhodians,” declare Rhodes the referent, or claim new vocalization. It normalizes the retained WLC base, replaces only the final consonantal name, archives stale assessments without preserving their approval, retains original generation metadata, and moves the note from Elishah to the affected name. The [companion Chronicles proposal](../sources/textual_restoration/candidates/chronicles1_7_ethnonym_note.2026-10-10.v1.json) leaves its Hebrew and marker-free English unchanged and explicitly identifies the *Masoretic* Genesis contrast, so it would remain accurate if POB later selected R.

One independent exact-source/full-record critique may support provisional adoption or hold it. Neither result is a vote proving the original text. Source-stage integration, schema/history/export checks and explicit consumer binding remain separate from this comparison; a research proposal cannot silently become the canonical Hebrew corpus. No new decipherment, exhaustive apparatus coverage, optimal-corpus verdict or canon recommendation follows.
