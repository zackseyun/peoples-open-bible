SECTION 1

Peter to James, the lord and bishop of the holy church.

May he always be at peace from the Father of all through Jesus Christ. Knowing, my brother, that you eagerly hasten toward what benefits all of us together, I ask and implore you not to share with anyone from the nations the books of my preachings that I sent you, nor with one of our own people before a test. But if someone, after being tested, is found worthy, then make the handover to him according to the procedure by which Moses also made the handover to the seventy who had received his seat. Because of this, the fruit of that safeguarding also remains evident to the present. For their fellow nationals everywhere preserve the same standard of sole sovereignty and communal order, since the writings that point in many directions have in no way been able to lead them off into thinking otherwise. For, according to the standard handed down to them, they try to bring into order what is discordant in the writings, if indeed someone who does not know the traditions happens to grow numb before the many-meaning voices of the prophets. For this reason they permit no one to teach unless that person first learns how the writings must be used. Because of this, among them there is one God, one law, one hope.

SECTION 2

To ensure, then, that the same thing happens among us, give the books of my preachings to our seventy brothers together with the corresponding mystery of the procedure, so that, as for equipping also those who wish to take on a share of the teaching, they may equip them. For if it does not happen this way, our message of truth will be divided into many opinions. I know this not because I am a prophet, but because I already see the beginning of this evil itself. For some of those from the nations rejected the law-conforming proclamation delivered through me and accepted a certain lawless and frivolous teaching of the enemy man. And this while I am still alive: some have attempted, through certain varied interpretations, to reshape my words into the abolition of the law, as though I myself did think in this way but did not proclaim it openly. May that be far away! For such a thing is to oppose the law of God, which was spoken through Moses and attested by our Lord concerning its everlasting continuance. For he said as follows: “Heaven and earth will pass away; one iota or one small stroke will certainly not pass away from the law.” And he said this: “Before all things happen.” But they—I do not know how—while claiming to represent my mind, attempt to interpret the words they heard from me more wisely than I who spoke them, telling those whom they instruct that this is my view—a view I did not even entertain. If they dare to tell such lies about me while I am still alive, how much more, after my time, will those who come after me dare to do this?

SECTION 3

So that no such thing may happen, it was for this reason that I asked and implored you not to share with anyone the books of my preachings that I sent you, neither with one of our own people nor with one of another people before a test. But if someone, after being tested, is found worthy, then make the handover to him according to Moses’s procedure, by which he made the handover to the seventy who had received his seat. Thus they may guard the pledges and everywhere transmit the standard of truth, interpreting everything in accordance with our tradition; and they may not, while themselves being dragged down through ignorance and drawn into error by speculations within the soul, bring others into the same kind of pit of destruction. And I have set out well for you what seemed good to me; but carry out what seems good to you, my lord, as is fitting. Farewell.

Translator's notes for section 3 (not part of the ancient text):

1. Greek: τὰς πίστεις φυλάξωσιν
The plural πίστεις may mean pledges/assurances of fidelity or beliefs/points of faith. “Pledges” is a provisional contextual choice, not established by the plural alone.

2. Greek: τὰ μὲν ἐμοὶ δόξαντα καλῶς ἐσήμανά σοι
The English takes καλῶς with the act of setting out (“well”). Alternatively it may qualify what seemed good to Peter; “I have indicated to you what seemed good to me” represents that attachment.
