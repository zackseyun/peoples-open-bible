# Exodus 4:24–26: encounter, circumcision and release

2026-10-10. A bounded source comparison, not a new manuscript discovery or
reconstruction of the autograph. Canonical application and delivery are recorded
below only when actually verified.

## Result and significance

The provisional decision keeps the declared Hebrew literary form: Yahweh meets
an unnamed male, Zipporah causes contact at his feet and speaks of a bridegroom
of blood, and the threat ceases. This is not a claim that every pronoun refers to
Moses or that the earliest literary form has been established.

There is discriminating evidence, not merely model preference: the published
4Q1 transcription preserves a masculine-form ending in 4:26, against the
consulted Samaritan feminine ending. The Greek version instead has angelic
agency and different action and speech across the episode. These differences
matter to interpretation and deserve visible notes even where the selected
Hebrew remains unchanged.

The proposed main-text change is modest: **At that time she said** in 4:26,
rather than **Then she said**. Both are possible English; the former avoids
unnecessarily suggesting another utterance after release. It does not prove
that the clause is retrospective or causal. The more definite **That is why**
is not selected. Circumcision remains singular in English, with its actual
plural Hebrew form and uncertain force disclosed.

## Reasoning, controls and remaining uncertainty

The contract is frozen after acquisition but before recorded assessment. Root
tests the existing temporal rendering first; one identity-withheld independent
comparator follows the reversed order, without reading canonical English, root
assessment or application history. Both prefer the same provisional source
choices and the modest temporal clarification. This agreement is a review
control, not evidence about ancient wording. Complete knowledge blinding is
not claimed. No preference vote is repeated until agreement.

The strongest countercases remain live. Greek and Onkelos may reflect another
Hebrew form, and their independence is not established by matching angelic
agency. Connected interpretive changes make recasting plausible but do not
prove it. The feminine Samaritan object coherently brings Zipporah into focus.
Early masculine attestation gives a local preference, not a guarantee of
originality. The comparator's preference weakens but does not reverse if modest
chronological weight is removed. No ancient copying date is invented from a
modern publication date.

The WLC/OSHB tag for *mimmennu* is first-person plural. The pointed form can
have that parsing; third-person narration supports the customary from-him
interpretation here. The annotation is flagged rather than silently declared
infallible. The masculine-form ending cannot identify Moses, and the male
referents need not all be identical.

The Hebrew contact action contains no explicit object corresponding to English
**it**. Supplying the cut foreskin is a contextual inference; a note must say
so. Human or divine feet possessors, the possible euphemism, the addressee and
exact ritual/emotional meaning remain open. Importing only the Greek angel
into an otherwise Hebrew narrative must not masquerade as the complete Greek
form or an attested reconstructed Hebrew source.

## Application and efficiency

The baseline has misanchored notes, a *pgʿ* rationale where the verb is *pgš*,
an inadequate Hiphil-contact explanation and a rationale definitively naming
Moses despite the translation's unresolved pronoun. These are repairable
quality problems, independent of recovering ancient wording.

The full candidates propose ten anchored notes across three verses, correct
connected rationale and change only the temporal phrase in marker-free English.
All exact source and initial generation fields are preserved. Seven old-field
archives per verse retain the previous record without transferring its 100%
historical agreement to this candidate. Active status resets to draft and
needs_review. Each baseline has no revisions; one explicit new revision is
added. No claim of fresh HALOT consultation replaces the historical labels.

The actual Exodus overlay export contains 40 chapters and 1,213 units. Only
4:24–26 change, with all ten notes exported; 1,210 other verse files retain
their byte manifest. The
[application receipt](../sources/textual_restoration/applications/exodus4_24_26_encounter.2026-10-10.v1.json)
binds exact candidate, serialized YAML, assessment, schema and export digests.
Separate exact-record review and canonical application are not inferred from
the comparison or preflight.

Acquisition is bounded. CAL's no-scraping notice ends that route; its failed
Peshitta link is not bypassed. One local PDF download returns 403; actual
relevant PDF text is read through web, with no verified local PDF or fresh
pixel inspection claimed. Screenshot output exposes no inspectable pixels;
an oversized metadata dump is accidental serialization noise, not evidence.
An initial multi-file patch has a blank separator error and is rejected before
writing; it is corrected without changing the source decision. Original Latin
and Syriac remain unacquired. No full licensed corpus is vendored. Private
SP/QDR controls are CC BY-NC 4.0; Qumran-Digital attribution/CC BY-SA 4.0,
WLC public-domain and OSHB CC BY 4.0 boundaries remain explicit in the assessment.

Stop condition: one comparison and one exact application review, not repeated
preference votes. Reopening requires discriminating source or linguistic
evidence. This contributes a calibrated translation clarification, substantive
variant disclosure and record repairs—not a newly discovered Hebrew reading,
comprehensive OT/NT coverage, canon expansion or authorization to publish an
About-page claim. Manuscript attestation of another work is a separate question
from a community's decision to recognize it as canonical.
