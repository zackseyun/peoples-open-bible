## Why select this reading provisionally?

Read 32:7–9 as a connected argument: remembrance of ancient generations,
allocation of peoples, then Jacob as Yahweh's portion. The attested divine
referent makes the general allocation and particular portion intelligible
together without importing an unstated Israelite headcount. The Greek divine
interpretation is compatible with that reading. This cumulative case is more
persuasive here than treating the single Hebrew excerpt as an isolated
innovation, though it does not prove that the Greek and 4Q37 are independent.

The strongest countercase is substantial. Israel has direct Hebrew support
in both Masoretic and Samaritan transmission and a coherent retrospective
interpretation: the arrangement of nations corresponds to Israel's number
and special place. The divine wording could have arisen by interpretation or
adaptation. An excerpt collection makes that possibility worth testing, but
genre alone does not demonstrate alteration of this particular clause. The
present record supplies no passage-specific adaptation mechanism that clearly
outweighs inheritance. Conversely, it does not establish that a scribe changed
God to Israel to suppress a theology. Either direction requires inference.

The unattested exact Deuteronomy proposal בני אל offers a possible common
ancestor, and the reported hymn allusion merits scrutiny. It is not preferred
over an intelligible, directly attested divine reading merely because it can
generate a parsimonious story about the other forms. This is not a blanket ban
on conjecture; this case does not yet need it.

Sensitivity checks: remove the modest chronological preference and the same
provisional choice remains, from direct Hebrew attestation plus context and
qualified versional support. Remove the Armenian and later Latin evidence and
the decision also remains. Remove the Greek divine interpretation and the
case becomes weaker: a single Hebrew excerpt against MT/SP does not by itself
establish priority. Remove all contextual discrimination and attestation alone
does not decide the direction of change. These are qualitative checks, not
independent votes or calculated probabilities.

No decision is made here about whether Most High and Yahweh designate distinct
beings in an earlier theology. The English must not insert that conclusion,
an explicit number of gods, or an allocation to each god absent from the words.

phrase follows the base; the changed phrase is understood as “sons of God.”
Keep its attested spelling rather than silently converting אלוהים to אלהים.
