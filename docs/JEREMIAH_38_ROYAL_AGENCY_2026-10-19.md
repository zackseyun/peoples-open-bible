# Jeremiah royal responsibility and the rescue of the prophet

Jeremiah 38:9 / Greek 45:9 changes who is accused, not merely the style of
the accusation. Swete's Greek body addresses the king directly; POB follows
the Hebrew accusation against the officials. This is a consequential textual
difference, but its literary effect does not by itself establish an earlier
Hebrew source. Retain POB provisionally; no source, English, note, approval,
reader-layout or canon change is applied.

## The original modern arguments

[Tucker2020](https://helda.helsinki.fi/server/api/core/bitstreams/1b192bd6-23e1-43e6-b73e-0a3d6ebd32ce/content),
printed pp. 366–367 / PDF pp. 380–381, explicitly argues that the Greek
accusation and killing infinitive reflect an earlier Hebrew source, subsequently
recast in MT. His argument connects changes in grammatical forms with a
different portrayal of Zedekiah. These complete pages and notes were read and
viewed; surrounding §7.4 was read as extracted text, not a whole-thesis audit.
This supplies a local argument beyond his previously consulted conclusions.

[Birdsong's complete June2017 author essay](https://bibleinterp.arizona.edu/articles/2017/06/bir418009)
interprets the Greek king more harshly than the Hebrew king. She treats his
knowledge of the cistern, despite its absence from Ebed-melech's Greek speech,
as an ironic disclosure of complicity. That is a literary inference, not an
explicit narrator statement about how he learned the location. Her essay also
acknowledges the Greek inability clause, while interpreting the surrounding
story against exoneration. The monograph's pp. 216–228, cited by Tucker, were
not acquired or read; this essay does not replace their historical derivation.

## Connected Hebrew and Greek controls

The current source, main English and footnotes of eighteen POB records were
read: 37:17–21 and 38:1–13. SweteIII printed pp. 330–332 / PDF pp. 354–356
were viewed completely, with the relevant apparatus. The following observations
refer to that body, not every Greek witness or an independently recovered
Old Greek archetype.

| Unit in Hebrew numbering | POB and WLC | Swete Greek body and apparatus |
|---|---|---|
| 37:18 / Greek44:18 | The final imprisonment verb נתתם is plural; English “you” does not expose the number. | σὺ δέδωκας is singular. Qmg reports a plural alternative. |
| 37:20–21 / Greek44:20–21 | A plea not to return to Jonathan's house; commitment to the guard court and a daily bread ration. | The plea remains. The first placement is a prison house, but bread is still ordered and the verse ends with Jeremiah in the guard court. |
| 38:5 / Greek45:5 | The king says he cannot act against the officials. | After handing Jeremiah over, the Greek narrates the king's inability against them. The change in speaker framing does not remove the inability. |
| 38:9 / Greek45:9 | These men acted wickedly; they cast Jeremiah into the cistern, where he will die. | ἐπονηρεύσω and ἐποίησας accuse a singular addressee; τοῦ ἀποκτεῖναι explicitly introduces killing. Qmg reports a longer, third-person accusation. |
| 38:10–13 / Greek45:10–13 | The king orders rescue, followed by its execution and guard-court confinement. | The king also orders rescue from the cistern so that Jeremiah does not die; the extraction and guard-court endpoint remain. |

The charge in 38:9 therefore changes meaning substantially. Conversely, a
summary that the Greek king simply refuses all aid would erase retained
counterevidence. The rescue's motive can be read critically, as Birdsong does,
without turning that interpretation into an additional textual variant. Likewise,
the infinitive's killing language is explicit; an account of the king's private
intention remains interpretation rather than an independently narrated event.

## Historical judgment and English consequence

Tucker's earlier-Hebrew explanation is plausible. A Greek-stage recasting of
the accusation is also a viable countermodel: the preceding surrender of the
prophet to the officials supplies a contextual reason to direct blame at the
king. That possibility is not proof of Greek authorship. Nor does deleting
Hebrew words alone explain all the changed persons and constructions.

The selected evidence establishes distinct rhetoric, not transmission direction
or exact lost Hebrew wording. Do not retrovert the accusation into the Hebrew
corpus or silently substitute it in POB. A future Greek-form presentation must
preserve the connected rescue controls rather than splice one accusation into
the Hebrew narrative. This also does not propose an addition to the canon.
