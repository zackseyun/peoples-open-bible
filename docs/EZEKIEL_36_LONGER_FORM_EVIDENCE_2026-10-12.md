## The author argument and its limits

[Patmore's2008 thesis](https://etheses.durham.ac.uk/id/eprint/2381/) supplies a
lawful substantive fallback for the inaccessible2007 journal article. The entire
chapter1, printed31–45/PDF36–50 and notes76–112, was read and visually checked;
its opening note connects its substance to that article. The journal body and
other thesis chapters were not read.

Patmore reports Papyrus967's minus, altered chapter order and an Old Latin
parallel, while accepting a possibly distinct shorter Hebrew source. He reports
Talmon's late-first-century-BCE Masada dating and argues against automatically
privileging the shorter form. His printed41 list misleadingly attributes7:6–9
to Papyrus967, which does not preserve chapter7. His labels36:23b–38 and
36:23c–38 are not silently harmonized into an exact clause boundary here.

Our inference is narrower than his broad skepticism about reconstruction:
an older long-form copy constrains its latest possible origin, but cannot rule
out expansion before both surviving copies. Coexistence is compatible with
literary development. A Greek witness does not directly preserve a second
Hebrew form, and Old Latin independence from this physical codex would not
prove independence from its Greek textual ancestry. Reading Patmore's citations
is not independent inspection of Lust, Tov, Talmon or the Latin witness.
