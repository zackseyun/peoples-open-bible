# Psalm 40 reader note qualification

Psalm 40:6's existing note treats “body” as the single Septuagint reading.
The [source comparison](PSALM_40_EARS_AND_BODY_COMPARISON_2026-10-18.md)
and [quotation follow-up](PSALM_40_QUOTATION_AND_EDITION_FOLLOWUP_2026-10-18.md)
instead establish differing Greek edition texts and an unresolved transmission
history. Replace only note b's body with:

> Hebrew: “ears you have dug for me,” likely an idiom for enabling hearing or obedience. Greek texts differ: Swete’s edition prints “a body you prepared for me,” as in Hebrews 10:5; Rahlfs–Hanhart’s edition prints “ears you prepared for me.” Which Greek reading is earlier remains disputed.

The literal Hebrew gloss and its qualified hearing/obedience explanation are
unchanged. Naming editions makes a limited, checked claim: Swete selects body,
whereas the inspected Rahlfs–Hanhart-derived surface text selects ears. It does
not pretend that either edition is an additional ancient manuscript, that
Hebrews demonstrably changed the Psalm, or that Greek body proves Hebrew body.
The prepared/body and prepared/ears English clauses are close glosses of the
consulted Greek, not borrowed modern English Bible wording.
