# Saul's commission in 1 Samuel 10

## Judgment

The longer Greek form of 1 Samuel 10:1 makes Saul's rule, deliverance from
surrounding enemies and confirming sign explicit. This is a consequential
alternative to POB's shorter Hebrew-based commission, not a newly discovered
reading. Historical priority remains unresolved; retain POB's Hebrew and main
English provisionally, without treating Masoretic brevity as automatic priority.
No exact reconstructed Hebrew or English replacement is approved here.

Swete's complete printed 561–563 / PDF593–595 supply the connected Greek
context. Its 562 apparatus reports Alexandrinus omitting ἐν and the second σύ,
and κεχρικεν for the repeated anointing verb. It reports no A omission of the
long commission/sign. The own-volume key and preface establish A/B identities
and the restricted comparison; silence is not agreement among every Greek copy.

The larger Cambridge edition's complete target pages, printed 28–29 / PDF44–45,
add a material distinction: `κυριος—(2) σε sub ÷ c₂` marks an intervening span
with a hexaplaric obelus, not omission. The Samuel key identifies c₂ as Moscow
Synodal Library Gr31; the edition's own first-volume conventions define the
sign and connecting dash. Do not create another short Greek witness by
misreading this notation. No whole commission/sign omission is recorded in
this inspected apparatus unit; that is not a claim about all Greek witnesses.
The preface identifies Old Latin ILᵇ as Belsheim's Vindobonensis edition with
9:21–10:7 coverage, and the target apparatus quotes Latin variants. This adds
edition-report evidence, not a direct reading of that Latin edition or manuscript.

Thus generic statements that the Vulgate has the Greek plus need an edition
and feature qualification. The Clementine partial plus is not a second exact
copy of the full Greek form, nor independent proof of lost Hebrew wording.
Old Latin Vindobonensis/Napoli support remains the original study's report,
not newly inspected manuscript evidence.

The pinned QDR biblical index returns no exact `1Sam 10:1` hit. This is a
bounded indexing result, not proof that every discovered Hebrew witness lacks
the passage. It supplies no positive surviving Hebrew letters for the plus.

## Original competing arguments

Müller, Pakkala and ter Haar Romeny's [Evidence of Editing](https://www.sbl-site.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/11/EvidenceEditing_MullerPakkalaRomeny_SBL.pdf),
chapter 6, 69–77, favors an earlier longer form and deliberate shortening.
Its strongest points concern the conjunction כי, the advance introduction of
the signs and the commission's narrative setting. It also acknowledges a real
problem for the usual repeated-anointing eye-skip explanation: that simple
skip would not retain כי in the Masoretic position.

Driver's original 1913 notes, complete 77–78, favor the longer form and an
eye skip, but accept הלוא כי as good Hebrew through the 2 Samuel 13:28
parallel. The direct WLC control confirms that parallel. Rarity therefore
does not make the shorter syntax impossible; no whole-Bible frequency claim
is independently established here.

The [original Keil commentary in digital reprint](https://biblehub.com/commentaries/kad/1_samuel/10.htm)
argues the other direction: expansion using 9:16–17 and an explanation of the
signs. He distinguishes the Latin wording and proposes Old Latin influence
on its addition. That ancestry is his hypothesis, not demonstrated by reading
the two Latin editions. His singular-sign objection is not conclusive either:
a singular introduction can summarize several subsequent confirmations.

## Direct countertests

In the editing study's explicitly reconstructed Hebrew, deleting from the
first משחך יהוה through the repeated formula leaves a question without כי.
It does not yield the exact WLC question הלוא כי משחך יהוה. This reproduces
the study's objection to that simple skip, not a proof that all copying-loss
models fail. The reconstructed Hebrew and any subsequent repair remain
hypotheses, not surviving letters.

The anti-Saul deletion motive also needs qualification. WLC retains his
divinely appointed rescue role at 9:16–17 and God's presence at 10:7. At
14:47–48 it describes fighting enemies around him and delivering Israel.
Those controls limit a simple whole-book suppression story, while not ruling
out an isolated editorial change. A commission against the Philistines at
9:16 does not logically exclude action against other enemies. Later rejection
can coexist with an earlier genuine commission. The editing study recognizes
the retained 9:16–17 material; it does not establish an actual deleting hand.

Conversely, a longer form's fit with 9:16–17 and 10:7,9 works both as evidence
for continuity and as a possible expansion model. An editor could reshape
the opening and reuse the existing anointing clause as a sign explanation;
the assumption that an expansion must simply follow an intact short question
does not exhaust the alternatives. Neither textual length nor contextual
smoothness alone determines the transmission direction.
