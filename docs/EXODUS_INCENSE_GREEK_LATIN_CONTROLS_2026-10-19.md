# Exodus incense altar in Greek and Old Latin textual forms

Date: 2026-10-19. Baseline: `c5ae88ba53dffde352cf1da75c36292a0692f858`.

## Result

The ordinary Greek construction account lacks the Hebrew incense-altar
construction report, but this is not true of all reported Greek forms.
The newly consulted original Old Latin edition positively prints a golden
incense-altar placement command. Its damaged construction and implementation
passages do not warrant a blanket omission claim. Retain POB's Hebrew and
English provisionally; the earliest whole account remains unresolved.

This closes part of the direct-edition gap in the
[material reconstruction control](EXODUS_TABERNACLE_RECONSTRUCTION_CONTROL_2026-10-19.md).
It does not resolve that study's terminal-length mismatch or independently
establish the proposed four-stage Hebrew genealogy.

## Greek construction and placement are different units

Brooke and McLean, *The Old Testament in Greek*, I.2 (1909), printed
285–288 / one-based PDF 143–146, was read in complete-page views with its
apparatus. The B-based ordinary text proceeds through ark, table, lampstand,
metalwork, bronze altar, oil/incense and laver without a report equivalent
to Hebrew 37:25–28. At Greek 38:18 it nevertheless mentions gilding bars;
do not turn missing detailed frame construction into absence of every bar
reference. Its 40:24–25, printed 292 / PDF 150, places the golden altar
and burns incense on it (Hebrew 40:26–27). Construction omission and absence
of the altar from the narrative are not interchangeable claims.

The [edition's separate appendix](https://tmcdaniel.palmerseminary.edu/Brooke%26McLean/LXX_Brooke%26McLean_1-2.pdf),
printed 295 / PDF 153, explains why the substantially Hebrew-ordered form
cannot be represented adequately as ordinary variants. It uses G where
extant and c to fill its gaps. At Hebrew-numbered 37:25–28, printed
298–299 / PDF 156–157, this appendix **does** contain gold-altar construction.
Local reports include the later F form and c/k; G's coverage does not reach
this unit. An absent G reading here is not evidence that G omitted the altar.

The inherited own key identifies c = 38, Escurial Y.II.5, and k = 58,
Vatican Regin. gr. 10. This is not a transfer of the Kings or Chronicles
sigla. The appendix calls its later F form Fᵇ. Its critical signs and versional
ascriptions require their own conventions; neither a bracketed sign supplied
from Syriac nor a selected appendix line is a newly read Greek manuscript.

One local contrast matters: the c-based appendix prints dimensions **5 × 5 × 3
cubits** for this golden altar, while its Fᵇ report has **1 × 1 × 2**, matching
WLC 37:25. The larger dimensions match WLC's burnt-offering altar at 38:1.
Assimilation is a possible explanation, not a demonstrated copying event.
The supposedly Hebrew-aligned Greek form must still be evaluated unit by unit;
it cannot simply replace Hebrew as an undifferentiated block.

## Original Old Latin edition and its notation

Leo Ziegler's [1883 original](https://books.google.com/books?id=T6oGAAAAQAAJ)
edits the Munich palimpsest Clm 6225. Ranke's 1871 edition instead concerns
Würzburg; it is not an alternative edition of this object. Root read the
complete title, two-page preface and target pages 25–26,28,30,79 from actual
page images. The acquisition scout also inspected the complete 21–30 sequence.
These are edition readings, not newly deciphered manuscript photographs.

The [preface's final page](https://books.google.com/books?id=T6oGAAAAQAAJ&pg=PP18)
distinguishes bold letters with surviving traces from italic editorial supplies.
Ziegler limits supplements to gap-sized restorations and disclaims a continuous
reconstruction. At [printed 28](https://books.google.com/books?id=T6oGAAAAQAAJ&pg=PA28),
folio 77 right column, the 40:5 command has upright text:
`ET PONES ALTARE AUREUM AD INCENDENDUM ANTE ARCAM`.
This positively reports placing a golden altar for incense before the ark.
It agrees on that content with retained POB 40:5, without certifying every word.

Printed 25–26, folio 82, contain substantial loss and supplements in the
construction sequence. Printed 30, folio 94 left column, likewise has extensive
italic supplies in 40:20–29. Its apparent incense wording cannot be counted
wholesale as preserved; nor can an unprinted clause in damage prove an ancient
omission. At [printed 79](https://books.google.com/books?id=T6oGAAAAQAAJ&pg=PA79),
Ziegler explicitly records cut-away membrane at pages 21–22 and extreme erasure
in 30B. His washing-sequence copying-loss explanation is local, not proof
that the entire shorter account arose by the same mechanism.

Dold's 1956 revision remains unread: its ordinary JSTOR download returned
an HTML challenge, not a PDF. No bypass or revised-letter claim follows.
The ninth-century date of the manuscript's uppertext is not a date for the
Old Latin undertext. A current manuscript-pixel collation is still separate.

## A critical edition's silence has a defined scope

Wevers's [original-author English introduction](https://ccat.sas.upenn.edu/rak/temp/Wevers-Exod-n.html)
to the 1991 Göttingen Exodus edition, edited online by Kraft in 2006, identifies
Monacensis as Latin 104 and explicitly excludes parts of chapters 37–39 from
its collation because they have little contact with parallel Greek. Apparatus
silence in those portions is therefore not a reading or an agreement claim.
This introduction was consulted directly; the full target apparatus was not.

It also distinguishes the later Hebrew-aligned Fʰ insertion from F's other
corrections. Do not import its Fᵇ/Fʰ labels into Cambridge's notation. The
institutional HTML damages Greek typography, so no exact missing-Greek endpoint
is transcribed from its garbled characters.
