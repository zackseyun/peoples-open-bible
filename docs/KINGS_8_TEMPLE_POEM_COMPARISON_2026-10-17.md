# Solomon's temple poem in Hebrew Greek and Latin

## Result for POB

The temple poem at Hebrew 1 Kings 8:12–13 has a substantially different Greek
parallel after 8:53. The difference affects the sun, who speaks about building
the house, the dwelling expression and the poem's claimed source. These are
meaningful alternatives, not merely different verse labels. The current POB
represents the shorter Hebrew form; this comparison does not yet establish a
replacement Hebrew text or a superior main English rendering.

Source, English, order, reader notes and canon remain unchanged. The
[evidence packet](../sources/textual_restoration/comparisons/kings8_temple_poem.2026-10-17.v1.json)
records exact inputs, source and render pins, consultation scopes and review.
This is comparison of published evidence, not a newly deciphered reading or a
discovery new to scholarship.

## Align the actual forms

Swete I, fourth edition 1909 reprinted 1925, printed 692 / PDF 724 and
695 / PDF 727, supplies the Greek control. Its own key identifies B as
Vaticanus and A as Alexandrinus. Its B-based manual text and limited A collation
are not a complete modern reconstruction of every Greek family.

| Feature | Current Hebrew and POB | Swete's B-based Greek form |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Location | After the cloud fills the temple, before Solomon turns to the assembly | 8:11 proceeds to 8:14; the longer poem follows 8:53 and precedes the end-of-prayer notice at 8:54 |
| Introduction | Solomon said | Adds that the statement concerns the house and follows completion of its construction |
| Sun | No corresponding colon | Accusative **Ἥλιον**, with nominative **Κύριος** and the verb **ἐγνώρισεν**: the Lord is the expressed grammatical subject, not a speaking sun |
| Darkness | Dwelling in thick darkness | Main line has **ἐκ γνόφου**, while the A apparatus reports **ἐν γνόφῳ**; do not silently substitute “in” for this B-based reading |
| Building | Solomon: “I have indeed built” a house for God | Imperative **Οἰκοδόμησον**, “my house,” then “for yourself”; the addressee is not expressly named in that clause |
| Dwelling duration or condition | “Forever” | **ἐπὶ καινότητος**, a newness expression, not the ordinary Greek eternity expression |
| Source citation | None at this location | A Book-of-Song citation |

The apparatus complicates a simple two-form picture. Before 8:14 it explicitly
reports an A insertion with Solomon's first-person building statement and an
eternity expression. At the later poem it gives positive A local readings,
including the in-darkness wording, the adjective, reflexive pronoun, **κενοτητος**
and **ουχι**. The latter replaces **οὐκ ἰδοὺ**, rather than simply omitting it.
The kenotetos spelling must not automatically become an approved “emptiness”
interpretation. A thus participates at both locations in this printed report;
its complete late wording, including unchanged sun/build clauses, would be
assembled using the edition's collation conventions and apparatus silence.
No complete newly read Alexandrinus transcription is claimed.

## The Latin report is marginal and dependent evidence

Vercellone's *Variae lectiones* II (1864), printed 489 / PDF 526, explicitly
heads the target material as the margin of the Gothic Legionensis codex. At
VIII.53 it reports **Solem statuit**, with **stare fecit** as an alternative;
**aedifica mihi domum pulcherrimam**; the unusual **in habitare in novitate**;
and **in libro Cantici**. The reported building command has first-person
**mihi**, with no second-person **tibi** corresponding to Greek **σαυτῷ**.

The dedication-of-the-house wording and the alternative involving a cloud and
“my house” are reported separately. Vercellone's intervening editorial comment
is not part of one continuous Latin sentence, and the alternatives must not be
flattened together. Root and the Latin reader visually checked the complete
target page and preface XXI–XXII / PDF 28–29. That key describes these extracts
as old Latin material translated from Greek. The Latin is therefore relevant
to the Greek tradition, not another independent direct Hebrew witness.

This verifies an edition's marginal report, not the manuscript's physical date,
hand or a modern Vetus Latina siglum. The key sends the codex description to
another volume, not consulted here. The fourth/fifth-century description at the
top of XXI belongs to a preceding manuscript, not Legionensis. The official
Moreno Hernández thesis route failed to supply a PDF; its target pages were
not read. A quotation of that thesis in a modern study is kept separate from
consultation of the thesis itself.

## Direct Hebrew coverage does not settle the poem

A Hebrew source reader inspected all displayed primary rows, separately from
their biblical parallels, in the Qumran-Digital 2026-05-21 editions of **4Q54,
5Q2 and 6Q4**. The related identity controls **4Q54a, 4Q54b, 4Q69c and 4Q47a**
were also checked. No identified target poem or continuous relevant junction
occurs in this bounded set. Unassigned scraps and unindexed quotations prevent
turning that result into an exhaustive absence claim.

The important 4Q54 boundary is fragment 6 line 11: its supplied context reaches
the beginning of 8:9, where limited tablets wording is outside the brackets.
Fragment 7 resumes at 8:16–17. That physical gap cannot attest omission of
8:12–13, nor locate the longer poem. These reference ranges include supplies;
they do not mean that every intervening verse survives. Duplicate fragment
displays in 6Q4 and reciprocal 4Q54b/4Q69c labels are not extra witness votes.
The challenged 4Q54a/4Q47a reassignment remains held, not adopted from a release
date or a shared catalogue label.

## Competing reconstructions

Root read the complete institutional author reprint of Juha Pakkala's 2014
[“Yahweh, the Sun-god, Wants a New Temple”](https://helda.helsinki.fi/server/api/core/bitstreams/1084f9a0-157b-4b2d-a4de-0f83cf814e48/content),
including notes. His reconstruction combines evidence across Hebrew, Greek
and Latin. It makes Sun and Yahweh parallel subjects, treats the building
command as divine speech and reads the dwelling as a new temple. He explains
the extant differences as theological correction. His completed reconstruction
is explicitly not found intact in a witness; it is a historical hypothesis,
not a transcription. Its resegmentation must not be presented as what the
extant Greek grammar says.

Root also read all 24 pages of Matthieu Richelle's 2018
[“How to Edit an Elusive Text?”](https://research.dial.uclouvain.be/server/api/core/bitstreams/20fc6dfc-3d77-4848-a354-7bff8f116062/content),
*Textus* 27, 205–228. He distinguishes plausible from unstable retroversions,
tests expansion and omission, and connects the poem's form with its location.
He considers both transfer directions and parallel insertions, then proposes
representing the literary forms separately rather than merging an ideal poem.
His Hebrew column is a tentative reconstruction with editorial choices,
not newly attested Hebrew. His treatment of newness also allows a translation
explanation from wording close to the current Hebrew, rather than requiring
the same lost word as Pakkala's reconstruction.

Root visually checked the key argument/reconstruction pages and Richelle's
synopsis and editorial presentation. The packet distinguishes those visual
checks from complete extracted-text reading. Earlier authors discussed in
these studies are reported positions, not additional independently consulted
works or witness votes.

## Connected controls and our judgment

Root checked the current source, main English and reader notes for the target
and connected passages recorded in the packet. The Greek source reader checked
Swete's target pages, conventions and Chronicles parallel; root also inspected
the decisive Greek pages and Latin report.

Several controls prevent a premature theological reconstruction:

- The printed Greek's sun is an object. Making it a speaking subject involves
  a proposed Hebrew precursor and different clause division, not translation
  of the extant Greek as it stands. Poetic parallelism can support a proposed
  relation but does not require two subjects to be identical deities.
- Both Hebrew 8:20 and Swete's Greek retain Solomon's claim to have built the
  house for the divine name. A blanket account in which Greek removes every
  royal building claim is therefore inadequate, although a local alteration
  at the poem remains possible.
- Hebrew 8:13's dwelling statement coexists with 8:27's denial that even the
  heavens can contain God, and with name-language in 8:16–20,29. These tensions
  can inform literary analysis; they do not identify a specific censor.
  The repeated **מכון שבתך** in 8:39,43,49 explicitly concerns heaven. It can
  support divine-abode vocabulary without proving a unique reference to an
  earlier earthly temple or recovering first-person suffixes in the poem.
- Swete II, printed 67–68 / PDF 85–86, places the Chronicles cloud and short
  poem together at 5:13–6:2. Its 6:2 has a house for the divine name and an
  explicit eternity expression. The Greek parallel is not identical to the
  current Hebrew, and it has no sun colon here. The scan's precise issue date
  remains unverified; it is not assigned volume I's reprint date.
- “Newness” does not by itself prove that Solomon replaced an earlier Jerusalem
  temple. Nor can a smoother or more symmetrical combined poem establish its
  own antiquity. The lexical equivalent of one expression cannot
  be selected by importing a preferred religious history.

There is a serious case for an earlier Hebrew precursor behind parts of the
long form, but the consulted evidence does not recover its exact wording or
establish that every longer clause is earlier. A difficult reading can be old
or corrupt; a clearer one can preserve or revise. Swete's descriptions at
xvii and xxii date B to the fourth century and A to the fifth. Within this
pair, the late-placement Greek form thus has earlier physical attestation than
A's early insertion. That gives it modest chronological support, not priority
for every proposed Hebrew word. Removing that preference does not change the
held outcome. Dates of Greek or Latin translation are not dates of all wording
in a later copy or marginal report.

The held source decision is therefore provisional retention of the current
Hebrew form, not proof that it wins historically. A reader-facing disclosure of
the attested alternative is worth considering separately. It would need a
pinned candidate and application/export review; this research pass does not
silently add it or certify the current English as optimal.

## The cited song book and the next evidence

The Book-of-Song expression is actual Greek and reported Latin evidence.
Equating it with the Hebrew Book of Jashar in Joshua 10:13 and 2 Samuel 1:18
requires an identification or letter-transposition hypothesis. Root checked
those Hebrew controls; neither supplies the missing book. A source citation
does not recover a complete work or establish its canonical status, so this
pass offers no new text for inclusion in the canon.

The next discriminating evidence is the actual broader Greek/Antiochene
target collation and conventions, a modern Latin target edition or its
manuscript context, and direct witness checks at the two poem locations when
the claim depends on their complete wording. These could test harmonization,
mixed forms and the direction of local revisions. They should precede a
combined Hebrew reconstruction. Do not repeat these completed article, selected
text, Latin-report or bounded Hebrew-coverage checks as fresh progress.
