## Hebrew meaning and morphology

The whole Psalm addresses Yahweh, contrasts human smallness with the heavens,
then gives humanity glory, honor and dominion over earthly creatures. Genesis
1:26–27 supplies a compatible image/dominion context for God, but the connection
is an interpretation, not an explicit identification of the Psalm's noun.

אֱלֹהִים has both singular-deity and plural divine-being uses. Genesis 1:1
combines its formally plural noun with a singular creation verb; Psalm 82:1
likewise has singular agreement at its first occurrence, while 82:6 addresses
plural persons. Psalm 97:7 also addresses a plural group. These controls show
why context and syntax matter; they do not settle all those passages' referents.
The comparative phrase in Psalm 8 supplies no agreeing predicate or modifier
that forces either referent. Original-language priority cannot choose one
meaning among viable meanings of the same original-language word.

The displayed [BDB noun entry](https://biblehub.com/bdb/430.htm) expressly
discusses Psalm 8:6 under divine/superhuman uses and records competing God and
angel interpretations. This is the lexicographers' analysis, not another ancient
witness or a decisive vote. “Heavenly” is also interpretive: the noun does not
explicitly name heaven here, and choosing a plural excludes the God reading.

The actual WLC/OSHB verb annotation is HC/Vpw2ms/Sp3ms, Piel with a second-person
subject and third-person object suffix. The displayed [BDB verb entry](https://biblehub.com/bdb/2637.htm)
also places this occurrence under Piel, distinguishing its Hiphil forms.
POB's inherited Hiphil rationale is factually wrong. Correcting that label does
not require different Hebrew letters or a different comparative English clause.
The electronic BDB reproduction has formatting/reference glitches; no print-page
collation or new HALOT consultation is claimed.

## Greek interpretation and surviving Hebrew coverage

The actual [Rahlfs/Hanhart 2006 publisher text](https://www.die-bibel.de/bibel/LXX/PSA.8)
has βραχύ τι παρ᾽ ἀγγέλους. This directly verifies angels in the selected Greek
edition, not uniform Greek manuscript support. It can translate a divine-being
sense of the same Hebrew noun; it does not recover different Hebrew letters.
Pinned local MorphGNT/SBLGNT Hebrews 2:5–10 verifies angels at 2:7 and the
application to Jesus at 2:9. A quotation using that Greek interpretation is not
an independent Hebrew manuscript. The Psalm's degree comparison and Hebrews'
humiliation/application should not be collapsed by automatic harmonization.
This pass does not adjudicate the separate degree/duration question.

The located [5/6Hev1b transcription](https://lexicon.qumran-digital.org/transcriptions/5~6Hev1b/2026-10-06/index.html?v=2026-10-06),
fragment 1 column i line 24, places מאלהים and the preceding comparison wholly
inside supply brackets. Part of the crowning predicate survives outside them.
Its pointed biblical parallel is a comparison text, not manuscript ink. This
lacuna neither confirms a referent nor attests an omission. The excerpt retains
the project's bracket/uncertainty notation and [CC BY-SA attribution](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-sa/4.0/).
No new pixels, dating judgment or exhaustive manuscript census is added.
