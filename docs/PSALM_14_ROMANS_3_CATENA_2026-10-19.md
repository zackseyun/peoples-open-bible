# Psalm 14 and the Romans 3 quotation chain

## Decision

Retain POB's short Hebrew Psalm 14 and the distinct Greek text of Romans
3:10–18. The longer passage in Greek Psalm 13:3 is real transmission evidence,
not grounds by itself for adding Romans 3:13–18 to the Hebrew psalm. Its eight
lines connect descriptions of throats, tongues, lips, mouth, feet, paths and
eyes. That expansion changes the poem's presentation, not just its English style.

The origin of the chain and the earlier wording of the psalm are separate
questions. A pre-Pauline collection of quotations, even if established, would
not automatically establish an original Hebrew Psalm 14 containing it. No
source, English, reader note, approval, layout or canon change is applied.
Baseline: `f6bb9a904dba3d8528ced61e92d4066df9a13e5c`, 2026-10-19.

## Actual Greek evidence

Swete II, printed pp. 224–225 / PDF 242–243, selects Vaticanus (B) here.
Page 225 prints the complete longer passage after “not even one” and before
verse 4, without body brackets or critical signs. Its word sequence agrees
with POB's six SBLGNT source bodies at Romans 3:13–18, apart from punctuation.
This is edition evidence, not a newly collated photograph.

The Psalm location is `V-B26K13V3-26-PS`, quire 59, folio 3 verso, column 1,
lines 20–29, words 14–61. No modern crossdating of correction nomenclature or
new manuscript-image reading is claimed. The same codex's Romans passage is
not a second independent manuscript vote.

## Hebrew survival is narrower than a complete quotation

Written WLC Psalm 14:3 proceeds from “not even one” to verse 4 without this
passage. Psalm 53:2–4 supplies a related short Hebrew sequence, not an
independent copy of Psalm 14. Root read the complete WLC Psalm 14 and the
selected Hebrew source contexts corresponding to Romans' chain: Psalms 5:10,
140:4, 10:7, 36:2 and Isaiah 59:7–8. These parallels explain available material,
not the date or language in which it was assembled.

Pulkkinen reports 11Q7 as lacking the expansion. The actual
[Qumran-Digital 11Q7 transcription](https://lexicon.qumran-digital.org/transcriptions/11Q7/2026-05-21/index.html#c381607-i381736)
requires a preservation qualification: fragment group 4–7, line 13 supplies
most of Psalm 14:2–3 and the following verse-4 opening, while printing `אחד`
outside supply brackets. Line 14 again supplies much of verse 4, with partial
later words outside brackets. Root read all fifteen primary rows, excluding
parallel rows. The edition reconstructs a short sequence; it does not display
a wholly surviving verse-3-to-4 junction. A reference range is not that stronger
claim. The original DJD reconstruction/plate rationale is not newly consulted.

## Opposing original arguments

Marika Pulkkinen, [2017, pp. 384–409](https://helda.helsinki.fi/server/api/core/bitstreams/5844c646-c9fc-4558-945e-7b7db57644a1/content),
favors Paul's composition followed by harmonization of Greek Psalm 13.
Differences from the individual source passages and fit with Romans support
her argument. She nevertheless allows particular wording to derive from his
source and admits that an earlier composition could have been modified.
Root read the complete target section, pp. 392–409, including notes, and viewed
complete pp. 401 and 408; the source agent visually checked all eighteen pages.
Her wider witness lists, papyrus date and ancient-author reports are not fresh
collations here.

Eveline van Staalduine-Sulman, [2011, pp. 93–112](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/292653604_Where_is_god_Romans_313-18_as_an_addition_to_Psalm_14),
argues instead that poetic organization, subjects and links to the surrounding
psalm favor composition for Psalm 14 before Paul. She rejects the extant Leiden
Hebrew plus as an ancient exemplar: her analysis identifies one medieval
Christian witness behind two catalogue numbers and argues for retrotranslation.
Her proposed ancient Hebrew is hypothetical. Root and a source agent read
pp. 103–112 through author-uploaded full-text HTML, not its abstract; no PDF
or Leiden manuscript image was acquired. The identification remains her checked
argument, not our direct catalogue/manuscript verification.

## What the comparison can decide

Exact agreement establishes relationship, not direction. A coherent poem can
be excerpted, expanded or repurposed; contextual fit alone does not date it.
Conversely, the extant later Greek distribution does not prove that Paul had
no earlier collection. Neither argument supplies the missing ancient Hebrew
exemplar. Do not manufacture one by joining the Hebrew parallels.

For POB, retain the current distinct texts. A separately reviewed compact
reader note could explain the longer Greek form, but no disclosure candidate
is installed here. Reopen historical selection for the actual early papyrus
collation, the original 11Q7 reconstruction/plate, or discriminating ancient
quotation evidence—not another comparison of these same texts. These are
known published alternatives, not a new decipherment or a canon recommendation.

records pins and consultation boundaries.
