# Saul's commission in the original and revised Old Latin editions

## Result

The longer commission in 1 Samuel 10:1 is now directly checked in Belsheim's
1885 edition and Fischer's 1983 revision, rather than only through the Cambridge
apparatus and a later study. Both editions represent the same Old Latin
manuscript. They support the longer sequence but do not establish an earlier
Hebrew original. Historical priority remains HOLD; retain POB's current Hebrew
and main English provisionally.

## What the editions actually supply

[Belsheim 1885](https://archive.org/details/palimpsestusvin00belsgoog), printed
14–15 / PDF27–28, gives anointing over the people Israel, rule over the Lord's
people, rescue from surrounding enemies, a confirming sign and repeated
anointing over the inheritance. It begins directly with `unxit`, not an
interrogative `nonne`. The rescue verb is printed `saluabis`; the sign begins
`Hoc`, without a preceding `et`.

His complete preface iii–viii / PDF8–13 identifies the manuscript and explains
that doubtful decipherments use italics. These target clauses are upright;
adjacent 9:27 includes italics. That is the editor's stated confidence, not our
independent verification of surviving ink.

[Fischer, with Ulrich and Sanderson, 1983](https://septuaginta.uni-goettingen.de/static/ioscs/pdf/volumes/bioscs16.pdf),
printed 50–51 / PDF26, retains the longer sequence but supplies damaged letters:
`[et libera]bis`, `he[r]editatem` and `principe[m]`. Its target apparatus expressly
allows `[et salva]bis` too. Therefore the exact rescue verb is unresolved, not
newly deciphered as either *liberare* or *salvare*. The revision also prints
`et hoc` at the sign introduction.

The complete introduction, 13–39 / PDF7–20, describes renewed manuscript
examination in 1941–42, comparison with Dold's photographic work and a further
manuscript check. It criticizes the first edition's reliability and explains
the revised format. Its target-specific damage notes control the claim; the
1885 unqualified spelling must not be promoted to an ink observation. Neither
inspected target is a manuscript photograph.

## One manuscript, not two names counted twice

The revised introduction identifies former Vienna 17 as Naples, Biblioteca
Nazionale, lat.1. The reused volume contains distinct biblical undertexts:
VL101 for the Pentateuch and VL115 for Samuel–Kings. The Samuel target belongs
to VL115, fol.5; these are not two Samuel witnesses.

*Evidence of Editing*, 71 note 5, names “Codex Vindobonensis and the Napoli
codex” without a second shelfmark there. That wording alone cannot establish
two independent witnesses. If “Napoli” means lat.1, it is the same object;
another manuscript would require identification and passage-level verification.
Likewise, the original and revised editions, their online mirrors and two local
files with identical 1983 PDF hashes are not additional votes.
