# Amos 5 and Acts 7 source forms and quotation evidence

## Judgment

Amos 5:25–27 and Acts 7:42–43 preserve consequential differences: a deity-name
or tent interpretation, different arrangements of the star and images, and
Damascus versus Babylon. The Damascus Document supplies a further Hebrew
quotation with its own interpretive context. These forms should be compared,
not combined into a newly reconstructed Hebrew verse.

Retain POB's current Hebrew and distinct Acts Greek provisionally. Earlier
Hebrew priority, the precise deity identifications and the temporal force of
Amos's carrying verb remain unresolved. This comparison changes no canonical
source, main English, note or reader code. It establishes a new connected
comparison and corrects potential evidence counts, not a previously unknown
ancient reading or a decision to expand the canon.

## The connected readings

The local WLC source fields were read for Amos 5:18–27, 9:11, Numbers 24:17 and
Exodus 32:1–8; Acts 7:39–50 was read in its SBLGNT source fields. The rejection
of worship and announced exile frame Amos's question about wilderness
sacrifices. Acts explicitly places the quotation after rejection of Moses,
the calf and worship of the heavenly host, then contrasts another tent in
7:44. Those contexts matter before assigning a deity or carrying tense.

| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Carrying or exile | ונשאתם, then והגליתי | ἀνελάβετε, then μετοικιῶ | ἀνελάβετε, then μετοικιῶ | Begins והגליתי with the objects of Amos 5:26 |
| Tent or name | סִכּוּת מַלְכְּכֶם | σκηνὴν τοῦ Μόλοχ | σκηνὴν τοῦ Μολὸχ | Unpointed סכות מלככם |
| Star and images | כיון צלמיכם, then כוכב אלהיכם | Star of your god Ῥαιφάν, then images of them | Star of the god Ῥαιφάν; images made to worship | כיון צלמיכם; star clause absent from this quotation |
| Destination expression | מהלאה לדמשק | ἐπέκεινα Δαμασκοῦ | ἐπέκεινα Βαβυλῶνος | מאהלי דמשק |

The last CD expression literally invokes the tents of Damascus with a
from-prefix, not the same wording as Hebrew beyond Damascus. Its quotation
does not contain Babylon. It also does not preserve Amos's carrying verb and
therefore cannot decide that verb's tense. The selected Greek ἀνελάβετε is
past-tense wording; that is evidence of an ancient interpretation, not proof
that the Hebrew must have that temporal force. A future carrying into exile
and a retrospective indictment remain competing contextual readings here.

## Actual Greek apparatus rather than a uniform Septuagint

Swete III, printed p. 23 / PDF p. 47, has a B-based target and collected A/Q
variants. Its selected continuation is τοὺς τύπους αὐτῶν οἷς ἐποιήσατε ἑαυτοῖς.
The apparatus reports Ῥεφάν in Q and omission of αὐτῶν in A/Q, with Q's margin
retaining it. The appendix, printed pp. 837–838 / PDF pp. 861–862, records
αναλαβετε in A at 5:26 and μετοικειω B* (corrected -κιω Bᵇ) A at 5:27.
Do not silently normalize these into an identical all-witness text or infer
the grammatical force of A's unaccented form solely from that spelling.

The actual [official SBLGNT Acts XML](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Faithlife/SBLGNT/c4d241a9c1c479a55b989ba35a4976c1d0b8052c/data/sblgnt/xml/Acts.xml)
has no ὑμῶν after θεοῦ in 7:43 and continues τοὺς τύπους οὓς ἐποιήσατε
προσκυνεῖν αὐτοῖς. Its [edition-comparison apparatus](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Faithlife/SBLGNT/c4d241a9c1c479a55b989ba35a4976c1d0b8052c/data/sblgntapp/xml/Acts.xml)
reports added ὑμῶν in NA28/RP and deity-name forms Ῥομφά in WH, Ῥεφάν in
Tregelles and Ῥεμφάν in RP, against selected Ῥαιφάν. The publisher's
[apparatus explanation](https://sblgnt.com/about/introduction/apparatus/)
makes its comparison of edited texts explicit: those labels are not a census
of physical Acts witnesses. Silence at 7:42 cannot establish manuscript
unanimity. Swete and SBLGNT attest a close quotation relationship but not
identical selected wording or an independently surviving Hebrew ancestor.

## What the Damascus evidence actually preserves

Schechter's 1910 *Documents of Jewish Sectaries*, volume I, printed Hebrew
p. 7 / PDF p. 90, prints CD A 7:14–15 without supply brackets at the quotation.
His introduction, printed ix–x / PDF 17–18, identifies A as Cambridge T-S 10 K 6
and B as T-S 16.311, estimating A around the tenth century. The medieval
copy's date must not be replaced with the date of the ancient composition.
This is published transcription, not a new inspection of manuscript ink.
The [historical scan](https://archive.org/download/documentsofjewis01scheuoft/documentsofjewis01scheuoft.pdf)
and [Qumran-Digital CD column 7](https://forum.qumran-digital.org/transcriptions/CD/2026-05-21/index.html#c1205)
agree on the relevant unpointed quotation. The adjacent interpretation links
the tent to Torah books, king to congregation, images to prophetic books,
and star to the interpreter, quoting Amos 9:11 and Numbers 24:17. Quotation
and commentary must not be merged into continuous Amos testimony.

The [4Q266 fragment 3 iii body](https://forum.qumran-digital.org/transcriptions/4Q266/2026-05-21/index.html#c70858)
has a gap through the quotation and resumes at line 17 with ש[וכת דויד --].
The supplied deity-related wording in line 18 belongs to commentary. The
preserved Damascus in line 20 belongs to the interpreter/Numbers context,
not to a surviving Amos 5:27 quotation. In
[4Q269 fragment 5](https://forum.qumran-digital.org/transcriptions/4Q269/2026-05-21/index.html#c74739),
the deity-related wording and Damascus are supplied; its small preserved
commentary remnants do not attest the quotation's objects. CD's antiquity
therefore does not turn these two copies into direct votes for every word
printed in the later Cairo quotation. Nor does quotation genre alone exclude
the possibility that a genuine Hebrew variant lies behind it.

A bounded Qumran-Digital screen of 4Q82, Mur88, 4Q78 and 5Q4 obtained no
target wording in continuous Amos. For example, the actual 4Q82 chapter-5
bodies reach 5:18 before the next aligned bodies concern chapter 6. Tiny
unassigned scraps and unindexed material remain outside that conclusion;
this is not proof that every discovered object lacks the passage. QDR states
its Qumran-Wörterbuch/Abegg lineage and CC BY-SA 4.0 licence. Its parallel
displays are navigation aids, not additional manuscripts or verified joins.

## The original study and competing explanations

Gert J. Steyn's complete nine-page [2013 open-access study](https://hts.org.za/index.php/hts/article/view/2006/3833)
was read, including its footnotes. It usefully traces the three expressions
across the contexts. It considers translation and a different Hebrew exemplar
for tent wording, and several explanations for the Greek deity-name form.
It favors an Acts adaptation and proposes that Babylon avoids a Damascus
interpretation associated with the sectarian document.

Our assessment is narrower. Shared quotations do not establish Luke's direct
knowledge of CD or identify who first changed the destination. A medieval
Egyptian copy does not establish a first-century Egyptian transmission route.
The distinct CD and Acts contexts support different uses; they do not prove
that one was a response to the other. A different Greek exemplar and later
cross-influence also require target-specific testing. The article's broad
reference to Qumran Damascus copies cannot replace their local preservation
check. Its p. 4 quotation locator and p. 8 Numbers reference need checking
against actual CD 7:14–15 and Numbers 24:17, not mechanical repetition.

Specific Saturn/Sagkud equations, supposed Egyptian deity equivalents and
precise letter-confusion histories have not been independently established
here. Borger's 1988 original study remains unread: the publisher download was
empty and the bounded institutional/OA-index routes supplied no article.
Neither a secondary description of his objections nor Steyn's report of his
proposal is treated as consultation of the original. These access limits
prevent a stronger deity-name or Hebrew-priority decision.
