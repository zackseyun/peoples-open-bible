# Jehoram's reign: eight, ten, or forty years

Date: 2026-10-19. Baseline: `6c26731d2834b0888af4411f88db6bd4de92ea40`.

## Decision

Retain POB's WLC source and English **eight years** at 2 Kings 8:17.
The consulted Greek apparatus reports eight, ten, and forty, not a unanimous
Greek forty. This is a source-comparison result, not a demonstrated recovery
of an earlier Hebrew numeral. It qualifies the connected
[Ahaziah age comparison](CHRONICLES_22_2_GREEK_APPARATUS_2026-10-19.md):
agreement on an accession age is not agreement on the whole chronology.

No canonical verse, existing approval metadata, reader note, source corpus,
publisher code, or reader layout changes in this contribution. The old
high-agreement label at 8:17 is not new approval of a critical source choice.
There is no canon implication or newly deciphered ancient reading.

## What the Greek edition actually reports

Root and one bounded source scout checked the complete body and apparatus
of Brooke, McLean and Thackeray, *The Old Testament in Greek*, II.2,
*Kings* (1930), printed 325 / one-based PDF 137. The
[institutional scan](https://tmcdaniel.palmerseminary.edu/Brooke%26McLean/LXX_Brooke%26McLean_2-2.pdf)
selects accession age thirty-two and reign forty (`τεσσεράκοντα ἔτη`).
Its reign apparatus distinguishes:

| Reign | Reported authorities |
| --- | --- |
| Forty | B, A, d₂; Armenian manuscript readings |
| Ten | o, c₂, e₂ = manuscripts 82, 127, 93 |
| Eight | N, `rell`; Armenian edition, Ethiopic, Syrohexaplar, Josephus reports |

Here `rell` refers to the remainder of the edition's selected cursives, not
every known Greek manuscript. Versions, an edition, and Josephus are not
additional Greek manuscripts or direct Hebrew manuscript votes. The selected
printed text is not itself an independent ancient witness.

Kings' preface, PDF 11, inherits the Samuel volume's symbols. Its own
addition d₂ is described as an eleventh-century Göttingen fragment covering
4 Reg. 8:12–9:24; no Rahlfs number is supplied there. It is not the separate
Aquila palimpsest Z₃. The inherited Samuel key, PDF 11–12, identifies o as
82 (Paris, Coislin 3), c₂ as 127 (Moscow, Synodal Greek 31), and e₂ as 93
(London, British Museum Royal 1 D. ii). A preliminary scout OCR reading of
83 for o was corrected against the key image **before this record**.
These are published identifications, not fresh catalogue verification.

The local age apparatus also reports thirty-eight in manuscript 71 against
selected thirty-two. d₂'s word-order difference still gives thirty-two.
These additional differences rule out describing the page as a uniform
Greek chronological scheme.

## Connected Hebrew and Chronicles controls

The actual WLC OSIS at 2 Kings 8:17 has eight in word `12jY6`.
Its ketiv/qere distinction concerns the following year noun (`12XbX` /
`12jxp`), not the numeral. It is not Hebrew evidence for ten or forty.
Root read local source, English and notes at 8:16–29 and 9:29, and
2 Chronicles 21:5,20. Kings' twelfth-year synchronism at 8:25 and
eleventh-year synchronism at 9:29 are not resolved here. Simple addition
cannot adjudicate a whole chronology without calendar, accession-year and
possible co-regency assumptions.

Cambridge II.3, *Chronicles* (1932), printed 517–518 / PDF 139–140,
was checked in complete-page views by root and scout, against its own key:

| Locus | Selected body | Relevant apparatus differences |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 21:5 | Age 32; reign 8 | c₂ = 127 has age 35; y = 121 has reign 50; p*,q,t,z add “and ten” after eight |
| 21:20 | Age 32; reign 8 | c₂ has abbreviated age 32 here; y has reign 50; A,a,m omit the second reign clause |

At 21:5 the explicit addition yields eighteen arithmetically; eighteen is
not the apparatus's printed numeral. Preserve the p* first-hand qualifier.
p,q,t,z are 106,120,134,554. Manuscript m omits the larger accession/reign
unit at 21:5 and must not be counted for eight there. Chronicles a and m
are 60 and 71; do not import different siglum mappings from Samuel.
Abbreviated thirty-two in d and g is not a different age.

The Hebrew Chronicles parallels have thirty-two and eight at both loci.
They are parallel accounts, not extra Kings manuscripts. Greek manuscript
reports likewise cannot be counted as independent votes without examining
their relationships. No ancient manuscript ink, newly recovered Hebrew
target witness, or comprehensive Dead Sea Scrolls coverage was inspected
for this contribution.

## Counterargument, evidence gap, and stopping rule

Eight's agreement across the retained Hebrew and Chronicles could reflect
harmonization; it does not prove eight's priority. Conversely, forty's
presence in B/A and a fragment does not prove a Hebrew forty. Ten is a real
Greek alternative that a binary MT-versus-LXX comparison would conceal.
Neither an age-plus-reign conflation nor a numeral-letter copying event
has been demonstrated by these local reports.

The useful next evidence is a modern argument explaining the local
transmission and connected chronology, not another model's preference.
Tetley's *The Reconstructed Chronology of the Divided Kingdom* (2004)
has relevant chapters on manuscript c₂ (pp. 54–63) and early divided-kingdom
chronology (pp. 119–144). Publisher metadata and contents were consulted;
the chapters require authentication and were **not read**. Hughes's
*Secrets of the Times* was likewise available only as publisher metadata,
not its target argument. Unofficial mirrors and snippets are not substitutes
for reading those arguments. No claim about either author's preferred
numeral is adopted. Reopen for lawful access to the actual relevant argument
or modern target apparatus, not repeated broad searching.
