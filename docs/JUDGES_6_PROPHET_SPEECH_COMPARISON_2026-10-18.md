# Judges 6 prophet speech and the shorter Qumran form

## Judgment

4Q49, also called 4QJudgesᵃ, supplies published evidence for a shorter Judges
6 narrative without the prophet's rebuke in 6:7–10. The editor's surviving-line
transcription constrains that result, but the literal 6:6→6:11 junction is
partly reconstructed. It is not a newly deciphered continuous junction.

Prefer literary expansion as the working explanation of the longer unit,
while retaining accidental paragraph loss as a substantive alternative.
This preference does not establish the addition's date, the entire book's
earlier edition or the precise history of the surviving Qumran copy.
It is not a complete source-and-English application approval. POB's current
longer Hebrew and English remain unchanged pending that separate assessment;
their retention is not a verdict that the Masoretic form is necessarily earlier.

## What the Hebrew witness preserves

The [versioned Qumran-Digital transcription](https://lexicon.qumran-digital.org/transcriptions/4Q49/2026-05-21/index.html?v=2026-05-21)
has nine primary physical lines. All were inspected separately from parallel
Masoretic rows. Brackets and uncertain letters must remain visible:

| Physical line | Published preserved material | Reconstructed continuation relevant here |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 5 | Parts of the Israelites' cry and YHWH in 6:6 | The angel's arrival and sitting beneath the tree in 6:11 |
| 6 | Joash, the Abiezrite designation and the start of Gideon's name in 6:11 | Ophrah before that material and the rest of Gideon's activity after it |
| 8 | Gideon's address and אלהים, God, in the 6:13 question | Much of the following question, including the wonders wording |

Thus surviving portions of adjacent lines constrain the shorter sequence.
The angel's arrival is not surviving ink immediately after the cry. The
digital edition and the editor's publication represent the same manuscript,
not two independent Hebrew witnesses. Its palaeographic dating to the third
quarter of the first century BCE is a published assessment, not a fresh dating.
The אלהים/יהוה difference at 6:13 is a separate feature; preferring a shorter
narrative does not automatically select every Qumran word.

Trebolle Barrera's [original 1991 article](https://revistaseug.ugr.es/index.php/meahhebreo/article/download/12910/11133/0),
*MEAH* 40/2, 5–20, prints the transcription on 6 and the reconstruction on 7.
His later discussion on 10–11 reconstructs about 62 letter-spaces per line,
with 61 in his proposed line 3; those are editorial arguments, not independently
measured capacity bounds here. His extrapolation to other missing expansions
in an otherwise lost complete Judges remains a hypothesis. The section heading's
6:10–12 is a reference slip; the discussion identifies the minus as 6:7–10.
The final DJD XIV pages 161–164 and plate XXXVI were identified through
Rezetko's bibliography but were not acquired or inspected in this pass.

## Greek comparison and the repeated cry

Swete I, printed 489–491 / PDF 521–523, gives Vaticanus B as its body and
Alexandrinus A in its apparatus. The own-volume key identifies these sigla;
these are not two freshly transcribed manuscript photographs or a complete
modern Greek apparatus.

The B body retains the prophet and the rebuke but does not repeat the cry in
the same way as Hebrew 6:6–7. Its verse 7 contains the cry with the Midian
qualification; A's reported wording adds the repeated-cry linkage. A also
omits the Egypt/slavery-deliverance block extending across 8–9, while retaining
the prophetic unit. Do not call these uniform longer Greek texts or confuse
the smaller Greek difference with 4Q49's absence of the entire speech.

The Hebrew cry repeats around 6:6–7. That repetition can motivate discussion
of a smaller copying skip; it does not by itself explain losing all of 7–10,
which ends with the voice/obedience clause rather than the same cry ending.
A proposed paragraph skip is a different mechanism and must be evaluated
separately from a word-ending skip.

## Expansion and copying loss

The case for expansion combines the independently identifiable speech unit,
its repeated connection to the cry and its absence in the published Qumran
reconstruction. It explains why the audience hears a rebuke before Gideon
asks why God has abandoned Israel. Smooth continuity without the speech is
compatible with this model but cannot alone prove it.

Rofé's [original 2011 author manuscript](https://www.academia.edu/39113214/Studying_the_Biblical_Text_in_the_Light_of_Historico_Literary_Criticism_The_Reproach_of_the_Prophet_in_Judg_6_7_10_and_4QJudg_a)
agrees that the speech was added to the Gideon story. He argues that the
addition is relatively early, not late Deuteronomistic, and proposes later
accidental loss. His concrete circumstance is the two open-section boundaries
known from Aleppo and Leningrad; he suggests their existence in an ancestor.
The omission could remain unnoticed because the story is coherent without
the added speech. His chronology rests on interpretations of language,
passive conquest, expulsion and commands given after settlement. The proposed
ancestor's layout is not observed, and these literary features do not by
themselves give an absolute date. Do not describe him as denying all growth.

Rezetko, [*JHS* 13 (2013), article 2](https://jhsonline.org/index.php/jhs/article/view/20646),
distinguishes this accident model from Hess's proposed liturgical selection.
His actual discussion on 27–28 contests Rofé's dating through Joshua 24 and
finds no obvious repeated-start/end trigger for the whole omission. His
literary dating is an argument, not an independently dated composition.
Neither the number of agreeing scholars nor a modern edition's choice settles
the transmission event. The local expansion model is preferred provisionally;
the extra loss stage remains possible, not disproved.

## Meaning and POB consequences

Source-only Hebrew controls in Judges 2:1–5 and 10:10–16 confirm related
deliverance, disloyalty and confrontation language. They do not independently
date any of these speeches. The current WLC encoding marks open sections after
6:6 and 6:10, supporting the stated later-layout circumstance without proving
that a lost ancestor had identical divisions.

With the speech, the reader receives an explicit accusation of disobedience
before Gideon's complaint. Without it, the cry leads into the angel's visit
without that intervening human prophet. This is a consequential narrative
difference, not merely an alternative English word. It does not establish a
wholly nonjudgmental earlier book: the retained Hebrew narrative's 6:1 already
links evil conduct and Midianite oppression. 4Q49 does not preserve 6:1, so
that opening cannot be attributed to this copy on the basis of this fragment.

POB currently prints the longer sequence and no 4Q49 disclosure at 6:7.
A justified future note could identify the shorter published Qumran form,
its partly reconstructed junction and debated historical relationship. A
main-text omission requires an explicit literary-form/source choice and exact
connected-source, English and reader-provenance review; it is not approved
by this comparison. No outside English Bible wording was used to draft POB.
No canonical record, note, reader layout, publisher or About edit is applied.
4Q49 is a Judges witness, not evidence for adding a different book to the canon.
