# Jeremiah national oracles and the Hebrew Greek order comparison

Retain POB's declared longer Hebrew arrangement provisionally. The compared
Greek form places the national oracles after 25:13 rather than in chapters
46–51, in a different internal order. Its titles and endings also differ:
moving POB chapters alone would not reproduce that form or recover its lost
Hebrew source. Earliest arrangement remains unresolved.

POB/MT instead orders Egypt, Philistines, Moab, Ammon, Edom, Damascus, Kedar
and Hazor, Elam, Babylon. Babylon ends the national collection there; in the
compared Greek form it is third. That changes the collection's literary
culmination even where individual oracles share content.

Swete's Egypt opening lacks a continuous counterpart of the general nations
title in MT 46:1. The selected Ammon block lacks MT 49:6's restoration ending;
the selected Moab block ends before MT 48:45–47, including its restoration
promise. Longer Moab material appears in the apparatus. These are selected-form
differences, not claims that all Greek witnesses lack those words.

The nine complete opening/closing print pages were inspected with adjacent

## The bridge changes paragraph function and wording

POB 25:13 ends with which Jeremiah prophesied against all the nations, then
25:14's repayment statement and 25:15's cup command. The national collection
comes much later. Swete's selected 25:13 ends at the book clause; the next
unit introduces Elam. A diagnostic rendering of this Greek junction is:
...all that is written in this book. What Jeremiah prophesied concerning the
nations—Elam. Unlike the current Hebrew clause, that Greek introduction has
no explicit all before nations. This is our partial research gloss, not an
adopted translation or a recovered Hebrew sentence.

Swete places the Zedekiah dating clause after the Elam oracle, at 26:1,
before Egypt at 26:2. MT 49:34 places the date before Elam's speech. Reordering
POB's intact verses would miss this split title and changed paragraph function.

Greek transmission is not uniform. On printed 297, the apparatus attributes
an introductory prophetic-nations clause before the cup to A and Q, and the
MT 25:14 repayment material to Qmg. These are not B's continuous text; Qmg
must not become a main-text attribution to Marchalianus.

The [Codex Sinaiticus Project XML](https://codexsinaiticus.org/en/project/transcription_download.aspx)
provides a separately identified published control. Its Elam introduction
follows the book clause at quire 47, folio 8 recto, column 1, lines 43–45.
An article addition there is tagged ca, not an extra original word. Its Elam
date is labelled 25:20, corresponding by content to Swete 26:1. Its cup opening
is labelled 32:15, corresponding to Swete 32:1, at quire 48, folio 4 verso,
column 1, line 17; the consulted running encoding has no separate nations
title before it. The added from-my-hand phrase has its own ca tag. These are
facts about the published encoding, not fresh ink or correction-date judgments.
Do not join all XML word descendants into a single reading: correction branches
would duplicate alternatives. Full NC-SA XML remains private.

## Hebrew fragments do not settle collection placement

The complete [published 4Q72b segment](https://lexicon.qumran-digital.org/transcriptions/4Q72b/2026-05-21/index.html?v=2026-05-21)
has four lines aligned with MT 50:4–6. It preserves some Babylon-oracle
wording, including covenant material in line 3, alongside extensive supplies,
uncertain letters and a raised/deleted correction in line 1. Credit
Qumran-Digital, DFG project 465277421, Qumran-Wörterbuch and the Abegg baseline;
the published transcription is CC BY-SA 4.0. No full transcription is vendored.

The fragment does not preserve where Babylon stood relative to Egypt or Elam.
Neither its reference label nor the six-record Jeremiah discovery index proves
an ancient book order. The old screen's zero hit at 25:13 is likewise only an
anchor query, not proof that Qumran has no national-oracle material. The earlier
[Jeremiah 10 comparison](JEREMIAH_10_LITERARY_FORM_COMPARISON_2026-09-05.md)
already separates surviving Hebrew line ends from reconstructed sequence;
that bounded evidence cannot be transferred to this larger order question.

## Competing histories and decision

[Peels 2018](https://scielo.org.za/scielo.php?pid=S1015-87582018000200010&script=sci_arttext),
sections 2–9, presents both the Greek-priority case and objections. The former
links the collection to 25:13 and sees Hebrew ordering as later thematic
reshaping. His contrary case emphasizes Elam's title/date distribution,
Egypt's introductory function and Babylon's concluding role. He proposes
later reordering with Elam/Persia foregrounded. These are published arguments
and a historical hypothesis, not observed editorial events. Other scholars'
positions in that article remain mediated here.

Our inference: retain both histories. A coherent Egypt-to-Babylon arrangement
could be inherited or constructed later; an awkward Elam title could reflect
either rearrangement or an early form later regularized. Block placement,
internal order and individual title changes need not share one direction.
Early Greek attestation deserves weight, but does not supply the exact lost
Hebrew or decide the collection's earliest order. Removing the modest age
preference leaves the same held priority and provisional POB retention.

Reopen priority for passage-specific modern Hebrew/Greek apparatus or
hand evidence at the bridge/date, or an independently testable early order
attestation or transmission argument distinguishing the directions. The
[official publication list](https://septuaginta.uni-goettingen.de/publications/septuaginta/)
confirms Ziegler's Göttingen XV, third edition 2006; its target apparatus and
the Hebrew University Bible apparatus were not consulted in this pass.
Do not repeat this completed boundary acquisition or infer an optimal book
by moving chapters. A parallel-form presentation and qualified reader note
remain possible separate applications, not changes installed here.
