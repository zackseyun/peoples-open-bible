# Zephaniah 3 17 silence and renewal comparison

Date: October 10, 2026. Baseline:
`330d06e9eed7c301dde580f51338e786daa5ffac`.

## Decision and contribution

Retain the received Hebrew and “he will be quiet in his love” provisionally.
The published Murabbaʿat transcription preserves the silence verb יחריש.
Selected Greek texts instead describe renewal. That contrast matters to the
meaning, but does not establish which wording is earliest or recover a precise
lost Hebrew spelling.

The immediate contribution is a corrected disclosure: the existing note
misclassifies Greek renewal as a less likely interpretation of the Hebrew
silence verb and attaches to “in your midst.” The
[complete candidate](../sources/textual_restoration/candidates/zephaniah3_17_reader.2026-10-10.v1.json)
separates the possible causative interpretation “quiet you” from the Greek
versional alternative “renew you,” and anchors both to the disputed clause.
Marker-free main English and the complete pointed WLC source remain unchanged.
This is not a new scholarly discovery, fresh decipherment, or source adoption.

## Direct Hebrew evidence

The pinned private QDR transcription has scroll Mur88, fragment21, physical
lines13–14. Reading all available lines of that fragment establishes that
earlier supply brackets close before יחריש. That word is unbracketed and
unmarked. In the following אהב#[ ת׳ו sequence, the ending is supplied.
Only the verb is claimed as a published preserved-word reading, not the whole
phrase, vowels, or our own reading of manuscript pixels.

The [Qumran-Digital transcription](https://lexicon.qumran-digital.org/transcriptions/Mur._88/2026-05-21/index.html?v=2026-05-21)
likewise gives column21, line14 as יחריש באהב[תו יגיל עלי]ך.
Its attribution connects the Göttingen dictionary and Martin Abegg precursor.
These publications are not independent manuscript votes. The machine
[comparison record](../sources/textual_restoration/comparisons/zephaniah3_17_silence_renewal.2026-10-10.v1.json)
pins versions, locations, acquisition limits and byte hashes. Restricted
corpora are not vendored into this repository.

The bounded QDR Zephaniah screen finds labels4Q77,4Q78,4Q82 and Mur88, with
only Mur88 returning a3:17 hit. This is not a complete manuscript census.
The actual embedded content inventory in Göttingen’s
[Ra943 catalogue](https://septuaginta.uni-goettingen.de/catalogue/Ra_943/)
reports 8HevXIIgr Zephaniah1:1–6,1:13–18,2:9–10 and3:6–7, not3:17.
It therefore provides no reported target-verb coverage. Catalogue inclusion,
a recently found fragment, or a scan of another passage is not target evidence.

A precise Mur88 copy date is not used here. An author-hosted date lead could
not be inspected because ordinary access failed; certificate validation was
not bypassed. Ancient direct-Hebrew attestation is a substantial objection to
replacement by Greek retroversion, not automatic proof of historical priority.

## Greek and Latin controls

The publisher’s [Rahlfs–Hanhart2006 selected Greek](https://www.die-bibel.de/bibel/LXX/ZEP.3)
reads καὶ καινιεῖ σε ἐν τῇ ἀγαπήσει αὐτοῦ. The actual verb is καινιεῖ,
not ἀνακαινιεῖ. It is a future active third-person singular of καινίζω;
σε explicitly expresses “you.” Renewal is therefore a real difference in
these selected texts, not a normal lexical gloss of חרש.

Swete volumeIII, third edition1905, printed68–69, gives the same clause.
The inspected preface explains its general Vaticanus text/order basis.
This is an edition-mediated control, not a fresh Vaticanus transcription.
Importantly, its apparatus reports εὐφροσύνη for ἀγαπήσει in S*, with
ἀγαπήσει attributed to S correction sigla c·a·b. The reported transmission
is not uniform across the complete love phrase. No separate variant for
καινιεῖ or σε appears in this limited inspected apparatus; that is not a
claim of unanimity or a complete Greek collation.

The full scan is recoverable from its
[Archive source](https://archive.org/download/theoldtestamenti03swetuoft_202003/theoldtestamenti03swetuoft.pdf).
A compact private derivative preserves originalPDF pages7–13 and92–93
(title, edition, complete preface and passage). The Greek acquisition agent
visually inspected all those pages; root visually inspected complete printed
68–69 and the apparatus. The latest Göttingen passage apparatus and physical
Greek manuscript leaves remain unacquired. Adjacent Greek wording and
segmentation around3:17–18 also differ; this comparison does not import that
surrounding Greek passage into the Hebrew.

The publisher’s [Weber–Gryson2007 Latin](https://www.die-bibel.de/bibel/VUL/ZEP.3)
reads silebit in dilectione tua: silence, but “your love,” not “his love.”
It is agreement at the verb level, not complete-phrase agreement, an
independent Hebrew manuscript, or an apparatus-based Latin adjudication.

Greek renewal could reflect a different Hebrew source or interpretive
translation. Hypotheses such as יחדש or יחדשך are not attested Hebrew
readings in the acquired controls. No choice between those hypothetical
spellings is justified here.

## Grammar and strongest contrary case

The actual WLC/OSHB target is יַחֲרִישׁ, lemma2790b, morphologyHVhi3ms,
wordID364LW. It has no expressed object or object suffix. The executed
whole-local-WLC Hiphil screen gives39 wordforms in35 verses, excluding the
plough/devise homonym2790a. That screen locates controls; it is not a semantic
majority vote or independent analysis of all39 occurrences.

Root reads the complete Hebrew and current local English of Exodus14:14,
Isaiah42:14, Psalm50:21 and Job11:3, as well as Zephaniah3:14–20.
The first three demonstrate intransitive silence or divine restraint.
Job11:3 is the strongest causative counterexample: empty talk silences people.
Hiphil therefore does not make “quiet you” impossible. Here that interpretation
supplies an unexpressed object, soothing sense and instrumental ב. The
provisional intransitive retention makes fewer semantic commitments.

[BDB printed361](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Page:A_Hebrew_and_English_Lexicon_(Brown-Driver-Briggs).djvu/385)
distinguishes intransitive usage from causative Job11:3 and places Zephaniah
under the former. This consultation is explicitly unproofread OCR, not a
visually verified lexicon scan. Garbled Hebrew OCR is not used to certify
a conjectured renewal spelling. No fresh HALOT consultation is claimed.

The strongest case for Greek-associated renewal is its coherent positive
action within the restoration passage, where silence between rejoicing
clauses can appear difficult. The strongest objection is actual published
direct-Hebrew יחריש together with a viable Hebrew silence construction.
A difficult clause can be original, corrupt or differently interpreted;
difficulty and theological attractiveness alone do not settle transmission.
Neither the proposed note nor this working retention resolves priority.
