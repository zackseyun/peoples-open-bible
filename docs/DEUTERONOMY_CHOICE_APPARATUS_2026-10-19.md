## Sigla identify objects and editorial intermediaries

The [1906 volume I.1 key](https://tmcdaniel.palmerseminary.edu/Brooke%26McLean/LXX_Brooke%26McLean_1-1.pdf),
pp. ii, vi and viii, identifies m as Holmes–Parsons 72, Oxford Bodleian
Canon. Gr. 35; it is not uppercase M or the unrelated Laud. Gr. 35. It places
16, Florence Laur. V.38, among readings occasionally quoted from
Holmes–Parsons. Angular brackets explicitly mark that intermediary: Cambridge
and Holmes–Parsons are not two Greek copies. The 1911 preface retains the
method while adding authorities; it does not turn every numerical citation
into a new Cambridge collation.

The same key identifies Lr as Robert's edition of the Lyon Octateuch, and
Bohairic superscripts l/w as Lagarde's and Wilkins's editions. Two editions
are not two additional ancient manuscripts or independent Hebrew votes. The
underlying Latin/Coptic target pages were not newly collated here; only the
positive Cambridge reports are accepted. The Lyon control at the mountain
name remains governed by its earlier separate study.

## The date of manuscript 16 must stay qualified

Felix Albrecht's [Göttingen catalogue entry for Ra 16](https://septuaginta.uni-goettingen.de/catalogue/Ra_16/)
maps the object to Florence BML Plut. V 38, Diktyon 15986. It favors circa
1300 and records early-fourteenth-century and later-thirteenth-century
assessments alongside the older eleventh-century attribution. It explains
the archaising minuscule that encouraged an early date. This is a catalogue
judgment, not our new palaeographic result; none of those dates is the age of
the lost exemplar or of the Greek translation. Credit: Albrecht, catalogue
publication May 1, 2024, displayed CC BY-NC-SA 4.0.

## What remains open

The primary apparatus now establishes selected attestation rather than relying
on a modern author's witness count. The evidence is compatible with inherited
בחר wording, contextual translation, later adjustment or a mixed transmission
history. An indicative in Greek or a perfect in Latin does not alone identify
the exact Hebrew verb, its time reference or an original Gerizim sanctuary.
The earlier future-anterior and Qumran-context cautions remain in force.

Schenker's full 2008/2010 originals were not authenticated or acquired after
bounded publisher, institutional and book-preview routes. A blog copy supplied
retrieval leads only, not an authenticated scholarly edition or an accepted
global count. No restricted route was bypassed. This does not establish that
the originals are unavailable everywhere or refute their unread arguments.
