# Coislin 17 and the Ezekiel renewal passage

## Result

Coislin 17 now has verified image locations for selected Ezekiel 36:25–38
lemmas and the following 37:1 opening. This closes a passage-location gap in
our Greek witness investigation. It does not establish a new reading or
decide whether Papyrus 967's shorter text and different order originated in
Hebrew composition, Greek revision or copying. Retain POB's Hebrew and English.

The [Göttingen catalogue entry for Ra 533](https://septuaginta.uni-goettingen.de/catalogue/Ra_533/)
identifies Paris, BnF Coislin 17, Diktyon 49159, as a thirteenth-century Greek
Ezekiel catena with Hexaplaric notes, reportedly used by Montfaucon. This is
catalogue testimony, not a verified identification of a versional note at our
target. The [custodian's viewer](https://gallica.bnf.fr/ark:/12148/btv1b10037903r)
dates the object to 1201–1300 and describes the Ezekiel catena at ff. 6–302v,
preceded by introductory material and followed by a life of Ezekiel. Its
313 numbered digital views are not 313 biblical pages or verse records.
BnF explicitly says the digitization was made from a substitute document.

## What this changes in the comparison

The new-heart/new-spirit passage is now located in another known Greek
manuscript, rather than inferred from its book-level catalogue description.
However, its biblical lemmas alternate with author-labelled explanations.
Labels such as ἄλλως occur within that commentary sequence. They do not by
themselves identify Aquila, Symmachus, Theodotion or a competing biblical
reading. No securely attributed Hexaplaric variant is accepted from this pass.

The distinction matters to the all-source method: a manuscript described as
containing Hexaplaric material cannot supply a versional vote at every verse.
Nor can a medieval copy acquire an ancient author's date merely because a
commentary block bears that author's name. A demonstrated textual relationship
would be needed to use those blocks as earlier quotation evidence.

Against the [P967 transition evidence](EZEKIEL_36_LONGER_FORM_EVIDENCE_2026-10-12.md),
this catena supplies the longer passage's selected lemmas and local 36→37
sequence. It does not discriminate among the proposed origins of P967's minus
or 36→38→39→37 order. It cannot be counted independently of the printed
apparatus until that apparatus's use of this manuscript is checked.

The next useful action is a passage-specific comparison with Montfaucon's
reported use of Coislin 17 or a modern critical apparatus, tracing actual
lemma, author and version labels. Do not repeat these images as another vote,
reconstruct an absent Hebrew text by back-translation, or treat an unobserved
variant as absent throughout the codex. No ImageGen output was used, and no
damaged letter has been restored here.

## Acquisition and verification

Root read the cached primary catalogue entry and BnF's expanded description,
then inspected all five downloaded JPEGs. A separate image reader confirmed
the view-250 lemmas and commentary structure, and the view-252 local transition.
This is a bounded reading check, not independent manuscript ancestry evidence.
The [receipt](../sources/textual_restoration/comparisons/coislin17_ezekiel36.2026-10-16.v1.json)
pins catalogue and image bytes, URLs, and local files. Raw catalogue content
and manuscript images remain outside Git. The catalogue declares CC BY-NC-SA
4.0; BnF labels the object public domain. No third-party content is relicensed
under POB's license.

The ordinary Gallica viewer succeeded after an earlier manifest route failed.
One view-252 download timed out; checking the live viewer and local downloads
established that no file had arrived, then acquisition of the loaded image
succeeded. This is one image input, not two captures. The catalogue's lack of
its own image-viewer permission did not describe access to the linked BnF site.
