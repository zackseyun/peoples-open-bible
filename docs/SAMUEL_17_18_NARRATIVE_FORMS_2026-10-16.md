# First Samuel 17 and 18 narrative forms

## Result and consequence for POB

The David–Goliath comparison must distinguish narrative forms, not just the
giant's height. The consulted Greek edition directly verifies four major
junctions in its Vaticanus-based text and reports longer material in other
witnesses. Its 18:6 transition also changes wording, while 18:28 has both a
different subject and a combined reading in specified witnesses. Deleting
selected verses from POB would not reproduce this Greek narrative.

Direct consultation of DJD XVII p. 80 adds a consequential Hebrew control:
4Q51 fragment 17 preserves small portions assigned to **18:4–5**, inside a
block absent from the B-based Greek text. This is not a complete Hebrew
counterpart to that block. It extends the earlier project's
[17:41 control](SAMUEL_SOURCE_COMPARISON_PASS_1.md#djd-reading-notes-and-provisional-critical-source-application--2026-09-06)
without turning either fragment into evidence for an entire uniform edition.

**Retain the current POB source and English provisionally.** These checks
establish consequential textual alternatives, not their original priority or
an exact earlier Hebrew reconstruction. The applied four-cubit selection is
unchanged and does not commit POB to the shorter narrative. No source, verse,
note, book order or canon status changes in this research pass.

## Printed Greek junctions and apparatus layers

The primary edition is Brooke, McLean and Thackeray,
[*The Old Testament in Greek*, II.1 (1927)](https://tmcdaniel.palmerseminary.edu/Brooke%26McLean/LXX_Brooke%26McLean_2-1.pdf).
Root inspected complete printed pp. 56–64 (PDF 72–80), the Samuel preface v
(PDF 11), and the general conventions in the
[1906 volume](https://tmcdaniel.palmerseminary.edu/Brooke%26McLean/LXX_Brooke%26McLean_1-1.pdf),
preface i–ii (PDF 9–10). The outer-margin B identifies the base manuscript;
the edition does not claim to print a reconstructed original Greek text.
These are published readings and collations, not newly inspected codex photographs.

| Unit | B-based printed sequence | Main apparatus and scope |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 17:11 to 17:32, p. 56 | Saul and Israel fear; David immediately addresses Saul. | `hab 12–31` includes A. The longer material introduces David's family, errand, questions and presentation to Saul. |
| 17:40 to 17:42, p. 59 | David approaches the Philistine; Goliath sees him. | Verse 41 is printed in the apparatus with A among its witnesses. It is not a blank in the edition's B text. |
| 17:49 to 17:51, p. 60 | The stone fells the Philistine; David runs to him and takes his sword. | Verse 50's summary of victory with sling/stone and no sword is printed in the apparatus, including A. |
| 17:54 to 18:6, p. 61 | David deposits the weapons; dancing women come out. | `hab 55–XVIII 5` includes A, followed by `om 55, 56 cx`. Do not assign every verse of the whole block to c and x. |

A here is the edition's Alexandrinus siglum. These four junctions are an
initial control, not a complete list of differences in chapters 17–18.
There is also variation inside numbered verses, including 17:48 and 18:6.
The block-level lists contain internal qualifications; they are not lists of
identical complete texts. The separate bottom apparatus's named-revision
material is not interchangeable with continuous-text manuscript support.
Samuel's preface warns that some lowercase sigla differ from Volume I and
defines b as b′ plus b; no count of independent witnesses follows from letters.

At **18:6**, p. 61, B has the dancers going to meet **David**. Its apparatus
reports a longer opening about the return after defeating the Philistine and
an alternative destination naming Saul. POB follows its retained Hebrew:
the women meet **King Saul**, after the return clauses. Thus the textual
contrast affects whom the celebration greets and how the scene connects to
what precedes; it is not merely a shorter English rendering.

## Hebrew portions inside a longer block

DJD XVII (2005), printed p. 80 / PDF 103, identifies fragment 16 with
17:40–41 and fragment 17 with 18:4–5, both under Plate XIIa. Root inspected
the complete page and enlarged fragment 17's transcription and reading notes.
The latter's image locator is **PAM 43.113* 6.5**, museum inventory 1107;
the locator was read, not the original photograph newly acquired.

Fragment 17's two tiny lines contain portions of the sword expression in
18:4 and Saul plus the succeeding verb in 18:5. The edition explicitly says
the sword word's **het is reconstructed from context**, with only the base
of its right leg preserved; the **waw head** in Saul is preserved; the next
verb's **kaf is damaged by a tear**. Do not turn the normalized word readings
into wholly visible letters. The notes also distinguish variants from
reconstructed variants.

This supports the presence of limited Hebrew material within the larger
17:55–18:5 block absent from the B-based text. It does not attest every word
of 18:4–5, the whole block, or either complete narrative form. The assignment
and readings remain those of the consulted edition, not a fresh physical join
or independent decipherment. In particular, the older four-cubit and 17:41
findings and this fragment cannot be combined into a complete reconstructed
4Q51 chapter without evidence for its missing parts.

## A specific literary argument checked at 18:28

Julio Trebolle's [1990 study, pp. 16–30](https://ccat.sas.upenn.edu/ioscs/bulletin/volumes/bioscs23.pdf)
connects the Michal/Israel alternatives at 18:28 with 18:20 and 18:16 and
interprets combined readings and repeated phrases as editorial joins.
He favors expansion of an earlier shorter edition, while allowing old material
in the longer form and declining to reconstruct two complete parallel stories.
He reports harmonizing abbreviation and a coherent-long-narrative defense as
alternatives; his own 18:1 discussion rejects a later reviser/copyist explanation.

The local evidence can test the proposed join without adopting that historical
conclusion. POB's retained Hebrew at 18:28 names **Michal**. BMT p. 64 prints
**all Israel**. Its main apparatus at the second καί reports the prefix
`και μελχολ η θυγατηρ αυτου` for **b h o c₂ e₂**, with **Chr** and its separate
Saul-name qualification. Because this is a prefix, it retains the Israel
clause: **Michal plus Israel**, not a Michal-only substitution. The A in the
preceding entry belongs to a different addition, “and knew”; it must not be
transferred to this entry. A generic “LXX” or “LXXᴸ” label would hide these details.

Our inference is limited: the apparatus confirms a combined Greek reading
compatible with the literary argument. It does not establish when or in which
language the combination arose. A later Greek conflation of available readings
remains a counter-explanation. Neither coherence nor a compound reading alone
establishes an earlier Hebrew editorial join. The actual witness history and
local translation behavior must discriminate those explanations.

Root read the study's complete extracted text and notes, visually checking
pp. 18–21 and 28–30; a research agent read and visually checked all pp. 16–30.
An attempted Tov author-site route failed certificate validation; no bypass
or full Tov reading is claimed. Trebolle's p. 28 prose and p. 29 table differ
over the 17:48a/b label; use the actual clause wording and apparatus, not that
label alone. The 2020 Chavel–DeGrado study was located only at abstract level,
not consulted as a complete opposing reconstruction.
