# Esther 3:7 lot casting and reader clarification

## Decision and scope

The applied candidate qualifies the lot-casting explanation and moves its note
beside “until the twelfth month.” The Hebrew and marker-free main English are
retained provisionally. The note and three dependent rationales must not imply
that casting necessarily continued from Nisan through eleven elapsed months.
This is a contextual English clarification, not recovered Hebrew wording or
a decision that WLC is the earliest attainable text. The independent scoped
review passed before application; the verification below checks the actual record.

The [earlier Esther comparison](ESTHER_8_9_SOURCE_COMPARISON_2026-10-16.md#connected-calendar-and-the-countermanding-letter)
identified this question at its frozen research baseline. Its source-text,
literary-form and papyrus questions remain separate from this application.

## Source and competing explanations

Root and a delegated source reader checked WLC Esther 3:6–13 and the complete
current POB 3:7 record. Nisan dates the casting; the ending names month twelve,
Adar, without an explicit result verb or chosen day. The decree is already
written on Nisan 13 in 3:12 and specifies the execution date in 3:13. Traversing
candidate calendar dates is therefore distinct from casting across elapsed
months. The absence of an explicit “until” particle alone does not prove that
English “until” is wrong: it can connect the calendar sequence with its named
endpoint. No precise procedure is established by this wording.

The actual commentary pages supply contrasting textual explanations:

- Streane's *The Book of Esther* (1907), printed pp. 21–22 / PDF pp. 54–55,
  interprets the casting as testing candidate dates. Separately, he proposes
  a lost result clause, while acknowledging the difference between his
  proposed day thirteen and Greek fourteen. The interpretation does not
  require adopting his reconstruction.
- Keil's commentary in Sophia Taylor's English translation (1873), printed
  pp. 344–345 / PDF pp. 347–348, explicitly rejects casting that actually
  continued month by month. He considers a lost clause possible but also
  allows a concise Hebrew expression that names the month here and the day
  subsequently. His blanket dismissal of Greek Esther as critical evidence
  is not adopted.

Root and the source reader visually checked both complete target-page pairs,
including footnotes. Root also read the title/imprint text. These are primary
scholarly arguments, not additional ancient witnesses or proof of an original
Hebrew clause. Their source files remain private and are pinned in the
[application contract](../sources/textual_restoration/comparisons/esther3_7_reader_contract.2026-10-16.v1.json).

The [NET translators' own notes](https://classic.net.bible.org/verse.php?book=Est&chapter=3&theme=false&verse=7)
likewise distinguish the interval between determination and execution from the
casting event. Their Greek addition report and their supplied English result
do not establish surviving Hebrew words. This is an interpretive control, not
a vote among modern translations or a fresh Greek collation.

## Reader note and retained main sentence

The applied note reads:

> Here 'until' can describe testing possible calendar dates rather than casting that continued for months; the decree is already written in Nisan (3:12). The Hebrew ending names Adar but gives no selected day.

The marker moves from “first month” to “until the twelfth month”; removing
markers leaves exactly the baseline sentence. The three explanations for the
day sequence, month sequence and month-twelve expression are qualified together.
Their unverified HALOT labels are removed from the revised entries rather
than presented as fresh consultation; the prior entries are archived. All
other lexical entries and original generation provenance remain unchanged.

The strongest objection to retaining the sentence is that unnoted “until” can
still sound like elapsed time. This check does not certify it as the optimal
main rendering. A natural recast remains eligible for a separate source-bound
candidate comparison. Neither thirteen nor fourteen is inserted into the
unchanged-Hebrew main line. Historical source priority remains on hold.
