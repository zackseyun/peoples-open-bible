# Job 24 21 pointing and lexical uncertainty

Retain POB's source and English provisionally. The published segol versus tsere
difference does not establish a different Hebrew root or require a meaning
change. It does reveal a useful distinction between grammatical state and
lexical interpretation. The earlier audit's categorical claim that the feeding
image needs no qualification is not sustained by the lexicon and versional
evidence inspected here. This is a negative source-comparison result and a
research clarification, not a newly discovered reading or a translation revision.

is SHA-256 `7f19541df0f50a03fac0916bfe623364dddb4d96d42a2e0b382ddb941976b356`.
Root read all current Hebrew and English in chapter 24 and the complete target

The August 28 revision replaced “preys on” with “feeds on” and asserted that

Historical model agreement and audit labels remain historical records, not

## Published pointing and grammar

The pinned [UXLC distribution](https://www.tanach.us/) is version 2.5, build
27.6. Its actual `Books/Job.xml` reads `רֹעֵ֣<x>t</x>ה` at 24:21 word 1.
Root extracted both embedded correction records for that exact citation.
Daniel Holman's correction 32, dated June 10, 2023, distributed October 19,
2023, changes segol to tsere. Ben Denckla's correction 90, dated February 5,
2026, distributed April 1, 2026, instead directs examination of tsere versus
segol and adds `t`. The archive defines `t` as uncertain transcription requiring
examination of the Leningrad image. Thus the latest published state is not a
certain newly verified vowel. No fresh codex image was inspected here.

Tsere is consistent with the construct form of the same Qal active masculine
singular participle; segol is the absolute form. Grammatical state can differ
without changing root, stem, voice, gender or number. BDB's participial examples
and [GKC section 116 f and g](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Gesenius%27_Hebrew_Grammar/116._The_Participles)
support the distinction between an absolute participle governing an object and
a construct participle with an objective genitive. Either construction can
relate the action to the following woman. This does not prove that every
pragmatic distinction disappears, but no necessary English change is established
by the vowel contrast alone. It does not turn `רעה` into `רעע` or `הֵרַע`.

## Lexical and Greek controls

Root and one bounded read-only assessor inspected complete native pages
2297–2299 and 2308–2309 of the privately retained
[Hebrew College BDB](https://hebrewcollege.edu/wp-content/uploads/2018/10/BDB.pdf).
These are one-based PDF page numbers in the enhanced edition, not printed page
numbers. Feeding / grazing can extend figuratively to stripping or devastation;
association is a separate lexical possibility. Page 2298 specifically treats
Job 24:21 as difficult and reports Budde's conjecture `הֵרַע`. The harm and
breaking entries do not authorize silently substituting that conjecture for
the transmitted participle. BDB is a scholarly control, not another manuscript.

The [NET publisher note](https://classic.net.bible.org/verse.php?book=Job&chapter=24&verse=21)
objects to deriving predation naturally from the feeding participle. It reports
Gordis's proposed relationship with a geminate crush/break root and a possible Greek
source behind that interpretation. Gordis and Budde were not consulted directly;
these are attributed scholarly proposals, not newly observed Hebrew letters.

Root read the complete official
[Rahlfs–Hanhart 2006 Greek chapter](https://www.die-bibel.de/bibel/LXX/JOB.24).
Verse 21 has `στεῖραν γὰρ οὐκ εὖ ἐποίησεν καὶ γύναιον οὐκ ἠλέησεν`:
approximately, he did not treat the barren woman well and did not pity the woman.
The second noun does not explicitly mean widow. The first clause expresses
negated benefaction, not an explicit feeding image or a uniquely recoverable
Hebrew spelling. A hostile-treatment interpretation is compatible with it;
translator interpretation or parallelism can also explain the rendering.
No full Greek apparatus or separate ancient witness collation was performed.

## Decision and stopping rule

The strongest case for retention is that figurative feeding can express
exploitation and fits the hostile context. The strongest contrary case is the
exact-clause lexical difficulty, the scholarly harm proposals and the Greek
rendering without feeding. A generalized “mistreats” could communicate the
context but would resolve the lexical image rather than establish its source.
Neither modern translation counts nor further model agreement select a root.
Chronological weighting does not settle two digital readings of the same codex.

Retain the labelled WLC source and current English with lexical uncertainty
explicitly recorded here. Do not adopt the conjecture, silently normalize to
UXLC, or certify the August no-note rationale as settled. Reader disclosure and
metadata correction remain unapplied; this report does not make the current
reader communicate that uncertainty. If a disclosure is promoted later, use
the full-record application procedure and supersede/archive the old audit
claims instead of transferring their approval. No candidate preference test
was commissioned because this pass establishes no necessary wording change.

Reopen exact pointing for the relevant codex image; reopen lexical choice for
direct examination of the cited analyses or discriminating passage-specific
apparatus/versional evidence. A second digital copy or another preference vote
is insufficient. The separate Enoch 1:9 / Jude 14–15 reception comparison remains
the next distinct question; it cannot confer canonical authority by quotation.
