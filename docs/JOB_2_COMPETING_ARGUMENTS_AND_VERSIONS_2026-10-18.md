# Job 2 competing arguments and version evidence

## Judgment

Retain the Hebrew **ברך** and POB's contextual main rendering provisionally,
while treating literal blessing as a serious interpretive alternative. The new
evidence does not require different Hebrew letters. The longer Greek speech's
authorship also remains open: two consulted scholars do not agree about it.
Do not turn either a shared citation chain or a sympathetic portrait of the wife
into proof of the source text or its English meaning.

This follow-up preserves the [first comparison](JOB_2_WIFE_SPEECH_COMPARISON_2026-10-18.md)
and [Sinaiticus record](JOB_2_SINAITICUS_CORRECTIONS_2026-10-18.md) unchanged.
No source, main English, reader note, metadata, implementation or canon change
is applied. The [packet](../sources/textual_restoration/comparisons/job2_arguments_versions.2026-10-18.v1.json)
binds the consulted assets, contextual controls and decisions.

## The original argument corrects a secondary attribution

The actual [Fernández Marcos chapter](https://digital.csic.es/handle/10261/405644)
tentatively assigns the wife's expansion to a later hand, while calling the
vocabulary argument inconclusive, at printed page 261. It is not another
endorsement of original-translator authorship. Lier's page 106 attributes such
agreement to page 257, which discusses other matters. Cook's page 276 invokes
Fernández Marcos for the broader shortening question, not this local addition.
Those claims must not be collapsed.

The entire chapter text and notes at printed 251–266 were read. In the acquired
two-page-per-image PDF, the target is PDF 8, right side; printed 267 starts the
next author's chapter. The repository's 251–267 metadata is therefore not the
article boundary. Complete PDF 6, 8 and 11 images were inspected. This resolves
one previously indirect position, not Heater's still-unread target pages.

## Testing the proposed blessing interpretation

[Lier 2018](https://doi.org/10.18820/23099089/actat.v38i2.7) argues for blessing
through repeated wording, an alternating pattern and farewell customs. All
18 article pages, including notes and references, were read; PDF 2 and 10–13
were also inspected visually. The proposal concerns meaning, not recovered ink.

Our independent contextual checks limit how much those arguments establish:

- The WLC blessing-family screen yields six prologue occurrences and three
  later ones. The later group includes a noun at 29:13, not nine identical
  Piel verbs across the book. Speaker, subject, recipient and morphology vary.
- The first five prologue values can be arranged negative, positive, negative,
  positive, negative. Assigning a positive sixth value completes a pattern but
  does not independently establish that pattern as the author's rule.
- The cited farewell parallels do not all have God as the blessing's recipient.
  Their association with impending death alone cannot determine Job 2:9.

| Hebrew control | Actual relation in context | Limit of the analogy |
|---|---|---|
| Genesis 48:19–22 | Jacob blesses the boys, then tells Joseph he is dying | Not an instruction to bless God before dying |
| Genesis 49:27–29 | Their father blesses the sons and gives burial instructions | The recipients are his descendants |
| Deuteronomy 33:1–2 | Moses blesses Israel before his death | Again the people, not God, receive the blessing |
| Job 29:11–15 | A perishing person's blessing comes upon Job in a rescue context | Gratitude directed to a human benefactor, not an explicit deathbed blessing of God |

Job 31:19–22 and 42:10–13 further connect blessing with care for the needy and
God's restoration of Job. These links matter literarily, but they do not erase
the local question and rebuke at 2:9–10. A compassionate account of the wife's
motives is compatible with more than one interpretation of her words.

Keeping an ambiguous source lexeme in English is also not automatically keeping
its communicative effect: ordinary English blessing language may convey only
approval unless its possible indirect force is explained. Conversely, rendering
the contextual sense can obscure the repeated blessing vocabulary. POB's present
literal-form note acknowledges that trade-off; it does not settle every reading.

## Direct Aramaic and Syriac controls

The actual [Rabbinic Targum text](https://www.sefaria.org/Aramaic_Targum_to_Job.2.9?lang=he)
was read through both prologue chapters using Sefaria's `he` field, labelled
Mikraot Gedolot. Its metadata does not specify a particular printed exemplar.
It is Aramaic in Hebrew script, not the Qumran Targum and not a dated ancient
manuscript transcription.

The [Syriac chapters](https://www.peshitta.eu/ot/job/2.html) were likewise read
directly, not through a modern English Peshitta translation. The site's
[source statement](https://www.peshitta.eu/about.html) identifies the OT as the
Syriac Orthodox Patriarchate's 2020 publication. This is a named edition control,
not a critical manuscript census or direct dating evidence. Short extracts in
the table remove combining points only; the private snapshots retain them.

| Unit | Targum wording | Syriac wording |
|---|---|---|
| 1:5 | וארגיזו, negative provoking language | ܘܨܚܝܘ, reviling language |
| 1:10 | ברכתא, blessing | ܒܪܟܬ, blessing |
| 1:11 | ירגזנך, provoking language | ܢܨܚܝܟ, reviling language |
| 1:21 | מברך, blessing | ܡܒܪܟ, blessing |
| 2:5 | ירגזינך, provoking language | ܢܨܚܝܟ, reviling language |
| 2:9 | בריך מימרא דיי ומית, blessing the divine Memra | ܨܚܐ ܠܐܠܗܟ: ܘܡܘܬ., reviling one's God and dying |

The Targum selectively preserves blessing language for the wife, rather than
retaining it mechanically in every negative context. That strengthens the
case for considering a distinct interpretation here. It still does not exclude
indirect or ironic force. The Syriac explicitly chooses a negative verb in all
four disputed prologue units while retaining positive blessing elsewhere.
Neither pattern uniquely recovers a different Hebrew spelling.

The response matters too. At 2:10 the Targum adds a qualification about Job's
thoughts after denying sin with his lips; the Syriac additionally denies
blasphemy. These are versional interpretation, not extra Hebrew words to splice
into POB. The Targum's naming of the wife as Dinah is likewise not newly
recovered Hebrew identity evidence. The Greek's separate expanded scene remains
distinct from these short forms. The Latin claim in Lier remains secondary here;
no Latin manuscript or critical edition was collated in this follow-up.

## Consequences for the method

Check the exact primary passage before promoting a secondary attribution. Broad
agreement about translation technique does not imply agreement about a local
addition. For versional evidence, compare how the same edition handles positive
and negative controls, not just its isolated target word. Keep literary pattern,
pragmatic interpretation, physical source wording and historical priority
separate. The next Greek discriminator remains the actual modern apparatus or
Heater's target study through new lawful access, not another citation vote.
