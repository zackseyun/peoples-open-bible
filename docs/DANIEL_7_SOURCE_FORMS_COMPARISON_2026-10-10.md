The publisher OG's comparative clause differs meaningfully from the Aramaic
destination. It permits a closer relation between the figures, but comparison
does not itself prove identification; subject continuity and referent remain
interpretive questions. The frozen contract's B shorthand favors a continued
subject reading more than syntax alone warrants. The assessment preserves this
independent correction without changing the frozen contract.

NETS exposes a further editorial contrast, not another ancient witness vote.
Its introduction distinguishes revised OG from reprinted Theodotion and warns
that their relationship varies across the book. Do not import an English
rendering into the publisher's Greek or call every OG witness the same form.
Likewise, attendants being present is not identical to attendants bringing
the figure. The OG service participle agrees with the glory clause; importing
the Aramaic peoples/nations/languages syntax erases that difference.

## Transmission argument and stopping rule

The strongest countercase to retaining Aramaic is that a late surviving copy
may preserve regularization while early Greek preserves an older scene.
Conversely, interpretation or intra-Greek change may produce the comparison;
correction toward Aramaic or Theodotion may produce an endpoint within OG.
None is locally demonstrated. The inspected differences include case and
construction; do not declare a proven one-letter error or invent a motive.

Chronology makes P967 worth investigating, not automatically victorious.
Removing the modest age preference leaves the same provisional retention
and hold. Source-language primacy likewise does not establish Masoretic
historical priority. No exact competing Aramaic spelling was acquired.

Reopen source priority with decisive apparatus units/conventions and dependency
controls, Geißen's complete surrounding transcription preserving supplies and
corrections, usable target pixels where required, or a discriminating direct
Aramaic witness/full transmission reassessment. Repeating a judge to obtain
agreement is not new evidence.
