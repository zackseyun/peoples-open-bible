# Jeremiah 27 heading review and decision

The comparison identifies a meaning-bearing alternative, but the present evidence does not justify replacing POB's Jehoiakim with Zedekiah. Zedekiah remains a plausible conditional correction within the longer Hebrew form. Historical priority and the separate choice between heading-containing and shorter forms remain unresolved. Canonical Hebrew and English are unchanged.

This is the current disposition. The [original proposal](JEREMIAH_27_1_HEADING_COMPARISON_2026-10-10.md) and its v1 machine record are retained as reviewed history, including the spelling error described below. The [corrected v2 comparison](../sources/textual_restoration/comparisons/jeremiah27_1_heading.2026-10-10.v2.json) supersedes v1 for current claims.

## The independent review corrected the attested spelling

The independent critic inspected the complete de Rossi printed pages 98–99 and confirmed that the reported Hebrew name is **צדקיה**, without a final waw. The original proposal recorded **צדקיהו**. Both denote Zedekiah, but the longer spelling cannot be attributed to these inspected pages. A normalized editorial expansion is not an exact witness reading. [De Rossi volume III](https://archive.org/details/VariaeLectiones/Variae%20Lectiones%203/page/n112/mode/2up).

The reviewer also challenged the proposed moderate ranking. Agreement between the chapter context, published Hebrew reports and selected Syriac may reflect preservation of the earlier name, but it may also reflect later contextual harmonization. That causal ambiguity is not resolved by assigning more agents or counting editions. The root withdrew the moderate ranking and retained Zedekiah as a plausible alternative without canonical promotion.

## Evidence that affects the choice

The contextual case is substantial: 27:3 and 27:12 name Zedekiah, 27:20 presupposes an exile, and 28:1 continues into Zedekiah's reign. A similar Jehoiakim heading in 26:1 gives a concrete route for intrusion. Conversely, compositional staging can explain an earlier heading alongside a later enactment. Context alone cannot choose the earlier textual state.

Published Hebrew attestation is more useful than treating Zedekiah as a wholly unattested conjecture, but reported text, initial and marginal readings must remain distinct. Their manuscripts and relevant hands have not been freshly collated or reliably dated here. Manuscript body dates cannot date marginal corrections.

The [4Q72 fragment transcription](https://lexicon.qumran-digital.org/transcriptions/4Q72/2026-05-21/index.html?v=2026-05-21) contains limited heading-tail or lead-in material, not either royal name. It cannot break the tie. Related public and private transcription streams are not independent copies, and supplied parallel text is not surviving ink.

The selected Syriac control supports Zedekiah, but CAL's [edition description](https://cal.huc.edu/get_file_info.php?coord=62013) specifies a Leiden-derived text with some manuscript 7a1 corrections. It does not certify Syriac unanimity. The selected Greek boundary lacks the heading, while [Swete's page 302 apparatus](https://archive.org/download/theoldtestamenti03swetuoft_202003/theoldtestamenti03swetuoft_jp2.zip/theoldtestamenti03swetuoft_jp2%2Ftheoldtestamenti03swetuoft_0325.jp2) reports a Jehoiakim heading in a Greek margin. “All Greek omits” would therefore be false. The marginal label alone does not date the individual hand.

## What counts as a valid contribution

This packet contributes a corrected, auditable comparison and prevents an unsupported promotion. It does not contribute a newly recovered original reading or an applied English change. The focused tests protect provenance, historical baselines and claim boundaries; their initial pass did not catch the scholarly spelling error. Passing software checks is not textual adjudication.

The next useful evidence would verify the reported Hebrew manuscript states and hands, consult a modern local apparatus, clarify the Syriac variants, or examine the Greek marginal and translation evidence. Repeating the same judgment with more tokens is not a substitute. If that evidence supports adoption, a complete critical-source candidate must identify editorial pointing, preserve prior records and pass matching English, source-selection, schema and export checks before application.

Only one independent source review was commissioned. Its correction and disagreement are preserved; it was not rerun until agreement. No English preference vote was needed for this unresolved source choice. This case has no bearing on admitting additional works into a biblical canon: textual readings and canonical authority are different questions.
