# Ezra and 1 Esdras on wives and children

Ezra 10:44 and 1 Esdras 9:36 preserve materially different endings: the
former has a difficult clause interpreted as childbirth; the latter explicitly
reports dismissal with children. Keep the distinct POB texts provisionally.
Neither clearer Greek nor difficult Hebrew establishes the earlier wording.
This comparison also corrects an unsupported source-history assertion in the
alignment documentation and identifies a separate chapter-ending import issue.
No verse, note, approval, reader layout or canon status changes here.

## Connected source comparison

The comparison reads source and main English in 77 current records: Ezra
9:1–15 and 10:1–19,42–44; Nehemiah 13:23–31; and 1 Esdras 8:84–92 and
9:1–21,36. Both complete target records were read. The WLC XML separates
Ezra 10:44's opening written/read forms from the final clause; the opening
qere does not supply a different dismissal verb at the end.

| Unit | Observed wording | Consequence |
|---|---|---|
| Ezra 10:44 | ויש מהם נשים וישימו בנים underlies POB's interpretation that some marriages had children. | The construction and the childbirth sense are difficult; fluent English is not proof of an uncomplicated Hebrew idiom. |
| Esdras B 10:44 | Swete prints καὶ ἐγέννησαν ἐξ αὐτῶν υἱούς, a birth statement. | This supports an old birth interpretation, but is not a word-for-word equivalent of every Hebrew element, including “some.” |
| Esdras A 9:36 | καὶ ἀπέλυσαν αὐτὰς σὺν τέκνοις explicitly dismisses the women with children. | Restoring this sense in Hebrew Ezra would change what its final sentence expressly reports, not merely improve its style. |
| Ezra 10:3,5,16–19 and the Greek contexts | Proposed removal includes offspring, the assembly swears and investigates, and the named priests pledge removal. | Distinguish proposal, commitment, process and final report. Scope beyond the priests is an interpretive question, not a new Hebrew verb. |

Swete II's complete printed pp.158–160 and179–182 / PDF176–178 and197–200
were viewed with the relevant apparatus. At A9:36 it records an Alexandrinus
first-clause variant, not a reported replacement of the dismissal clause;
no B10:44 variant is listed on the inspected page. These limited edition
reports are not universal manuscript agreement or a modern critical collation.

## Opposing original arguments

[Oswald2012](https://jhsonline.org/index.php/jhs/article/download/18388/14373/42940),
printed p.2 n.4, prefers a dismissal emendation based on 1 Esdras, following
Rudolph. His inference that the wider narrative implies comprehensive removal
does not itself establish the proposed Hebrew wording. His reports of
Talshir, Williamson and Eskenazi are not newly consulted originals here.
Root read title/PDF1 and printed pp.1–4/PDF2–5, and viewed complete p.2/PDF3;
the remaining article was not audited.

[Keil1873](https://biblicalstudies.org.uk/pdf/e-books/keil-delitzsch/ezra-nehemiah-esther_keil-delitzsch.pdf),
printed pp.134–135, instead retains the difficult ending. He assigns the
masculine forms to the women, explains their gender as late usage, and treats
the birth notice as emphasizing the painful thoroughness of separation.
He judges Esdras A a contextual conjecture. These pages supply neither a
named manuscript collation nor a parallel establishing the childbirth sense
of ישימו; his transmission explanation remains an argument, not an observed
editing event. Root and the scout read both complete pages as text and images.

[Széchy2009](https://etheses.dur.ac.uk/1/1/Ezra_9-10_PhD.pdf), printed p.179 n.308
and235 n.415, distinguishes MT's childbirth statement from Esdras A's explicit
dismissal while still inferring intended removal from the wider story.
Root read and viewed both complete pages, not the complete thesis. Her
comparison uses modern translations, not an independent manuscript apparatus.
Nehemiah's later marriage problem does not alone prove that Ezra's action
failed, never happened, or had a different source ending.

## Correct the source assumption and preserve the import problem

The alignment JSON and README asserted that 1 Esdras was composed in Greek
and had no Hebrew original. Wooden's complete NETS introduction, printed
pp.392–393, expressly leaves the source relationship open. Replace the
categorical assertion with that uncertainty; WLC parallels remain parallels,
not an attested Esdras A Vorlage. No alignment row or executable lookup changes.
