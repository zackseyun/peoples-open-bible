## Meaning and exact explanatory treatment

The opening two commands and all trees are covered by Dillmann cols. 1245–46, 297–98 and 1025 and, separately, by Charles's Greek printed p.7, whose text stops here before the reported gap; it does not support the later clauses. Dillmann col. 298 specifically cites Enoch 3 for appearance/resemblance, col. 1071 specifically cites Enoch 3 under the dry participle, and col. 826 supports a comparative connective without correlative (col. 828 also permits content clauses). “How they appear as though dry” therefore has positive verse-specific evidence; this does not assert the trees were not dry or exclude the content-clause construal.

Foliage is grounded in col. 472. Col. 696 separately gives both the participle's fallen/shed sense and the derived stem's own-leaves-shedding or foliage-stripped sense, each with an Enoch 3 citation. Thus the English may distinguish the general fallen foliage from the excepted trees that do not shed theirs, without adding a species, an evergreen claim, or a source noun at W14. The actual selected count is fourteen; apparatus27 reports variants, not a changed body. Flemming 1902 pp.2–3 has a related but distinct selected text, including a separately spelled count, W15 form and a different W20 initial family; it is not used to replace Charles's body.

The old/new pairing likely refers to foliage: both Dillmann col. 491 (old) and 129 (new) cite Enoch 3 of leaves. The selected text does not restate the noun, so the English leaves it implicit. Col. 1284 supports remain/wait, and cols. 191–94 admit source/time uses of em. Actual col. 834 expressly cites Enoch 3 in sense 3 **annus** (“year”), separately from sense 2 rainy season/winter; this materially supports “years,” not merely on comparative grounds. The OCP edited Aramaic parallels likewise contain two and three; 4Q201's years continuation is bracket-restored, and 4Q204's years has marked uncertain letters. Neither establishes the attachment of the two em phrases in Geʿez. Grammar §191 pp.487–488 supports a singular numbered noun and a standalone numeral, not the proposed scope. Therefore render the repeated “from two and from three years” conservatively and do not replace the text with “for two or three years,” “until two or three years have passed,” or an added botanical mechanism. The exact attachment remains disclosed, not ignored, and no printed lacuna is alleged.
