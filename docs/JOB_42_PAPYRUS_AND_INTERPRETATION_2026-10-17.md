# The Job ending in the original papyrus edition

## Result for POB

The original P.Oxy. 3522 edition does not establish omission of Job 42:17a,
the Greek resurrection sentence. Its tiny second-column remnant has several
possible placements, not a securely recovered ending. The provisional Hebrew
base and existing Greek-ending note remain unchanged. This is a new consultation
of an old edition, not a newly discovered witness or restored source text.

This follow-up supersedes the access gaps, not the frozen evidence pins, in
the [original comparison](JOB_42_GREEK_ENDING_COMPARISON_2026-10-17.md) and
[reader application](JOB_42_READER_DISCLOSURE_2026-10-17.md). The

## What Parsons actually publishes

P. J. Parsons, “3522. LXX Job 42:11–12,” in *The Oxyrhynchus Papyri* L
(1983), printed 1–3 and plate I, preserves part of a first column at 42:11–12
and a thin remnant of another column. His certain starting point there is the
second line's three letters, σαν. The first line admits beta or kappa, with
kappa preferred; the third has ambiguous traces. These are the editor's
readings, not new letters independently certified from the reproduced plate.

| Proposed placement of column II | Missing lines below column I | Reconstructed column | Written height / total height |
| --- | ---: | ---: | --- |
| 42:15 | 6 | 15 lines | about 9 / 14 cm |
| 42:17c | about 30 | 39 lines | about 24 / 29 cm |
| 42:17d | about 37 | 46 lines | about 27.5 / 32.5 cm |

These dimensions follow from the proposed alignments; they are not separately
measured complete columns. The first proposal needs an otherwise unrecorded
third-line reading. The second needs long lines, an exceptionally long second
line and a doubtful beta. The third also needs a long first line, shortened
only through an alternative opening or an assumed scribal omission. Parsons
does not choose a secure placement. No numerical confidence follows from
these competing fits.

Printed 3 says the last two proposals assume 16³ and 17 were present. Those
references must not silently become 17a. The editor's judgment about the
earliest Greek translation is distinct from the fragment's surviving letters.
Plate I supplies neither a complete second column nor a demonstrated physical
book-ending. It therefore establishes neither omission nor complete presence
of the resurrection and genealogical continuation. The proposed early first-century
AD date is palaeographic, not a laboratory date or automatic proof of the
fragment's religious community.

The complete conventions distinguish loss brackets and doubtful letters from
preserved wording; modern accents and punctuation are editorial. Reconstructed
lines in the notes cannot be counted as surviving text. Parsons, Kraft's
discussion and the photographed papyrus are not three independent witnesses.

## Häner on interpretation and canon

The complete author-posted text of Tobias Häner's 2019 article, printed 34–49,
treats 17a and 17b–e as interpretive additions, assuming their later placement
rather than establishing it from a newly dated witness. He considers scriptural
parallels but does not securely identify the cited Syriac book; Jubilees lacks
a diagnostic link. His stable-text and canonization argument is a historical
inference, not a canonical ruling. This supports keeping attestation,
interpretation and reception separate; it does not supply lost Hebrew or
justify adding a book to a canon.
([Author text](https://www.academia.edu/123142024/The_Exegetical_Function_of_the_Additions_to_Old_Greek_Job_42_17a_e_),
[official record](https://uscholar.univie.ac.at/detail/o:1078926),
[DOI](https://doi.org/10.2143/BIB.100.1.3286048))
