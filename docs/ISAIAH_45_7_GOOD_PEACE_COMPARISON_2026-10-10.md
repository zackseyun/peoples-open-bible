# Isaiah 45 7 good and peace source comparison

The Great Isaiah Scroll's published Hebrew reads “making good” where the
Masoretic text underlying POB reads “making peace.” This changes the positive
side of the verse's contrast, not its attribution of the adverse side to
Yahweh. **Retain the declared Masoretic base provisionally and hold historical
priority.** The known variant warrants reader disclosure, but neither the
scroll's age nor a preferred theological interpretation establishes priority.
This comparison does not change the canonical record.

## Competing source explanations

Good and bad make a familiar broad antithesis parallel to light and darkness.
That makes טוב intelligible and consequential, but also gives a plausible
counter-explanation: a copyist could simplify the less symmetrical peace/bad
pair to good/bad. Conversely, peace could be an interpretive specification of
a broader good. These are competing editorial hypotheses, not observed
scribal motives or demonstrated copying events. The readings are different
lexemes, not alternate vowel pointings or an established one-letter error.

The inspected Greek and Latin retain peace and provide contrary versional
evidence to the direct good reading. Their shared sense does not demonstrate
the exact Hebrew exemplar, independence or manuscript unanimity. The two
fragmentary Hebrew peace displays add no surviving decisive letters. The
method's modest preference for older physical evidence gives the Great Isaiah
Scroll serious consideration; removing that preference still leaves this
pass unresolved. Operational base retention is not proof of Masoretic priority.
Reopen for discriminating local copying controls, target commentary addressing
the direction of this change, or additional preserved witness evidence.

## Biblical wording and later liturgical wording

The actual [HUBP preview](https://www.magnespress.co.il/api/magnes/book/3285/preview),
native p.3, printed Hebrew p.רו, displays `שלום] Is-a טוב` in apparatus II.
The [official edition explanation](https://bibleproject.huji.ac.il/%D7%94%D7%9E%D7%94%D7%93%D7%95%D7%A8%D7%94-0)
places scrolls and rabbinic quotations together in this apparatus. The acquired
pages lack a siglum legend; this pass does not claim an independently verified
HUBP identification of Is-a. The actual named Qumran Digital main text above
independently establishes the Great Isaiah Scroll's good reading.

The adjacent apparatus reports a Yalkut hermeneutical variant at the adverse
term, and its English note associates a change with the morning liturgy.
This is not another direct biblical scroll reading. In the actual
[Berakhot11b Hebrew/Aramaic text, segment4](https://www.sefaria.org/api/texts/Berakhot.11b?context=0),
the discussion explicitly distinguishes written רע from recited הכל and
calls the latter improved/euphemistic language. This is reception and liturgy,
not proof that Isaiah's source should say “all.” The displayed English at
segment1 expands the short original-language quotation; it cannot supply
additional original-language words. No theological smoothing or canon change
follows from these reception controls.

## Consequence for POB English

[Current POB](../translation/ot/isaiah/045/007.yaml) says “making peace and
creating calamity.” The scroll alternative would broaden the positive side
to “making good.” It does not remove רע. The selected Greek and Latin adverse
terms likewise do not alone settle moral evil versus calamity in English.
The context of 45:5–9 concerns Yahweh's exclusive divine agency and creative
action; a desired theological outcome cannot decide either the Hebrew reading
or the gloss of רע. The present record already lists evil as an alternative,
but its note is misplaced at darkness[a] rather than calamity.

The next separate reader comparison must test peace versus well-being against
the retained שָׁלוֹם, calibrate the adverse-term explanation without declaring
that theology rules out moral evil, disclose טוב as a different source reading,
and repair the adverse-term anchor. Source substitution and English rendering
must remain separate decisions. Existing high-agreement scores do not certify
that future candidate. The stale LORD lexical label also does not match current
Yahweh main English; no unrelated divine-name rationale receives approval here.
