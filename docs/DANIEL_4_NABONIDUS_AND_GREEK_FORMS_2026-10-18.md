# Daniel 4 Greek forms and the Prayer of Nabonidus

## Judgment

Daniel's royal-affliction story survives in substantially different Aramaic
and Greek forms. The Prayer of Nabonidus supplies a related Aramaic composition,
not a newly recovered Daniel chapter. Compare them separately; do not merge
them into an allegedly original Hebrew text. Daniel's source here is Aramaic.
Retain POB's source and main English provisionally. This comparison establishes
consequential differences and limits on historical arguments, not their earliest
form, new letters, an English application or a canon addition.

The principal contribution is a connected comparison of agency, punishment,
restoration and proclamation, with direct Qumran preservation controls and an
actual Papyrus 967 collation. It is distinct from the completed
[Daniel 7 comparison](DANIEL_7_SOURCE_FORMS_COMPARISON_2026-10-10.md).

## Numbering and the Greek editions

POB and WLC place the opening proclamation at 3:31–33 and the dream narrative
at 4:1–34. The publisher's [Rahlfs–Hanhart Old Greek](https://www.die-bibel.de/en/bible/LXX/DAG.4)
and [alternative Theodotion text](https://www.die-bibel.de/bibel/LXXA/DAG.4)
use the familiar English-equivalent 4:1–37 numbering. Thus WLC 4:5–6 is
publisher 4:8–9, not publisher 4:5–6.

Swete III (1905), printed pp. 526–534 / PDF 550–558, alternates the two Greek
forms and gives both numberings. Its Chigi-based Old Greek predates Papyrus
967's discovery; its selected wording is not the modern reconstructed Old Greek.
Swete prints an opening proclamation at Greek 3:98–100 and a related expanded
ending. The modern publisher Old Greek starts at 4:4, retaining the proclamation
in the expanded ending at 37c. Swete also places its 4:9 before 4:8; the modern
publisher orders the corresponding 11 before 12. These are edition-specific
observations, not evidence that every Greek copy has the same arrangement.

The electronic Swete Old Greek chapter-4 container also encloses the short
chapter-5 preface. The actual printed V heading, not that XML boundary,
controls this comparison. Bel and the Dragon XML initially retrieved under a
neighboring work identifier was identified by its title and excluded.

## Consequential narrative differences

These observations come from the complete Aramaic source unit and both Greek
forms, with Swete's actual printed pages controlling its electronic reproduction.
References in this table use publisher/English-equivalent numbering.

| Unit | Aramaic form | Old Greek selected form |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 4:4–18 | Failed sages and Daniel's credentials interrupt the dream account | Eighteenth-year setting; enlarged tree/destruction scene; Daniel called the following morning |
| 4:22–27 | King's greatness, announced expulsion, and counsel about wrongdoing and mercy to the poor | Explicit destruction of the living God's house, angelic pursuit, prison and punishment; counsel includes supplication |
| 4:31–33 | Kingship withdrawn and seven periods of animal-like life | Explicit replacement by a despised person of the household, repeated seven years, binding and another's enjoyment of the kingdom |
| 4:33–37c | Reason returns, praise and kingship restored | Extended prayer about sins and ignorance, angelic summons, sacrifices and circular proclamation, including a death threat against opponents of heaven's God |

The expanded ending is not simply a gentler conversion story. Nor does greater
length alone make it later. Theodotion generally tracks the Aramaic narrative
more closely here but is not identical: its animal comparisons and holy-spirit
wording must not silently be normalized to the Aramaic. None of these differences
establishes an exactly recoverable lost Aramaic sentence behind the Old Greek.

## Direct Daniel fragments and the early Greek witness

The [current 4Q115 transcription](https://lexicon.qumran-digital.org/transcriptions/4Q115/2026-10-06/index.html#c363684-i363684)
has sixteen displayed rows for fragments 3–7, numbered 1–7 and 11–19. Rows
1–3 preserve parts of Belteshazzar's name and the end of the no-secret clause
aligned to WLC 4:5–6. The chief-magicians title, spirit formula and much of the
request are supplied. Row 4 has unidentified traces, not a secure surviving
4:7 vision opening. Row 16, including Nebuchadnezzar's name, is wholly supplied.
This is partial ancient Aramaic support for introduction wording, not four
intact verses or a secure reconstruction of the dream's transition.

In [4Q112 fragment 8](https://lexicon.qumran-digital.org/transcriptions/4Q112/2026-10-06/index.html#c361862-i361862),
the only Daniel 4 rows are 17–18, aligned to WLC 4:29–30. They preserve small
portions of the sovereignty/grass wording, not the court introduction;
Nebuchadnezzar's name is supplied. Adjacent Masoretic parallel displays are
navigation aids, not surviving words of either fragment. Qumran-Digital's
transcriptions derive from its Qumran-Wörterbuch/Abegg lineage and are supplied
under CC BY-SA 4.0; release dates are not new manuscript dates.

The [Cologne catalogue](https://papyri.uni-koeln.de/features/rahlfs) dates
Papyrus 967 approximately to 200 CE and identifies Hamm's 1977 edition for
Daniel 3–4. Acquired images PT19v, PT20r and PT20v concern codex pages
146–148 and surviving lower portions, not three complete chapter copies.
The scout inspected their fragmentary layout; no new letter reading is accepted.

Tim McLay's [1995 collation](https://openscholar.huji.ac.il/sites/default/files/he_bible_project/files/t.mclay_.pdf),
*Textus* 18, pp. 121–134, compares published Papyrus 967 readings with Ziegler
1954. Its actual key and target pp. 129–130 report alternatives including
ὤφθη / ὑψώθη at 4:19(22), ἤκουσε / ἠκούσθη at 4:28(31), and
ἐπεδήθην / ἐδόθην at 30a. Its square brackets mark letters or words omitted
in 967, not editorial supplies of missing ink. This is useful local critical
evidence, not a complete modern Göttingen apparatus or another physical witness.

## What the Prayer of Nabonidus preserves

The complete [4Q242 versioned transcription](https://lexicon.qumran-digital.org/transcriptions/4Q242/2026-10-06/index.html)
has nine rows for fragments 1–3 and five for fragment 4. It preserves נבני,
identified with Nabonidus, severe affliction, seven years, בתימן, a Jewish
diviner and imperatives to declare and write. It does not preserve the name
Daniel, madness or animal behavior. The exile affiliation, speech-introduction
bridge, full Most High title and bronze/iron in the gods list cross supplied
gaps. These must not become independent agreements with Daniel.

The [IAA record](https://www.deadseascrolls.org.il/explore-the-archive/manuscript/4Q242-1?locale=en_US)
identifies an Aramaic parchment composition and points to Collins, DJD 22
(1996), pp. 83–93. Its thirteen photographic records are not thirteen witnesses.
The three downloaded public images have a maximum dimension of only 512 pixels;
they do not enable fine-letter restoration. The catalogue's non-biblical
classification is not, by itself, an ancient community's canon declaration.

Christian Stadel, [2024](https://czasopisma.kul.pl/index.php/ba/article/view/17101),
*The Biblical Annals* 14/4, pp. 637–649, challenges the usual parsing of the
six surviving words in line 4. His preferred construction attaches וחטאי to
the preceding clause and takes the diviner as subject of שבק לה, followed
by the clause identifying him as Jewish. This avoids an unusual parenthetical
construction but retains an unusual analytic object expression. His restoration
of line 3 is explicitly hypothetical. The argument makes agency genuinely
disputed; neither divine nor human forgiveness is a photograph-level fact.
Only the introduction and complete first case study, pp. 637–643, were read,
not the whole article or an independently replicated Aramaic corpus survey.

## Literary relationship and historical priority

McLay's [author-posted 2005 argument](https://timmclay.com/articles-to-read/old-greek-translation-of-daniel-chapters-4-6-and-the-book-of-daniel/)
favors an earlier separate collection/translation of tales while challenging
linear derivation of Masoretic Daniel from the Old Greek ancestor and automatic
priority of 967's chapter order. He specifically criticizes reconstruction of
4QDanielᵈ's largely missing transition. This is his explanatory model, not a
result established by the present comparison. The full author HTML reproduction
was read; its damaged font encoding and absent full printed-note apparatus do
not substitute for consultation of the published article's complete layout.

Andrew Steinmann's [2002 contrary study](https://ctsfw.net/media/pdfs/steinmannthechickenandtheegg.pdf),
*Revue de Qumrân* 20/4, pp. 557–570, argues that the prayer draws vocabulary
and motifs from Daniel 2–5 rather than preserving Daniel 4's antecedent. He
acknowledges that this is not proof and does not exclude a historical Nabonidus
tradition behind Daniel. The complete article and notes were text-read, with
its transcription, material-list argument and conclusion inspected in renders.
His material-list locator 5:24 should be 5:23 in the local Aramaic; the common
order contrast with 5:4 remains. His fragment counts, lexical exclusivity and
theological-direction argument were not independently reproduced as statistics.

Our assessment is that shared motifs justify a literary relationship question,
not a demonstrated direction of copying. Surviving-copy dates do not date the
composition of either form. A material list can support comparison without
uniquely establishing its donor; the partly supplied list cannot be counted as
a wholly preserved phrase. Likewise, a hypothesized theological correction is
not a dated event. The contrasting kings and afflictions must remain visible.
Do not replace Nebuchadnezzar with Nabonidus or construct an eclectic story
from whichever detail seems historically or theologically preferable.
