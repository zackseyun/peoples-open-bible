# Ezekiel 4 numerical differences and reader notes

The applied reader notes disclose the numerical differences between the
retained Hebrew and Swete's Greek edition at Ezekiel 4:4, 4:5 and 4:9. Six
records, 4:4–9, also receive corrected semantic note anchors. Every source
character and every word of the main English remain unchanged. This improves
readers' access to consequential evidence without claiming a recovered original
number.

## Evidence and decision

The [source comparison](EZEKIEL_4_NUMBERS_AND_FRAGMENT_ATTRIBUTION_2026-10-16.md#direct-greek-edition-control)
records direct visual consultation of Swete III, third edition (1905), printed
390–391, including the apparatus. The notes name the edition and its Vaticanus
basis; they do not present a fresh transcription of that manuscript or a
unanimous Greek reading.

| Verse | Retained Hebrew | Swete main Greek | Reader consequence |
|---|---|---|---|
| 4:4 | No numeral | 150 days | Add a note at “number of the days” |
| 4:5 | 390 days | 190 days | Add a note at the numeral phrase, cross-referencing 4:9 |
| 4:6 | 40 days | 40 days | Retain the number; correct the day/year note anchor |
| 4:9 | 390 days | 190 days | Add a matching note, cross-referencing 4:5 |

Swete's 4:5 apparatus also reports 390 through Qmg's “the others.” A marginal
report is not an independently inspected continuous-text witness. Nor does
the proposed placement of 11Q30 fragment 17 with 11Q4 yet establish either
number: the complete Puech/DJD reconstruction and plates remain to be evaluated.
The notes therefore leave historical priority unresolved. They do not derive
a chronology, harmonize the numbers, or change the declared WLC source.

The existing note bodies remain exact. The only additional note bodies are
the three edition-qualified numerical disclosures. Existing lexical entries,
theological metadata and generation provenance remain historical material,
not newly certified scholarship.
