## The actual Greek control

[Swete III, 1905](https://archive.org/details/theoldtestamenti03swetuoft),
printed p. 59 / PDF p. 83, prints καὶ οὐ μὴ ἀποθάνωμεν, “and we shall not die.”
The verb is first-person plural, not second-person singular. The selected
Greek therefore cannot be cited as evidence for “you do not die.” Its
surrounding apparatus records other verse-12 differences, but no alternative
subject at this verb; the spelling appendix, printed p. 841 / PDF p. 865,
also has no verse-12 entry. This is bounded edition evidence, not proof of
unanimity in Greek manuscripts or an exact Hebrew retroversion.

The volume's preface, PDF pp. 9–10, explains its generally Vaticanus-based
Prophets. The sigla key, PDF p. 24, identifies B, Sinaiticus, A and Q;
PDF pp. 12–13 distinguish Marchalianus correction and marginal layers.
Root read these complete pages and visually inspected the target, appendix,
key and relevant Q conventions. No target manuscript photograph or complete
modern critical apparatus is newly collated.

## Competing arguments and their limits

Kingsley Yorgio's [2022 doctoral thesis](https://research.vu.nl/ws/portalfiles/portal/171874288/K%20B%20%20Yorgio%20-%20thesis.pdf),
section 4.1.12, printed pp. 82–85 / PDF pp. 90–93, defends the plural as
communal survival. Root and the source agent read and viewed all four pages,
including the notes. His percentage-of-versions argument is not a genealogy;
the proposed Syriac letter repair is conjectural, not attested manuscript text.
His claims about Targum and Latin remain reports, not newly checked editions.
His p. 84 denial of even implicit second-person support sits uneasily beside
his own p. 83 indirect-support discussion. None of this supplies new Hebrew ink.

The independently read [Mariottini argument](https://claudemariottini.com/2010/08/26/who-will-never-die-god-or-us/)
instead favors a genuine scribal change: divine endurance continues the address
to God's ancient existence, whereas a switch to the people seems disruptive.
That is a coherent argument for the alternative. Its further claims of
Masoretic unanimity and an actual historical correction are not established
here by inspection of correction lists or an altered manuscript.

Our Hebrew control is written WLC Psalm 118:15–19, particularly לא אמות כי
אחיה in verse 17 and the following statement that Yah has disciplined the
speaker but not given him to death. This singular expression can describe
deliverance from death without promising individual immortality. It is not
an independent copy of Habakkuk or an identical plural/discourse construction.
It defeats the claim that the expression alone must mean immortality; it does
not force the communal interpretation in Habakkuk. The complete current
Habakkuk 1:11–17 source and main English were also checked: severe judgment
and killing remain, rather than being explained away by the survival reading.
