# 2 Samuel 21 and the mother of the five sons

## Source judgment

The mother-name alternatives in 2 Samuel 21:8 are real, but the consulted
evidence does not establish which belongs to the earliest recoverable Hebrew
form. Retain POB's **Michal provisionally**, not because the current Hebrew
edition is immune to correction. **Merab remains a serious alternative.**
Neither a smooth genealogy nor reconstruction of the oldest Greek reading
alone resolves Hebrew priority. No canonical source, English, note, approval
status or canon change is applied by this comparison.

This is a new comparison of an existing textual problem, not a newly discovered
variant. Its concrete contributions are identifying the old Hebrew apparatus
entries, excluding a supplied Qumran name from the evidence count, and testing
the competing histories against the connected narrative and Josephus. The
[evidence packet](../sources/textual_restoration/comparisons/samuel21_8_mother.2026-10-17.v1.json)
pins the sources and canonical baseline. The governing
[method](TEXTUAL_ADJUDICATION_METHOD.md) separates attestation, transcription,
historical priority and application.

Kennicott's preface I–III, PDF 33–35, controls this decoding: paragraph XIII
defines the omission mark, X explains the connecting dash, and VIII permits
variants without repeating the base lemma. Entry 172 is **not** a third Merab
witness. The main text prints Michal. Kennicott's declared base is van der
Hooght's edition, not the vendored WLC, and its numbered apparatus is historical
reported evidence rather than a new inspection of those codices.

The Cambridge volume is likewise a selected historical apparatus, not an
exhaustive modern census or a reconstructed earliest Greek text. Its own key
and inherited conventions govern the sigla: i = 56, g = 158, z = 554;
`rell` refers to the remaining selected cursives. Composite b must not be
counted as an independent third codex beside its two constituents. No count
of printed supporters substitutes for assessing their relationships.

The Qumran edition's note further limits even the surviving בני: only the
upper portions of nun and yod are visible. That observation comes from the
edition, not a fresh leather-image inspection. Agreement between DJD and its
digital transcription does not create two ancient witnesses.

## Connected passages

Root read the source and current English fields of 43 records: 1 Samuel 14:49,
18:17–30, 25:44; 2 Samuel 3:12–16, 6:16–23 and 21:1–14. All 43 source fields
match the pinned WLC renderer. The full target YAML, including its rationales
and note, was also read. This is not a review of every rationale in all 43
records or of the entire Samuel corpus.

The longer 1 Samuel 18 narrative gives Merab to Adriel and Michal to David.
1 Samuel 25:44 and 2 Samuel 3:12–16 connect Michal's intervening marriage with
Palti/Paltiel. 2 Samuel 6:23 states her childlessness through the day of death.
Read together, these passages make **Merab–Adriel** the cleaner genealogy in
21:8. But the [previous narrative-form comparison](SAMUEL_17_18_NARRATIVE_FORMS_2026-10-16.md)
also establishes that the shorter Greek form lacks 18:17–19. It does not
establish a continuous reconstructed Hebrew Merab ending. The conflict cannot
be resolved by treating every received scene as a single unaltered historical
family register.

Nor may a translation silently solve it. Hebrew ילדה and the Cambridge Greek
ἔτεκεν describe bearing children here. Rendering this as raising adopted
children imports an interpretation. Changing Adriel into Paltiel would be
another source alteration, not the translation of the extant name. Neither
intervention is approved.

## Competing explanations

**Merab earlier, Michal secondary.** The coherent Merab–Adriel pairing has
positive Hebrew apparatus support and a substantial Greek branch. Substitution
of the more familiar daughter-name is possible without graphic similarity.
Driver's copying-slip diagnosis is therefore plausible, but it does not
demonstrate how this particular alteration occurred. A reconstructed Old Greek
Merab would strengthen the history of that branch, not date the Hebrew name
change automatically.

**Michal earlier, Merab secondary.** A scribe could replace the difficult name
to agree with 1 Samuel 18:19. Hays's complete 2017 article argues that an Old
Greek Merab and a later Vaticanus correction to Michal can coexist with an
earlier Hebrew Michal. Her proposed chain connects this correction with removal
of the marriage episode; note 37 also allows Greek-level editing. It is a
countermodel, not surviving intermediate copies. Her stronger conclusion that
the longer 1 Samuel narrative must therefore be earlier is not adopted here.
Knowledge of a marriage tradition need not prove the priority of its written
placement. The familiar-name copying explanation also remains viable.
[Hays, ZAW 129, 220–233](https://doi.org/10.1515/zaw-2017-0013).

Hays's note 28 describes Josephus 7.89 as explicitly substituting Palti. The
consulted Greek does not name that husband there. This correction limits her
supporting example without refuting the larger transmission model. Josephus
does show that a Michal/five-children account could be accommodated through a
different marriage explanation; it does not authenticate that explanation.

**Different narrative forms without a securely recoverable single genealogy.**
McNaughton's 2000 dissertation compares M, the Masoretic text and B rather
than repairing them into one smooth story. Its conclusion, especially printed
115–116, challenges the graphic-error explanation for the two names and warns
against letting coherence determine reconstruction. That objection deserves
weight. However, absence of graphic similarity does not rule out a familiar-name
substitution, and a recoverable reading need not have survived as an entire
unaltered codex. The methodological warning does not itself prove Michal's
priority or prohibit all eclectic criticism.
[Institutional thesis](https://www.collectionscanada.ca/obj/s4/f2/dsk2/ftp02/NQ54050.pdf).

These explanations predict the consulted mother-name distribution without
requiring new visible letters. The modest chronological preference does not
break the tie: the early Qumran name is missing, Josephus is a retelling, and
the age of a Greek codex does not date every reading or its Hebrew ancestor.
Removing that preference would not change the present decision.

## Application boundary

Attestation passes at the stated published-edition and digital-transcription
level. No new transcription or image-based restoration is accepted.
Earliest Hebrew mother-name priority remains unresolved. The working reader
decision is provisional retention of Michal with the existing alternative,
not a historical victory for the current base.

The existing target rationale says original-language primacy means Michal
must not be emended. That inference is inadequate: an edition is not the
entire Hebrew tradition. This dossier supersedes that rationale as a research
judgment but does not silently edit the canonical record or transfer its prior
approvals. A later source or reader application requires its own exact delta,
review and validation. No main-English candidate is drafted here, and modern
commercial English Bible wording is not a drafting input.

This case changes the evidential assessment, not POB's printed meaning. It
provides no inference about adding books to a biblical canon. ImageGen is not
used: the missing Qumran name cannot be turned into evidence by generating it.
