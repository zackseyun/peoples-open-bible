# 1 Samuel 2 22 Alexandrinus contrary evidence

## Result and effect on the decision

The longer women clause is confirmed in the continuous body of Codex
Alexandrinus through a photographic facsimile. Its distinctive verb is
**ΕΚΟΜΙΖΟΝ**, without the additional iota in the edition's general
**ΕΚΟΙΜΙΖΟΝ**. This validates a known contrary reading against reproduction
of the manuscript, not merely another typeset text. It does not reverse the
provisional shorter-source choice: the
[original comparison](SAMUEL_2_22_SANCTUARY_CLAUSE_2026-10-17.md) already retained
A's longer body and unusual spelling as counterevidence. No source, English,
footnote or approval changes in this follow-up.

A has a mixed profile. Its opening includes σύμπαντα, “all,” while its
recipient expression is “sons of Israel,” as in the short B-based text rather
than Masoretic “all Israel.” Its longer clause therefore must not become a
vote for every Masoretic word, or an assured identical sexual formulation in
every Greek branch. Conversely, the shorter B body does not erase A's positive
long-form attestation. The two manuscript controls reinforce feature-level
comparison rather than a whole-witness winner.

## Artifact and passage identity

The [accessible facsimile](https://archive.org/details/TheCodexAlexandrinusV.2)
is the British Museum's reduced photographic reproduction, Old Testament
Part II, covering 1 Samuel through 2 Chronicles. The photographed title page
and Kenyon's introduction date this part to **1930**; the archive's **1915**
metadata is not the correct publication date for this artifact. The manuscript
it reproduces is separately dated to the **fifth century** in the
[British Library catalogue](https://searcharchives.bl.uk/catalog/040-001614686).
Neither date assigns every target stroke to an original or correcting hand.

The target is the third manuscript plate, **PDF9**, captioned **1 Sam. ii.12–32**,
right column, manually counted body lines 1–7. A displayed number **162** is
preserved as a locator, not silently equated with modern catalogue foliation.
The catalogue places 1 Samuel at modern ff.165r–185r, but the exact modern
target folio was not independently verified. Facsimile Part II is not the
same numbering as the manuscript's physical Volume 1, Royal MS 1 D V.

The longer unit runs from the end of line 3 through line 6, followed
continuously by Eli's speech in 2:23. Its normalized comparison wording is:

> καὶ ὡς ἐκόμιζον τὰς γυναῖκας τὰς παρεστώσας παρὰ τὴν θύραν τῆς σκηνῆς τοῦ μαρτυρίου

This comparison expands spacing and adds accents; it is not a new diplomatic
edition. The reproduced unit is main-column text, not a marginal addition at
this locus. No target intervention is apparent, but the stark black-and-white
reproduction cannot exclude subtle correction or certify first-hand status.

Root also read the actual Brooke–McLean Genesis symbol key, printed v/PDF13,
and Samuel printed 8/PDF24. The key identifies capital A as Alexandrinus and
distinguishes original and later correction labels; the local apparatus
specifically reports A's unusual verb. The agent additionally checked Samuel's
carry-forward key, printed v/PDF11, including lowercase a's different identity.
These are edition controls on one physical manuscript, not extra witnesses.

## Lexical and historical limits

The primary [Perseus LSJ entry for κομίζω](https://atlas.perseus.tufts.edu/dictionaries/entry/urn:cite2:scaife-viewer:dictionaries.v1:lsj-n58790/)
includes caring, conveying and bringing; the
[Perseus vocabulary entry for κοιμίζω](https://vocab.perseus.org/lemma/46697/)
gives putting to sleep. That supports keeping the written forms distinct,
not automatically rendering A's verb as intercourse. A copying loss of iota
or contextual sexual implication remains possible; the dictionary alone
cannot determine A's intended word or the earlier Greek. Neither can it
establish a different surviving Hebrew verb. POB's footnote explicitly gives
the Masoretic allegation, not a supposedly identical translation of A.

The strongest longer-text account remains live: an early short ancestor could
have lost material retained in longer branches, while those branches developed
different wording. A's presence and Josephus's earlier retelling prevent
dismissing the longer reception as late medieval invention. They do not prove
its priority. The independent Qumran join/space constraint remains unresolved;
the facsimile supplies no new Hebrew letters or missing-width measurement.

## Reproduction check and broader scope

One bounded source agent located the facsimile and its exact target. Root
independently inspected the complete plate, native target region, title,
introduction, symbol key and local apparatus, then reproduced the crop directly
from PDF9 with Poppler. The two crop files are byte-identical. The full-page
display was reported resized from 3270×4400 to 2734×3679; the 1350×1600 target
region had no reported resize. Rendering at a larger pixel size is not new
optical resolution. Both readings were context-informed, not blinded or
calibrated fresh transcription. The
[observation record](../sources/textual_restoration/comparisons/samuel2_22_alexandrinus_image.2026-10-17.v1.json)
preserves input hashes, derivation and scope. Raw assets remain outside Git.

The catalogue also lists 1 and 2 Clement in the codex's New Testament volume.
That is a concrete reception source for the
[existing canon-research question](CONTRIBUTIONS_AND_CANON_REVIEW_2026-10-06.md#additional-works-worth-considering),
not a new discovery or automatic authority to add either work to a Bible.
Physical inclusion, textual reliability and a community's canon are distinct.
No canon label changes. The facsimile is available for later passage checks;
its acquisition is not collation of every book it contains.
