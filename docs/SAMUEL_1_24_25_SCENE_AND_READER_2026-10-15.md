# 1 Samuel 1:24–25: whole-scene comparison and reader disclosure

## Result and scope

The longer Greek scene and the published reconstruction of 4Q51 are not one
identical alternative to the Masoretic scene. The exact animal-only research
proposal does not resolve their relationship. Retain the current WLC Hebrew
and marker-free main English; disclose the narrative alternative and qualify
the unsupported claim that the doubled boy expression is certainly emphatic.
This improves the reader's access to the evidence without selecting new Hebrew.

This follows the [exact animal proposal](SAMUEL_1_24_EXACT_SOURCE_PROPOSAL_2026-10-15.md)
and [competing literary arguments](SAMUEL_1_24_NARRATIVE_COUNTERCASE_2026-10-15.md).
Those frozen research inputs remain unchanged. The scope is a connected
1:24–25 comparison with 1:22–23 context, not a new edition of the chapter,
complete manuscript census or approval of every inherited translation choice.

## Edition check and preservation boundaries

Root reacquired the previously consulted scans and visually read complete
DJD XVII pp.31–35 and Cambridge II.1 p.4, including the target apparatus.
Their byte hashes match the earlier consultation copies. This is a new
comparison task using existing editions, **not newly discovered evidence**.

[DJD XVII](https://messianicessenes.wordpress.com/wp-content/uploads/2014/07/djd-17.pdf)
prints a reconstructed blank in line 10 (p.31). Its p.32 note argues from
alignment and repetition against supplying another presentation clause.
Pages 33–35 distinguish surviving variants from reconstructed ones; the
proposed opening and final Hannah clause belong to the latter discussion.
Page 35 strongly defends the longer reconstruction, but expressly leaves
original-text priority unsettled and suspects conflation. Its confidence in
reconstructing this scroll must not become our confidence in the earliest text.
No physical blank, missing letters or revised line measurement was independently
established by this reading.

The [Cambridge edition](https://tmcdaniel.palmerseminary.edu/Brooke%26McLean/LXX_Brooke%26McLean_2-1.pdf)
provides the Greek sequence below. Its p.4 prints the extra presentation clause
before the bull slaughter. Its apparatus reports variation in verbs and
participants; its main text is not unanimous Greek testimony. The separately
checked [Rahlfs–Hanhart 2006 control](https://www.die-bibel.de/bibel/LXX/1SA.1)
has the same relevant sequence. Numbering differs at the boundary: Cambridge
places more of the expansion under verse 24, whereas the digital control
starts verse 25 earlier. Align by words, not a verse number alone.

## Competing histories: what each still owes

**Shorter scene earlier, longer scene expanded.** This can explain explicit
participants and sacrificial elaboration, but must account for the older
Hebrew traces and every connected change rather than labelling all pluses
late. The difficult boy expression and unspecified plural agents remain
part of the explanation, not proof that the shorter form is earlier.

**Longer scene earlier, shorter scene damaged or revised.** A repeated-word
skip is possible, but a full transition must specify the restart point and
account separately for the opening, doubled noun, verbal number and final
presentation. The [previous join illustration](SAMUEL_1_24_NARRATIVE_COUNTERCASE_2026-10-15.md#the-proposed-omission-specify-the-joins-not-just-the-catchword)
does not establish an attested lost line. A particular longer Greek clause
cannot be placed in 4Q51 merely because it would make that illustration work.

**Multiple stages or conflation.** Local agreement may preserve an earlier
animal reading alongside a later narrative feature, or the reverse. This
possibility warns against selecting a whole package by its oldest feature;
without a discriminating sequence it is not permission to assemble any hybrid.

Our outcome is a held whole-scene selection, not a forced ranking disguised
as certainty. An explicit critical reconstruction remains useful for testing,
but the frozen animal-only candidate is not a recovered complete exemplar.
The previous moderate semantic animal preference does not become high confidence.

## Reader changes proposed

At 1:24, a new note after “young” explains the compressed Hebrew, the selected
Greek's different boy clause and longer scene, and 4Q51's partial preservation.
The final lexical rationale becomes provisional rather than asserting emphasis
as established. The animal note and its anchor remain unchanged.

At 1:25, move the existing slaughter and boy notes to their actual words.
Add a sentence-level note identifying the narrative difference and referring
back to 1:24. Qualify only the two connected verbal rationales: the plural
agents are unspecified in the retained source, and a versional narrative
difference is not simply an English synonym. No particular slaughterer is
inserted in the main text or certified by the note.

The reader is not given an invented continuous Hebrew restoration. The change
does not adopt bread, an age-qualified animal, a different opening, or a new
sacrifice sequence. Old approvals must be archived without certifying the new
notes. Full-record review and actual export verification precede application.
