# Genesis 11 genealogy and age systems

## Decision

Genesis 11 requires comparison of genealogical structure as well as ages.
The selected Greek editions contain an additional Cainan between Arpachshad
and Shelah. The consulted Samaritan edition lacks that generation but agrees
with the selected Greek begetting ages for every shared figure. It also
supplies explicit lifetime totals absent from the Masoretic and selected Greek
texts. These patterns cannot be represented by a single Hebrew-versus-Greek
contrast or the uniform hundred-year adjustment seen in part of Genesis 5.

POB's numbers and direct Arpachshad-to-Shelah link accurately represent its
declared WLC source. They remain provisionally unchanged. Reader disclosure
of the additional generation and competing ages is warranted; this comparison
does not yet establish which complete textual history best explains the others.
These are established published readings, not newly deciphered letters.

## Compared ages

Pairs are **age at the named son's birth / years afterward**. The two selected
Greek editions agree on these pairs, but do not represent all Greek witnesses.
“Absent” means no corresponding genealogical entry, not a missing or zero age.

The Samaritan edition explicitly prints all eight lifetime totals and death
formulas. WLC prints neither in these eight entries. The selected Greek texts
print death formulas but no separate lifetime totals. Consequently, WLC's
Eber lifetime of 464 and the selected Greek 504 are **computed sums**; the
Samaritan 404 is both explicitly printed and arithmetically consistent. Treating
all three as separately attested total clauses would manufacture evidence.

Five Samaritan pairs differ from WLC by +100/−100, conserving the sum:
Arpachshad, Shelah, Peleg, Reu and Serug. Nahor instead differs by +50/−50;
Eber differs by +100/−160, lowering the sum by 60. The Greek pairs have their
own pattern, including Arpachshad's 430 rather than WLC's 403. A uniform rule
cannot silently repair these actual readings into a preferred chronology.

If every link is consecutive, the begetting ages yield **220 years** from
Arpachshad's birth to Terah's in WLC, **870** in the Samaritan edition, and
**1,000** in each selected Greek edition. The Greek calculation includes
Cainan's 130 years. These relative sums exclude Shem's age of 100 and Terah's
grouped age of 70; they are not historical dates or a proof that the genealogy
must be used as a continuous clock. Verse 10's Hebrew two-year expression and
Greek second-year expression are retained as distinct wording rather than
silently equated with one exact elapsed duration.

## Terah and the limits of chronological harmonization

All four selected profiles give Terah 70 in 11:26, followed by Abram, Nahor
and Haran as a group. That wording does not establish simultaneous births or
Abram's individual birth at Terah's seventieth year. POB's existing note already
warns against treating the list as an exact birth order.

At 11:32, the Samaritan edition gives Terah's lifetime as **145**, whereas
WLC and both selected Greek editions give **205**. Neither side supplies a
separate number of years after the grouped births. Subtracting 70 from 145
gives 75, matching Abram's departure age in 12:4; this arithmetic alone does
not establish Abram's birth age, the sequence of every event, a scribal motive,
or which Terah reading is original. The family narrative beginning at 11:27
must not be treated as another ordinary two-line age entry.

## Sources and preservation boundaries

The [numerical record](../sources/textual_restoration/comparisons/genesis11_age_systems.2026-10-16.v1.json)
pins the repository baseline, source hashes and all 32 chapter files. Root
read WLC's actual XML at 11:10–32 and the corresponding POB source, English and
notes; the displayed ages agree. This is not a new validation of every lexical
decision or footnote anchor in the chapter.

**Samaritan.** A delegated source reader visually checked the Hebrew, English
and apparatus on printed pp. 119–122 / PDF pp. 136–139 of Florentin and Tal's
2024 [edition](https://doi.org/10.11647/OBP.0415). Its MS 6 (C) basis and notation
are explained in the [Genesis 5 report](GENESIS_5_AGE_SYSTEMS_2026-10-16.md#edition-identity-and-preservation).
The lifetime/death clauses are marked as absent from MT, not as damaged text
restored by us. The Terah 145 expression crosses printed pp. 121–122. This is
a selected Samaritan edition, not a complete manuscript census.

**Greek selected texts.** Root read the complete Bible Society
[Rahlfs–Hanhart 2006 chapter](https://www.die-bibel.de/bibel/LXX/GEN.11) and
visually checked Swete I, fourth edition (1909; reprinted 1925), printed
pp. 17–19 / PDF pp. 49–51. Swete's local base is Alexandrinus. Their agreement
on the displayed ages and generation is edition agreement, not two additional
independent ancient witnesses.

**Greek apparatus.** Root inspected Brooke–McLean (1906), printed pp. 27–28 /
PDF pp. 43–44, including an enlarged 11:13 apparatus region. It records
omission of the Cainan life paragraph in **E*** and its presence in the margin
by **Eᵃ**. The full key on printed p. v / PDF p. 13 identifies E as Codex
Bodleianus, * as original writing, and a as a later correction. Swete's
printed p. xxvi / PDF p. 30 supplies its own correction description. These
are editorial reports of manuscript states, not a new inspection of the codex.
The bounded paragraph omission must not become a claim that E lacks Cainan's
name in every clause. Other apparatus entries vary the Arpachshad and Nahor
numbers. Neither selected main text is unanimous Greek testimony; Swete and
Brooke–McLean also share editorial dependencies described in the prior report.

**Qumran.** No newly checked Qumran numeral or Cainan clause enters this
comparison. The large Genesis index could not be fetched through the web tool.
That access failure is not evidence that no relevant fragment exists, and
supplied parallel text must not be counted as surviving ink.

## Cainan across other works

The current WLC/POB texts at Genesis 10:24 and 1 Chronicles 1:18 directly
connect Arpachshad and Shelah. POB's declared SBLGNT text at Luke 3:36 includes
Cainan. This local cross-reference check establishes what POB currently prints;
it is not a fresh NT apparatus review or evidence that Luke independently
preserves an earlier Hebrew Genesis. No omission motive is inferred here.

A separate source reader directly checked [Jubilees 8:1–5](https://www.tau.ac.il/~hacohen/Jubil/Jubil%208.html)
in the TAU digital VanderKam edition: 8:1 identifies Kainan as Arpachshad's son,
and 8:5 makes him Shelah's father. The [provenance statement](https://www.tau.ac.il/~hacohen/Jubil/InformationVdK.html)
identifies the 1989 CSCO 510–511 Ethiopic edition and English translation,
without its apparatus and notes. This is a relevant literary parallel.
Whether this particular clause survives in an ancient Hebrew fragment, and
whether its wording is independent of Greek Genesis, remain unverified.
The composition date of Jubilees cannot substitute for those missing checks.

Jubilees therefore merits comparison as a witness to a related genealogical
tradition. That is a different question from recommending its inclusion in
a biblical canon; this passage alone cannot settle that community-dependent
judgment. Nor should its genealogy be inserted into Hebrew Genesis simply
because it agrees with a selected Greek text.

## POB implications and next discriminating evidence

The present comparison establishes consequential differences: an additional
generation, altered age relationships, explicit-versus-computed lifetimes and
a different Terah lifespan. POB's current Genesis 11 notes largely discuss
wording and name forms rather than these differences. A qualified note at
11:12–13 and a linked chapter summary are warranted, but not yet applied.

A concrete note for 11:12 is:

> The Masoretic text, followed here, makes Arpachshad father Shelah at 35.
> The Greek text printed by Rahlfs–Hanhart instead gives Arpachshad 135 at
> Cainan's birth, then Cainan 130 at Shelah's birth (Greek verse 13). The
> Samaritan text printed by Tal and Florentin directly connects Arpachshad
> and Shelah but gives Arpachshad 135. The traditions also differ in the
> subsequent years and other ages in this genealogy.

This note describes the structural difference without treating a figure's
absence as a zero age. Its application still requires comparison of the full
verse record, preservation of existing notes and review history, and a check
of the reader export. A link should be added only when the comparison has a
verified reader-accessible destination.

The strongest reopening argument is the agreement of the selected Samaritan
and Greek begetting ages, combined with the Cainan parallel in Jubilees. The
strongest restraint is that those agreements coexist with different subsequent
years, genealogy structure and transmission states. The current comparison
does not determine whether an age was expanded, reduced, harmonized or inherited
from a different Hebrew exemplar. Mechanical preference for older, shorter,
more coherent or more numerous witnesses cannot choose the original system.

Next, consult the modern critical apparatus at the Cainan clauses and the
Arpachshad/Nahor numerals, and establish the earliest physical attestation of
Jubilees 8:1–5. Those checks can discriminate histories more effectively than
another arithmetic tally. A reader application should preserve the entire
linked unit, archive prior review data and verify the exported notes. No Hebrew,
English, reader note, canonical boundary or generated-image evidence changes
in this research packet. ImageGen cannot recover an unattested numeral or
generation by drawing plausible missing letters.

local source-file hashes and the unchanged 32-file Genesis 11 manifest. These
checks validate the record's arithmetic and inputs; the editions and inspected
pages supply the textual evidence.

All four existing Genesis 5 numerical-comparison tests passed. Validation also
checked 520 relative link targets across the four affected documents, JSON and
workflow YAML parsing, and the diff. No biblical record changed against the
pinned baseline.
