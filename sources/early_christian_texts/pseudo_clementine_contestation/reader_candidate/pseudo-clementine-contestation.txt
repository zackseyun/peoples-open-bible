SECTION 1

After James had read the letter, he summoned the elders, and after reading it to them he said, “Necessarily and fittingly, our Peter has reminded us to take precautions concerning the truth, so that we should not pass on indiscriminately to anyone the books of his proclamations that have been sent to us, but only to someone good and devout, one who chooses to teach and is a circumcised believer. And we should not pass on all of them at once, so that if he is discovered to be untrustworthy with the first, he will not be entrusted with the later ones. Therefore, let him be tested for no fewer than six years. Then, after bringing him in accordance with the way of Moses to a river or a spring—which is ‘living water,’ where the rebirth of the righteous takes place—do not make him swear an oath, since that is not permitted. Instead, command him to stand beside the water and make a solemn attestation, just as we ourselves did when we were commanded to do so as we were being reborn, for the sake of not sinning.”

Translator's notes for section 1 (not part of the ancient text):

1. Translation choice — Greek: ὡς ἔτυχεν
Rendered as: indiscriminately
The idiom means without careful selection, rather than merely by chance.

2. Translation choice — Greek: ἐνπεριτόμῳ τε ὄντι πιστῷ
Rendered as: is a circumcised believer
Takes ἐνπεριτόμῳ adjectivally of the qualified recipient without altering the supplied form.

3. Translation choice — Greek: ἀγνώμων
Rendered as: untrustworthy
In the context of testing a recipient’s handling of restricted books, this conveys deficient judgment or reliability better than the narrower ‘ungrateful.’

4. Translation choice — Greek: κατὰ τὴν Μωϋσέως ἀγωγὴν
Rendered as: in accordance with the way of Moses
ἀγωγή denotes a prescribed way, discipline, or practice here.

5. Translation choice — Greek: ἐπιμαρτύρασθαι
Rendered as: make a solemn attestation
The verb introduces the formal invocation of witnesses stated in the following passage without making the act an oath.

SECTION 2

And let him say: “May I have as witnesses heaven, earth, and water, in which all things are contained, and besides all these also the air that permeates everything, without which I do not breathe, that I will always be obedient to the one who gives me the books of the proclamations, and that, as for my books themselves, whichever he may give me, I will certainly not pass them on to anyone in any way: not by writing them out, not by giving away what has been written, not by handing them over to someone who is writing; not myself, not through another person, not through any other course of action or deception or device; not by guarding them carelessly, or leaving them somewhere, or giving a signal, or by any other method or scheme passing them on to someone else. The only exception is if I test someone and find him worthy—as I myself was judged, or after testing him even more extensively, though for no fewer than six years. Then I will hand them on, just as I received them, to a devout and good person who will undertake to teach, and I will do this with the approval of my bishop.”

Translator's notes for section 2 (not part of the ancient text):

1. Translation choice — Greek: αὐτάς μου τὰς βίβλους, ἃς ἂν μοι δῷ
Rendered as: as for my books themselves, whichever he may give me
Preserves both the possessive μου and the emphasis of αὐτάς, while ἃς ἂν is represented by the open-ended ‘whichever.’

2. Translation choice — Greek: οὐ μὴ μεταδῶ
Rendered as: I will certainly not pass them on
The paired negatives emphatically deny any future disclosure.

3. Translation choice — Greek: οὐ γράψας, οὐ γεγραμμένον δούς, οὐ γράφοντι ἀναδιδούς
Rendered as: not by writing them out, not by giving away what has been written, not by handing them over to someone who is writing
Preserves the three distinct prohibited routes of copying, distributing written material, and supplying it to a copyist.

4. Translation choice — Greek: ἢ τιθῶν ἢ νεύων
Rendered as: or leaving them somewhere, or giving a signal
Preserves the terse actions without specifying an unstated recipient or purpose.

5. Translation choice — Greek: πλεῖον δοκιμάσας
Rendered as: after testing him even more extensively
πλεῖον may concern greater duration or rigor; ‘more extensively’ leaves that dimension open.

6. Translation choice — Greek: διδάσκειν ἐπαναρουμένῳ
Rendered as: who will undertake to teach
The supplied form is analyzed as a future-middle participle of ἐπαναίρω. In context it prospectively describes taking up or undertaking the work of teaching.

7. Translation choice — Greek: ἐπὶ τῇ τοῦ ἐπισκόπου μου γνώμῃ
Rendered as: with the approval of my bishop
γνώμη here denotes the bishop’s judgment or consent governing the transfer.

SECTION 3

But otherwise—not even if he is my child or brother or friend, or is ever related to me by family in some other way, if indeed he is unworthy—not to pass them on for this person’s sake, since it was not proper. I will not fear a plot, nor will I be cajoled by gifts. But even if at some point the books of the proclamations given to me should seem to me not to be true, not even in that case will I pass them on; instead, I will return them. When traveling, I will carry with me whichever of them I happen to possess. But if I do not wish to carry them around with me, I will not allow them to remain at my house; instead, I will entrust them to my bishop, who holds the same faith and proceeds from the same starting points. And if I should happen to fall ill and suspect that I may die, if I am childless, I will do likewise. But even if I die while I have a child who is unworthy or not yet capable, I will do likewise, for I will entrust them to my bishop, so that if the child, on reaching maturity, should happen to be worthy of trust, the bishop will return them to the child as a paternal deposit, in accordance with the wording of the solemn attestation.

Translator's notes for section 3 (not part of the ancient text):

1. Greek: ἄλλως δὲ οὐδ’ ἂν τέκνον ἢ ἀδελφὸς ἢ φίλος ἢ ἄλλως πως ποτὲ γένους μοι ἀνήκων, ἐάνπερ ἀνάξιος ᾖ, τοῦδε μὴ μεταδοῦναι χάριν, ὡς οὐ προσῆκεν.
The verified printed sentence is syntactically anacoluthic and does not express a finite governor for μεταδοῦναι. The main translation adopts the strongest contextual allocation: conditional ἄν with an understood copula, personal τοῦδε governed discontinuously by χάριν, and μή negating an infinitival resumption of the preceding undertaking. It therefore preserves an English infinitival fragment rather than inventing a finite predicate. A material alternative takes οὐδέ with an omitted finite refusal and regards μὴ μεταδοῦναι as its restatement, but that requires more unexpressed structure. A neuter partitive τοῦδε, “some of this,” is also grammatically possible, but it leaves the force and attachment of χάριν substantially less secure. No change to τοῦδε, either negation, or the printed word division is proposed. The chosen analysis is contextual and defensible, though not uniquely established; this is ordinary syntactic ambiguity rather than an unreadable source or an invented restoration.

2. Greek: εἰ δὲ μὴ βουληθῶ μεθ’ ἑαυτοῦ αὐτὰ περιφέρειν
The third-person reflexive form ἑαυτοῦ refers contextually to the first-person speaker of βουληθῶ. This is an unusual reflexive alignment but does not require an emendation or prevent a defensible translation.

3. Greek: παρακαταθήσομαι·,
The selected edition prints a middle dot immediately followed by a comma. This is an editorial punctuation anomaly and does not affect the lexical text or the construction.

4. Greek: ἵνα εἰ μὲν τύχοι, ἐφ’ ἡλικίας γενόμενος, ἄξιος εἶναι τῆς πίστεως, ὡς πατρῴαν παρακαταθήκην τῷ τέκνῳ ἀποδώσει
The clause has an unpaired μέν and an implicit subject shift: the child is the natural subject of reaching maturity and being worthy, while the bishop is the natural subject of returning the deposit. The custody context supports this reading, but “worthy of the faith” remains a viable lexical alternative to “worthy of trust.” These are ordinary anacoluthic and referential ambiguities, not grounds for restoring omitted text.

5. Translation choice — Greek: ἄλλως δὲ οὐδ’ ἂν τέκνον ἢ ἀδελφὸς ἢ φίλος ἢ ἄλλως πως ποτὲ γένους μοι ἀνήκων, ἐάνπερ ἀνάξιος ᾖ, τοῦδε μὴ μεταδοῦναι χάριν, ὡς οὐ προσῆκεν.
Rendered as: But otherwise—not even if he is my child or brother or friend, or is ever related to me by family in some other way, if indeed he is unworthy—not to pass them on for this person’s sake, since it was not proper.
The main rendering follows a defensible unchanged-text analysis without pretending that it is unique. It takes ἄν conditionally with an understood copula governing the coordinated nominative descriptions; ἐάνπερ ἀνάξιος ᾖ further qualifies the prospective recipient. It takes τοῦδε as a masculine personal genitive governed by the postpositive χάριν across μὴ μεταδοῦναι, while μή negates the infinitive. The preceding books supply the unexpressed object of μεταδοῦναι. The English deliberately retains the anacoluthic infinitive “not to pass” and does not insert an unwritten governing verb such as “I promise” or “I will.” The scalar οὐδέ and the infinitival μή jointly express the emphatic refusal rather than canceling one another.

6. Translation choice — Greek: ἄλλως πως ποτὲ γένους μοι ἀνήκων
Rendered as: is ever related to me by family in some other way
γένους is retained as specifying the sphere of family or kinship, while μοι marks the relationship to the speaker. The precise idiom is unusual, but the family relationship is contextually defensible without changing the genitive to a dative.

7. Translation choice — Greek: οὔτε ἐπιβουλὴν φοβηθήσομαι, οὐ δώροις κολακευθήσομαι
Rendered as: I will not fear a plot, nor will I be cajoled by gifts
ἐπιβουλή denotes a hostile design or plot. “Cajoled” preserves the flattering or persuasive force of κολακεύω without replacing the printed “gifts” with the more interpretive “bribes.”

8. Translation choice — Greek: τὰ διδόμενά μοι τῶν κηρυγμάτων βιβλία μὴ ἀληθῆ εἶναι
Rendered as: the books of the proclamations given to me should seem to me not to be true
The negation remains within what the books might seem to be; it is not recast as a claim that the books actually are false.

9. Translation choice — Greek: ἀποδημῶν δὲ σὺν ἐμοὶ αὐτὰ κομίσω, ἅπερ ἂν τύχω κεκτημένος
Rendered as: When traveling, I will carry with me whichever of them I happen to possess
ἀποδημῶν describes travel or absence from home, while τύχω κεκτημένος indicates whichever books the speaker happens to have in his possession.

10. Translation choice — Greek: εἰ δὲ μὴ βουληθῶ μεθ’ ἑαυτοῦ αὐτὰ περιφέρειν
Rendered as: But if I do not wish to carry them around with me
The reflexive ἑαυτοῦ is understood as referring contextually to the first-person speaker. The translation does not emend it to ἐμαυτοῦ.

11. Translation choice — Greek: τῷ ἐπισκόπῳ μου τῷ τὴν αὐτὴν ἔχοντι πίστιν καὶ ἀπὸ τῶν αὐτῶν ὁρμωμένῳ παρακαταθήσομαι
Rendered as: I will entrust them to my bishop, who holds the same faith and proceeds from the same starting points
παρακαταθήσομαι describes placing the books with the bishop as a deposit or trust. “Proceeds from the same starting points” retains the metaphor in ἀπὸ τῶν αὐτῶν ὁρμωμένῳ rather than replacing it with an unexplained doctrinal conclusion.

12. Translation choice — Greek: τὸν θάνατον ὑποπτεύσαι
Rendered as: suspect that I may die
This preserves the apprehensive or uncertain force of ὑποπτεύω rather than presenting death as a settled expectation.

13. Translation choice — Greek: ἄξιος εἶναι τῆς πίστεως
Rendered as: be worthy of trust
The immediate context concerns whether entrusted books may safely be returned to the child, so πίστις is taken in the fiduciary sense “trust.” “Worthy of the faith,” referring to the shared religious faith mentioned earlier, remains a viable alternative.

14. Translation choice — Greek: ἵνα εἰ μὲν τύχοι, ἐφ’ ἡλικίας γενόμενος, ἄξιος εἶναι τῆς πίστεως, ὡς πατρῴαν παρακαταθήκην τῷ τέκνῳ ἀποδώσει
Rendered as: so that if the child, on reaching maturity, should happen to be worthy of trust, the bishop will return them to the child as a paternal deposit
The child is the contextual subject of τύχοι, γενόμενος, and εἶναι, whereas the bishop who holds the deposit is the contextual subject of ἀποδώσει. πατρῴαν παρακαταθήκην preserves the legal image of a deposit originating with the child’s father. The unpaired μέν and the shift of implicit subject are treated as anacoluthic features rather than silently rewritten.

15. Translation choice — Greek: κατὰ τὸν τῆς διαμαρτυρίας λόγον
Rendered as: in accordance with the wording of the solemn attestation
διαμαρτυρία refers to the formal attestation introduced in the preceding sections, while λόγος here denotes its stated wording or terms.

SECTION 4

“As assurance that I will do so, I once again, for a second time, call as witnesses heaven, earth, and water, in which all things are contained, and besides all these also the air that permeates everything, without which I do not breathe, that I will always be obedient to the one who gives me the books of the proclamations, and I will observe in every respect everything I solemnly attested, or even something more. Therefore, if I keep the agreements, I will have a share with the holy ones; but if I act contrary to what I agreed, may the whole universe be hostile to me, as well as the ether that permeates everything and the God who is above all, than whom no one is superior, than whom no one is greater. But even if I come to entertain the supposition of another god, I now swear by that one too that I will not act otherwise, whether he exists or does not exist. Besides all this, if I lie, I will be accursed while alive and after death, and I will be punished with eternal punishment.” After this, let him partake of bread and salt with the one who hands the books on.

Translator's notes for section 4 (not part of the ancient text):

1. Greek: οὗ κρείττων, οὗ μείζων οὐδείς
κρείττων can express general superiority or greater strength. “Superior” preserves the broader comparison here; “mightier” is a possible alternative if the invocation emphasizes power. It is not restricted to greater moral goodness.

2. Translation choice — Greek: πάλιν τὸ δεύτερον
Rendered as: once again, for a second time
Preserves the source’s emphatic repetition rather than reducing it to a single temporal expression.

3. Translation choice — Greek: ἢ καί τι πλεῖον
Rendered as: or even something more
Preserves the unqualified additional undertaking without introducing an uncertainty marker.

4. Translation choice — Greek: φυλάσσοντι οὖν μοι τὰς συνθήκας
Rendered as: if I keep the agreements
συνθῆκαι are the solemnly accepted terms or agreements established by the attestation.

5. Translation choice — Greek: ὁ διὰ πάντων διήκων αἰθὴρ
Rendered as: the ether that permeates everything
Renders αἰθήρ distinctly from the earlier ἀήρ, ‘air,’ while preserving the image of permeation.

6. Translation choice — Greek: εἰς ἑτέρου θεοῦ ὑπόνοιαν γένωμαι
Rendered as: if I come to entertain the supposition of another god
ὑπόνοια can denote supposition or suspicion; the rendering leaves the precise epistemic force open while preserving the hypothetical claim.

7. Translation choice — Greek: κἀκεῖνον νῦν ὄμνυμι
Rendered as: I now swear by that one too
The accusative with ὄμνυμι identifies the additional being invoked in the declaration.

8. Translation choice — Greek: κατάθεμα ἔσομαι ζῶν καὶ θανὼν
Rendered as: I will be accursed while alive and after death
Preserves ἔσομαι as a future assertion and distinguishes it from the preceding optative εἴη, while representing the contrast between living and having died.

9. Translation choice — Greek: αἰωνίῳ κολασθήσομαι κολάσει
Rendered as: I will be punished with eternal punishment
The future indicative is retained as an asserted consequence rather than recast as a wish.

SECTION 5

After James said these things, the elders turned pale with anguish. Seeing that they had become very afraid, James said, “Listen to me, brothers and fellow servants. If we make the books available to everyone indiscriminately and they are adulterated by certain daring men or distorted by interpretations, as you have already heard that some have done, the result will be that even those who truly seek the truth will thereafter continually go astray. Therefore, it is better for the books to remain with us and for us to pass them on, with every safeguard previously mentioned, to those who wish to live devoutly and save others. But if anyone, after making such a solemn attestation, acts otherwise, he will justly fall under eternal punishment. For on what grounds will someone who has become responsible for the destruction of others not himself be destroyed?” The elders were pleased with James’s judgment and said, “Blessed is the one who, foreknowing all things, appointed you well as bishop for us.” And when they had said these things, we rose and prayed to the Father and God of all: “To whom is the glory throughout the ages. Amen.”

Translator's notes for section 5 (not part of the ancient text):

1. Greek: καὶ· αὗται
The selected edition prints a middle dot immediately after the conjunction καί, interrupting the conjunction from its coordinated pronoun. This is an editorial punctuation feature; the syntax continues with αὗται and does not indicate unresolved textual corruption.

2. Translation choice — Greek: ὠχρίασαν ἀγωνιάσαντες
Rendered as: turned pale with anguish
Preserves both their visible pallor and the distress that caused it.

3. Translation choice — Greek: νοθευθῶσιν
Rendered as: are adulterated
The verb depicts the books as corrupted by the introduction of spurious material.

4. Translation choice — Greek: ταῖς ἑρμηνείαις διαστραφῶσιν
Rendered as: distorted by interpretations
Preserves both the interpretive means and the resulting distortion; ἑρμηνεῖαι may also encompass explanatory renderings or translations.

5. Translation choice — Greek: ἔσται λοιπὸν καὶ τοῖς ὄντως τὴν ἀλήθειαν ζητοῦσιν ἀεὶ πλανᾶσθαι
Rendered as: the result will be that even those who truly seek the truth will thereafter continually go astray
Expresses the impersonal construction while retaining λοιπὸν and the continuing force of ἀεί.

6. Translation choice — Greek: εὐλόγως αἰωνίᾳ ὑποπεσεῖται κολάσει
Rendered as: he will justly fall under eternal punishment
εὐλόγως marks the punishment as warranted or reasonable, not as verbal praise.

7. Translation choice — Greek: Εὐλόγηται ὁ σὲ ὡς πάντα προειδὼς καλῶς ἡμῖν καταστήσας ἐπίσκοπον
Rendered as: Blessed is the one who, foreknowing all things, appointed you well as bishop for us
Εὐλόγηται is rendered as a perfect passive assertion of a resulting blessed state, not as a wish. The foreknowledge describes the one who made the appointment, and καλῶς evaluates that appointment.

End of Pseudo-Clementine Contestation.
