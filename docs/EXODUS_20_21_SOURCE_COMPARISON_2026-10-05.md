# Exodus 20 21 and the longer Sinai speech

The longer Samaritan form makes a consequential narrative difference: God's
approval of the people's request, the promise of a prophet like Moses, the
false-prophet rules and instructions for Moses and the people occur explicitly
at Sinai in Exodus. This is worth showing readers. This comparison provisionally
retains POB's shorter WLC source and marker-free English, adds a qualified draft
note, and repairs the existing darkness-note anchor. Historical priority remains
unresolved; no newly deciphered reading or canonical addition is claimed.

## Complete comparison of the longer form

node, including eleven separately retained spaces. Its 37-consonant core agrees
with WLC Exodus 20:21. The other 530 consonants form ten added blocks. These
are human-declared boundaries validated against the controls, not a new search
result or ten independent manuscript witnesses. Raw offsets are zero-based,
half-open character positions; source offsets remain explicit in the receipt.

Every added block matches the specified Samaritan control span exactly. The
first block matches WLC consonants, not its pointing or word division. Root
read Exodus 20:18–23 and Deuteronomy 5:28–31 and 18:15–22 in both controls.
The Deuteronomy 5:28 retrospective first-person introduction is not repeated;
the added narrative instead supplies a divine speech frame. Against WLC,
Samaritan 5:29 lacks all before my commandments; 18:19 has his words rather
than my words; 18:21 also differs in the wording/spelling of its question.
Do not silently replace these with the familiar Masoretic wording.

transcription of a particular textual control, not a complete census of

exports metadata only and first reproduces the frozen whole-Torah screen.

## Ancient attestation and its limits

Root inspected the manuscript-owned rows, excluding the embedded comparison
texts, in these dated Qumran-Digital transcriptions:

- [4Q22](https://lexicon.qumran-digital.org/transcriptions/4Q22/2026-05-21/index.html),
  column 21 lines 20–28 and the next extant column 22 from line 31: the former
  preserves parts of the preceding expanded people's speech; the latter resumes
  at Exodus 21:5. No complete Exodus 20:21 prophet speech was identified in
  these own rows. Missing coverage is not an attested omission.
- [4Q158](https://lexicon.qumran-digital.org/transcriptions/4Q158/2026-05-21/index.html),
  complete fragment 6 lines 1–10 and fragments 7–8 lines 1–15: fragmentary
  obedience, prophet and related material survives. The latter group also
  contains Decalogue material and an added statement that the people returned
  to their tents while Moses remained. It does not preserve every word of the
  compared Samaritan form. Kratz's grouping 7–9 and QDR's 7–8 are kept distinct.
- [4Q175](https://lexicon.qumran-digital.org/transcriptions/4Q175/2026-05-21/index.html),
  column 1 lines 1–8: an anthology combines related Deuteronomy obedience and
  prophet material. It is not a continuous Exodus copy or proof of placement
  at this locus. Its you heard, my words, repeated prophet and four-dot divine
  name differ from the compared Samaritan wording.

Anderson and Giles, *The Samaritan Pentateuch* (2012), printed pages 61–63
(PDF 71–73), were inspected natively. Their table row 16 associates Exodus
20:21's Deuteronomy parallels with 4Q22. That broad attribution cannot replace
line-level preservation checks or certify every word; the underlying DJD
apparatus has not been newly collated here. [Publisher PDF](https://www.sbl-site.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/11/SamaritanPentateuch_SBL_Anderson-Giles.pdf).

Kratz's discussion of the Decalogue, printed pages 514–518 (PDF 14–18), was
inspected natively. It argues for stages of harmonization and discusses
alternative arrangements; its reconstructed sequence is an argument, not
preserved ink. More importantly, footnote 46 on page 515 gives the speech verb
as וידב[ר], whereas QDR fragment 6 line 4 presents ו֯[יאמר]. Preserve the
different supply brackets in both presentations. The disputed verb
is therefore held, not used as decisive evidence of direction. [Institutional PDF](https://ub01.uni-tuebingen.de/xmlui/bitstream/handle/10900/172627/Kratz_052.pdf?isAllowed=y&sequence=1).

The custodial [IAA image B358486](https://www.deadseascrolls.org.il/explore-the-archive/image/B-358486)
is labelled 4Q158, plate 138, fragment 6, recto, full-spectrum color, May 2012.
Root viewed its actual pixels in the browser, including a 1280×720 full-screen
view and a 0–1.62 cm scale. The crosswalk to the literary fragment/line numbering
was not secured, and the disputed verb could not be read. This image check
does not confirm the locus or produce a new transcription. ImageGen was not used.

## Greek control and competing explanations

The pinned [Rahlfs-based Greek control](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/OpenScriptorium/lxx-morph/c91f6b1e8fb3ba37df701e6ae31f675ace71a2b2/db/seeds/lxx_morph/exodus.json)
has the shorter narrative at 20:21 and the heaven-speech transition at 20:22.
Root checked complete 20:18–23 surfaces, not all Greek manuscript traditions.
Brooke and McLean's 1909 edition, printed pages 220–221 (PDF 78–79), was also
inspected natively. Its Greek main text has the shorter form; the bottom
apparatus explicitly reports the Samaritan Hebrew extension in Latin under
20:21. That Latin editorial report is not a Greek manuscript preserving the
extension. These editions must not become two independent ancient votes.
[Edition PDF](https://tmcdaniel.palmerseminary.edu/Brooke%26McLean/LXX_Brooke%26McLean_1-2.pdf).

**Working explanation:** contextual harmonization with Deuteronomy strongly
explains the added sequence and adapted introduction. **Strongest countercase:**
an inherited fuller Sinai narrative could have been shortened in another branch.
The presence of ancient related expanded forms establishes a serious literary
alternative, not its priority or identity word for word with this control.
Neither shorter nor older alone decides. POB retains its declared shorter form
provisionally rather than constructing an unattested Hebrew hybrid.
