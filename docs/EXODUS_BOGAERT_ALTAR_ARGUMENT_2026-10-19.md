# Exodus altar omissions and the Bogaert argument

Date: 2026-10-19. Baseline: `5297ca25c306f24e163d3548c1c681cb1820d72a`.

## Decision

Bogaert's original altar synopsis distinguishes a lost Latin passage from an
omission and disputes the verse assignment of a surviving altar reference.
Those distinctions sharpen the [earlier Greek and Latin comparison](EXODUS_INCENSE_GREEK_LATIN_CONTROLS_2026-10-19.md);
they do not establish a recoverable earlier Hebrew wording. Retain POB's
source, English and notes. No reader-layout change follows.

Pierre-Maurice Bogaert, “La construction de la Tente (Ex 36–40) dans le
Monacensis de la plus ancienne version latine : l'autel d'or et Hébreux 9,4,”
in *L'enfance de la Bible hébraïque* (2005), 62–76, is available in a
[lawful partial preview](https://books.google.com/books?id=yiX7-CsSDK0C&pg=PA62).
Root inspected complete original page images 62–65, 68–72 and 75–76.
Pages 66–67 and 73–74 remain unavailable. Table III, the altar synopsis,
is on 69–70 and **was** read; the missing earlier tables must not be described
as the unread altar synopsis. This is not a full-chapter consultation,
particularly of its intervening Hebrews argument.

Bogaert explains on 63 that Latin references use Ziegler's page, column and
line, while Greek verse references use Wevers, with Rahlfs numbers in
parentheses where needed. Those coordinates are safer than transferring a
Hebrew verse label onto a damaged Latin column.

## Loss is not an observed omission

[Table III](https://books.google.com/books?id=yiX7-CsSDK0C&pg=PA69) marks
Monacensis at 35:15 as *perdu* (lost), whereas it marks 37:25–29 and 40:26
as *rien* (nothing). On 72 Bogaert expressly infers the missing Latin 35:15
from the Greek omission. It is not a second preserved witness to that minus.
His judgments at the other units remain published omission claims, not new
manuscript readings made here. The damage and editorial supplies in the
original Latin edition still require unit-specific continuity controls.

His [70, note 16](https://books.google.com/books?id=yiX7-CsSDK0C&pg=PA70)
rejects Dold's supplied *et arae* at Latin 30/1/14–17 and assigns the certain
burnt-altar mention to Hebrew **40:33a**, immediately before completion at
40:33b, rather than 40:29. Ziegler's original page 30 visibly places the altar
reference before the completion wording, but much of the line is supplied.
This supports recording Bogaert's alternative alignment, not certifying all
of Dold's revised letters. Dold's actual 1956 article remains unread.

The previous report and packet retain Ziegler's printed “40:20–29” column
label as historical records. That label is **not** a settled modern alignment
of every surviving clause. This follow-up supplies the qualification rather
than silently changing the frozen comparison.

## Positive altar mentions and the proposed history

On [71–72](https://books.google.com/books?id=yiX7-CsSDK0C&pg=PA71), Bogaert
prints two Latin contexts: the damaged inventory at 27/2/9–27 and the
continuous placement command at 28/2/1–14. The latter agrees with Ziegler's
upright gold-altar command at printed 28. An altar without a separate surviving
construction report is not an altar absent from the narrative.

Bogaert interprets the inventory order and absence of an intervening inner
veil as placing the gold altar with the ark in the Holy of Holies. That is an
interpretation of narrative order and wording, not an explicit surviving
Latin sentence naming that room. His proposed sequence on 75–76 goes further:
an initial account without a gold altar, its introduction, fuller Hebrew
coordination of the two altars, and Greek revision toward Hebrew. The Latin
does not directly preserve all those stages; his 65 also acknowledges an
unresolved problem concerning the lost Latin instructions in chapters 25–31.
The sequence must remain a historical hypothesis, not a reconstructed source
corpus or a basis for deleting POB's construction and placement clauses.
