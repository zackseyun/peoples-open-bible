# Obadiah 1 7 and 1 21 source comparison

Two passages offer useful, meaning-bearing comparisons without warranting a
replacement of POB's Hebrew or main English. Verse 7 has positive published
Hebrew support for wording absent or different in the selected Greek. Verse 21
contrasts agents of deliverance with people who have been delivered. Its published
unpointed Hebrew does not decide between those interpretations. Source priority
and English replacement remain HOLD; retaining the current text provisionally
is not a finding that it is optimal.

This is a two-unit published-source comparison, not fresh manuscript decipherment,
a complete Obadiah collation, or a new discovery. It follows the
[adjudication method](TEXTUAL_ADJUDICATION_METHOD.md) and the
[transcription policy](../sources/OPEN_TRANSCRIPTION_POLICY.md).

## Hebrew preservation and supplied text

[Qumran Digital Mur. 88, version 2026-05-21](https://lexicon.qumran-digital.org/transcriptions/Mur._88/2026-05-21/index.html?v=2026-05-21),
column 9 line 12, transcribes **לחמך** and **בו** without brackets or uncertainty
marks on those words. This supports the bread consonants and singular suffix,
not the vowels, the suffix's precise referent, or every letter of the surrounding
clause. The nearby peace word is supplied and several surrounding letters are
uncertain. These are published readings, not independently inspected ink.

For verse 21, column 9 line 34 has **מ֯שעים**, preserving the publisher's uncertain
mem and defective spelling. It is unpointed, unlike POB's pointed WLC. It cannot
certify the active or passive vocalization. The line also contains **בה֯ר ציון**;
the place preposition is not the Greek equivalent of “from.” Line 35's final
clause is partly supplied, not complete positive attestation of every word.

[Qumran Digital 4Q82, same version](https://lexicon.qumran-digital.org/transcriptions/4Q82/2026-05-21/index.html?v=2026-05-21),
fragment 70–75 lines 13–14, places the decisive verse 7 bread wording and final
pronoun inside supplied brackets. They are not surviving 4Q82 support. Parallel
Mur. 88 and WLC quotations underneath the primary rows are cross-references,
not additional 4Q82 ink or independent votes. A verse tag is therefore insufficient
to count preserved agreement. This comparison makes no claim about 4Q82 verse 21
coverage.

## Greek differences that affect interpretation

The publisher's [Rahlfs Hanhart 2006 Obadiah](https://www.die-bibel.de/bibel/LXX/OBA.1)
has no explicit bread expression in verse 7 and ends with **αὐτοῖς**, a plural
“them,” rather than the Hebrew singular suffix. Its trap or ambush language
offers a semantic parallel, not a uniquely recoverable Hebrew spelling.

Verse 21 has **ἄνδρες σεσῳσμένοι**, men who have been saved, and **ἐξ ὄρους Σιων**,
from Mount Zion. This differs from POB's active “Deliverers” and “on Mount Zion.”
The Greek purpose verb also requires its own assessment; adopting only the
passive interpretation does not authorize reconstructing all other Greek
differences as Hebrew.

[Swete volume III, printed page 47](https://archive.org/download/theoldtestamenti03swetuoft_202003/theoldtestamenti03swetuoft/page/n70_w1500.jpg)
selects **ἀνασωζόμενοι**, a present middle/passive form involving rescue or escape,
not the identical perfect passive form selected by Rahlfs Hanhart. Its apparatus
reports *ανδρες σεσωσμενοι* for A and *ανδρες εσωσμενοι (sic)* for Q's first hand.
This is positive published Greek variation, not direct inspection of those
manuscripts. An unresolved tiny corrector superscript is excluded. The selected
text is not automatically attributed to a physical manuscript without consulting
the edition's controlling description.

## Competing explanations and present decision

For verse 7, the Greek may simplify a difficult Hebrew phrase, or reflect a
different Hebrew exemplar. Mur. 88 makes bread plus the singular ending a
positively attested Hebrew combination, so deleting bread or substituting a
plural ending solely to match this selected Greek is not justified. That does
not establish the earliest form of either tradition. POB's “Those who eat your
bread” is an interpretation of the terse construction, not a word-for-word
transcription. [NET's own verse 7 note](https://classic.net.bible.org/verse.php?book=Oba&chapter=1&verse=7)
proposes revocalization for the bread-fellowship reading and recognizes uncertainty
in the trap noun. Ellipsis and revocalization must be distinguished in any
subsequent lexical adjudication.

For verse 21, the pointed Masoretic reading gives deliverers; a passive reading
gives delivered people. [NET's own textual note](https://classic.net.bible.org/verse.php?book=Oba&chapter=1&verse=21)
explicitly proposes the passive vocalization. Its references to Aquila, Theodotion
and Syriac are mediated reports here, not three directly collated witnesses.
Greek could represent an alternative Hebrew vocalization or a translator's
interpretation; subsequent Greek variation is another layer. The unpointed
Hebrew does not distinguish these explanations. Agreement among modern English
translations is not additional ancient evidence.

The practical difference is who ascends: rescuers or rescued people. POB's
existing theological explanation of active human agents presupposes its chosen
vocalization; it cannot establish that choice. Neither interpretation by itself
identifies these people as a particular messianic figure or settles a doctrine.
Source replacement and an English preference remain held pending a bounded
comparison of the Hebrew construction, relevant translation practice and the
Greek apparatus. A fuller apparatus and actual target readings for the reported
versions could discriminate between explanations; more repetitions of the same
selected editions would not.

## Reader issues and next application boundary

The current YAML has identifiable note-anchor problems. In verse 7, the trap
note b belongs with “trap,” and the pronoun note c with “him,” not with “prevailed
against you.” In verse 21, note a belongs with “Deliverers,” note b with Esau's
hill country, and note c with the kingdom clause. No marker, note, source,
English, metadata or About content is changed by this research packet.

A separate, frozen application can correct the anchors and disclose the
verse 21 passive alternative while retaining the present main text. It must
pin the actual baseline, distinguish Hebrew vocalization from Greek wording,
review exact proposed notes once, and validate the complete reader export.
It must not describe a disclosure as recovered Hebrew or approved source
priority. These passages do not provide evidence for adding a book to a canon;
textual transmission and community reception remain separate questions.
