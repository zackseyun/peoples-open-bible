# Jeremiah 27 heading image check in Reuchlin 3

The actual Reuchlin 3 page appears to show Jehoiakim in its current Hebrew heading. Ordinary color-image inspection does not securely recover an earlier name. This does not independently verify or refute de Rossi's reported initial Zedekiah reading: a published report about an earlier state and a photograph of the current state are different evidence.

The [prior source-selection hold](JEREMIAH_27_1_HEADING_DISPOSITION_2026-10-10.md) remains in force. Neither canonical Hebrew nor English changes. The [observation record](../sources/textual_restoration/controls/2026-10-10-reuchlin3-jer27/observation.v1.json) stores the tentative readings, disagreement, image pins and limits. ## Current writing and unresolved earlier state

One acquisition agent tentatively reads the current name as יהויקם. Root tentatively reads יהויקים. Both identify Jehoiakim, but the exact consonantal spelling disagreement remains unresolved and neither spelling is promoted into accepted source text. Lighter or irregular ink and nearby surface marks do not establish an erased Zedekiah, a particular correcting hand, or its date.

The Hebrew heading's middle phrase is visible in the next physical row. Root initially mistook that row for Aramaic and questioned whether the phrase was absent. A single targeted reinspection distinguished the final nun in בן and resolved the extent question. The absence hypothesis was withdrawn. Following Targum material is Aramaic, not additional Hebrew continuation or an independent Hebrew witness.

Both observers had passage context and belong to the same model family. There was no blinded two-family transcription or calibrated error measurement. The useful contribution is an accessible, pinned primary image and a narrower account of what it can establish—not consensus certification of difficult marks.

## What could change the source decision

De Rossi's initial-state report remains separately attributed to the printed edition. The current photograph alone neither recovers that earlier state nor proves the report mistaken. Any historical inference needs independently justified manuscript-state and hand evidence, a modern local apparatus, or genuinely higher-information imaging. More ordinary enlargement cannot recreate erased information.

The comparison still distinguishes correcting a name within the heading from adopting a shorter literary form without it. This inspection warrants neither operation. It is not a novel manuscript discovery, an autograph recovery, a canon decision, or an applied English revision.
