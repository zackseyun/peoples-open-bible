# 1 Samuel 1:24–25: testing the narrative behind the animal proposal

## Decision

The [exact animal-only proposal](SAMUEL_1_24_EXACT_SOURCE_PROPOSAL_2026-10-15.md)
remains a useful research comparison, **not a selected whole-verse source**.
Newly consulted arguments make its inherited short narrative a substantive
contested choice, not merely untouched background. Keep the moderate semantic
preference for an age-qualified bull; do not raise confidence in the exact short
Hebrew phrase, remove the bread, or select a narrative by agreement counting.
No canonical Hebrew, English, notes or approval metadata change in this pass.

This is progress beyond the committee record: named local expansion hypotheses
and an omission countercase can now be tested. The next selection must compare
the connected 1:24–25 scene, including participants and verbal number. The
frozen animal-only JSON remains unchanged as a historical test candidate.

## New primary studies and consultation boundary

Christian Seppänen, *The Hebrew Text of Samuel: Differences in 1 Sam 1 –
2 Sam 9 between the Masoretic Text, the Septuagint, and the Qumran Scrolls*
(Helsinki, 2018), ISBN 978-951-51-4297-9:
[institutional PDF](https://helda.helsinki.fi/server/api/core/bitstreams/6e381d49-3bc5-4e67-a8d2-b2afa567a1a2/content).
Root visually read complete printed pp.6–7, 31, 38, 40 and 52, including notes;
method/source pp.1–5 were read as extracted text. Not the whole dissertation.

Seppänen treats the herd phrase and bread as expansions, connecting the latter
to Leviticus 23:15–21. He nevertheless prefers the longer narrative: the
repeated boy expression could result from an eyeskip, followed by a change
in verbal number. His identification of Eli as the later slaughterer's
subject is an interpretation, not an explicit name at that Greek verb.
He also acknowledges rival explanations for the opening. This is scholarship
using editions, not new ink or our independent consultation of his cited
Aejmelaeus study. See pp.6–7, 31, 38, 40, 52; notes 8, 97, 138–140, 148.

Zipora Talshir, “Textual Criticism at the Service of Literary Criticism and
the Question of an Eclectic Edition of the Hebrew Bible,” BETL 246 (2012),
33–60: [author-uploaded chapter](https://www.academia.edu/3713119/2012_Textual_criticism_at_the_service_of_Literary_Criticism).
Root read the complete target section VI.1, pp.43–45 and notes 21–28 in HTML.
No PDF or visually verified Appendix 1 table is claimed.

Talshir favors the short age expression but treats the enlarged sacrificial
scene as a cultic revision. Her bread parallel is Leviticus 7:13, not the
same passage used above. She disputes the reconstructed blank in DJD and
links the boy's presence to changes in the opening and participants. Her
own short-scene proposal also changes the presentation verb to feminine
singular; it does not endorse our unchanged verse 25. These are competing
literary/transmission arguments, not two independent manuscript votes.

## Direct controls on the arguments

### Bread and herd language: resemblance is real, a unique donor is not

Root extracted the words of WLC Leviticus 4:3, 7:12–14, 23:8 and 23:15–21
from `sources/ot/wlc/Lev.xml`, stripping combining marks and segmentation
slashes for comparison. The actual source and its punctuation remain intact.

| Control | Observation from the retained Hebrew | Discriminating limit |
|---|---|---|
| Leviticus 4:3 | `פר בן בקר` occurs in a sin-offering instruction. | The herd formula is real, but its occurrence is not confined to the proposed bread donor. |
| Leviticus 7:12–14 | Bread occurs with thanksgiving/peace offerings. | This is a plausible cultic association, not a complete match for Samuel's inventory or proof of copying. |
| Leviticus 23:15–21 | Bread, a bull, other animals and offerings belong to a specified festival; verse 17 specifies two loaves and verse 18 one bull. | The detailed inventory and setting are not Samuel's. Common sacrificial vocabulary permits an expansion hypothesis without demonstrating one. |
| Leviticus 23:8 versus 23:18 | The herd phrase occurs in 23:18, not 23:8. | Seppänen p.6 n.8 prints 23:8. Record the mismatch; do not silently treat that citation as a verified occurrence. |

Our inference: bread plus flour is not inherently redundant, and an unnumbered
item need not be secondary merely because its neighbors are measured. Neither
feature alone excludes an original offering list. Conversely, a scribe could
adapt only part of familiar sacrificial language; the imperfect match does not
refute expansion. The new arguments supply mechanisms worth testing, not a
decisive reason to delete the preserved bread or herd word.

### The proposed omission: specify the joins, not just the catchword

The current WLC ending is `והנער נער`, followed by plural slaughter and
presentation verbs. The [Greek edition control](https://www.die-bibel.de/bibel/LXX/1SA.1)
at 1:24–25 has the boy with them, presentation before the Lord, the father's
recurring sacrifice, presentation of the boy, a singular slaughter verb,
and Hannah's approach to Eli. It does not name Eli at the singular slaughter
verb. This digital edition is not another ancient witness or a fresh full
apparatus collation.

To test the bare omission claim, distinguish two illustrative string joins:

```text
Input schema only:        והנער X הנער Y
after נער -> after נער:  והנער Y
after נער -> recopy נער: והנער נער Y
```

Here X and Y are placeholders, **not a proposed Hebrew reconstruction**.
Root executed both joins and checked their exact outputs. The same-end skip
does not yield the doubled noun. A restart that recopies the second anchor
can yield it, so this check does not show that an omission is impossible.
Neither operation changes singular slaughter to plural, selects the opening,
or fixes the final presentation clause. A complete account must state those
additional decisions. Mechanical possibility is not evidence that it occurred.

The [versioned 4Q51 transcription](https://lexicon.qumran-digital.org/transcriptions/4Q51/2025-03-11/index.html),
fragments 2a–d, lines 8–11, independently constrains what can be claimed from
the publication: line 8 preserves the boy expression before the gap; the
following accompaniment and father clauses are supplied; line 9 preserves
part of the sacrifice expression; line 10 retains the end of a slaughter
verb. Line 11's presentation to Eli is supplied before surviving speech.
Thus the whole longer scene, exact second catchword and named agent are not
all surviving Hebrew. This consultation checks published brackets, not new
letter readings, line-length measurements or the DJD blank's physical extent.

## Consequence for source and English selection

For a critical source, leaving a base reading untouched is still a choice.
The earlier JSON deliberately exposes its inherited short framework. It must
not now be promoted as the earliest attainable scene solely because its animal
phrase has support. Nor should the competing longer scene be imported wholesale
from Greek or a modern reconstruction. Our own criteria require explanatory
fit across the connected differences and explicit surviving/supplied labels.

For English, the current “three bulls” remains aligned with the declared WLC.
The research alternative “a three-year-old bull” remains available. A future
longer-scene selection would also affect who accompanies whom, the sequence
of sacrifices and the presentation to Eli; it is not an animal-word swap.
No new confidence percentage, recovered autograph, canon inference or
ImageGen-derived evidence follows from these studies.

Next discriminating work: compare the actual DJD line-9–11 reconstruction
and its blank with the Greek alternatives, and construct explicit competing
transition accounts for the whole scene. Do not rerun the same committee
ratings or repeatedly retry denied downloads. Neither a source-image review
nor a better reconstruction alone determines which literary form came first.
