# Genesis 4:13 — punishment, guilt and forgiveness

## Outcome and application boundary

A candidate-identity-blinded assessment prefers “My punishment is greater
than I can bear” in the immediate narrative context. “My guilt is too great
to forgive” remains a serious alternative. Neither is uniquely forced by the
selected Hebrew. This is a contextual translation preference, not recovered
letters, a new manuscript discovery, or proof of Cain's mental state. The
qualified English choice was applied locally on October 9 after the separate
full-record review and reader-export checks described below.

The canonical verse is **unchanged in this comparison**. Its main English says
“My iniquity is too great to bear,” while its lexical and theological choices
already prefer punishment and bearing. That mismatch warrants a full-record
application review, not automatic adoption based on old metadata. The existing
reader note is anchored after Yahweh although it concerns the lament; an
application should repair that anchor and preserve the contrary interpretation.

## Evidence actually inspected

The [frozen contract](../sources/textual_restoration/comparisons/genesis4_13_contract.2026-10-06.v1.json)
pins the baseline verse and WLC Genesis, Numbers and Lamentations XML hashes.
Root read the full canonical record and the actual Hebrew for Genesis 4:11–15,
Genesis 50:17, Numbers 14:19,34 and Lamentations 4:6.

- Genesis 4:11–12 announces loss of livelihood and wandering; 4:14 describes
  expulsion, separation and danger of being killed. The protection in 4:15
  answers danger without explicitly declaring pardon.
- Genesis 50:17 and Numbers 14:19 establish genuine forgiveness uses of נשא.
  The former names wrongdoing; the latter concerns pardon for people, not an
  identical object construction. Numbers 14:34 associates bearing iniquities with forty-year
  consequences. These support competing senses, not mechanically identical
  translations in every occurrence.
- Lamentations 4:6 is a catastrophe comparison, but its own offense/punishment
  ambiguity makes it insufficient to settle Genesis independently.
- The actual [Rahlfs/Hanhart selected Greek](https://www.die-bibel.de/bibel/LXX/GEN.4),
  including its edition footer and 4:11–15 context, reads at 4:13:
  Μείζων ἡ αἰτία μου τοῦ ἀφεθῆναί με. Its release/forgiveness interpretation
  is consequential contrary evidence; it does not uniquely recover Hebrew
  consonants. This was an edition-body comparison, not manuscript-hand collation.
- Root read the actual [NET verse and author notes](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Gen.4.13&version=NET).
  Their contextual punishment argument and discussion of comparative מן are
  interpretive evidence, not another ancient witness or proof of psychology.
  Direct classic NET endpoints timed out; the alternate presentation supplied
  the consulted body. No new BDB, HALOT or grammar-body consultation is claimed.

עוני can be construed through wrongdoing, guilt or consequence; נשא can bear
or forgive/release in relevant constructions. “Punishment” selects the
consequence sense. “I can” supplies a contextual bearer rather than translating
an explicit first-person subject on the infinitive. A preference must disclose
these costs, not call this English an ambiguity-free literal rendering.
