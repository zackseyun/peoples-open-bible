# Kings numeral witnesses and the dating of their readings

## Judgment

The original Holmes–Parsons apparatus confirms reported exceptions to a
perfect association between 480 and ἐγένετο at 1 Kings 6:1. Its own keys
also resolve the apparent witness-number problem: historical-book 144
redirects to 130. A modern lookup of 144 alone cannot invalidate that report.
These are published-edition and identity findings, not newly read manuscript
letters or an independently dated pre-Christian 480.

Retain the [connected comparison's provisional Hebrew and English](KINGS_6_TEMPLE_CHRONOLOGY_2026-10-18.md).
The combination of a numeral and a verb may inform a transmission hypothesis,
but these reports do not demonstrate that the two entered a lineage at the
same time. Nor does the methodological counterargument prove a late correction.

## What Holmes and Parsons actually report

The [public split facsimile](https://archive.org/details/VetusTestamentumGraecumCumVariisLecVol101Genesis)
contains *Vetus Testamentum Graecum*, volume II (1818). Its 3 Kings PDF
page 28 has the complete unnumbered target page, headed ΒΑΣΙΛΕΙΩΝ Γ,
chapter VI. The base has καὶ ἐγενήθη and 440. Its local apparatus yields:

| Printed witness | Verb | Numeral | Nature of the report |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 44 | ἐγένετο δὲ | 440 | Verb explicitly reported; numeral inherited from the base |
| Historical-book 144 | καὶ ἐγένετο | 440 | Verb explicitly reported; numeral inherited from the base |
| 246 | καὶ ἐγενήθη | 480 | Verb inherited from the base; eighty component explicitly reported |

These are not three separately printed complete manuscript transcriptions.
Silence within a selective apparatus is weaker than direct examination of
the manuscripts. Nevertheless, the reported cross-pairings contradict an
exclusive association at that edition's reporting level. For 44, preserve
the different conjunction order rather than silently writing καὶ ἐγένετο.
The adjoining report for 71 omits the whole clause and cannot supply a
missing numeral vote. A Complutensian edition report is not an extra codex.

The volume-II appendix's inspected III Regum section has no chapter-VI
entry. This bounds that addenda check; it is not an exhaustive audit of
every correction in all five volumes or of the collators' original notebooks.

## Correcting the witness identity

The Joshua preface, PDF page 2, explicitly sends 144 to volume I's 130 and
describes a numbering interchange. The final manuscript catalogue in
volume V (1827), addenda PDF pages 4–5, says that 130 was elsewhere
incorrectly designated 144; it separately lists another manuscript as 144.
Its entries also identify 44 as Zittaviensis and 246 as Vaticanus 1238.
The date of Joshua's preface, 1809, is distinct from volume II's 1818 titlepage.

The current Göttingen catalogue's actual public data identify
[Ra 130](https://septuaginta.uni-goettingen.de/catalogue/Ra_130/) as Vienna,
ÖNB Theol. gr. 23, a late-thirteenth-century Bible with Reigns I–IV at
folios 144v–199v. [Ra 144](https://septuaginta.uni-goettingen.de/catalogue/Ra_144/)
is a different record, Theol. gr. 228. The historical correction and the
current identities must be kept separate from a new reading of either copy.
No exact 6:1 folio or scribal hand was verified here. A catalogue's broad
book range does not prove survival or wording at the target.

This resolves the bare number-based objection mentioned in Smith's 2024
footnote 32; it does not certify all of his witness dates or collations.
The original report must be read with the correct edition-local alias,
not discarded because the same number denotes another object elsewhere.

## The original debate about ancestry

Law and Kauhanen's [2010 response](https://ccat.sas.upenn.edu/ioscs/journal/volumes/bioscs43.pdf),
*BIOSCS* 43, 73–87, disputes treating either Vaticanus or the Antiochene
text as the original Greek throughout a section. Their controls include
a proposed older Antiochene reading in non-kaige 3 Reigns 8:24 and an older
Vaticanus reading in kaige 2 Reigns 19:13. Their reconstructed original at
19:10 lies outside both forms; that reconstruction is itself disputed,
not additional surviving manuscript evidence. They also distinguish
potentially corrected Old Latin margins and ask which way dependence
between Symmachus and Antiochene readings runs.

Kreuzer's [2009 argument](https://ccat.sas.upenn.edu/ioscs/journal/volumes/bioscs42.pdf),
*BIOSCS* 42, 34–51, explains several patterns as formal Hebrew alignment
of an older Antiochene text. His 2 Kings 6 controls invoke Old Latin support
for such features as man-of-God and heart wording. His inference extends
beyond the exact limits of surviving fragments and quotations, while allowing
corruptions and some revision. A statistical resemblance alone has no date.

His full rejoinder, *BIOSCS* 43, 89–95, matters to the assessment. It asks
for concrete reasons to reject the particular Old Latin evidence, challenges
isolated judgments detached from a wider transmission profile, and contests
the need for the 19:10 conjecture. It also acknowledges competing close
witnesses in non-kaige sections. Local examination should therefore use
transmission context without making that context an automatic verdict.

Neither article nor the rejoinder analyzes 1 Kings 6:1's 440/480 contrast
or dates its joint numeral–verb distribution. Kreuzer's 2009 table includes
a different local verb contrast at 2 Samuel 15:5; it must not be promoted
to a target-specific explanation. These papers inform how to test the
argument; they do not add manuscripts or settle the target numeral.

## Consequence and stopping rule

The [new packet](../sources/textual_restoration/comparisons/kings6_verb_ancestry.2026-10-18.v1.json)
records sources, scopes, aliases and unchanged inputs. The original studies
were fully read by a source scout; root separately checked their relevant
arguments and complete key renders, the Holmes–Parsons target and identity
pages, and the current catalogue data. This is not a claim that root read
the complete journals, volumes or all underlying studies.

The next discriminating evidence is a direct target collation or critical
discussion that connects the numeral and verb to the same transmission
stage, with the exceptions and later corrections accounted for. An inherited
verb can coexist with a later numeral change; equally, a shared Hebrew
reading can be genuinely early. These remain competing possibilities.
Stop repeating the acquired keys, target page and methodological debate.
Keep the first report and packet frozen as historical snapshots.

No source, main English, canon, reader, publisher or About change is applied.
No new photographs, restored strokes or generative-image evidence are claimed.
The current on-demand provenance design remains separate from source judgment;
no additional inline disclosure is implemented.
