# 1 Samuel 1:24–25: an exact research proposal, not recovered wording

## Result

Follow-up: the [passage-specific narrative countercase](SAMUEL_1_24_NARRATIVE_COUNTERCASE_2026-10-15.md)
now tests competing literary arguments and their sacrificial parallels.
It leaves this frozen candidate unchanged, but bars treating its inherited
short narrative as settled merely because the animal alternative is favored.

The age-related bull remains the preferred **reading to investigate**, with
moderate confidence at the semantic level. This pass makes the previously
unspecified alternative testable: an unpointed WLC-based verse with only
`בפרים שלשה` replaced by reconstructed `בפר משלש`. It does **not** select
that exact phrase as the earliest Hebrew, approve its canonical application,
or raise confidence merely because a committee preferred it.

The [proposal record](../sources/textual_restoration/decisions/samuel1_24_research_source.2026-10-15.v1.json)
binds the baseline, exact patch, resulting source and prospective English.
The [existing dossier](SAMUEL_SOURCE_COMPARISON_PASS_3.md) supplies the prior
manuscript, edition, image and grammatical work. Its old receipts and gates
remain unchanged. Canonical verses 24 and 25 remain WLC, with “three bulls”
and “the bull” respectively.

## Three decisions, not one package vote

**Animal.** Published 4Q51 preserves `בקר משלש ולחם`, with `[בפר בן ]`
supplied before it. Thus the age word is not simply retroverted from Greek.
The Greek age expression agrees semantically, with possible textual
dependence. The previously checked Syriac has an age-related singular animal
without bread and with a short narrative. This supports testing the proposed
combination, but does not establish an independent third Hebrew exemplar.

There are at least two different age-related Hebrew proposals: the short
`בפר משלש` and the fuller published reconstruction `[בפר בן ]בקר משלש`.
The short proposal leaves out **surviving `בקר`**, not just supplied letters.
Its rationale is an economical hypothesis for an ancestor of the MT animal
expression, with the herd expression in 4Q51 possibly an expansion. Neither
that expansion nor its reverse has been demonstrated. The shorter Greek
expression does not uniquely decide the Hebrew length. Therefore the short
form is chosen as a clearly specified test candidate, **not because economy
proves priority or because it is a complete 4Q51 reading**.

**Bread.** The proposal retains the base's absence of bread. This is a
separate conservative editorial choice while priority remains unresolved,
not a consequence of changing the animal. Early published Hebrew and Greek
support bread; MT and the Syriac control lack it. HOTTP factor 13 supplies a
category of explanation, but no target-specific account of which expressions
were combined. We cannot claim demonstrated conflation, dismiss bread as a
late interpolation, or infer omission just from the animal agreement.

**Narrative.** The proposal explicitly retains the WLC framework: weaning,
Hannah taking the child, the child's youth, then slaughter and presentation
with plural agents. It does not import Greek changes to the opening or
ending, or the longer father/mother sacrifice sequence. The Syriac control
shows that an age animal can coexist with a shorter narrative; it does not
prove this proposed Hebrew composite existed. Longer Greek wording can
clarify participants and actions, while loss from an earlier longer account
remains a rival explanation. The available handbook recommendation does not
resolve that history. Neither automatic preference for the shorter text nor
an assumed single recension decides all three units.

## Transmission and the strongest countercase

One possible age-to-number path is redivision of `בפרמשלש` as `בפרם שלש`,
followed by spelling and grammatical changes yielding `בפרים שלשה`. The
intermediate is **hypothetical and unattested in the consulted evidence**;
this illustrates the additional changes the familiar word-division argument
requires. It is not proof of that sequence or of continuous writing in an
ancestor. HOTTP's broad factor 12 does not independently attest these steps.

The reverse remains viable: three bulls is grammatical; verse 25 can refer
to one of them or use a collective singular. Contextual interpretation could
favor one age-qualified animal, possibly assisted by familiar sacrificial
language. No exact donor passage or observed act of harmonization is proved.
The early Hebrew age word must be accounted for in that explanation, rather
than dismissed as a Greek mistranslation. Conversely, its antiquity does not
prove that the age reading preceded every three-bull ancestor.

The already checked Numbers 21:6–7 collective construction remains a relevant
counter-control, not a second Samuel manuscript. A bounded renewed search did
not find lawful full text of Ratner's 1987 article. It rediscovered the same
Yale 1993 excerpt already documented in September; that is not new evidence
or original Ratner consultation. No confidence increase is assigned to it.

Sensitivity: remove the new committee recommendation and the earlier moderate
semantic preference survives, but the short candidate loses an independently
identified editorial precedent. Remove singular verse 25 as a discriminator
and the Hebrew/Greek age evidence survives. Remove Greek and Syriac support
and published 4Q51 still establishes an age-related variant, but cannot choose
our short phrase or establish its priority over MT. Remove the modest age
preference and the exact-form judgment remains unresolved.

## Complete proposed source and English consequence

Target: a **research-only eclectic consonantal verse within the retained
short narrative**, not a diplomatic scroll edition. Normalization removes
combining marks and segmentation slashes only. Spaces, word order, inherited
maqaf and final punctuation remain base conventions, not recovered ink.
Unchanged units retain the base's interpretation without new apparatus-wide
approval. No ancient vowel points are supplied for the changed phrase.

`ותעלהו עמה כאשר גמלתו בפר משלש ואיפה אחת קמח ונבל יין ותבאהו בית יהוה שלו והנער נער־׃`

The prospective English changes only the animal phrase:

> When she had weaned him, she took him up with her, with a three-year-old
> bull, one ephah of flour, and a skin of wine, and brought him to the house
> of Yahweh at Shiloh. Now the boy was young. Then they slaughtered the bull
> and brought the boy to Eli.

The record also supplies the complete 1:22–25 English comparison context,
with current footnote markers removed for comparison only. No existing notes
are deleted or approved for reuse. A future reader note must disclose MT's
three bulls, the surviving versus supplied 4Q51 material, bread, and the
reconstructed short source form. A synchronized verse-25 metadata correction
would be necessary if verse 24 ever changed: its current rationale correctly
describes today's three-bull POB baseline and would otherwise become stale.

## What is closed and what remains open

Closed: direct interim-committee consultation; separation of its two ratings;
one exact full-verse test proposal; and an explicit account of the inherited
bread/narrative decisions and competing transmission explanations.

Still open: exact source priority, full literary-form argument, original
Ratner consultation, remaining Greek/Syriac apparatus work and the previous
image/master/uncertain-feature gates. Published readings support this research
proposal under method 2.0.8, but no old gate is marked passed. A new physical
reading would still need the image work; a modern image cannot by itself
determine ancestry. An independent report-aware assessor agrees that the
candidate is testable, not canonically approved. This is not a blind vote.

Next obtain the final CTAT discussion or another primary, passage-specific
argument that discriminates the short animal phrase, surviving herd term,
bread and longer narrative; assess it against this exact candidate. Repeating
the same committee ratings or seeking more model agreement is not that work.
No ImageGen output, newly recovered letters, canon inference, whole-verse
optimum or all-OT completion is claimed.
