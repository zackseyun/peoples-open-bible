# Deuteronomy33:2: holy myriads, fire, law and angels

The bounded comparison provisionally prefers **“from holy myriads”** over
“from myriads of holiness” for the unchanged pointed Hebrew. Whether these
attendants are heavenly or human is not settled. The final clause's original
meaning remains held: “fiery law,” an alternative fire-verb analysis and the
Greek angel clause require distinct explanations, not a silent combination.

## What the Hebrew evidence preserves

Actual WLC distinguishes the written אשדת from two separately identified qere
words, fire and law. The current English already interprets the written form
through that reading tradition. It is not a newly discovered separate manuscript
variant, and the noun/lemma annotations do not establish historical priority.

The actual [4Q45 published transcription](https://lexicon.qumran-digital.org/transcriptions/4Q45/2026-05-21/index.html?v=2026-05-21),
fragments42–43 line1, preserves only the prefix מרבבו before the restoration
bracket. The final tav, holiness noun and entire disputed right-hand clause are
supplied. Complete physical lines1–7 were checked, not a clipped verse match.
[4Q39](https://lexicon.qumran-digital.org/transcriptions/4Q39/2026-02-11/index.html?v=2026-02-11)
covers part of the verse beginning, not its disputed tail.
[4Q175](https://lexicon.qumran-digital.org/transcriptions/4Q175/2026-05-21/index.html?v=2026-05-21)
starts its Deuteronomy33 quotation at33:8; it was checked rather than excluded
because it is an excerpt collection. Missing coverage is not an omission.
Qumran-Digital credits DFG465277421 and the Qumran-Wörterbuch/Abegg predecessor,
CC BY-SA4.0. These are published readings, not freshly inspected scroll pixels.

The actual [pinned Samaritan transcription](https://github.com/DT-UCPH/sp/tree/2f2120286ac48d4ff3d04e0107e33efd864aa9e1),
TF7.1.3 node405388, reads אשדות with a vav. Its late-Deuteronomy basis is
Garizim1, not Chester Beatty751. Qumran-Digital's separately printed Samaritan
parallel has אשדת without vav. Both controls are preserved; uniform Samaritan
agreement is not claimed, and this spelling's priority and meaning are not
settled. Actual README/used feature hashes match the existing pins. CC-BY-NC4.0
input rights are retained; no complete restricted corpus is added to Git.

## What Greek, Latin and linguistic arguments contribute

The [selected Greek edition](https://www.die-bibel.de/bibel/LXX/DEU.33)
uses Kades in the myriads colon and angels with him in the final colon. Kades
is not direct lexical support for translating the Hebrew noun as holy ones.
The [selected Latin edition](https://www.die-bibel.de/bibel/VUL/DEU.33)
has accompanying holy ones and fiery law in/at the right hand. Neither supplies
a uniquely recoverable Hebrew source, and their modern edition dates are not
ancient copy dates. No complete versional apparatus was collated.

[Gilhooly's authorial article](https://ccat.sas.upenn.edu/ioscs/bulletin/volumes/jscs50.pdf),
JSCS50(2017), pp155–159, was read completely. It makes the Greek angel clause
a serious contextual interpretation, with a tentative warriors cognate, rather
than an automatically dismissible addition. That also leaves an interpretive
gloss as a counter-explanation to different Hebrew wording. The cognate remains
uncertain, and later reception is not a chronological cause of the early Greek.

The actual [BDB fire](https://biblehub.com/bdb/784.htm) and
[law](https://biblehub.com/bdb/1881.htm) sections discuss the difficult law noun
and conjectural fire solutions. Those emendations cannot become attested letters.
No fresh HALOT or accessible BDB holiness-entry consultation is claimed.
Habakkuk3:3–5 provides Paran and radiant hand imagery, but thematic resemblance
does not prove an ashdat etymology. Deuteronomy28:49 has an uncontracted flying
verb with aleph; its different inflection is not a categorical disproof of a
proposed contraction in33:2.

Two bounded attempts did not obtain the Steiner/Leiman2009 chapter body. Its
preview is not treated as a fully inspected argument; the actual article above
provides a separately attributed report and objection, not substitute primary
consultation. The actual NET HTML and PDF page418 both print an eshhat proposal
attributed to Samaritan. This is unresolved against the pinned SP control, not
an established HTML-only glitch or a directly verified Samaritan reading.

## Separate outcomes and review

For the holy phrase, singular holiness qualifying myriads can be expressed as
“holy myriads” without replacing the Hebrew noun with plural holy ones. The
formal abstract English suggests counted units of holiness; fidelity is not
guaranteed by preserving that calque. “Myriads of holy ones” is a defensible
contextual interpretation, supported by verse3, but adds the beings explicitly.
The close gloss and concrete interpretation are both disclosed. No geographic
Meribah-Kadesh alteration is adopted.

For the final clause, the Masoretic qere and nearby Torah in verse4 provide a
real law countercase. The Persian-loan difficulty, divine-fire imagery and Greek
wording keep alternatives serious. “Fire flew” needs a verbal/contraction source
analysis; generic “flaming fire” can hide an emendation. Greek angels are not
unattested Hebrew words recovered by back-translation. Retaining fiery law is
conditional use of the pinned reading tradition, not its original-priority
victory. Removing the modest chronological tilt leaves both outcomes unchanged.

## Value and stopping rule

This produces a bounded English proposal and prevents a concrete false claim:
the inspected DSS fragments do not attest the disputed tail. It is not a novel
source-text discovery. Syriac, complete Targum editions, full Greek/Latin apparatus
and all Hebrew/Samaritan manuscripts remain uncollated here. Reopen final-clause
priority for discriminating textual or morphological evidence, not additional
agreement votes or repeated inaccessible downloads.

No canon inclusion follows from any of these readings or interpretations. The
[contributions/canon review](CONTRIBUTIONS_AND_CANON_REVIEW_2026-10-06.md)
keeps source reconstruction separate from a community's canon judgment. No
About-page publication or corpus-wide improvement claim is made.
