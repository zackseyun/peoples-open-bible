# Ezekiel: Melphictensis coverage and the four-part judgment

## Result

**Rahlfs 927 is not evidence at Ezekiel 39:29 in the consulted edition.**
Vaccari's chapter-39 fragments cover 39:8–18, not the disputed Spirit/wrath
clause. The same edition does provide an individual Greek witness at 5:2 and
5:12: quarter fractions, two burning portions at 5:2, and separate death/famine
portions followed by scattering then sword at 5:12. This differs from the
[P.Grenf. I 5 comparison](EZEKIEL_5_12_QUARTERS_COMPARISON_2026-10-14.md),
which supports quarters but sword before scattering in its surviving clauses.

These are newly checked published readings for POB, not newly discovered
variants or newly deciphered manuscript letters. They strengthen the
feature-by-feature Greek comparison, not a decision to replace the Hebrew.
POB's source, English and reader notes at 5:2, 5:12 and 39:29 remain unchanged.

## What the edition actually prints at 5:2 and 5:12

The edition's own signs control the reading. Printed pp.18–19 / PDF pp.26–27
say that square brackets supply letters cut away at the margin; angle brackets
supply unreadable letters on surviving parchment; underdots mark uncertain
readings. The apparatus uses angle brackets for supplied letters of either
kind. Thus apparatus and transcription notation must not be silently equated.

At **5:12**, printed p.27 / PDF p.35, P4 column b, folio 38v:

- Lines 2, 5 and 9 print the complete fraction `τεταρτον` without internal
  supply brackets or underdots. Line 12 gives `τεταρτ[ον`, with its ending
  supplied. This is substantially stronger fraction preservation than the
  mostly supplied first occurrence in P.Grenf. I 5.
- The sequence is death, famine, scattering, then falling by the sword.
  Parts of the surrounding words are supplied; this is not a completely
  intact verse. The scattering clause occupies lines 8–11 and the falling
  clause lines 12–15, so the ordering is represented, not inferred from the
  fraction alone.
- The pursuing-sword verb at line 16 is partly supplied. It must not become
  an independently recovered complete verb or an incidental POB revision.

At **5:2**, printed p.25 / PDF p.33, P2 column b, folios 37v/36r,
the printed passage has four fraction expressions, with supplies/uncertain
letters. The two burning clauses precede sword and scattering. The edition
therefore represents four-part groupings in both related passages, not just
one isolated numerical variant. Its apparatus, printed p.48 / PDF p.56,
explicitly contrasts the Hebrew third and its lack of the extra clause.

The local 5:12 apparatus, printed p.49 / PDF p.57, records the alternative
clause order and an asterisked attribution in other Greek evidence. Printed
p.46 / PDF p.54 defines the sigla, including P as Melphictensis and Q as
Marchalianus. These reports are not new direct readings of the named other
manuscripts, and their critical signs must not be transferred onto P4.

The added witness agrees with the selected Greek control on the four-part
grouping and scattering-before-sword order. P.Grenf. I 5's mixed agreement
remains significant: shared fractions do not imply identical clause history.
Neither manuscript count nor internal coherence determines whether Hebrew or
Greek preserves the earlier literary form. Before changing POB, compare the
actual modern critical discussion of both verses, explaining the linked
groupings, ordering and possible revision. No automatic Hebrew retroversion
or extra reader note follows from this acquisition.
