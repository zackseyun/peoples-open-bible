# Zephaniah 3 19 and 20 source comparison

Date: October10,2026. Baseline: `08930ce8b20e54896f153537bfd8d636eef21248`.

Retain the complete pointed Hebrew provisionally. Published direct Hebrew
supports the oppressors clause, with damaged letters qualified. The selected
Greek acts “in you, for your sake” and moves the shame content into a plural
future-passive clause across the modern verse boundary. Those are real
differences; they do not establish an exact lost Hebrew source or its priority.
No3:19/20 canonical record is changed by this source assessment.

## Direct Hebrew preservation

Root read every available line of 4Q77 fragmentf3 and Mur88 fragment21 in the
pinned private QDR transcription, not just normalized reference hits.
The [machine comparison](../sources/textual_restoration/comparisons/zephaniah3_19_20_source.2026-10-10.v1.json)
retains physical lines, supply context, uncertainty and acquisition pins.

In4Q77f3 line1, the opening “behold/I act” lies inside supply brackets.
The following object marker, “all” and afflicters word are outside that bracket
but carry uncertainty markings. Line2 preserves the gathering verb's last
letter, set-them, praise and the first letter of name. A bracket opens within
name and includes the rest of the geographic/shame tail. Normalized rows naming
those words do not make them preserved ink.

Mur88 lines15–17 also report the oppressors object sequence outside supplies,
though the verb, object marker and afflicters word carry marks. The shame noun
is unbracketed and unmarked; surrounding geographic words are marked. The
saving ending/object span is supplied. Neither witness provides a complete
undamaged verse or the received pointing. Their different preservation matters;
two manuscript identities are not two fully legible sentence votes.

The received final word is a shame noun with third-person plural suffix.
It does not separately spell a preposition, “where,” or a finite passive verb.
Current POB's “where they were put to shame” is a contextual expansion,
not a literal word sequence. Its adequacy remains separate from word preservation.

## Greek Latin and ancient interpretation

The version-control agent inspected the publisher's
[Rahlfs–Hanhart2006 Greek](https://www.die-bibel.de/bibel/LXX/ZEP.3)
and [Weber–Gryson2007 Latin](https://www.die-bibel.de/bibel/VUL/ZEP.3)
through3:18–20 and their edition footers. Greek's positive “in you/for your sake”
wording cannot be reduced to an absent object. Its plural future-passive shame
verb appears at publisher3:20. A numbering boundary is not an omission, and
the Greek subject is not explicitly named in that clause.

The same agent visually inspected complete SweteIII third1905 printed68–69
and the relevant apparatus, reusing the compact derivative. Swete's selected
honor clause lacks the added set/make verb that Rahlfs–Hanhart selects.
Swete reports the addition in ℵAQ. The two editions therefore must not be
quoted as identical whole verses. Limited apparatus reports are not complete
collation, same-hand reconstructions or unanimity. VolumeI conventions and
the latest local Göttingen apparatus remain unacquired.

Latin names all those who afflicted Zion and interprets the action as killing.
That is stronger than neutral Hebrew “do/deal with”; it does not prove another
Hebrew verb. Its shame expression remains a noun phrase before the following
gathering segment. No Latin apparatus or physical Latin hands are adjudicated.

[Jerome III §§55–64](https://monumenta.ch/latein/text.php?nf=1&rumpfid=Hieronymus%2C+Commentarii%2C+in+Sophoniam%2C+03&tabelle=Hieronymus)
interprets Greek's opening as Zion's vindication in§59 and assigns shame to
her former adversaries in§63. This supplies an ancient coherent interpretive
control, not restored Hebrew letters. Later bracketed manuscript annotations
are distinct from his own argument; his anti-Jewish polemic is not a criterion.

The strongest reason against importing the selected Greek is actual published
direct-Hebrew object wording, together with a viable Hebrew reading. The
strongest contrary case is that some Greek differences could come from a
different Hebrew form rather than translation alone. Neither source age,
selected-edition date nor theological appeal decides between those explanations.
No precisely verified physical-copy chronology is used here.

## English comparison and application boundary

[printed240](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Page:A_Hebrew_and_English_Lexicon_(Brown-Driver-Briggs).djvu/264)

a uniquely correct English phrase. No fresh HALOT consultation is claimed.

The [frozen English contract](../sources/textual_restoration/comparisons/zephaniah3_19_20_english_contract.2026-10-10.v1.json)
compares noun-imagery wording with “praised and renowned” in19 and “renowned
and praised” in20, preserving the pair's reversed order. This is a connected
same-source English trial, not a Greek-source adoption. Common shame, geography,
gathering-time and fortunes wording is not newly certified. One masked
assessment and same-reviewer reversed display constitute one vote; tie/hold
or retention is allowed.

Observable reader defects also remain: oppressors notes attach to “Behold”;
geography and shame notes attach to “outcast”; the shame explanation is wrongly
classified as a textual variant; the fortunes note attaches to bring-you-in.
A3:20 lexical rationale also contradicts actual Yahweh. Those defects must not
replace the source/English question with an easier annotation-only task.

The [recorded assessment](../sources/textual_restoration/comparisons/zephaniah3_19_20_english_result.2026-10-10.v1.json)
favored the explicit honor pair moderately in both display orders. Its strongest
objection is genuine: “praised” more readily suggests actual acclaim than being
an object of praise, and both predicates lose the Hebrew noun imagery. Context
warrants the additional explicitness moderately, not uniquely. Hebrew morphology
does not change; English predicate forms are a translation adjustment.
