in *Das Buch Ezechiel*, pp. 1–42, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1515/9783110624250-001),

The copyright page says 2020. The consultation covers the complete 11Q4
section on printed pp. 11–14, including its notes, not the whole chapter.

Fabry reports Herbert's approximately 11 cm column-width assumption and a
nearly 2 cm increase for the Greek-like 150-day addition at 4:4; he regards
the resulting omission argument as vague. His 4:9 display places 390, with
the Greek alternative in parentheses, inside a reconstruction gap. These
are a criticism of an argument and a published preservation boundary,
respectively—not direct inspection of Herbert's edition or manuscript ink.
Fabry also uses geometrical arguments elsewhere in this section; his criticism
does not reject reconstruction categorically.

## What this can and cannot decide

The 4:4 presence/absence question must not become direct evidence for the
different 390/190 question at 4:5 or 4:9. A numeral printed between restored
context boundaries is not a second Hebrew attestation. Nor can the roughly
13 cm result implied by one reported reconstruction be equated with Puech's
separately reported layout merely because the rounded widths coincide.
Different fragment placements and supplied text may underlie them.

Our inference is methodological: a fit can test a candidate only against
constraints established independently of that candidate. Fitting a preferred
text to an adjustable width is compatibility, not historical priority. A
criticism of one reconstruction also does not prove its rival.
