# Genesis 2 2 English wording and source disclosure

Date: October 10, 2026. Historical baseline:
`a4a37ccad1cccdf05a9d330b221f31232c834e41`.

## Working choice

Prefer “On the seventh day God finished” moderately as a local English
choice against the retained pointed Masoretic Hebrew. The independent masked
comparison prefers it modestly. “By the seventh day God had finished” remains
a plausible retrospective interpretation, not a grammatical impossibility.
The complete draft preserves that alternative and discloses the sixth-day
source difference separately. Historical source priority stays unresolved.

This is a change in how the retained source is presented, not recovered
Hebrew, a newly discovered reading or a claim of the uniquely best translation.
The [source comparison](GENESIS_2_2_SIXTH_SEVENTH_COMPARISON_2026-10-10.md)
and its frozen receipt remain unchanged. Application and exact-record review

## Frozen English comparison

The [contract](../sources/textual_restoration/comparisons/genesis2_2_english_contract.2026-10-10.v1.json)
pins the retained source, baseline, connected Genesis 1:31–2:3 and controlling
policy before evaluation. The first displayed round gives K then M; the second
reverses them. K is current by/had-finished and M proposed on/finished, but this
mapping is withheld from the reviewer until assessment. One fresh-context agent
reads the policy, contract, actual WLC context and primary grammar sections;
it does not inspect the target YAML, source report, historical reviews or PRs.
This is identity masking, not a statistically randomized or replicated test.

The [actual result](../sources/textual_restoration/comparisons/genesis2_2_english_result.2026-10-10.v1.json)
records M as modestly preferred in both orders. Rechecking order by the same
reviewer is one vote, not two. Source meaning and additions take priority over
naturalness; both candidates remain intelligible modern English.

M preserves the repeated seventh-day phrase with completion and cessation.
It does not supply a new creative act or a duration of work on that day.
Its principal cost is that English readers can hear a sequence after the
completed universe in 2:1. K makes the completed state and retrospective
relationship clearer, but puts an anterior/deadline interpretation in the main
wording. “By” does not itself establish completion before the day began.

The actual [GKC §111](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Gesenius%27_Hebrew_Grammar/111._The_Imperfect_with_W%C4%81w_Consecutive)
and [§119h](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Gesenius%27_Hebrew_Grammar/119._The_Subordination_of_Nouns_to_the_Verb_by_means_of_Prepositions)
support general narrative/contextual and temporal-preposition controls, not an
automatic English tense rule. Root's moderate preference is a translation
judgment: preserve the audible repeated day phrase while noting the plausible
retrospective interpretation. No theology-driven contradiction veto decides it.
The existing 2:3 seventh-day cessation provides limited consistency context;
adjacent records and their notes are not newly certified or rewritten.

## Complete draft and review boundary

The [complete candidate](../sources/textual_restoration/candidates/genesis2_2_reader.2026-10-10.v1.json)
reads:

> On the seventh day[a] God finished his work that he had done[b], and he rested
> on the seventh day from all his work that he had done.

Note a attaches to the completion day. It identifies the edited Samaritan
digital Hebrew and selected Rahlfs–Hanhart Greek sixth, retained Masoretic
seventh, seventh cessation in those controls, and the supplied—not surviving—
numerals in the published 4Q10 transcription. It does not claim complete version
apparatus, independently inspected Syriac or settled priority.

Note b attaches to the completed first clause and preserves by/had-finished
as a retrospective interpretation. This separates a different written numeral
from two English renderings of the same seventh-day Hebrew.

Two lexical entries change to match finished/on, with qualified grammar
rationales and no claim of fresh HALOT consultation. Five other lexical
entries and the generation metadata are preserved. The one theological
decision is revised to preserve tension and avoid asserting compulsory
completion before seventh. Seven exact baseline fields archive translation,
lexical/theological rationale, generation and prior reviews. Status is draft
and cross-check needs review; old agreement scores do not certify the candidate.
