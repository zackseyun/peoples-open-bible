“When I was young, before I went astray, I sought her.” The published 11Q5
unit does not contain Greek's explicit wisdom noun or prayer phrase.

Provisional research preference for 16: `הטיתי כמעט אוזני והרבה מצאתי לקח` —
“I inclined my ear a little and found much instruction.” The clause crosses
physical lines 13–14; Greek additionally has receiving and a reflexive benefit.

## Reader form and English decision

The applied reader passage uses the already available Swete Greek at all
four records. At 13 and 14, promote the exact stored Greek parallel to the
primary source; retain Kahana verbatim as a composite parallel. Greek 15 and
16 remain primary. This is a specified working literary form while Hebrew
adjudication continues, not a claim that Greek automatically overrides Hebrew.
The Q-based candidates above are not adopted as primary translation sources
through a license, schema-label or reconstruction workaround.

13 becomes “While I was still young, before I went astray, I sought wisdom
openly in my prayer.” 14 becomes “Before the temple I asked for her, and to
the end I will seek her.” Their old walking/learning and knowledge material
remains in the preserved Hebrew parallel and disclosure, rather than being
repeated alongside the Greek units. Semantic English at 15–16 is retained;
note anchors and source-form disclosures are repaired.

Compared with the previous reader passage, this changes the opening from
loving wisdom to seeking before straying, specifies temple-related petition
and continued pursuit, and removes unsupported cross-form repetition. It does
not prove a theological interpretation of straying or recover the Hebrew
behind every Greek phrase. The distinctive B form and Q-based alternatives
remain accessible. The source mismatch at 17 and full-poem priority remain
separate unfinished work.
