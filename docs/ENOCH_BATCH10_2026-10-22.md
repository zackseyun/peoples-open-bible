## What the source checks actually establish

**18:8:** the first consonant in the throne's upper-part word remains unclear.
The diplomatic source uses **ወ⟦?⟧ማሑ**, with a POB-added uncertain-consonant
marker, low confidence and structured uncertainty. This is not a glyph printed
by Charles or a claim that either competing consonant has been certified.
Actual Dillmann lexical evidence supports a provisional upper-part rendering,
not silently replacing the unresolved printed glyph. The mandatory explanation
makes that distinction explicit. Thus complete chapter treatment does not mean
every printed glyph is resolved.

**18:12:** the source's final nun-shaped form and negative survive unchanged.
The new metadata distinguishes the selected printed form, a reported Geʽez
water reading and edited Greek beyond; it does not present editor-reported
readings as freshly collated manuscripts.

**65:7:** actual **እምብለ** supports “without the earth,” not an invented dust
noun. At **67:2** no ark is inserted; **67:5** uses clamor rather than roaring,
and **67:10** retains the source's pleasure language.

**69:23:** the accepted lexical correction is **mist**, not frost; the separate
snow word stays snow. A separately reviewed metadata-only followup records
that Charles's outer brackets open in 69:22 and close in 69:24. Unit 23 is
inside that range but does not itself contain the delimiters. Source-only
translation prompts retain this context; neither neighboring wording nor a
specific theory of the brackets' purpose is approved by that metadata.

The independent 68–69 review first examined the primary photos, then the
candidate. Its full original assessment remains immutable; a later exact

Original adverse proposals and receipts are preserved.
