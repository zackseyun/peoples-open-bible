# Ezekiel 5:12: quarters, thirds and a revised Greek witness

## Result

**Retain POB's Hebrew and English provisionally.** The newly consulted
P.Grenf. I 5 editions establish a useful mixed Greek reading, not a recovered
Hebrew replacement. The papyrus supports a quarter fraction while its surviving
punishment clauses follow the Hebrew order. Neither agreement can be assigned
automatically to the entire witness. This is new evidence checked by this
project, not a newly discovered variant.

This bounded lead arose from the previously unadjudicated thirds/quarters
contrast in the [Ezekiel context comparison](EZEKIEL_36_OMISSION_MECHANISMS_2026-10-12.md).
It does not cover, or resolve, the Ezekiel 36–39/Papyrus 967 question.

## Identity and consulted sources

P.Grenf. I 5 = Rahlfs 922/0922 = TM 61986 = Bodleian MS. Gr. bibl. d. 4 (P).
It is a Greek papyrus codex fragment, not Papyrus 967 or a direct Hebrew copy.
Its catalogue span, Ezekiel 5:12–6:3, includes gaps and partial verses.

- Grenfell's 1896 first edition, printed pp.9–11, was visually read in the
  [publicly downloadable reprint](https://archive.org/download/alexandrianeroti00gren/alexandrianeroti00gren.pdf),
  PDF pp.27–29. The Archive metadata identifies this copy as a 1972 reprint.
- Francesca Schironi, *BASP* 52 (2015), 181–223, supplies a new collation and
  [author-hosted PDF](https://sites.lsa.umich.edu/schironi/wp-content/uploads/sites/259/2015/12/25_BASP-52_Origen.pdf).
  Root visually read complete printed pp.184–191, 200–203 and 216–219
  (PDF pp.3–6, 11–12, 19–20), including Figure 1 and Tables 1 and 3. The
  title/identity, additional relevant prose and appended errata were also
  extracted; this is not a claim to have read the complete article.
- The [4CARE catalogue](https://4care-skos.mf.no/4care-artefacts/559/) supplies
  identity/access corroboration. The re-edition's uncertainty controls dating:
  late third/early fourth century is Schironi's preference, not an exact date.
  Her p.218 n.82 clarifies that Fayum is acquisition provenance, not a verified
  excavation findspot. The catalogue's Ω is a modern edition, not another
  ancient manuscript; its oldest-witness language is not imported as a ranking.
- The [publisher's Rahlfs–Hanhart text](https://www.die-bibel.de/bibel/LXX/EZK.5)
  was checked at 5:2 and 5:12. It is an edition-specific Greek control, not a
  collation of all Greek copies. Schironi's Ω is Ziegler's edition; the two
  controls differ in the scattering verb and are not silently equated.

## What is and is not preserved

At printed p.185, Schironi's recto line 1 gives the first fraction as
`τ[ὸ τέτ]α̣[ρτόν]`: most of the fraction is supplied, with an uncertain alpha.
Line 3 gives `τέτ̣αρ̣τ̣[όν]`, much stronger published support for “quarter,”
although three letters are underdotted and its ending is supplied. Brackets
denote absent/unreadable material and underdots doubtful visible letters.
These are published readings, not a fresh transcription from target pixels.

Grenfell p.10 prints the second fraction more fully. His pp.9–10 explain that
remounting had already damaged writing and that much of his transcription
used a prior copy. Schironi p.184 reports further faded/unreadable letters in
her 2011 collation and subsequent image examination. Older and newer editions
therefore preserve different observation histories of **one object**; they
are neither two independent votes nor proof of machine recovery.

The beginning of 5:12 is outside the surviving fragment. Do not attribute its
first two punishment clauses, or four fully preserved portions, to this leaf.

| Feature at 5:12 | WLC Hebrew / current POB | Rahlfs–Hanhart Greek | P.Grenf. I 5 published recto 1–5 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Fraction | Third, three groups | Quarter, four groups | Quarter support in the latter clauses; first occurrence mostly supplied |
| Initial punishments | Pestilence and famine in one group | Death and famine in separate groups | Not preserved |
| Later clause order | Sword, then scattering | Scattering, then sword | Sword, then scattering |
| Pursuing sword | After the scattering clause | After the sword-fall clause | After scattering; wording partly supplied |

Both selected Greek passages, 5:2 and 5:12, use four portions; the vendored WLC
uses three in each. At 5:2 the selected Greek has two burning clauses. This
context makes a one-word fraction substitution in POB inadequate: any proposed
source change must explain both passages, the groupings and the order.
Internal coherence in either edition does not establish the direction of change.

## Critical signs and dependence

The papyrus's demonstrated asterisks occur at 5:14 (recto 12) and 5:16
(verso 4–5), marking material absent from Schironi's edited Greek control but
corresponding to Hebrew material. They are **not demonstrated on the 5:12
fractions or inverted clauses**. Figure 1 shows a different locus, recto 12;
it must not be presented as a photograph of the quarter reading.

Schironi's Table 3 and p.203 report that Marchalianus has similar wording but
also marks the 5:12 inversion, with an Aquila attribution. That later witness's
sign must not be transferred into the papyrus. Her p.191 also notes that a
gap prevents confirmation of an expected obelos at 5:14. Catalogue descriptions
of a system using asterisks and obeloi do not establish a surviving obelos here.

The revised/Hexaplaric context supplies a concrete alternative to assuming a
new Hebrew exemplar whenever Greek agrees with Hebrew. It neither invalidates
the papyrus's Greek evidence nor proves the quarter fraction was inserted by
a reviser. Schironi's conclusions explicitly leave uncertain whether this
edition originated with Origen himself or subsequent work associated with
Pamphilus/Eusebius. Our comparison does not resolve that historical attribution.

## Consequences and next decision

POB 5:12 currently renders three groups and sword-before-scattering, consistent
with its declared WLC source. No source, English, reader note or historical
approval changes in this pass. A future note could disclose the Greek's four
groups and ordering difference without claiming a recovered original. That
would require its own scoped candidate and actual reader-export review; the
existing misplaced lexical anchors are not repaired incidentally here.

Before selecting thirds or quarters historically, obtain the actual 5:2/5:12
critical apparatus and the argument referenced by Schironi (Olley 2009 p.264),
distinguishing inherited Greek, revision and possible Hebrew variation. A
bounded search did not acquire that target commentary. Do not repeat this
same edition comparison or count extra English translations as new witnesses.
The present evidence supports better variant disclosure, not a forced Hebrew
replacement or a change to biblical canon.

For restoration, distinguish genuinely missing material from faded ink and
earlier human observations. New target photographs or spectral captures could
test legibility; no such recovery was performed here. ImageGen was not run:
generated completion would not answer which letters remain on the papyrus.
This case implements feature-level agreement/dependence in the
[adjudication method](TEXTUAL_ADJUDICATION_METHOD.md), not another model vote.
