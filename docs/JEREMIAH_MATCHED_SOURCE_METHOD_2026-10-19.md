# Jeremiah matched source and Greek revision controls

Separate inherited Hebrew differences, ordinary translation and later Greek
revision before assigning a variant to the translator. Three local controls
now illustrate why. They refine the [previous parallel test](JEREMIAH_52_PARALLEL_CONTROL_2026-10-19.md),
not overturn its provisional retention of POB. No source, English, note,
approval, reader-layout or canon change follows.

## Two modern original studies

[Tucker's 2020 dissertation](https://helda.helsinki.fi/server/api/core/bitstreams/1b192bd6-23e1-43e6-b73e-0a3d6ebd32ce/content)
distinguishes shared translation features from directional revision. His
criteria examine syntax and semantics, not merely a list of different Greek
words. He permits incomplete or inconsistent revision and distinguishes some
more natural Greek from changes toward formal Hebrew correspondence. Within
his analyzed material, he rejects pervasive translator omission of large
passages. This directly contests Fischer's general abbreviation model; it does
not independently decide every omission. Root read printed pp. 57–58, 65–71
and 406–407 / PDF pp. 71–72, 79–85 and 420–421, including notes, and viewed
65–71 and 406–407 completely. This is not a full 440-page thesis audit or a
reading of the separate 2022 book. The cover identifies Helsinki 2020.

[Frohlich's original 2022 preview](https://api.pageplace.de/preview/DT0400.9783161610844_A42952315/preview-9783161610844_A42952315.pdf),
printed pp. 1–7 / PDF pp. 20–26, requires both supporting and contrary cases,
with matching Hebrew as a translation-profile control. Repetition strengthens
an ideological explanation but is not mandatory for a one-off interpretation
(p. 4 n. 14). He announces divergent Hebrew sources as his conclusion; the
local derivation is outside this preview. Its 26 pages are not the full book.
Root and scout viewed all seven body pages with notes; corrupt text extraction
was not used as readable Greek/Hebrew. The scout's TOC locates the Jeremiah 39
analysis at pp. 155–178, still unread. The 2018 dissertation body was not acquired.

## Our three local controls

These are selected-unit illustrations, not a measured translation profile or
three identical whole-verse pairs. The six current WLC/POB records were read.
The already pinned Swete I pp. 800–801 / PDF pp. 832–833 and Swete III
pp. 347 and 349 / PDF pp. 371 and 373 were viewed completely again. Reusing
these editions applies a new control; it is not new manuscript collation.

| Selected unit | Observed comparison | Permitted inference |
|---|---|---|
| Jeremiah 52:11 / 2 Kings 25:7, bringing the king to Babylon | Both written Hebrew units have ויבאהו; both Greek bodies use ἤγαγεν. Subjects and object expression in the wider clauses differ. | A matched lexical rendering, not whole-clause identity or a frequency estimate. |
| Jeremiah 52:12 / 2 Kings 25:8, the royal official | WLC Jeremiah has עמד לפני, standing before; WLC Kings has עבד, servant. Both Greek bodies express standing before, with κατά πρόσωπον versus ἐνώπιον. | Do not count this as a clean comparison of how two translators render the same surviving Hebrew construction. Source variation or Greek harmonization remains possible. |
| Jeremiah 52:27 / 2 Kings 25:21, execution and deportation | Near-parallel Hebrew clauses include putting the prisoners to death and Judah's exile; surface forms differ. Swete Jeremiah's body lacks both the explicit killing predicate and the national-exile clause; Kings has both. Qmg reports corresponding additions in Jeremiah. | A quantitative textual difference requiring its own history, not a matched lexical-choice statistic or proof of personal translator deletion. |

The third row adds a local qualification to our earlier deportation comparison:
the Greek minus also includes the explicit killing predicate, although the
striking of the prisoners remains. Do not silently supply the predicate when
describing that Greek body. The narrower Greek wording does not prove survival
of those prisoners either. POB continues to translate the longer Hebrew.

## Method consequence

Our comparison records should label exact matching units, near-parallels and
source-mismatched constructions separately. Establish the local Greek reading
and correction layer first; a printed main text is not automatically untouched
Old Greek. Keep contrary examples and compare directional patterns only within
an appropriate regional sample. A single natural phrase is neither a blanket
abbreviation license nor proof that no revision occurred. These three examples
cannot identify a reviser, its date, or the earliest Hebrew literary form.

The new studies make automatic translator-abbreviation attribution less secure;
their conclusions are not substitutes for a target apparatus or local argument.
Next require Frohlich's actual local analysis, the relevant modern apparatus
and its correction history, or sufficiently matched regional controls. Park
the completed preview and selected thesis pages, not the larger corpus goal.
