# Habakkuk 1 17 net sword and the limits of Greek corroboration

Habakkuk 1:17 has a genuine ancient Hebrew alternative: **חרבו**, “his sword,”
in place of POB's retained **חרמו**, “his net.” A newly consulted scholarly
discussion also reports sword in the Greek Minor Prophets scroll. Sword therefore
deserves serious consideration, not dismissal as a modern conjecture. Historical
priority nevertheless remains **HOLD**: the quotation, its interpretation and
the reported Greek agreement do not yet discriminate inherited wording from
interpretive alteration or dependence. No canonical Hebrew, English or notes
change in this source comparison.

This follows the [pesher quotation map](HABAKKUK_PESHER_QUOTATION_MAP_2026-10-12.md)
under [method 2.0.4](TEXTUAL_ADJUDICATION_METHOD.md). The
[receipt](../sources/textual_restoration/comparisons/habakkuk1_17_source.2026-10-12.v1.json)
records actual consultation scopes and reproducible inputs. This is newly
collated evidence for POB, not a newly discovered variant or fresh decipherment.

## Hebrew quotation and missing coverage

The [published 1QpHab VI.8–9 quotation](https://lexicon.qumran-digital.org/transcriptions/1QpHab/2026-10-06/index.html?v=2026-10-06#c9628-i9675)
runs from word 9678 through 9686. All these quotation words are unqualified in
the inspected edition: no supplied-letter brackets, uncertainty marks,
correction markup or alternative descendants. Word 9687 is a divider;
interpretation starts with פשרו at 9688 in VI.10. The three words preceding
the quotation in VI.8 finish the previous interpretation, not this verse.

| Unit | Retained WLC | Published quotation | Consequence |
|---|---|---|---|
| Opening | העל כן | על כן, words 9678–79 | The quotation lacks the written interrogative prefix. This does not alone rule out a question pragmatically. |
| Verb and object | יריק חרמו | יריק חרבו, words 9680–81 | Net versus sword changes the image; “draw his sword” is not a lexical alternative for unchanged חרמו. |
| Continuation | ותמיד | תמיד, word 9682 | The conjunction before the adverb is absent. |
| Negative | לא יחמל | ולוא יחמל, words 9685–86 | A conjunction appears before the negative, alongside orthographic variation. |

Root checked complete primary columns V and VI, including the decisive word's
markup. Earlier fishing terms really survive in this same publication:
בחרמו at V.13 word 9608, לחרמו at V.14 word 9614 and VI.2 word 9642, and
למכמרתו at VI.3 word 9644. The witness does not systematically replace all
fishing imagery with military wording. Parts of the V.14–16 bridge are supplied,
however; its entire altered sequence cannot be called surviving text.

The commentary before the quotation describes military equipment, exploitation
and devastation; the following VI.10–12 interpretation depicts the Kittim killing
with a sword and without pity. These are dependent interpretive controls, not
additional manuscript attestations of the quotation's noun.

[Mur88 column XVIII](https://lexicon.qumran-digital.org/transcriptions/Mur._88/2026-10-06/index.html?v=2026-10-06#c388729-i388870)
does not counter-attest net at 1:17. Its physical lines 13–14 supply much of
1:14–15, including the earlier net term. Lines 15–17 are gap placeholders;
line 18 moves toward 2:2. There is no surviving decisive 1:17 noun, nor an
explicitly supplied noun for that verse in these placeholders. This is missing
coverage, not an attested verse omission. The pinned biblical QDR navigation
index likewise has zero exact Hab 1:17 hits across 266 scroll records; that is
an index limitation, not an exhaustive absence claim.

## Greek and Latin images do not give one uniform reading

Swete III, third edition 1905, printed 59 / PDF 83, selects
**διὰ τοῦτο ἀμφιβαλεῖ τὸ ἀμφίβληστρον αὐτοῦ**. Its action is casting a net,
not emptying a net or drawing a sword. The publisher's selected
[Rahlfs–Hanhart 2006](https://www.die-bibel.de/bibel/LXX/HAB.1) has the same
casting/net expression. Both preserve the fishing imagery of 1:14–16.
ἀμφιβαλεῖ is future active; the middle form of the subsequent sparing verb
does not turn casting into a passive or middle action.

The complete Swete page and its small apparatus were visually checked. It
reports present-versus-future casting and killing-verb variants, with corrected
hands and a qualified reading. No sword noun appears in this bounded apparatus.
Its silence is not agreement of all Greek manuscripts. The additional small
Q correction is not assigned an exact hand here because its notation was not
resolved confidently. The actual modern Greek locus apparatus remains unread.

Selected [Weber–Gryson 2007 Latin](https://www.die-bibel.de/bibel/VUL/HAB.1?ebc=true&scrollPositionRestoration=disabled&slug=einfuehrung-das-buch-habakuk)
has **expandit sagenam suam**, spreading his net. It supports the net image,
not a uniquely retroverted Hebrew emptying verb. This is edition text, not a
fresh Latin manuscript collation or proof of an independently established
Hebrew exemplar. No fresh Syriac text was consulted; prior CAL anti-scraping
responses are not text and were not retried as equivalent acquisition routes.

### Early Greek sword and the question of dependence

[Pieter B. Hartog's institutional article](https://www.pthu.nl/over-pthu/organisatie/medewerkers/p.b.hartog/downloads/pb-hartog-textual-fluidity-and-fixity.pdf),
“Textual Fixity and Fluidity in the Hellenistic and Roman Periods,” Revue de
Qumran 30(2) [112] (2018), 173–190, DOI 10.2143/RQ.30.2.3285630, explicitly
reports **μαχαιραν αυτου** in 8ḤevXIIgr on p. 184. Complete pp. 183–185,
including notes 35–45, were extracted and visually checked; this is not a
whole-article claim. Footnote 42 identifies Timothy H. Lim's 2000 chapter,
p. 71. Lim's actual chapter was not obtained.

Hartog presents Lim's possibility that the pesherist used a nearby Greek
manuscript, then argues that straightforward interpretive changes could arise
without consulting another manuscript. These are competing historical
explanations, not observed copying events. The report establishes that the
Greek sword lead is substantive scholarship; it does not provide diplomatic
brackets, uncertain letters or a plate. The reported definitive-edition locator,
DJD VIII pp. 53 and 152, plate XI, remains an acquisition target rather than
consulted evidence. The corrected edition and its actual preservation must
be checked before assigning individual Greek letters surviving or supplied.
Internet Archive metadata marks its available book item as restricted; private
PDF/OCR and an unrelated blog mirror were not used.

This distinction materially changes the comparison: net is not a universal
Greek reading, but the reported sword agreement cannot yet be counted as two
independent Hebrew exemplars. Even verified Greek letters would still require
translation-practice and dependence controls.

## Lexical context and competing transmission explanations

The actual Sefaria BDB entries distinguish the Hiphil emptying/pouring verb
(BDB09326) from the fishing-net noun (BDB03477). The verb entry includes drawing
a sword, with biblical parallels, and proposes חרבו for חרמו at Habakkuk 1:17.
That pre-Qumran editorial proposal is not extra ancient manuscript support,
nor a sword meaning for the unchanged net noun. BDB03470's separate ban/devoted
thing homonym does not certify such a meaning either. These are digital BDB
controls, not fresh HALOT consultation or separately consulted cited scholars.

Local WLC contexts at Exodus 15:9, Leviticus 26:33 and Ezekiel 12:14 confirm
the sword/drawing combination. Psalms 35:3 provides a spear/drawing parallel.
Habakkuk 1:12–17 supplies the contrary strength: fish, hooks, nets and sacrifices
to fishing implements culminate in a question about continued conquest.
These parallels explain linguistic plausibility, not target manuscript priority.

[Richard D. Patterson's exposition](https://bible.org/seriespage/habakkuk2),
1:17 and notes 335–338, weighs sword's usual verbal pairing against net's
context. It also reports Cathcart's spear conjecture and alternative clause
analyses. Spear is not a third attested noun. Its BHS, Syriac and grammatical
citations remain mediated reports. A harder reading cannot win automatically.

Hartog's 2017 *Pesher and Hypomnema*, printed 158 and 253–254 / PDF 174 and
269–270, explains the interpretation's allusion to Isaiah 13:18 in the form
of 1QIsaa and its analogy between the Kittim and Medes. All three complete
pages, including notes, were read and visually checked. The local Isaiah
13:15,18 wording was separately read; this packet does not independently
collate 1QIsaa. The literary relation is Hartog's argument, not proof that the
quotation's sword was created for that interpretation.

Net could become sword through military concretization or interpretive
adaptation. Conversely, an original sword could become net through assimilation
to the repeated fishing imagery. An inherited divergent Hebrew exemplar also
remains possible. A bet/mem copying change is conceivable, but no palaeographic
or manuscript-pixel control establishes that mechanism. Missing interrogative
and shifted conjunctions are compatible with adaptation, not proof of it.

The earlier direct Hebrew quotation gives sword a chronological strength in
attestation, not automatic priority. Removing that preference still leaves both
directions plausible. **HOLD is not a finding that WLC is best:** it retains the
declared base provisionally while the transmission question stays unresolved.

## English consequence and next discriminating evidence

No main-English replacement or uniquely optimal rendering is established.
“Draw his sword” would require source selection; “cast/spread his net” would
require a separately defended interpretation or source decision, not merely
agreement that the object is a net. The whole opening, conjunction placement
and continuation must be compared before adopting an alternative clause.

A separate reader application should disclose the verified Hebrew alternative
after **net** and move the existing dragnet note from the sentence end to that
noun. Suggested disclosure:

> An ancient Hebrew quotation has 'his sword' rather than 'his net' and also
> differs in the clause's opening and conjunctions. Which wording is earliest
> remains uncertain.

This is a note recommendation, not a frozen full-record candidate or application
approval. It intentionally avoids presenting reported Greek preservation as
directly checked. Any application must archive prior review fields and verify
the complete reader export, as in the [2:5 and 2:16 application](HABAKKUK_2_READER_DISCLOSURES_2026-10-12.md).

The next discriminating source work is the actual corrected DJD VIII locus
and plate, followed by Hebrew/Greek critical apparatus and the independent
transmission argument. Repeating the same model preference assessment cannot
replace those controls. This case contributes no evidence for canon expansion.
