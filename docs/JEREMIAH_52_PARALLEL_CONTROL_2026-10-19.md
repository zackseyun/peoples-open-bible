# Jeremiah 52 deportation and parallel source controls

The selected Greek Jeremiah text reduces the national-deportation account,
but does not erase captivity from the chapter. Comparing it with Greek and
Hebrew Kings makes an abbreviation hypothesis testable; agreement alone does
not prove which form of Jeremiah came first. Retain POB provisionally. No
source, English, note, approval, reader-layout or canon change follows.

## An original opposing argument

Georg Fischer's [author manuscript of Jeremiah Septuagint](https://publikationen.uni-tuebingen.de/xmlui/bitstream/handle/10900/126866/FischerG_231_Manuskript.pdf?isAllowed=y&sequence=1),
THB 1B §7.3, favors Greek abbreviation and adaptation rather than generally
earlier short Hebrew. PDF pp. 5–6 use the parallel fall accounts in Kings as
a control; pp. 12–13 discuss linguistic-profile and copying-loss alternatives.
He recognizes that Greek can occasionally preserve an earlier reading.
His rejection of an older Hebrew model is an argument, not fresh Hebrew ink.
The [author's institutional bibliography](https://www.uibk.ac.at/de/bibhist/fischer/)
dates the published entry to 2016, pp. 543–556. The deposit cover's conflicting
2007/2013 labels are not adopted; manuscript PDF page numbers are used here.

Root read the complete 15-page author manuscript, including cover, notes and
bibliography, and visually inspected complete PDF pp. 5–6 and 11–13. Its cited
Rofé, Person, Abate and Smith originals were not newly read. Nor does it replace
Joosten's original language argument or Stipp's original 2008 discussion.

## Direct comparison of the selected accounts

Root read and viewed complete Swete I printed pp. 800–801 / PDF pp. 832–833
and [Swete III](https://archive.org/details/theoldtestamenti03swetuoft)
printed pp. 347–349 / PDF pp. 371–373, including their apparatuses. Volume I
is the existing fourth-edition/reprint copy, not a newly dated ancient witness.
These are historical B-based editions, not a modern Greek-family census.
Hebrew references below follow WLC; Greek Kings is 4 Kingdoms.

|---|---|---|---|
| Deportation of remaining people | Verse 15 present | Verse 15 absent; Qmg apparatus reports verse-15 material | Verse 11 present |

The shorter body is not all Greek evidence: the marginal reports must remain
distinct from the main text. In the same Greek Jeremiah body, verse 11 still
takes Zedekiah to Babylon, verse 16 leaves agricultural workers, and verse 31
dates the royal-release episode from ἀποικισθέντος, deportation/captivity.
The corresponding Hebrew person is Jehoiachin; Swete prints the Greek name
Ἰωακείμ here, not a newly harmonized Hebrew spelling. Thus a shorthand “no
Exile” must not become a claim that all captivity, every survivor, or Babylon
has disappeared. The relevant reduction concerns the national-deportation
account in this fall narrative. Rofé's actual argument remains a report within
Fischer, not a newly verified theory of universal annihilation.

Our current-POB control read source and main English for all 40 records in
Jeremiah 52:11–34 and 2 Kings 25:7–22. Both longer accounts preserve deportation;
POB also retains the Hebrew totals and royal-release episode. These are
meaningful content differences, not interchangeable translation preferences.

## What this test can establish

The selected parallels support the plausibility of local abbreviation: Greek
Kings can render these deportation clauses, so their absence in Greek Jeremiah
is not a necessity of translating into Greek. It does not follow that the
Jeremiah translator personally deleted them. Earlier Hebrew differences,
Greek-stage loss/revision and harmonization of a longer Hebrew Jeremiah toward
Kings remain distinguishable histories. The agreeing forms also have literary
and genealogical relationships; they are not three independent votes.

Accordingly, neither “shorter is older” nor “parallel agreement proves the
translator's exact source” is a sufficient decision rule. This check strengthens
a concrete countermodel without settling Jeremiah 39 or the whole book.
Counts of Greek variants versus surviving Hebrew copies likewise require
comparable samples before becoming a reliability metric; later fidelity does
not alone establish the earliest literary form.
