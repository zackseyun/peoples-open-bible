# Judges 20 48 pointing and English interpretation

POB's existing English says “from each city, men,” while its pointed WLC
source has the soundness/entirety noun `מְתֹם`. A published alternative
`מְתִם` gives men, in the phrase city of men / inhabited city. This comparison
provisionally prefers that inhabited-city interpretation and a clearer
human-to-livestock range. It does not establish new ancient letters, a new
massacre narrative, exact codex pointing or the earliest wording.

The [Hebrew College BDB](https://hebrewcollege.edu/wp-content/uploads/2018/10/BDB.pdf),
complete native PDF pages 1466 and 2607 inspected in the preceding source
assessment, distinguishes males/men from soundness/entirety. It reports the

mean dead people. Deuteronomy 2:34 and 3:6 contain related city-of-men expressions;
Job 24:12 has city and men in a different construction. These are lexical and
contextual parallels, not manuscript witnesses to Judges.

Moore, *Judges* (1895), printed 444 / PDF 500, was inspected as a complete
native page in the preceding assessment. He prefers the men reading and cites
parallels and manuscript/edition reports through De Rossi. Those reports were
not newly collated; his rejection of entirety is an argument, not a measurement.
The [NET publisher's note](https://classic.net.bible.org/verse.php?book=Jdg&chapter=20&verse=48)
also explicitly selects city of men / inhabited city against the Leningrad
soundness reading. NET prints a plene yod; the minimal working vocalization
here follows UXLC's defective form and adds no consonant. NET is an editorial
comparison, not another ancient witness or a sentence to copy into POB.

The [Rahlfs–Hanhart 2006 publisher chapter](https://www.die-bibel.de/bibel/LXX/JDG.20)
has `ἀπὸ πόλεως ἑξῆς`: a city-by-city/onward expression without explicit men
in this slot. It does not uniquely recover a Hebrew vowel. Crucially,

to all Greek. B's exact Greek letters and full apparatus were not acquired;

The 2007 edition's historical introduction is not a current Göttingen-status audit.

## Working decision and strongest objection

The inhabited-city interpretation is intelligible and supported by the
published pointing correction and the related Hebrew idiom. Prefer it
provisionally for English. The strongest contrary case is that entirety can
describe destruction of the whole city, both vowel marks are reported, and
the Greek forms do not discriminate uniquely. Neither direction of alteration
has been demonstrated. Age weighting adds nothing between digital readings of
the same codex; NET and scholarly agreement are not independent ancient votes.

Retain the exact diplomatic WLC source and store a separate, explicit working
vocalization for English. The reader note discloses that choice and the
whole-city alternative. This is a source-interpretation decision, not merely
stylistic English revision, and not a canonical replacement of WLC's vowel.
The current critical-source composition tool strips points and cannot represent
this contrast; do not erase the contrast or mislabel a repointed text as WLC.
Any future migration to a pointed critical-source corpus must preserve both
layers and validate the point-level change rather than reuse consonantal approval.

No direct De Rossi collation, full BHL/BHQ apparatus, fresh codex image reading,
DSS passage-coverage census or complete versional collation is claimed. Reopen
the physical pointing or historical-priority question for relevant manuscript
controls or discriminating apparatus/transmission evidence, not another model vote.
