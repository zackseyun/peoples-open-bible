# Jeroboam at Shechem: different narrative forms, not a recovered Hebrew original

## Assessment

This comparison establishes a consequential difference: the current Hebrew
names Jeroboam as a participant in the negotiations with Rehoboam, while the
selected regular Greek account does not name him in those scenes. The expanded
Greek story after 12:24 changes the surrounding sequence substantially. These
are known published textual forms, not newly discovered letters.

There is **no sufficient basis here to delete Jeroboam from the Hebrew, insert
the expanded story into POB, or choose one complete form as the earliest**.
Historical Hebrew priority remains HOLD. Current Hebrew, main English and
reader notes are unchanged. A local Greek preference for **Ἰεροβοάμ** over
the selected B original-hand **Ῥοβοάμ** at regular 12:20 is defensible from
context and explicit other Greek support; it neither changes POB's already
correct name nor dates the surrounding story.

Chronicles is a connected literary parallel, not an independent manuscript of
Kings. Its 10:2 **וַיָּשָׁב … מִמִּצְרָיִם**, “returned … from Egypt,” must not be
silently substituted for Kings 12:2 **וַיֵּשֶׁב … בְּמִצְרָיִם**, “remained/lived …
in Egypt.” Its 10:3 has “all Israel” where Kings has “all the assembly of
Israel.” The observed differences do not by themselves establish borrowing
direction.

The royal marriage and release from Egypt also have a real comparison in the
read Hebrew Hadad episode, 11:19–22. This makes literary reuse a testable
explanation for the expanded Jeroboam episode. Shared motifs alone do not
determine the direction of reuse or recover its exact Hebrew wording.
The regular Greek apparatus also reports a 12:2 insertion and a 12:3
Jeroboam-summoning introduction for other witnesses. Absence from the selected
body must not become a claim of absence from every Greek manuscript.

The core b/o/c₂/e₂ witnesses have local variants in the expansion; an omnibus
“Lucianic extended story” would hide the c₂ gap. No synthetic continuous text
has been manufactured from generic `rell` entries. Superscript original hands,
correction hands, critical signs, `vid`, and H&P-derived bracketed reports keep
their governing edition meanings. None alone identifies a reviser's motive.

During the first rapid apparatus pass, the source reader attributed the long
24f/n/u units to b. Enlarged complete-unit checks corrected this to **z, z,
i**, before any report was frozen. The preliminary claim is withdrawn, not
counted as evidence of b's narrative profile. This records an actual correction
in the work, rather than concealing it behind a passing review.

## A consulted argument and a corrected reprint

D. W. Gooding's 1972 *JBL* rejoinder, printed **529–533**, was read in full,
including notes, stopping at his signature before the following article.
The [original journal scan](https://biblicalstudies.org.uk/pdf/jbl/1972_529.pdf)
was checked against the
[authorized estate resetting](https://www.myrtlefieldhouse.com/s/jeroboam-rise-power.pdf).
The latter reverses **A/B** in its table at PDF 5: the original printed 531
assigns the “came” line to **B**, the Israel-only line to **A**. Original
printed 532 and the resetting's PDF 6 agree on the corresponding footnote.
These are two presentations of one article, not two scholarly votes or a new
collation of Chronicles. A separate 8:55/8:25 reference discrepancy already
occurs in the original and is not silently repaired.

Gooding's useful cautions are limited: Greek “Israel/people” variation need
not imply different Hebrew; hearing of a return at 12:20 does not explicitly
date the return itself; secondary positioning at 11:43 does not prove a fresh
translation; and hypothetical Chronicles retroversions do not force
Chronicles-to-Kings borrowing. His alternative mechanisms remain possibilities,
not demonstrated acts of harmonization. The rejoinder explicitly does not
prove his earlier whitewashing/blackening thesis. That motive is not adopted.

Two potentially discriminating modern discussions remain **unread access
inputs**, not supporting authorities for this judgment: Jan Joosten's 2020
chapter, pp. 79–94
([official chapter](https://www.jstor.org/stable/j.ctv1qp9bfd.8)), and Guy
Darshan's 2024 *VT* article, pp. 561–583
([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1163/15685330-bja10156)). Darshan's public abstract
locates the Adoram/traditions question but cannot substitute for its argument.
Ordinary publisher/RG downloads returned 403; the JSTOR PDF route returned
HTML, not a chapter. No access control was bypassed. Do not present snippets,
a malformed full-text extraction or a chapter title as a complete reading.

## Decision and useful next step

**Observation:** PASS at the consulted published-edition level, with the above
witness qualifications. **Transcription:** no fresh manuscript letters or
complete reconstructed Hebrew approved. **Priority:** HOLD for the complete
Hebrew/Greek narrative history; tentative local Greek name correction only.
**Translation/application:** none. POB already follows the Hebrew participation
clauses and the name Jeroboam; its inspected target notes do not yet disclose
the alternative narrative. That is a reader-disclosure opportunity, not an
already-applied change or a license to splice accounts together.

The strongest reason to take a shorter negotiation form seriously is the
coherent Greek sequence without the Hebrew's named participation clauses.
The strongest objections are the relocated return material, real inner-Greek
variation, and the possibility of literary rewriting in either direction.
Neither ease of narrative nor a count of related manuscripts selects an
earliest Hebrew form. No chronology, theology or canon claim is forced by
the local B name correction.

Next obtain an actual modern local apparatus/transmission argument, including
the two identified discussions if lawfully accessible, and test its specific
predictions against these already documented witness profiles. A separate
reviewed reader disclosure could then explain the alternative without asserting
priority. Do not repeat these eight pages as new evidence, create full-book
exports, train a model on this small case, or measure progress by edits made.
The expansion is a variant within Kings, not evidence of a newly recovered
independent canonical book. ImageGen supplies no recovered letters. The wider
OT/NT comparison goal remains unfinished.
