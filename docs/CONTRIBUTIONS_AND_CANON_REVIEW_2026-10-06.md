Repository adoption, scholarly confidence and deployed publication are separate.
These differences can affect a reader’s understanding, but neither one rendering
nor a handful of corrections establishes a new theology or an optimal Bible.

A further valid provisional contribution is ready when the surviving evidence
distinguishes the alternatives, the strongest opposing explanation is addressed,
and the exact source, English and notes pass scoped application/export checks.
There is no honest completion date or token threshold for the next discovery.
A novelty claim additionally requires literature comparison and qualified
scrutiny beyond model agreement. A retain or unresolved outcome remains valid
research; it is not a failed attempt to manufacture a change.

## Correct sources and incomplete coverage

The [coverage audit](BIBLICAL_SOURCE_COVERAGE_AUDIT_2026-09-04.md) and
[controlling method](TEXTUAL_ADJUDICATION_METHOD.md) identify the right classes:
Hebrew/Aramaic manuscripts, Samaritan witnesses and ancient versions for OT;
Greek manuscripts and correction hands, early versions and quotations for NT.
Catalogues locate witnesses; critical apparatuses identify comparisons;
manuscript images and reliable editions establish what actually survives.
None of those layers alone proves that every relevant source has been collated.

Different editions of the same tradition are not independent witnesses.
Versions require local assessment before inferring a lost Hebrew or Greek word;
retellings are not continuous manuscripts of the work they retell. Where books
survive in distinct literary forms, compare those forms explicitly instead of
silently synthesizing an unattested original. English controls test interpretation,
not ancient-text majority support.

The NT’s 27-book SBLGNT apparatus coverage is not 27-book manuscript collation.
[Mark 1:41](../translation/nt/mark/001/041.yaml) retains angry while replacement
remains held; a compassion working preference is not an applied Greek change.
[Jude 15](JUDE_1_15_SOURCE_COMPARISON_2026-10-05.md) retains its selected Greek
and adds disclosure of the every-person alternative. Its failed live attestation
table leaves full correction-hand/version coverage incomplete. Fresh damaged-ink
accuracy is also uncalibrated; contextual image fits cannot be advertised as
newly recovered text.

## Additional works worth considering

“Worth considering” has a defensible historical answer before a universal canon
answer. The following evidence supports textual and reception studies:

| Work | Relevant evidence | Appropriate conclusion now |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 Enoch | Ancient Aramaic remains; Jude’s use of the judgment oracle; Ethiopian canonical reception. | Strong candidate for comparison and a labelled library. Jude’s positive use of one oracle does not authorize every section of the surviving collection. |
| Jubilees | Hebrew remains at Qumran; Ethiopian canonical reception. | Strong candidate for textual/reception comparison, not a manuscript of Genesis or evidence for importing all its expansions into Genesis. |
| Sirach and Tobit | Ancient original-language witnesses and existing Catholic canonical reception. | Already canonical under that framework; “additional” depends on the starting canon. Recovered witnesses help assess wording, not create their authority. |
| Hermas and Barnabas | Both occur with NT works in Sinaiticus. | Significant early-Christian reception; codex inclusion alone does not demonstrate equal canonical rank. |
| 1 Clement and Didache | Ancient public reading or instructional reception. | Further reception candidates; use and instruction are distinct from canonical status. |

Institutional controls: the IAA describes
[Enoch remains](https://www.deadseascrolls.org.il/featured-scrolls/?locale=en_US)
and [Hebrew Jubilees](https://www.deadseascrolls.org.il/learn-about-the-scrolls/introduction);
the [Ethiopian Church list](https://www.ethiopianorthodox.org/english/canonical/books.html)
names Enoch and Jubilees. The
[Catholic list](https://www.vatican.va/content/catechism/en/part_one/section_one/chapter_two/article_3/iv_the_canon_of_scripture.html)
includes Sirach and Tobit. The
[Sinaiticus project](https://codexsinaiticus.org/en/codex/content.aspx) records
Barnabas and Hermas. Counterevidence matters:
[Athanasius’s Festal Letter 39.7](https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/2806039.htm)
places Hermas and the Teaching of the Apostles among instructional works
outside his canon; [Eusebius III.16](https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/250103.htm)
reports public use of Clement without that fact deciding universal authority.

The next reception dossier should start with Enoch and Jubilees, recording the
exact textual form, ancient use, named community reception and strongest
objections. A labelled comparative library can include such works without
declaring a new theological canon. A canonical inclusion recommendation needs
the intended community and explicit authority criteria; they remain unspecified.
No canon labels are changed here. Older claims that Enoch was simply canonical
at Qumran need qualification: surviving copies establish circulation, not that
community’s complete canon. The existing
[extra-text policy](../EXTRA_CANONICAL.md) provides the careful scope.
