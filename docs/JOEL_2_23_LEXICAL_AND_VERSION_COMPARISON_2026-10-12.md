and timing interpretations. It does not recover different Hebrew letters.

Seasonal rains follow the first gift in the versions. Greek and Syriac food
agreement is not two independent Hebrew votes without checking translation
relationships and local translation practice. Latin's teacher also need not
reflect different consonants. The selected Latin has no sicut before its timing
expression. None of these inspected timing expressions explicitly says month,
but that does not exclude a calendrical Hebrew interpretation.

Chapter contexts, especially 2:18–27, and GBS edition footers were read.
These selected texts are not complete apparatus or individual manuscript
collations. [CAL's source description](https://cal.huc.edu/get_file_info.php?coord=62016&return=/ot_peshitta.html)
specifies Leiden's main text with some corrections based on 7a1; it does not
assign these target words specifically to that manuscript. Target lexical
controls support food, righteousness or justice, and the temporal adverb;
hasvariant=0 is not an all-manuscript unanimity certificate.
[Gelston's 1980 edition](https://syriaca.org/cbss/YB5SMESS) is modern edition
metadata, not a translation or manuscript date.

## Actual lexical entries

Complete relevant BDB entries were read from Sefaria's BDB Dictionary API and
visually checked in the [printed facsimile](https://tmcdaniel.palmerseminary.edu/BDB.pdf).
The scan's title identifies Houghton Mifflin, Boston and New York, 1907;
Sefaria's digital metadata identifies Oxford 1906. Neither is Enhanced BDB.

At printed page 435, the rain entry explicitly includes both Joel occurrences.
The separate teacher entry establishes the lexical possibility but does not
list Joel here. That omission is BDB's adjudication, not morphological
impossibility. Its parenthetic del. We is an editorial deletion proposal,
not manuscript omission evidence. In our contextual assessment, restored fruit,
grain, wine, oil and subsequent rains favor rain. Latin teacher remains a
substantial counter-reading.

At printed page 842, צדקה sense 6b specifically assigns the Joel rain phrase
to prosperity within the broader vindication and salvation range. This supports
examining the benefit of restored rain, not a physically correct quantity of
rainfall. POB's “for righteousness” preserves a lexical association but can
obscure its contextual function. Vindication and prosperity deserve comparison,
not rejection solely to preserve theological vocabulary. Conversely, prosperity
can suggest a modern wealth promise; a lexical gloss is not automatically the
best English. Versional grammatical relations do not settle purpose or result.

At printed pages 911–912, ראשון sense 2a includes Joel among first-month
constructions omitting חודש, then recommends **כָּרִאשׁוֹנָה** (consonants
כראשונה), citing Greek, Syriac and scholars. That changes the Hebrew form.
Sense 3a's formerly examples concern feminine prepositional forms; they do not
automatically establish the same sense under unchanged masculine בָּרִאשׁוֹן.
Root's direct API reading corrected the acquisition agent's spelling כרישונה
to the actual proposed form. Neither spelling becomes attested ancient letters.

This does not prove POB's timing impossible: another grammatical argument
might defend it. It does show that BDB is not straightforward support under
unchanged WLC. The retained construction is the strongest counterevidence to
the former-state interpretation favored by restoration context and versions.
Fresh HALOT and actual target apparatus remain unconsulted.

## Reception and interpretive restraint

[Pieter B. Hartog's chapter 7](https://brill.com/display/book/9789004354203/BP000014.xml),
section 2.3.3 and notes 81–82, connects rain and teaching language in Hosea
10:12 and Joel 2:23 with the Teacher of Righteousness tradition. He distinguishes
agricultural metaphor from interpretive development in the Damascus Document
and later Pesharim. Note 81 discusses an article marking a specific figure;
note 82 hesitates to accept a messianic identification there.

This is a scholarly reception argument, not newly collated continuous Joel
attestation or proof of the earliest sense. Reading Hartog's citations does
not mean their originals were all consulted. POB's rationale should not treat
teacher as automatically messianic: agricultural context is the case for rain,
not theological discomfort with an alternative. Historical authority or use
of related writings does not establish a modern canon addition.
