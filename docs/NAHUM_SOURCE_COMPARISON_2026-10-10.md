# Nahum: source comparison and new-fragment follow-up

Nahum provides a concrete meaning-bearing lead, not yet an approved replacement:
POB's 2:9 (often numbered 2:8 in English Bibles) contrasts a temporal description
of Nineveh with the Greek image of its waters. Published 4Q82 preserves an
additional Hebrew he, but the missing context prevents a unique interpretation.
A newer Greek fragment at 1:5 and Hebrew commentary quotations also show why the
two-label biblical discovery index is not a complete source list.

This is a bounded published-reading comparison following the
[adjudication method](TEXTUAL_ADJUDICATION_METHOD.md), not fresh decipherment,
an exhaustive Nahum apparatus or a preference vote. Source selection and main
English replacement remain **HOLD**. The present WLC/POB wording is retained
provisionally, not certified optimal. No canonical file changes in this packet.

## 2:9: days or waters?

POB's source is **מִימֵי הִיא**, rendered “from her earliest days.”
[4Q82, fragment 97R+99, line 1](https://lexicon.qumran-digital.org/transcriptions/4Q82/2026-05-21/index.html#c353075-i353075)
publishes **מימיה[ היא**: all five letters of מימיה are outside supply brackets
and have no uncertainty marks. Following היא is supplied. The surrounding
physical rows were checked; parallel WLC/Mur. 88 quotations are not extra 4Q82
attestation. This is a positive additional-he contrast, not only a vowel dispute,
but it does not attest the complete reconstructed phrase.

[Mur. 88](https://lexicon.qumran-digital.org/transcriptions/Mur._88/2026-05-21/index.html?v=2026-05-21),
column 16 line 35, publishes a temporal-looking **מ[י]מי היא** within a larger
row containing nested/unbalanced supply syntax. Preserve that syntax; it is not
secure complete counter-attestation merely because an extracted phrase appears
unbracketed. Resolve the edition context before counting decisive letters.

The selected [Greek 2:9](https://www.die-bibel.de/bibel/LXX/NAM.2) has
**τὰ ὕδατα αὐτῆς**, “her waters.” Its following flight/standing construction also
differs from the Hebrew imperatives. Adopting a water interpretation does not
authorize retroverting every Greek difference into Hebrew.

[NET's own 2:8 textual note](https://classic.net.bible.org/verse.php?book=Nah&chapter=2&verse=8)
explains the temporal construction and the water alternative, reports BHS's
emendation, and discusses Saggs's differently segmented proposal. We consulted
NET's argument, not BHS's actual target apparatus or those cited scholarly
works. Modern English adoption supplies no additional ancient votes.

A water interpretation makes the flight continue the pool image; the temporal
interpretation contrasts long-standing stability with present flight. The
extra-he spelling is compatible with a suffixed water form, but a suffixed
temporal analysis (“from her days”) must also be evaluated rather than excluded
by consonants alone. That second possibility is a grammatical hypothesis in
this packet, not a newly consulted lexicon finding. Scribal variation and
editorial reconstruction of the missing following context remain alternatives.
We have not shown which explanation best accounts for the whole transmission.

Priority stays held pending actual critical apparatus, the full published
4Q82 reconstruction context and a bounded grammatical comparison of the
competing constructions. This is a genuine reopening lead, not a newly
discovered variant or proof that older automatically wins.

## 1:5: the new Greek fragment and its reassessment

[Riestra et al., 2023, pp. 291–316](https://www.jstor.org/content/oa_chapter_edited/jj.10329820.15),
DOI 10.2307/jj.10329820.15, identifies P1348-Fr031 at Nahum 1:5–6 and assigns
it to Scribe A near the beginning of reconstructed column 13. Root consulted
the [author-uploaded body](https://www.academia.edu/117589117/Riestra_et_al_The_Mountains_Quake_the_Hills_Melt_and_the_Earth_NSAJD_2023);
its web extraction loses Greek transcription/table cells. Additional material
assigned to 8ḤevXIIgr is not another independent witness. The historical
one-scroll/two-scroll uncertainty is not erased by this catalogue grouping.

[Riestra, 2025](https://www.academia.edu/167834491/The_Text_of_Nah_1_5_in_the_Greek_Minor_Prophets_Scroll_8%E1%B8%A4evXIIgr_Between_Literality_and_Interpretation),
DOI 10.1163/15685330-bja10229, is a later primary reassessment, not just an
abstract lead. Root read the introduction, Table 3 and target discussion
(pp. 12–17) through web extraction. It reports remains **]η̣ρθη̣** and prefers
ἤρθη over possible compounds, differing from selected LXX ἀνεστάλη. The
partly reconstructed prepositional phrase **προ[προσωπου** differs from
LXX ἀπὸ προσώπου. The author considers both different Hebrew and
revision/interpretation; the Greek does not uniquely recover Hebrew spelling.

The primary 4Q169 fragment 1–2 line 10 publishes **הארץ ממנו ומלפני֯** after a
gap. The 2025 discussion presents a fuller reconstruction of the lemma; its
opening verb is not surviving evidence in the consulted Qumran Digital row.
This edition-context difference must remain visible. Nor does the pesher's
expanded prepositional wording prove the Greek's exact Hebrew exemplar.

POB already renders the earth as rising and offers upheaval alternatives.
A Greek passive or lifting verb does not itself establish the best Hebrew
analysis, the earliest Greek text, or that POB's directional emphasis is best.
Image/table visual checking, the complete critical apparatus and grammatical
comparison remain gates. Different publication survival counts are not
harmonized into one exact count without figure inspection.

## 3:18: agency and missing decisive wording

POB addresses the king and has his nobles lying down. The selected
[Greek 3:18](https://www.die-bibel.de/bibel/LXX/NAM.3) instead makes the king
the subject of a verb putting the mighty ones to sleep. This is an agency and
syntax difference, not merely the English choice of “rest” versus “sleep.”

Mur. 88 column 17 line 29 has **[ישכנו אדיריך נפ]שו**. The whole first verb
and nobles expression are supplied; they cannot decide ישכנו versus the
ישנו proposal reported in [NET's own note](https://classic.net.bible.org/verse.php?book=Nah&chapter=3&verse=18).
The surviving שו does constrain a final consonant sequence but does not supply
the missing prefix, vocalization or meaning. This packet does not invent an
attested competing verb from that partial survival. NET's evaluation of the
Greek is its argument, not an established scribal mistake. Its added sheep
simile is not a recovered Hebrew phrase. Source/English priority stays held.

## Reader defects and application boundary

Three local anchor issues are concrete independently of source priority.
At 1:5, note a explains the earth verb but attaches to the mountains clause.
At 2:9, note a explains “Stop! Stop!” but attaches to “earliest days”; note b
explains the days phrase but attaches to “fleeing.” At 3:18, shepherd note b
and nobles note c are grouped after the people/mountains clause with d, rather
than their actual phrases.

A subsequent application may correct anchors and qualify the 2:9 water
alternative. It requires exact frozen candidates, archived prior review fields,
one bounded note review and complete reader-export checks. This research
packet approves no notes, canonical metadata, source choice, main English,
canon addition or About edit. Ancient textual variation does not establish
that a different book should be canonized.
