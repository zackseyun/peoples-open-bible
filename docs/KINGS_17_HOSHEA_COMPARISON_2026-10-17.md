# Hoshea's evaluation: 2 Kings 17:2 in 17:1–6

## Result and limits

The competing readings make a consequential difference: Hoshea does evil in
both, but the Hebrew qualification contrasts him with earlier Israelite kings,
whereas Old Latin 115 intensifies his wrongdoing above his predecessors.
This is an established textual variant newly examined in this POB dossier,
not a newly deciphered letter or a discovery of a previously unknown reading.

**Earliest Hebrew priority: HOLD.** The surviving versional contrast is secure
at the consulted edition level; its direction of development is not. The
selected Hebrew, English, notes and canonical corpus remain unchanged.
Provisional retention does not establish Hebrew priority or certify the
existing reader note. No English replacement candidate was drafted.

WLC's negative is word `12FQt`; adjacent `12M82` is רק and `126U3`
כְּ/מַלְכֵ֣י. The Hebrew does not print an explicit “less” or “least evil.”
Current POB says “yet not like the kings of Israel who were before him” and
its rationale understands the qualification as mitigating. The alternative
would alter the degree and narrative significance of the evil, not turn a
good king into an evil king.

The [official Fischer edition](https://ccat.sas.upenn.edu/ioscs/journal/volumes/bioscs16.pdf)
is *Palimpsestus Vindobonensis: A Revised Edition of L115 for Samuel–Kings*,
BIOSCS 16 (1983), pp. 13–87. Its introduction identifies the Samuel–Kings
undertext as **115**, distinct from Pentateuch **101**, in Naples, Biblioteca
Nazionale, cod. lat. 1, formerly Vind. 17. The later grammar overtext is not
the biblical witness. Consulted conventions explain modern word division,
scriptio continua, suspensions and apparatus notation. The printed divine-name
contraction was not mistaken for a different comparator. La115 here has
**super**, not the **prae** reported for La91; they must not be homogenized.

Swete's selected B text is not automatically the earliest Greek. Equally,
the age or general value of an Old Latin witness does not make every one of
its readings original. Greek behind Latin and Hebrew behind Greek are two
reconstructions, not recovered Hebrew ink.

## Judgment and next discriminating test

Observation and edition-level transcription pass; the semantic contrast is
real. Historical priority remains unresolved because both mitigation of a
harsher predecessor and secondary assimilation to harsher royal formulas
remain viable. The evidence so far does not establish which mechanism better
explains the exact comparator and its distribution across witnesses.

The next bounded test is the translation and transmission of the **comparative
formula**, not another generic assessment of La115's reliability:

1. Acquire the actual Antiochene critical text/apparatus at 17:2 and its
   conventions; separate manuscript attestations from editorial reconstruction.
2. Compare the relevant Ahab/Ahaziah and neighboring royal formulas in the
   actual Hebrew, Greek and Latin sources. Test Richelle's παρά/ὑπέρ control
   and Hebrew comparative מן against the same translator/revision layer,
   rather than assuming one uniform translation practice across Kings.
3. Ask which account predicts those results and the connected 17:1–6
   differences with fewer unsupported changes. Retain uncertainty if the
   controls remain nondiscriminating; do not award priority by majority or age.

The 2012 Torijano chapter and the actual 2021 monograph were not acquired or
read. Sulpicius and La91 are reported evidence here, not newly collated
primary texts. These are specific acquisition limits, not a claim that no
further evidence exists. A later reader-disclosure proposal would require its
own exact candidate and application review; this dossier authorizes none.
