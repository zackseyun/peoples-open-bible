# Esther 8 and 9 source comparison

## Result and consequence for POB

The Hebrew and selected Greek endings differ consequentially in authorization,
plunder, agency and the scale of killing. These are not interchangeable English
glosses. They require comparison as connected literary forms before selecting
individual readings. Current POB retains WLC and its main English provisionally;
this comparison changes neither the source nor reader notes. It does not
establish which form is earliest or certify the current translation as optimal.

The [evidence record](../sources/textual_restoration/comparisons/esther8_9.2026-10-16.v1.json)
pins seven current records and the named controls. A bounded portfolio search
found no prior Esther source-comparison case; the local Esther note inventory
is empty. That is a local work-coverage result, not absence of ancient witnesses.

## Linked differences in the selected editions

The Greek columns below refer to the publisher's [Rahlfs–Hanhart chapter 8](https://www.die-bibel.de/en/bible/LXX/ESG.8)
and [chapter 9](https://www.die-bibel.de/en/bible/LXX/ESG.9), whose footers identify
the 2006 edition. These are selected edition readings, not a complete manuscript
collation. Addition E is included under the publisher's 8:12 lettered numbering.

| Unit | WLC and current POB | Selected Greek |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 8:11 | Life-defense wording, killing triad, children/women and plunder authorization | Jewish law, self-help and discretionary treatment of opponents; no corresponding killing triad or children/women |
| 8:13 | Explicit vengeance | Readiness for warfare |
| 9:5 | Sword, slaughter/destruction, action as they pleased | No verse 5 in the displayed 4-to-6 sequence |
| 9:10 | No taking plunder | Affirmative plundering |
| 9:14 | Unspecified hanging agents, rendered passively | Bodies handed to city Jews for hanging |
| 9:15 | Three hundred killed, no plunder | Same number and no plunder |
| 9:16 | Seventy-five thousand, life-defense and no plunder | Fifteen thousand, self-help and no plunder |

The Greek does not uniformly remove self-defense or gathering. Its 8:11 retains
self-help, 9:15–16 retain gathering, and 9:16 retains self-help. Addition E's
8:12s explicitly describes resisting attackers. Omitting a particular Hebrew
life-defense formula does not remove that whole semantic field. Conversely,
the Hebrew defensive language does not erase vengeance in 8:13 or discretionary
violence in 9:5; current POB already renders both. Its 8:11 rationale must not
be generalized into a judgment about every subsequent action.

## What the Swete apparatus actually establishes

Root and the delegated source reader visually read complete printed pp. 776–777
/ PDF pp. 794–795 in the acquired Swete volume II. They also read preface
pp. vii–viii / PDF pp. 9–10 and the siglum key at PDF p. 18. The preface states
that Vaticanus supplies the text where available; B appears at the target.
The key identifies B as Vaticanus, A as Alexandrinus and ℵ as Sinaiticus.
The preface's seven manuscripts concern the volume, not seven Esther witnesses.

At 9:10 the B-based text prints **καὶ διήρπασαν**. The plunder-verb entry
reports **pr απεκτειναν και A**: Alexandrinus has an added killing clause
before the retained plunder verb, not a negative in this report. No negative
is recorded at that entry. This supplies specific published Greek evidence,
not proof that no Greek copy ever had a negative or that a negative could not
have been lost earlier. Sinaiticus's apparatus silence is not a new image reading.

The adjacent page prevents a uniform word-for-word Greek description. Its
9:16 apparatus adds life-language under a Sinaiticus corrector. The final
negative-plunder clause is marked absent in ℵ* and A, while A's verse-17 entry
reports negative-plunder wording at a different position. Therefore an omission
at one location cannot establish that Alexandrinus lacks the thought everywhere.
These are the old edition's hand reports, not freshly adjudicated correction
dates or current manuscript inspection.

## The full article and competing explanations

Root and a delegated reader read the complete [Bezold 2024 article](https://judaica.ch/article/download/10004/14428?inline=1),
including all five sections, twenty-four notes and bibliography. He interprets
the changes through power and agency rather than uniform moral softening. He
argues for Alpha rewriting and a further battle addition; those directions and
his historical settings remain hypotheses, not independently dated source facts.
The direct Alpha edition below now corroborates the contrasting ending, with
important wording and manuscript qualifications; it does not recover that
proposed earlier layer or independently date the revisions.

His note 13 reports the lost-negative explanation at 9:10 from Fox, Kahana and
Macchi; their cited originals have not been consulted. Neither the constellation
of differences nor a coherent power reading disproves an early copying loss at
this one unit. Equally, that copying proposal does not explain away the other
wording, agency and numerical differences. Each requires its own transmission
test before combining them into a history.

Two qualifications follow from the directly checked wording. The Greek controls
above limit a blanket absence-of-defense claim. In Hebrew 9:14, third-plural
**תלו** leaves the agents unspecified; imperial soldiers are a contextual
proposal, not required by that verb. POB's passive preserves this uncertainty.
A smaller Greek casualty number changes the narrated scale but does not identify
the cause, source language or direction of change. Neither moral preference
nor inferred historical fit can select an earliest wording.

## Connected calendar and the countermanding letter

The calendar follow-up compares actual [RH chapter 3](https://www.die-bibel.de/en/bible/LXX/ESG.3),
chapters 8–9 and the named WLC units. These are selected edition readings, not
a complete Greek manuscript collation. Dates for issuing a letter, its intended
execution, fighting and rest must not be treated as the same event.

| Event and anchor | WLC | Selected RH Greek |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Lot casting, 3:7 | In Nisan; Adar/month twelve named, no chosen day stated | Lot falls on Adar 14 |
| Haman's scribes, 3:12 | First month, day 13 | Same date |
| Planned destruction, 3:13 and Addition B | Adar 13 at 3:13 | Only Adar at 3:13; Adar 14 specified in 3:13f |
| Counter-order written, 8:9 | Sivan 23, third month | Nisan 23, first month |
| Jewish action authorized, 8:12 and Addition E | Adar 13 | Adar 13 at both 8:12 and 8:12s |
| Narrated action, 9:1 and 9:16–17 | Adar 13; provincial rest on 14 | Same days |
| Susa's extension, 9:13–15 and 9:18 | Fresh royal permission; action on 14, rest on 15 | Fresh royal permission; action on 14, celebration on 15 |

Bezold's date contrast is real in this selected Greek text: Haman's originally
planned destruction falls one day after the authorized Jewish action. However,
Addition E's 8:12r explicitly directs recipients not to use Haman's letters;
8:12s speaks of repelling attackers, and 8:12t describes deliverance replacing
destruction. The assertion in his note 12 that the Jews must fight on the 14th
because Haman's decree is still about to be executed requires an explanation
of this contrary instruction. The broad force of sealed royal letters at 8:8
does not permit silently ignoring E. Nor does 9:2's lack of effective resistance
identify who struck first. A preemptive interpretation remains possible, not
required by the date contrast. These tensions do not by themselves establish
separate compositional stages or authorize harmonizing the dates.

Current POB's named dates agree with WLC, but its 3:7 note goes beyond that
result: it describes lot casting as extending through the calendar until Adar.
WLC has no explicit “until” particle here, and the issue date at 3:12 is already
Nisan 13. Testing candidate dates is distinct from eleven elapsed months of
casting. This flags a separate English/rationale question, not a new Hebrew
variant. The main line's “until” could denote traversing calendar choices;
an exact replacement needs grammatical review of the elliptical ending.
Neither “the lot fell” nor a chosen day may be claimed as preserved WLC wording.
No correction is applied by this calendar comparison.

Root and a separate context-aware reader checked these connected anchors.
Their agreement covers the selected texts and inference limits, not actual
ancient events, all manuscript dates or the priority of a literary form.

## Direct Alpha edition and its numbering

Lagarde's *Librorum Veteris Testamenti canonicorum, pars prior, Graece*
(Göttingen, 1883) is now directly checked through the
[BSB digitization](https://www.digitale-sammlungen.de/view/bsb11557385).
The actual title verifies the imprint. Root and the source reader visually
checked complete alpha pp. 526, 528, 530, 532, 534, 536 and 538, including
apparatus, and preface V, VI and XV. These are printed edition pages, not
photographs of ancient manuscript ink.

Lagarde prints this ending in **chapter 8**, whose heading is visible on p. 526.
His alpha 8:15–52 aligns by content with the modern AT 7:15–52 references in
Bezold. An initial source-reader assumption that the chapter numbers matched
was corrected against the heading before application. This is not a change
to the ancient story. The following references are Lagarde's own alpha units.

| Units and printed pages | Directly checked content |
| --- | --- |
| 8:15–21, p. 528 | Mordecai requests removal of Haman's letter; Esther requests lethal punishment, including Haman's children; royal approval and punishment follow |
| 8:22–32, pp. 530–532 | Royal letter directs recipients to disregard Haman's letters; retains resistance to attackers and festival instructions |
| 8:33–38, p. 532 | Mordecai's instruction to remain locally and celebrate God; his letter identifies Haman's intended killing date as Adar 13 |
| 8:39–45, p. 534 | Elevation, circumcision, nobody rising against the Jews, followed by their killing of seven hundred in Susa, a list including an unnamed brother and Haman's ten sons, and comprehensive plundering |
| 8:46, p. 534 | Permission to destroy and plunder whomever they wish; royal concession; seventy thousand one hundred killed |
| 8:47–52, pp. 536–538 | Writing, festivals on the fourteenth and fifteenth, Purim explanation, royal memorial and Mordecai's succession/elevation |

This verifies an actual selected Alpha ending with both delegated punishment
and later collective Jewish violence. It also qualifies two details of the
article's presentation. Alpha 8:46 does not explicitly say “tomorrow” or “a
second day”; those words cannot be imported from the longer Greek request.
At 8:20 the main singular “he struck” fits the preceding royal agency, but the
apparatus reports plural **ἐπάταξαν m**. The published Alpha witnesses cannot
all be assigned identical agency from the main line alone. Likewise Alpha's
Adar 13 at 8:38 must not be replaced by the other Greek form's Adar 14.

Preface XV explains the separately printed forms and their d/h/m versus A/B/S
variant reporting. Preface V–VI identifies d as Vaticanus 330 / Holmes 108,
h as Chisianus R VI 38 / Holmes 19, and m as Royal 1 D 2 / Holmes 93. The
preface says bracketed additions are editorial, beyond the manuscripts. The
spacing before 8:46 is printed layout, not a manuscript gap or proof of a
redaction boundary. Neither this edited recension nor its small apparatus is
a modern complete Alpha collation, and publication in 1883 does not date its
ancient readings. BSB's No Copyright–Non-Commercial Use Only statement is
recorded; acquired images remain private rather than being relicensed in Git.

## Papyrus 967 coverage and the modern apparatus

The early Greek papyrus must be considered, but not assigned readings outside
the surviving material. Root and a delegated reader read and visually checked
all of Kristin De Troyer's 2023 chapter, printed pp. 155–160 / PDF pp. 161–166,
including notes, tables and bibliography, in the university-hosted
[open-access volume](https://eprints.gla.ac.uk/274555/1/274555.pdf).
Its examples concern Addition A and 2:20–23, not the calendar or chapter 9
targets. The paper's possible Alpha influence is expressly tentative. Its
Hanhart quotations remain indirect evidence, not consultation of that apparatus.

The complete [CSNTM description](https://images.csntm.org/Manuscripts/Rahlfs_967/Rahlfs%20967%20%28LXX%20CBL%20BP%20IX%29.pdf),
dated August 1, 2013, lists Dublin's surviving pieces. Its ff. 17v/102v range
3:4–9 locates a potential 3:7 control; ff. 16r/103r and adjacent 16v/103v locate
3:13 and the addition. Its own note warns about Kenyon's apparently overlapping
legacy numbering. Four observed image requests failed with HTTP 500: no
papyrus pixels or target reading were acquired. Coverage labels do not prove
that the decisive day numeral survives. The listed Dublin pieces reach 8:2–6,
not 8:12 or chapter 9; this is non-coverage, not an attested omission. The
[Cologne institutional description](https://www.uni-koeln.de/phil-fak/ifa/NRWakademie/papyrologie/PTheol1.html)
identifies its published Esther portion as 1:1a–2:15, not those later targets.

The [Göttingen publication list](https://septuaginta.uni-goettingen.de/publications/septuaginta/)
identifies Hanhart's Esther second edition as 1983. The lawful licensed preview
shows front matter and part of the introduction, then stops before the target
apparatus; the publisher link returned 403. This bounded route check does not
mean the edition is unpublished or unavailable everywhere. No modern target
apparatus has been acquired, and the same failed routes are not new evidence
to repeat. Use a genuinely new lawful edition or image route for further work.
