# First Samuel 15 29 source comparison

## Result and consequence for POB

This passage contains three separate textual questions. Published 4Q51 supplies
direct Hebrew support for **ישוב**, “turn back,” against WLC **ישקר**, “lie.”
It does not preserve the proposed opening about Israel's division. The longer
Greek ending is another question again: establishing an early Greek clause
would not establish its exact Hebrew ancestor.

The [evidence record](../sources/textual_restoration/comparisons/samuel15_29.2026-10-16.v1.json)
registers the Hebrew alternative as an attested source candidate, not merely
another English gloss. POB's Hebrew, main English and notes remain unchanged
provisionally. No earliest-wording judgment, fresh decipherment or whole-verse
approval follows. The previous 14:41 disclosure remains intact.

## Hebrew survival and disputed reconstruction

Root read DJD XVII's complete printed pp. 77–78 / PDF pp. 100–101 visually,
including the transcription, reading notes, variants and reconstructed variants.
The edition groups fragments 8–10a–b and 11 under Plate XIIa, museum inventories
1097/1107. It gives PAM 43.111* for fragments 9 and 10a–b and PAM 43.113* 2.3
for 11. These are consulted edition locators, not newly acquired photographs.
Page 76 was also inspected for navigation and surrounding context.

The [versioned Qumran-Digital transcription](https://lexicon.qumran-digital.org/transcriptions/4Q51/2026-05-21/index.html?v=2026-05-21)
likewise displays **ישוב** without supply brackets or uncertainty dots. Its
division wording is bracketed. These two publications concern one physical
witness, not two ancient votes. Neither an uninterrupted preserved end of
verse 29 nor the longer Greek ending is established by the displayed supplies.

Yardney's chapter 4 challenges the fragment placements underlying the division
reconstruction. In particular, she reassigns 10a and removes constraints that
had motivated extra wording and omission of “today” in the neighboring line.
Her alternative retains **ישוב** while supplying the MT-like title. Both
reconstructed layouts can fit reasonably; fitting does not recover missing ink.
Her proposed Greek explanation and reassignment weaken the claimed Hebrew
support, but cannot exclude every lost alternative Vorlage. We have not
independently adjudicated the proposed joins or adopted her reconstruction.

## Greek ending and the Numbers comparison

Root visually read BMT printed pp. 51–52 / PDF pp. 67–68, including the target
apparatus. The [publisher's Rahlfs–Hanhart text](https://www.die-bibel.de/bibel/LXX/1SA.15)
confirms the division opening, turning-back verb and final pronoun. These are
edited/published readings, not fresh manuscript collation. BMT's old sigla must
not be silently equated with a later editor's manuscript groups.

Aejmelaeus's [2022 chapter](https://researchportal.helsinki.fi/en/publications/challenges-in-preparing-the-critical-edition-of-1-samuel/),
author pp. 10–11 / PDF pp. 11–12, selects the longer ending as Old Greek and
explains the isolated pronoun as residue of shortening toward Hebrew. Her
proposed Hebrew is explicitly a retroversion. Yardney accepts that Greek
selection; she is not an independent opposing witness against the ending.
The interrogative force is an interpretation, not a recovered Hebrew question.

The direct [Numbers 23:19 Greek control](https://www.die-bibel.de/bibel/LXX/NUM.23)
has passive threatening vocabulary earlier and a rhetorical final **ἐμμενεῖ**
question. The longer Samuel clause uses an active threatening verb and the
same final verb, but is not a verbatim copy of the whole Numbers sentence.
Local WLC Numbers instead has saying, doing, speaking and establishing.
This makes a Greek-level literary relationship a concrete possibility;
it does not prove Greek expansion or exclude a Hebrew antecedent. The shared
reliability theme alone cannot determine the direction or language of borrowing.

## Next discriminating work

Test the isolated-pronoun and longer-ending readings in identified witnesses,
including correction hands and local shortening behavior. Compare Josephus's
complete retelling and its position of the reliability statement; the present
Josephus evidence is quoted in the consulted studies, not a new direct edition
check. This can test early Greek history without promoting a Hebrew retroversion.

For the Hebrew verb, consult the actual staged-corruption/euphemism arguments
cited by Yardney before choosing between **ישוב** and **ישקר**. Local WLC
15:25–31 repeatedly uses returning language, while Numbers 23:19 provides a
lying/nonhuman/regret parallel: these are relevant controls, not proof of a
specific correction. For the opening, adjudicate the disputed fragment
identifications before another width fit. Do not repeat a same-image preview
or substitute the present Hebrew alternative for the entire Greek narrative.
