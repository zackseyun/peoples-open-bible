# Ezekiel 39 wrath in the Cryptoferratensis palimpsest edition

Cozza's 1867 edition of the Greek prophetic palimpsest at Grottaferrata
prints **`εξεχεα τον θυμον / μου`**, “I poured out my wrath,” at Ezekiel 39:29.
The decisive noun has none of the dots that the editor uses for unreadable
or doubtful text. This adds an inspected edition of an individual Greek
witness to POB's comparison. It does not establish different original Hebrew.
POB's Hebrew, main English and existing qualified note remain unchanged.

## Edition and exact location

The [Harvard and Google scan on Internet Archive](https://archive.org/details/sacrorumbiblior00cozzgoog)
contains Giuseppe Cozza, *Sacrorum Bibliorum vetustissima fragmenta Graeca et
Latina ex palimpsestis codicibus Bibliothecae Cryptoferratensis*, **Pars Prima,
Rome, 1867**, as printed on PDF page 7. Prolegomena section II, printed 1 /
PDF 11, identifies the old shelfmark **C.4** and then **E.β′.VII**. These are
edition-era identifiers; the separate proposed association with LDAB 9227
has not been independently resolved here. The acquired volume must not be
described as an inspected copy of the 1877 third part.

The target is **printed 147 / PDF 243**, headed **Codicis pag. 118** and
**Ezechiel XXXIX, 23–XL, 1**. These three page systems are distinct. The
printed footer controls over inconsistent OCR and derivative page metadata.
Counting the right column's text lines from the top, lines 16–17 contain
the quoted phrase; line 19 begins chapter 40. The printed page thus represents
the local 39-to-40 transition, with losses in both contexts. It does not
establish other chapter transitions or the order of the whole original book.

## What the notation permits

Prolegomena sections LXX–LXXI, printed LXVI–LXVII / PDF 72–73, explain the
layout and signs. Headers give the codex page and biblical range; the bottom
number gives the edition page. Dots mark unreadable **or doubtful** places.
Supplied titles and marginal or interlinear annotations are discussed in
the separate notes. The relevant annotation page, printed 308 / PDF 414,
contains neighboring loss notices but no correction to the target noun.

`θυμον` and the following `μου` are printed without internal doubt or loss
dots. The surrounding verse is incomplete: the preceding line has
`ανθ ω ..`, and the following house-of-Israel phrase has a gap in its
article and abbreviated sacred names. Neither a complete causal phrase nor
a complete verse should be silently reconstructed from this page. The short
quotation above preserves the line break but is not a typographic facsimile.

This is Cozza's published reading of manuscript text, not our independent
decipherment of the palimpsest. Absence of doubt dots records the editor's
judgment; it cannot certify every letter against modern manuscript imaging.

## Consequence for the source comparison and English

The [Ambrosian Syrohexapla comparison](EZEKIEL_39_SYROHEXAPLARIC_MARGIN_2026-10-13.md)
establishes a Syriac main-text wrath reading and an attributed marginal Spirit
reading with an erasure and uncertain hand. Cryptoferratensis supplies an
edition-level Greek attestation of the wrath noun, rather than another Greek
retroversion of that Syriac margin. These are distinct material witnesses,
but their genealogical independence is not established merely by agreement.
An edition, its later apparatus citations and its digital scan must not be
counted as separate ancient witnesses.

The finding strengthens the documented attestation of Greek `θυμος` at this
locus. It does not select between a different Hebrew exemplar, Greek lexical
interpretation or later textual development. The published noun also does
not resolve the Hebrew clause's causal versus temporal force. POB's
[current verse](../translation/ot/ezekiel/039/029.yaml) retains Spirit and
“when,” with the causal alternative and qualified Greek wrath note;
SHA256 `8d4d3343e5325ac09bc2252df7967624ccb88934f4aecb34959598425fb3589f`.
No additional reader note is necessary for this result.

The next discriminating inputs remain the actual Papyrus 967 target reading
and critical apparatus, with preservation and manuscript relationships kept
explicit. Modern photographs of this palimpsest would enable comparison with
Cozza's transcription; they have not been acquired in this check. ImageGen
cannot validate letters by making a plausible reconstruction resemble the
known edition. This case supplies neither a new recovered letter nor grounds
for changing the biblical canon.
