# First Samuel 6 19 Hebrew and Greek numeral variants

Date: 2026-10-19. Baseline: `9d50b00c245ddba0e6a9d81f42dafe2575b26c33`.

## Judgment

The seventy-only Hebrew reading is now verified as a published manuscript
collation, not merely an unnamed modern report. The wider evidence also has
fifty-thousand-only, reordered figures and a five-thousand Greek variant.
These are consequential alternatives, but they do not yet discriminate early
addition from later numerical repair or copying loss. Retain POB provisionally;
do not mistake that decision for a historical victory of the longer reading.

## Identified Hebrew collation

Kennicott, *Vetus Testamentum Hebraicum* I (1776), printed **525 / PDF 701**,
contains the actual numeral apparatus. Root inspected the complete page and
enlarged entry, and the complete preface I–IV / PDF33–36. Its caret marks
omission; occurrence labels 1º/2º are not hand labels. *Primo* indicates an
ascertainable first reading, whereas *forte* qualifies uncertainty. The preface
also cautions that words not regularly entered by the original scribe may be
counted as omitted. Thus the exact claim is “Kennicott reports omission,” not
a fresh certification of untouched manuscript letters.

| Published Hebrew feature | Kennicott identifiers | What it supports |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `חמשים אלף איש` omitted | 84, 210, 418 | Seventy remains; no local hand, margin or uncertainty qualifier on this entry |
| `שבעים איש` omitted | 253 | Fifty thousand remains, not seventy-only |
| `חמשים אלף איש שבעים איש` | 526 | Both figures retained in reverse order |
| Conjunction `וחמשים` | 160, 187, 198, 206, 559, 611, 651; *primo* 250 | Coordination is itself variable; 250's qualifier must survive attribution |

Idan and Nachum Dershowitz's
[manuscript key](https://dershowitz.net/assets/documents/idan-dershowitz-nachum-dershowitz-key-according-to-kennicott-number.pdf),
actual rows on PDF4,10,18, maps 84 to Bodleian MS Kennicott10 (Neubauer2324),
210 to Paris BnF hébreu32 (anciens fonds29), and 418 to Parma1834/1835
(DeRossi941). Its respective date labels are thirteenth, fourteenth–fifteenth,
and fourteenth century. These are key metadata, not new palaeographic findings
or proof that the copies are genealogically independent. The historical
collation number is not the Bodleian shelfmark number. No target manuscript
photograph was read.

## Wider Greek evidence

Brooke–McLean–Thackeray, *The Old Testament in Greek* II.1 (1927), printed
**20 / PDF36**, has both seventy and fifty thousand in its B-based body.
The complete page and own preface/key were inspected. The numeral apparatus
reports **five thousand** in c/x, while d and the separately quoted 44 have
reordered larger figures. There is no seventy-only numeral reading reported
in this inspected Greek unit. That is a bounded apparatus result, not a census
of every Greek copy.

The Samuel key identifies c = **376**, Escurial Y.II.5; x = **247**, Vatican
Urbin. gr.1; d = **107**, Ferrara Gr.188i. Do not transfer their identities
from another biblical book. The omission of a conjunction reported for 246 is
not omission of fifty thousand. Nor is h's omission of the much larger
6:13–7:1 unit a seventy-only witness.

The pinned QDR biblical file now permits an exact reference check absent from
the older comparison: zero `1Sam 6:19` tokens, with adjacent references in
4Q51 fragments6e_f and7a. This supplies no numeral reading and proves no
ancient omission. Reference indexing is not exhaustive manuscript coverage.

## Competing explanations

Driver's original 1913 *Notes*, complete **58–59 / PDF190–191**, favors
removing fifty thousand but expressly leaves its entry mechanism speculative.
He also favors the Greek opening while admitting uncertainty about its Hebrew
verb. Keil's [original commentary in digital reprint](https://biblehub.com/commentaries/kad/1_samuel/6.htm)
rejects the large number as a gloss but defends the repeated striking clauses
in the Hebrew opening. Their agreement about a number does not approve an
identical complete verse. Neither population estimates nor stylistic preference
is an observed scribal event.

A direct conditional check of written WLC demonstrates a viable countermodel:
in `שבעים איש חמשים אלף איש`, skipping from the first `איש` to after the
second loses exactly the fifty-thousand phrase. No ancestor's line layout or
actual copying event is supplied by that demonstration. Conversely, an old
numerical gloss and later attempts to coordinate, reorder or reduce it remain
possible. Josephus's seventy-person retelling remains the earlier dossier's
qualified early contrary evidence, not a newly collated Hebrew witness.

Seventy-only therefore must not be called conjecture-only; neither three
late reports nor repeated model agreement establishes its priority. The next
useful evidence is target-specific manuscript/correction verification or a
critical argument that discriminates these transmission histories. Park the
now-consulted apparatus pages rather than repeating them as a new vote.

preserves source pins and exact consultation limits. This is known published
variation newly verified for POB, not a newly discovered biblical reading.
