# Esther Alpha Text and the independent Hebrew source hypothesis

Fox's full 1990 argument strengthens the case for comparing Esther's Greek
forms separately. It does not establish a replacement Hebrew text or justify
changing POB at the two passages checked here. Both diagnostic Greek readings
are confirmed in Lagarde's Alpha edition, but Hebrew-informed Greek revision
remains a competing explanation. Retain the current Hebrew and English.

## The argument and its strongest alternative

Michael V. Fox, [The Alpha Text of the Greek Esther](https://openscholar.huji.ac.il/sites/default/files/he_bible_project/files/m.v._fox.pdf),
*Textus* 15 (1990), 27–54, argues that Alpha combines an independently
translated Hebrew narrative with later material taken from the other Greek
form. His evidence includes lexical clustering, Hebrew agreements, syntax and
narrative structure. His reconstructed proto-Alpha is an analytical layer,
not an extant Hebrew manuscript; he expressly allows an earlier layer (33).
His final preference is a collateral relative of the Masoretic narrative,
not a demonstrated direct ancestor (54).

The relevant alternative is not merely copying the selected Old Greek text.
A reviser could rewrite Greek while consulting Hebrew, drawing on another
Greek rendering, or preserving inherited translation language. Consequently,
evidence of Hebrew contact need not establish an independent translation,
and an independent translation need not preserve the earliest Hebrew wording.
These are separate historical questions, not a vote between whole editions.

Fox's clustered lexical matches support differences between stretches of
Alpha; they do not by themselves identify the language or date of each
underlying stage. His syntax analysis is important positive evidence, not
proof that a Hebrew-aware revision is impossible. Fox himself distinguishes
the translation-versus-recension question from the statistical profile alone
(52), before arguing that his combined evidence favors independence (52–54).
No new statistical fit or independent replication is claimed here.

## Two direct tests

The comparisons use actual content rather than matching chapter numbers:
Fox ii 16b is Hebrew 1:17; iii 7 is Hebrew 2:7. Lagarde's printed Alpha
pages 508 and 510 confirm the expressions. His small d/h/m apparatus reports
no variant at either diagnostic expression; that is not a comprehensive
modern collation or proof of unanimous manuscript support. The preface identifies
d as Vaticanus 330 / Holmes 108, h as Chisianus R VI 38 / Holmes 19 and m as
Royal 1 D 2 / Holmes 93. Swete's selected main text is a distinct Greek control.

A significant control qualifies the second test: Swete's 2:7 apparatus
reports an added beauty phrase in the margin of a Sinaiticus corrector,
with wording and order different from Alpha. Thus the extra beauty description
cannot be treated as unique evidence for Alpha's independent Hebrew source.
This directly confirms the relevance of Fox's own recensional qualification
(46). Swete's key identifies Sinaiticus; no date or historical direction is
inferred from the marginal report. At 1:17 his small apparatus varies the
report construction but does not report Alpha's diagnostic verb.

Lagarde's nearby apparatus also matters: at ii 16 it varies the pronoun
αὐτῆς / αὕτη, and at iii 7 it reports ειδη in m in the beauty clause. Neither
is evidence against the two target expressions, but both prevent presenting
the edited Alpha paragraph as a single unvarying manuscript text.

## Source text and translation decision

Keep WLC and POB's prospective wording at 1:17, and its bringing-up wording
at 2:7. Fox himself accepts the Masoretic pointing of the first verb as correct
in that context (45). Do not turn the Alpha past tense into a Hebrew emendation,
add faithfully to the Hebrew-based English, or combine Alpha's shorter account
with selected clauses from other literary forms. This comparison does not
certify every existing POB expression or note in these verses.

The result is a better constrained historical explanation, not a newly
discovered ancient reading. It leaves the earlier endings decision unchanged:
the literary forms are worth comparing and presenting distinctly, while their
direction of dependence remains disputed. Fox 1990 does not supply the original
lost-negative argument for 9:10 identified in the earlier report. Neither this
comparison nor a proposed Hebrew source decides biblical canon.
