The strongest objection remains material: “heaves” can suggest convulsion or
swelling more specifically than the Hebrew alone establishes. “Rises up”
better exposes the lifting interpretation and echoes the adjacent English
“rise up,” although the Hebrew verbs differ. “Before him” also removes the
explicit face image from the main text. The two revised notes preserve
rising/upheaval alternatives and the face idiom, including the from/before
range. The movement is poetic, without certifying a physical mechanism.

The unusual intransitive נשׂא analysis remains provisional. Poetic usage and
the related-language comparisons reported in the article counter automatic
corruption or repointing. A shortened Niphal of שׁאה is a separate proposal
with a devastation sense; the note discloses it without adopting it under the
retained pointing. Greek passive wording cannot decide original Hebrew
pointing or require inserted Hebrew letters.

A separate, concrete rationale correction removes the false statement that
הָרִים contains the definite article. Its first he belongs to the noun.
Mur88's published ההרים is distinct evidence, not automatic historical
priority. Generic English “the mountains” is retained. Historical HALOT
labels are not presented as freshly consulted entries.
