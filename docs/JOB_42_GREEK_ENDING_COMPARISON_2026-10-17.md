# The extended Greek ending of Job

## Result for POB

The consulted Greek ending explicitly anticipates Job rising again and identifies
him with the Edomite king Jobab. Those statements materially change the ending
readers encounter; they are not in POB's current Hebrew ending. They are attested
Greek literary material, not newly recovered Hebrew or a new discovery for
scholarship. Neither their exact Hebrew precursor nor their presence in the
earliest Greek translation is established by this comparison.

Retain POB's declared Hebrew form provisionally. Source, main English, notes,
order and canon are unchanged in this pass. A labelled parallel Greek ending
or reader disclosure merits a separate application; unresolved historical
priority need not prevent presenting a correctly attributed attested form.
The [evidence packet](../sources/textual_restoration/comparisons/job42_ending.2026-10-17.v1.json)
records the actual source, consultation and canonical-control pins. This is
published-source comparison, not fresh machine transcription or image restoration.

## Compare the connected forms

Root read the source, POB English and notes at Job 42:7–17, 14:10,12–14,
19:25–27, Genesis 36:10,13,31–35 and 1 Chronicles 1:43–46. These 29 controls
are not a complete rationale or lexicon audit. The Greek control is Swete II,
printed 600–603 / PDF 618–621, with its own key and preface. Vaticanus supplies
the selected text here; this manual collation is not the complete modern Greek
apparatus. No precise issue date is assigned to this scan.

| Feature | Current Hebrew and POB | Consulted Greek form |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Offering and speech at 42:8 | The friends offer; they have not spoken rightly to/about God | Selected B has a third-person offering verb and an accusation concerning Job; A reports a second-person offering verb, qualifying a uniform Greek agency claim |
| Acceptance and restoration at 42:9–10 | Yahweh accepts Job and restores his fortunes when he prays | Explicitly describes forgiveness through/on account of Job and prayer concerning the friends |
| Lifespan at 42:16 | 140 years after these events; no explicit lifetime total | Selected B has 170 after the calamity and 240 total; the apparatus reports an additional eight for Sinaiticus corrector c-a, A and C |

| 42:17a | No corresponding statement | Predicts Job rising again with those the Lord raises |
| 42:17b–e | No corresponding genealogy or royal list | Refers to a Syriac book, identifies Job as Jobab, gives family information, Edomite rulers and royal identities for his friends |

Keep the age pairs together. Cook's quoted modern translation gives 248 total,
where Swete's selected B has 240; that difference is supported by an actual
reported plus-eight branch, not a reason to normalize all Greek witnesses to
one number. No pre-calamity age is inserted into Hebrew by subtracting either
Greek pair.

The same edition gives positive Alexandrinus and Sinaiticus readings in the
resurrection sentence. At 17a, **A**, not Sinaiticus, omits the connective;
Sinaiticus and A have different reported word orders, and A places the Lord
after the final verb. A also has additional genealogical material after 17e.
Thus neither a single uniform long ending nor a complete transcription of all
codices follows from the manual's selected text. Root and the Greek reader
checked the small apparatus at higher resolution before fixing the siglum.

The key identifies C as Ephraemi. The preface explicitly warns that its damaged
palimpsest text was imperfectly read in Tischendorf's edition. A reported C
reading remains published evidence, not newly verified ink; apparatus silence
does not certify its complete wording at 17a.

## Literary relationship is not recovered Hebrew

Swete I, printed 70 / PDF 102, supplies an A-based Genesis 36 control at this
location, not surviving Vaticanus text. Its sequence of Greek royal names and
places closely parallels the Job appendix. The Hebrew Genesis and Chronicles
controls likewise contain the Edomite Jobab sequence. This supports investigating
literary reuse or interpretation; it does not independently attest every word
of a lost Hebrew Job appendix or establish that Job and Jobab were historically
the same person.

The two introductory formulas also need separate treatment. The resurrection
statement appeals to something written without naming a recoverable work.
The following Syriac-book reference does not identify that work with the
surviving Qumran Targum, the Peshitta, or a newly discovered complete book.
Do not silently convert either citation into an independent manuscript or a
claim that the whole Greek book was translated from that particular source.

Connected wording constrains a simple theological history. Greek 42:7 retains
speech before God while 42:8 concerns Job, so the entire accusation has not
uniformly changed its object. Greek 42:11 still attributes what happened to
the Lord, although the Hebrew calamity noun has no corresponding noun there.
That clause needs contextual interpretation; a local shift does not by itself
prove removal of all divine responsibility. At 19:26 Swete reports the skin/body difference
and correction layers; either noun still needs a contextual interpretation.
A healing interpretation does not by itself prove a late date for every
resurrection reading, nor does the ending require harmonizing every earlier
Hebrew question into an explicit resurrection assertion.

## What the full Cook study establishes and what remains unverified

Root read all 22 pages, including notes and bibliography, of Johann Cook's
2011 [study of Greek Job and afterlife interpretation](https://scielo.org.za/pdf/ote/v24n2/02.pdf),
printed 324–345, and visually checked the decisive discussion at 339–343.
He argues for a distinctive translation profile, interprets the disputed
earlier passages without resurrection, and assigns the explicit final statement
to a later reviser. Those are historical and interpretive judgments, not
additional witnesses. His **NO OG TEXT** label at the death notice must not
be mistaken for absence from Swete's attested selected Greek text. The original
Ziegler target apparatus was not consulted merely by reading this article.

At printed 342, Cook quotes Fernández Marcos's claim that the resurrection
colophon is missing from Sahidic and apparently P.Oxy. 3522, and is marked in
Syro-Hexapla. Neither those version entries nor the original P.Oxy. edition's
target notes are verified by that quotation. The papyrus claim required a
separate preservation check before being counted against 17a.

Oxford's catalogue range points to 42:11–12, but that is not the whole problem.
Robert Kraft's actual [published examination, section 14](https://ccat.sas.upenn.edu/rak/earlylxx/jewishpap.html#14),
read completely by root and the Greek reader, reports a second column with
only three legible letters. Its competing identifications yield markedly
different reconstructed column heights and line counts. These are conditional
fits, not independently measured complete columns. The original edition's
detailed notes and a verified second-column alignment remain unconsulted.
Consequently P.Oxy. 3522's alleged omission of 17a is **unverified here**, not
disproved simply because a catalogue names 42:11–12. Nor do possible traces
of another part establish the complete resurrection/genealogical continuation.

The older papyrus's reported date is therefore not yet a discriminating vote
at 17a. The older physical attestation preference cannot supply a missing
alignment or transfer an early object's date to an inferred complete ending.
Removing that modest preference does not change the held source decision.
Religious compatibility alone cannot date an addition as Christian, Jewish,
original translation or revision.

The official open-access record for Häner's 2019 study was found, but its PDF
route reached a bot challenge; the publisher route required access. The full
article was not read or weighed as a contrary argument. The old SBL Johnson
PDF route returned 404. No challenge, authentication or access restriction was
bypassed, and no unavailable work is silently counted as consulted.

## The Judean Desert witnesses do not settle the ending

The bounded Hebrew/Aramaic source check consulted current Qumran-Digital
2026-10-06 transcriptions of 2Q15, 4Q99, 4Q100, 4Q101 and 4Q101a, plus the
Aramaic translations 4Q157 and 11Q10. Actual rows, parallels and fragment
navigation were checked at the scopes in the packet. None supplies a verified
42:17 ending for this decision. This is not an exhaustive all-manuscript census.

There is also a positive Aramaic comparison. In 11Q10 column 38.2–3, the
published text describes forgiveness of the friends' sins on Job's account,
following God hearing Job. The relevant words are outside the edition's
supply brackets; a nearby superscript insertion is a separate feature.
This semantically parallels Greek 42:9, against the Hebrew face-acceptance
idiom. It is evidence about an Aramaic rendering, not proof of extra lost
Hebrew words or of independence from Greek. Shared contextual interpretation
remains a serious explanation.

The decisive 11Q10 boundary is numbered column 38. Root read its complete
displayed table; the source reader also checked column 37 and damaged-fragment
context. Rows 38.1–8 concern the restoration and gifts, while 38.9 partly reaches
the blessing at the start of 42:12 and 38.10 is fragmentary. There is no complete
42:17 death notice or demonstrated physical book-ending here. The automatic
reference links are inadequate for this alignment; the source reader checked
the content against the same release's Hebrew 42:9–17 display. Missing later
material cannot become an attested omission of resurrection or genealogy.

The 4Q101a display adds a control absent from the book map's annotated hits,
not proof of a newly discovered independent object. Its Add reference and the
reciprocal 4Q99/4Q100 fragment references need identity controls before counting
support. The current map's convenience index is not an exhaustive witness list.
No material reassignment, new letter, join or manuscript date is certified here.

## Source English and canon decisions

The secure result is a consequential Greek alternative with internal variants.
The unresolved questions are its component history, exact source-language
precursor and earliest recoverable Greek form. POB's Hebrew retention is
provisional, not a demonstrated historical victory. No English rewrite,
repointing or Hebrew addition is approved, and the current verse rationales
are not certified as optimal by remaining unchanged.

For the user's canon question, this is a literary-form question within an
already biblical book, not discovery of a new complete book. POB's existing
[scope policy](../DEUTEROCANONICAL.md) permits presenting differently received
material with clear labels without declaring it equally authoritative for
every community. A parallel Greek presentation could therefore be useful,
but no canonical-status change follows automatically from ancient attestation
or a source citation. No such reader application or About-page integration is
performed by this source comparison.
