## Useful ancillary findings

At Exodus 6:18, OSHB word `02pV4` has year lemma `c/8141` but morphology
`HC/Acmdc`. The published
[code guide](https://hb.openscriptures.org/parsing/HebrewMorphologyCodes.html)
decodes the main tag as a cardinal adjective, masculine dual construct.
That conflicts with contextual “years of Kohath’s life,” also framed by
6:16 and 6:20. The pinned upstream Exod.xml is byte-identical to the local
file and retains this tag. This is an annotation warning, not a recovered
ancient reading. Do not change the correct English to “two” or silently
rewrite the diplomatic source to match a parser.

For Ezekiel 48:28, [NET’s passage note](https://classic.net.bible.org/passage.php?passage=Eze+48%3A28&theme=false)
expressly treats Egypt as implied, though absent from Hebrew. The assessor’s
initial deletion proposal therefore became a retention-with-disclosure proposal:
move [a] from Gad to Meribath-kadesh and qualify [b] that the geographic
identification is contextual. Deletion would reduce main-text specificity for
readers who skip notes. Neither proposal is applied by this research record.
The note-placement defect is not counted as a semantic main-text improvement.

For 1 Chronicles 19:1, the inspected
[BDB reproduction](https://biblehub.com/hebrew/4427.htm) permits the relevant
royal senses of מלך. Accession follows from context rather than morphology
alone. The Samuel parallel supports a permissible English alternative, not an
additional Chronicles manuscript reading. Modern translation controls are
interpretations, not ancient witness votes.

Exodus 6:30’s incidental LORD/Yahweh policy mismatch is a separate repair lead,
not a fourth sample member. No chapter-wide corrections, historical-priority
judgment, manuscript-image decipherment or canon recommendation follow from
this three-verse assessment.
