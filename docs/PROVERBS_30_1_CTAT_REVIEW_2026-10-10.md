## The actual target discussion

773–775 (PDF802–804), contains the complete Pr30,1AB entry. The previously
acquired [institutional PDF](https://www.zora.uzh.ch/entities/publication/ab74a357-3f3e-43ce-a85d-469bc35229f4)
is a private study copy: 8,114,592 bytes, 1,009 pages, SHA256
`cb868ca3225e2eb90980947265d18e8e3ce42756b0baa32e57d79e56ccc2fd79`.
Root reads the complete rendered pages; broken Hebrew/Greek extraction is
navigation only. Copyright2015 and the preface's appearance2016 do not imply
newly updated target research; the preface describes an older research horizon.
No full copyrighted pages or source import enter Git.

The entry's A question concerns הַמַּשָּׂא. Its B question concerns repetition
of לְאִיתִיאֵל. The reported oneD/fiveC committee judgment retains the repetition
despite uncertainty between accidental duplication and accidental omission.
It does not constitute six votes for the weary interpretation. Convention
page xv defines C as probable with considerable doubt and D as possible but
very uncertain. The apparatus's `crrp` acknowledges an already corrupted form;
retaining the repeated sequence does not approve every received vowel or name
analysis. These grades are neither manuscript counts nor numerical probabilities.

## Distinct Hebrew interpretations

| Interpretation | Source operation | Semantic consequence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Current POB | Received pointing and recipient-name analysis | Address to Ithiel, repeated, and Ucal. |
| Existing POB note | Repointing/redivision, with an exhausted final clause | Repeated weariness addressed to God, ending worn out. |
| CTAT proposed primitive interpretation | Redivide both לאיתיאל as לאיתי אל; treat ואכל as ability | Repeated weariness addressed to God, directed toward succeeding. |
| CTAT accepted-text interpretation | Retain Ithiel but read the ending verbally and connect capacity to him | Speaker credits Ithiel for his capacity/knowledge. |

Preserving the consonantal sequence does not make these English-only variants.
Segmentation, vowels, morphology and syntax change. CTAT's ability ending is
not the shortened כלה exhaustion derivation examined earlier. Its discussion
does not add a negative or establish that POB's existing final “worn out” is
the uniquely correct interpretation.

CTAT cites Isaiah16:12, Jeremiah20:9 and Job4:2 as pairings of weariness with
יכל. Root checks the actual WLC passages. These strengthen the lexical
connection, but the first two explicitly negate ability, while Job asks who can
restrain speech. They do not attest the exact purpose relation proposed for
Proverbs, nor supply a missing negative there. POB30:2–4 supplies a plausible
context of limited understanding; it can also follow a superscription. Ithiel
is independently a personal name in the WLC genealogy at Nehemiah11:7.

## Greek reports and transmission argument

Printed774 reports that HP252 and Morin attribute singular Ithiel plus
τελέσω to Aquila; Morin alone attributes singular Ithiel plus δυνήσομαι to
Theodotion. Completion and ability are distinct semantic controls. These are
CTAT's published attributions, not freshly read underlying Greek copies,
independently checked Morin apparatus or direct attestation of repeated weary
vocatives. Their object dates and independent transmission remain unverified.

The earlier identified publisher Greek addresses people trusting God and ends
with first-person ceasing. Publisher Latin describes God's presence and a
strengthened speaker. CTAT proposes traditional explanation of names and a
tendency to obscure non-Solomonic authors as an account of version divergence,
using midrash and Proverbs31:1. This is its transmission hypothesis, not a
demonstrated motive of every ancient translator. Its Saadya discussion also
supports an Ithiel/ability interpretation; that is medieval reception, not an
ancient manuscript vote. Neither discussion proves the exact Hebrew source
behind the versions. The publisher bodies previously read are not reread or
counted as new independent witnesses in this task.

## Decision and next discriminator

The new lexical controls strengthen the ability analysis as a serious option.
The strongest countercase remains the actually pointed recipient reading,
Ithiel's name parallel and coherent superscription function. Source priority
and full English application remain HOLD. Provisional retention of names is
not a finding that they are earliest; CTAT's authority cannot settle the choice.

A future proposal must choose ability versus exhaustion explicitly, justify
the final verb, purpose/coordination and accents, and explain the divergent
version readings. Direct reviser apparatus or Fox's actual target commentary
could discriminate those explanations; the available Fox preview cannot.
No need to reacquire this volume or repeat judges on unchanged inputs.
