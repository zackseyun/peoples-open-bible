# Jeremiah 31 lexical and version controls

## Judgment

The negative interpretation of **בעלתי בם** is a genuine published lexical
proposal, not merely a conjectural replacement of Hebrew letters. Direct checks
now establish what the cited lexicon, commentary and versions actually say.
They do not establish that rejection is the best Hebrew interpretation or that
the Greek preserves a different, earlier Hebrew verb. Retain the distinct
Jeremiah and Hebrews main texts provisionally. The false equivalence in POB's
Jeremiah note still needs the separate correction identified in the
[first comparison](JEREMIAH_31_NEW_COVENANT_COMPARISON_2026-10-18.md).

The [packet](../sources/textual_restoration/comparisons/jeremiah31_lexical_versions.2026-10-18.v1.json)
preserves that predecessor and binds the new inputs. No source, main English,
reader note, metadata, code or canon change is applied here.

## The lexicon is qualified and its examples remain disputed

The actual [Gesenius and Tregelles scan](https://archive.org/download/hebrewchaldeelex00geseuoft/page/n149.jpg)
places the complete בעל verb entry on printed CXXX (130), across both columns.
Sense 3 begins “prob. to loathe, to reject.” It interprets Jeremiah 3:14 and
31:32 negatively, appeals to versions and Arabic, and cites Pococke pp. 5–10.
It also concedes that the ordinary dominion sense can work in 31:32 with a
concessive construction, although it judges that sense harsh. Its semantic
analogy moves from superiority to contempt. These are arguments, not additional
Hebrew examples independently establishing the rare sense.

The inspected [title page](https://archive.org/download/hebrewchaldeelex00geseuoft/page/n6.jpg)
names Tregelles and Samuel Bagster and Sons, Limited, London, without a printed
date. The catalogue's bracketed 1857 and handwritten date do not securely date
this impression. This is an undated Bagster printing, not a newly authenticated
1857 first edition. The older Latin entry and Pococke itself remain unconsulted.

Lane's *Arabic English Lexicon*, Book I, printed 228, middle column, was checked
in the actual [single-page scan](https://www.arabic-english-dictionary.com/assets/pdf/Page_0228.pdf).
The construction **بَعِلَ بِأَمْرِهِ** describes perplexity, fear or disgust
about one's affair, including inability to decide what to do. It does not by
itself demonstrate actively rejecting another person. Cross-language semantic
analogy is relevant but cannot substitute for Hebrew usage with that recipient.
The exact Lane impression was not established from this isolated page.

## Radak preserves competing explanations

The Hebrew of [Radak on 3:14](https://www.sefaria.org/Radak_on_Jeremiah.3.14)
and [31:32](https://www.sefaria.org/Radak_on_Jeremiah.31.32) was read in full
through the Sefaria API, version Radak on Nach. Its metadata identifies no exact
printed exemplar. David Kimhi's own 3:14 explanation explicitly uses a future
form, **אבעל בכם אחר שגרשתי אתכם**, after expulsion. His 31:32 explanation is
concessive and relational. Both entries separately report his father's negative
interpretation, associated with the following ב and Arabic.

At 31:32 the father's explanation compares בעלתי with בחלתי and cites
Zechariah 11:8's abhorrence expression. This is a semantic comparison, not a
reported Jeremiah manuscript spelling בחלתי. The actual WLC Zechariah 11:4–9
control confirms the cited clause and its different actor direction: their soul
abhors the speaker. A source-word replacement does not follow from the gloss.

Radak's future interpretation under the unchanged Hebrew form supplies an
actual interpretive counterexample to the inference that future Greek mastery
must entail a different Hebrew ancestor or no prior relationship. Medieval
commentary is not ancient manuscript attestation, and reporting father and son
does not create two independent Hebrew witnesses or unanimous rabbinic support.

## Direct version controls do not settle independence

The [Syriac target](https://www.peshitta.eu/ot/jeremiah/31.html) and
[3:12–18 control](https://www.peshitta.eu/ot/jeremiah/3.html) were read directly,
along with 14:21. The site's [source statement](https://www.peshitta.eu/about.html)
identifies the OT as the Syriac Orthodox Patriarchate's 2020 publication. This
dates the edition, not a newly collated ancient copy. The Targum controls use
Sefaria's Hebrew-script Aramaic, labelled Mikraot Gedolot, likewise without an
exact printed exemplar. Short extracts below remove combining points only.

| Control | Syriac edition | Targum Jonathan edition |
|---|---|---|
| Jeremiah 3:14 | ܨܒܝܬ ܒܟܘܢ, willing or favorable disposition toward you | אתרעיתי בכון, favorable disposition toward you |
| Jeremiah 31:32 | ܒܣܝܬ ܒܗܘܢ, despising them | אתרעיתי בהון, favorable disposition toward them |

The Syriac does not mechanically interpret both Hebrew ב constructions as
negative. Its 31:32 **ܒܛܠܘ** expresses covenant annulment; 31:33 has singular
**ܠܢܡܘܣܝ**, my law, rather than the inspected Greek plural laws. The same
annulment root occurs in 14:21's covenant plea corresponding to Hebrew הפר.
This is a mixed feature profile, not proof that the target's negative verb
cannot reflect selective Greek influence. Agreement at other features neither
proves independence here nor uniquely identifies a Hebrew precursor.

The Targum's positive wording is also what Radak quotes, not an additional
independent version supplied by his commentary. The new checks make the
competing interpretations more concrete; they do not turn versions, cognate
dictionary entries and medieval explanations into a manuscript majority.

## Consequence and next evidence

No original Hebrew example outside the disputed passages has been established
for this precise negative sense in this follow-up. That is an evidence gap,
not a claim that the sense is impossible. A discriminating next source input
would be such usage with its actor and recipient clear, a critical Syriac
target collation, or the complete modern Greek apparatus. Do not repeat the
now-consulted entry and version words as another priority vote. The exact
reader-note correction can proceed separately without resolving that history.
