# Reader disclosure for the Greek ending of Job

## Decision and scope

Readers should be told about the attested Greek continuation after Job's death
without silently adding it to a Hebrew-based translation. This candidate adds
one textual-variant note to Job 42:17. The complete Hebrew source object, main
English without note markers, and existing lifespan note stay unchanged. No
resurrection interpretation is inserted into earlier Hebrew passages.

The [source comparison](JOB_42_GREEK_ENDING_COMPARISON_2026-10-17.md) and its
[evidence packet](../sources/textual_restoration/comparisons/job42_ending.2026-10-17.v1.json)
remain the frozen no-application snapshot. This later application changes only
the reader-disclosure status if approved and applied. It does not repin that
snapshot's canonical controls or relax its unresolved historical judgments.
The [application contract](../sources/textual_restoration/comparisons/job42_reader_contract.2026-10-17.v1.json)
fixes the exact candidate, baseline, permitted changes and review criteria.

## Exact note

Keep the existing note `[a]` on “full of days” and append `[b]` at the end of
the verse. Its reason is `textual_variant`:

> Swete's Greek text continues after Job's death with a statement that he will rise again with those the Lord raises, then identifies him as Jobab and gives a genealogy and Edomite royal list. POB provisionally retains the Hebrew ending. The Greek continuation's earliest form and any Hebrew precursor remain uncertain.

This is a factual description of the consulted Greek edition, not a newly
translated parallel Bible text. It identifies the significant resurrection and
genealogical material without claiming a uniform Greek manuscript tradition,
an original Hebrew appendix, or a new discovery. It leaves the edition's
different ages and the Aramaic forgiveness parallel in the linked comparison;
neither is needed to explain this particular note.

## Evidence and strongest objection

Swete II, printed 602–603 / PDF 620–621, actually prints the death notice and
then the resurrection sentence, Jobab identification, family information and
Edomite rulers. The same-volume key and conventions were checked in the source
comparison. Local Alexandrinus and Sinaiticus variants qualify claims about
complete codex wording, but do not prevent this description of the selected
edition. The note does not depend on an unverified papyrus omission, a quoted
versional report, or Cook's judgment about the earliest Greek translation.

The strongest objection is that an end-of-book note can make the continuation
sound either uniformly Greek or historically original. Naming the consulted
edition and expressly keeping priority uncertain constrain that inference.
Provisional Hebrew retention is an editorial base choice, not a demonstrated
historical victory. The note does not identify the appendix's Syriac-book
reference with a surviving Targum or recommend adding another book to a canon.

The new access checks did not yield a complete additional study or edition.
JSTOR's article record supplied no readable full article; the newly indexed
encoded Vienna PDF address returned 502. Search hits for Oxyrhynchus volume L
led to bibliographic material, not its original target notes. No incomplete
snippet or abstract is counted as a complete argument. Those routes do not
justify reopening priority or repeating the already completed source review.
