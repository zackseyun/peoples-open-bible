# 1 Samuel 2:22 full Hebrew source and English

Date: 2026-10-17. Base revision: `7fe0843f2885ea54cf780739e6768bda4fb969b2`.
Status: **provisional editorial selection; not a canonical application**.

## Complete proposal and meaning

```text
ועלי זקן מאד וישמע את אשר עושים בניו לבני ישראל׃
```

> Eli was very old, and he heard what his sons were doing to the people of Israel.

This supersedes the earlier WLC-minus-one-clause research example as the
complete working proposal. It also changes the report's wording and recipients;
it is not a diplomatic transcription of 4QSamᵃ, a quotation of WLC or a claimed
recovered autograph. Interpretive reading outside the stated changes follows
the base; the Hebrew is unpointed and the final verse punctuation is editorial.

The main-text consequence is consequential: this verse would no longer
explicitly accuse the sons of intercourse with sanctuary women. The complete
longer reading remains in a reader note and apparatus. This selection neither
denies such misconduct historically nor erases its early reception in Josephus.
The source decision is textual, not a preference to reduce moral severity.

## Decisions across the complete verse

| Unit | Working choice | Evidence and strongest limitation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Eli's age | Retain the general old-age statement, without a number | MT and consulted Greek forms agree here. Qumran preserves a ninety-year component; its following eight-year completion is supplied. Assimilation to 4:15 is plausible, not proved; an earlier age notice remains possible. |
| Hearing verb | וישמע | Published Qumran letters are outside supplies. Greek's aorist supports hearing but does not determine this Hebrew form over ושמע. Priority is weaker than attestation; English stays “he heard.” |
| First כל | Omit provisionally | Qumran's crucial את junction is supplied; the absence is a published reconstruction, not surviving omission ink. The Greek body has no corresponding “all,” but c and Adpqtxz positively add totalizing wording; Greek compression remains possible. This is a relatively weak component of the selection. |
| Sons' actions | עושים | Qumran preserves שים, with עו supplied; that ending differs from MT's יעשון. Greek's imperfect can represent either construction. It supports ongoing actions, not a unique Hebrew retroversion. |
| Subject | His sons | MT, Qumran and the selected Greek body agree. BM a* loses αυτου τοις υιοις, changing the subject to the sons of Israel. Repeated forms of υἱός offer a plausible copying-loss mechanism; no scribal motive is claimed. |
| Recipients | לבני ישראל | Qumran preserves לבני; Israel's final two letters are marked doubtful. The Greek body's τοῖς υἱοῖς Ἰσραήλ agrees. This is stronger direct Hebrew evidence than the first כל gap, though Greek idiom alone would not establish its antecedent. |
| Longer clause | Omit provisionally, preserve in disclosure | Published Qumran space argument and the short Greek body support the choice. The gap/join model is not independently reproduced; positive longer Greek branches and Josephus attest early longer reception. An early shared omission or deliberate mitigation cannot be excluded. |

These choices are **feature-specific**, not a vote for every Qumran reading or
one uniform Greek recension. In particular, no chronological preference
turns the partly supplied ninety-eight-year restoration into preserved text.
The age notice at 4:15 is a separate source question; this pass changes neither
it nor a narrative chronology.

The selected short ending remains preferred because the published Hebrew
spatial argument and short Greek tradition jointly support it, while local
sanctuary language allows a plausible expansion history. Neither argument is
decisive: the dependence direction involving Exodus is disputed, and no
mechanical omission trigger is required for every possible ancient omission.
Without the modest preference for older direct-language evidence the short
choice would remain weaker, but still defensible from the same published and
literary arguments. The age and first-כל decisions remain particularly open.
The result is a best working proposal, not certification of the entire verse's
earliest exact wording. The proposal deliberately separates that uncertainty
from secure attestation of its individual published alternatives.

## Source controls actually consulted

Root read complete DJD XVII printed **43 / PDF 66**, including the connected
2:24 discussion, alongside the already retained transcription on printed39,
reconstructed-variants argument on printed45 and internal-join notes on
printed40–41. The edition itself treats some surrounding Qumran/Greek expansions
as secondary; agreement with an older object is not an automatic rule.

Root and the Greek reader read the complete BM Samuel printed **8 / PDF 24**
body and local apparatus. Its selected opening is
`καὶ ἤκουσεν ἃ ἐποίουν οἱ υἱοὶ αὐτοῦ τοῖς υἱοῖς Ἰσραήλ`.
Totalizing words occur both after the hearing verb and at the start of some
long insertions after Israel; z's margin omits that latter παντα. Do not
collapse these positions or call an entire manuscript group “without all.”
BM's a* is its lowercase siglum, not capital-A Codex Alexandrinus.

The QDR primary rows 13–14 retain their exact supplies and uncertainty marks.
The first כל decision relies partly on reconstructed space, while לבני and
the participial ending have preserved-letter support. QDR/DJD are the same
physical witness, not two independent votes. Their modern publication dates
are not the date of the ancient wording or a newly discovered manuscript.

Root read current source/English 2:21–26 and 4:15,18, and checked the actual
WLC XML target words and punctuation. No 37-record, whole-book or full-index
collation is claimed again. The existing
[long-clause dossier](SAMUEL_2_22_SANCTUARY_CLAUSE_2026-10-17.md),

## English and reader disclosure

A bounded English-only comparison withheld candidate identities and compared
three renderings against the fixed proposed Hebrew in current paragraph
context. It preferred “the people of Israel” narrowly over “the Israelites”;
both were faithful. The MT-based “all that ... all Israel” was unsupported
against this proposed source. That finding does not establish which Hebrew
source should be selected.

The repeated בניו / בני ישראל wording was explicitly compared with “his sons
... the children/sons of Israel.” “People of Israel” expresses the collective
without implying minors or a male-only biological group, but loses the overt
repetition of the kinship noun. The note preserves that literal expression.
“To” retains the broad relation to the affected people; “against” would make
the adverse relation more explicit. No fasting, visions, priestess office,
coercion or motive is added.

The complete candidate has an age-variant note after “old” and a combined
wording/long-clause note after “Israel.” The latter retains the existing POB
long-clause rendering and its served/assembled alternative. These are source
and translation disclosures, not an assertion that sexual misconduct was
unknown to early readers. Old notes and rationales are archived with the
baseline hash rather than silently reused as approval of changed source text.

## Composition and promotion boundary

The complete candidate lives under `sources/textual_restoration/applications/`.
Its source is explicitly `POB-critical-draft`, not WLC. Source changes are
recorded as exact unpointed base-plus-patch operations using the existing
composition verifier; no new general editing framework is introduced.

The WLC loader collects inline maqaf segments at the serialized verse's end.
The actual XML target contains four internal maqafs, not four manuscript
maqafs after meeting. Removing the long clause also removes that serialized
terminal tail; this punctuation cleanup is disclosed, not counted as an ancient
variant. No global WLC/parser change is made in this case.

Drafting uses the public-domain WLC base, independently selected ancient
wording facts and original English. Modern editions inform the selection;
no commercial modern English translation supplies its wording, no private
manuscript images or complete modern article/transcription are vendored, and
no broad licence clearance is asserted. Consultation and fact-level citation
do not license wholesale importing of editorial reconstructions.

Old cross-check scores and revision-pass flags are archived, not inherited.
A separate hash-bound full-record review must decide whether this **provisional
editorial source** is approved for the research corpus. Existing verification
tools can then bind a reviewed source and complete verse; they explicitly do
not authorize canonical application or publication. The legacy canonical
schema does not accept a critical-source label. That boundary must be addressed
deliberately with reader/export checks, not evaded by calling the source WLC
or merely adding a permissive enum.
