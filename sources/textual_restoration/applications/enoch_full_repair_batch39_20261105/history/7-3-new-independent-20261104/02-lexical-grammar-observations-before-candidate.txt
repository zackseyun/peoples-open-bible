Dillmann pp393-394 nutrition entry: I,1 alere, nutrire, cibum vel victum praebere, sustentare; III,1 reﬂexive/passive nutriri, vesci cibum and victum requirere, with Hen7:3 visibly cited in I,1 and III,1 vicinity. Noun on p394 alimentum/nutrimentum/cibus. Does NOT permit replacing nutrition with inherent inability without further morphology.
Dillmann pp489-490 consuming root supports eating/devouring/consuming; p1272 labour/toil root includes labores/gravis/molestas operas and work-derived noun. Supports first clause consume humans' labour (produce as contextual inference only).
Dillmann pp377-378 separately attests not-being-able root and III,1 language about inopia and exhausting resources; this is a genuine contrasting lexical family, not a license to read its first glyph into a different printed Sat form.
Source verb crop visible first sign Sat III (si), followed by alef-medial form, taw, plural -omu-like suffix. I provisionally read a nutrition verbal noun with suffix, NOT clear finite inability verb. I cannot claim the supplied grammar pages alone demonstrate precisely whose role the suffix expresses in this syntactic environment.
Grammar269-270: nominal infinitives with suffixes distinguished from gerund sense, formation in -ot/-o; grammar359: -o infinitive reverts to -ot before suffix, substantive infinitives take suffixes like nouns; suffix as subjective or objective genitive recognized. These support possibility of a suffixed nominal nutrition form; they do not themselves settle the full clause's English polarity.
Whole unit meaning secure only at coarse level: giants consume people's labour; second clause concerns humans and sustenance/nourishing them and contains an explicitly corrupt conjunction. Source's explicit apparatus and uncertainty must remain visible in any English. A smooth negative inability clause is not yet earned by my source observations.
