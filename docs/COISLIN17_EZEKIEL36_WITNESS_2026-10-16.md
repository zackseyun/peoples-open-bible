# Coislin 17 and the Ezekiel renewal passage

## Result

Coislin 17 now has verified image locations for selected Ezekiel 36:25–38
lemmas and the following 37:1 opening. This closes a passage-location gap in
our Greek witness investigation. It does not establish a new reading or
decide whether Papyrus 967's shorter text and different order originated in
Hebrew composition, Greek revision or copying. Retain POB's Hebrew and English.

The [Göttingen catalogue entry for Ra 533](https://septuaginta.uni-goettingen.de/catalogue/Ra_533/)
identifies Paris, BnF Coislin 17, Diktyon 49159, as a thirteenth-century Greek
Ezekiel catena with Hexaplaric notes, reportedly used by Montfaucon. This is
catalogue testimony, not a verified identification of a versional note at our
target. The [custodian's viewer](https://gallica.bnf.fr/ark:/12148/btv1b10037903r)
dates the object to 1201–1300 and describes the Ezekiel catena at ff. 6–302v,
preceded by introductory material and followed by a life of Ezekiel. Its
313 numbered digital views are not 313 biblical pages or verse records.
BnF explicitly says the digitization was made from a substitute document.

## What this changes in the comparison

The new-heart/new-spirit passage is now located in another known Greek
manuscript, rather than inferred from its book-level catalogue description.
However, its biblical lemmas alternate with author-labelled explanations.
Labels such as ἄλλως occur within that commentary sequence. They do not by
themselves identify Aquila, Symmachus, Theodotion or a competing biblical
reading. No securely attributed Hexaplaric variant is accepted from this pass.

The distinction matters to the all-source method: a manuscript described as
containing Hexaplaric material cannot supply a versional vote at every verse.
Nor can a medieval copy acquire an ancient author's date merely because a
commentary block bears that author's name. A demonstrated textual relationship
would be needed to use those blocks as earlier quotation evidence.

Against the [P967 transition evidence](EZEKIEL_36_LONGER_FORM_EVIDENCE_2026-10-12.md),
this catena supplies the longer passage's selected lemmas and local 36→37
sequence. It does not discriminate among the proposed origins of P967's minus
or 36→38→39→37 order. It cannot be counted independently of the printed
apparatus until that apparatus's use of this manuscript is checked.

The apparatus follow-up below completes the Montfaucon check and traces
Field's target entries to their stated sources. Do not repeat these images as another vote,
reconstruct an absent Hebrew text by back-translation, or treat an unobserved
variant as absent throughout the codex. No ImageGen output was used, and no
damaged letter has been restored here.

## Printed apparatus and Theodoret checked on October 16

### Montfaucon and Field

Montfaucon's 1713 volume II, [printed p.269](https://archive.org/details/bub_gb_cGDnzs0uq2YC/page/n275/mode/1up),
names a Coislin Ezekiel codex among the sources used. It does not give shelfmark
17; that identification still comes from the catalogue. The complete chapter-36
entries and notes on [pp.310–311](https://archive.org/details/bub_gb_cGDnzs0uq2YC/page/n325/mode/1up)
contain no explicit Coislin attribution. Entries at 11 and 18 cite *Ms. Jes.*;
the verse-38 observation belongs to the Drusius/Roman-edition-note chain.
There is no variant entry at verses 23–37. This sparse apparatus's silence
does not establish absence of readings in the manuscript.

Field's 1875 volume II, [printed p.868](https://archive.org/details/origenhexapla02unknuoft/page/868/mode/1up)
(one-based PDF page 884), does report readings inside the longer passage:

| Locus | Printed reading or mark | Actual source and scope |
|---|---|---|
| 36:23 | Asterisked λέγει ἀδωναὶ κύριος | Note 17 lists witnesses with and without the asterisk. The marked unit is the divine-speech formula, not 23c–38. |
| 36:27 and 30 | Asterisked αὐτά and ἔτι respectively | Notes 18 and 21 distinguish marked and unmarked witnesses. Each is a local addition with its own closing sign. |
| 36:30 | Σ. καὶ τὰς ὀπώρας | Note 20 cites *Syro-hex.*: a Syriac-mediated attribution, not newly inspected Greek manuscript ink. |
| 36:31 | Σ. καὶ σμικρυνθήσεσθε ἐνώπιον αὐτῶν | Note 22 likewise cites *Syro-hex.* and cross-references 20:43. Its following pronoun observation is separate. |
| 36:33,37 | ἀδωναὶ (alia exempl. κύριος) κύριος | The parenthetic alternative replaces the first term, producing κύριος κύριος, not single κύριος. Note 23 distinguishes the Syrohex margin at 33 from its text at 37. |
| 36:37 | Α. Σ. Θ. ζήτημα θήσομαι | Note 24 cites *Syro-hex.* and separately says this Greek wording occurs in Ald., Codices III,42,49 and others. The presence of the wording does not independently verify all three version labels in those copies. |
| 36:37 | Aliter: Σ. εὑρεθήσομαι | Note 25 explicitly cites Theodoret's contrast with ζητηθήσομαι. This source was then checked directly below. |

No Coislin name appears on this page. A full concordance of its numbered codices
has not been established, so this is not a claim that every possible indirect
Coislin connection has been excluded. Nor do Field's local critical signs
prove the historical origin of the entire longer passage. The
[previous Swete/digital title comparison](EZEKIEL_36_OMISSION_MECHANISMS_2026-10-12.md#actual-edition-and-hebrew-controls)
remains distinct: its single-κύριος reading must not be conflated with Field's
double-title alternative.

### Theodoret at the cited passage

Root and the source reader inspected four complete printed pages of
[PG 81, columns 1181–1188](https://archive.org/details/bim_early-english-books-1641-1700_patrologiae-cursus-completus-_1859_81/page/n590/mode/1up).
The Greek occupies columns 1181,1184,1185,1188, with alternating Latin columns.
Successive verse lemmas covering 36:23–38 accompany the explanations. This
verifies the longer passage in the transmitted commentary, not a new early
manuscript or an autograph of Theodoret.

At [1188B](https://archive.org/details/bim_early-english-books-1641-1700_patrologiae-cursus-completus-_1859_81/page/n593/mode/1up),
the Greek explicitly contrasts ζητηθήσομαι ("I shall be sought") with
Symmachus's εὑρεθήσομαι ("I shall be found"). The version attribution is
lexical, not a label over the entire passage. Footnote 96 separately reports
ζήτημα θήσομαι under siglum A in the quoted lemma. That siglum has not been
concordanced here; it must not become an additional named-version witness.

The commentary applies 36:24 to returnees, interprets 25–27 through Gospel
faith, baptism and the Spirit, then at 28 returns to material promises to Jews.
This is direct evidence of Christian reception of the longer passage. Our
inference is limited: Christian interpretation does not demonstrate Christian
composition, a Christian replacement translation, or the direction of Hebrew
literary development. Those historical alternatives still need discriminating
evidence. Theodoret's commentary, its printed edition, Field's excerpt and a
modern electronic transcription cannot be counted as four independent votes.

POB 36:37 currently reads "I will let the house of Israel ask me to act for
them" and notes the passive inquiry alternative. The reported Greek
"I shall be found" does not uniquely imply another Hebrew verb; this evidence
does not justify replacing אִדָּרֵשׁ or changing that English. This is a bounded
source-impact judgment, not certification of all wording or note placement.
