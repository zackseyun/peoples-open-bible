## Attested differences and preservation

The [Qumran-Digital transcription](https://lexicon.qumran-digital.org/transcriptions/4Q111/2026-10-06/index.html),
unit 3 lines 6–10, publishes the sequence 15→17→16→18. Line 8 includes
unbracketed מכול אוהביה צדיק אתה יהוה and צפה אדוני. Line 9 has unpointed
עיני/דמעתי wording and doubtful בכו֯, without the Masoretic explicit feminine
“I am weeping” construction; eye number and syntax require interpretation.
Line 10 nevertheless preserves צדיק הוא א[דוני כיא]: the title after its
initial aleph and the causal word are supplied. The rebellion clause does not
survive here; its retention or omission is unestablished. The extra justice
address is therefore not simply a relocated replacement for verse 18.

Adjacent masculine שומם at 1:13 supports investigating a narrator's voice,
not certifying one male speaker throughout. Deleted unidentified signs,
uncertain letters and supplies remain part of the edition. This is published
transcription, not a fresh reading of manuscript pixels. The October and May
versions have identical own-row text and notation events throughout unit 3;
embedded comparison texts are excluded.

## Greek and Hebrew controls

Swete III, printed 363–364 / PDF 387–388, puts weeping before Zion's extended
hands, with the justice statement and rebellion at 18. The weeping introduction
is printed at the end of his 15: continuous content, not row labels, establishes
the sequence. His selected Greek has one eye and water; it is neither the
complete repeated-eye Masoretic wording nor the scroll's distinct unpointed construction.
His command verb at 17 is ἐνετείλατο, not a direct attestation of the scroll's
צפה. The additional address has no counterpart in this selected Greek text.
([Public scan](https://archive.org/details/theoldtestamenti03swetuoft))

Root visually read both complete target pages, their apparatus, the same
volume's key and preface pages v–vi. The selection is Vaticanus-based; the
apparatus distinguishes other witnesses and margins. This manual is not a
complete modern Greek collation or proof of unanimous Greek absence.

Current Hebrew controls in 2:16–17, 3:46–51 and 4:16–17 already place the
pe group before ayin. Thus that sequence is not intrinsically a broken
acrostic. Chapter 1's opposing orders could reflect inherited arrangements,
regularization or harmonization; the alphabet alone cannot choose the direction.
These are ten checked controls, not a census of ancient alphabets or copies.

Jeremiah 12:1 and Psalm 119:137 contain the same three-word righteousness
formula. Ezra 9:15 has a related address with a different name sequence.
They establish available formulaic language, not a uniquely identified donor
or independent attestation of its placement in Lamentations. Jeremiah's address
introduces a dispute, so the formula alone cannot certify a confession of guilt.

## Competing transmission explanations

Kotzé's actual appendix, printed 201–202 / PDF 12–13, distinguishes an
interpretative insertion from a scribal change or error. It classifies the
added address as interpretative, leaves both orders possible, and favors צפה.
These are his historical evaluations, not additional witnesses. Its versional
sigla do not replace independent inspection of those editions.
([Article](https://scriptura.journals.ac.za/pub/article/download/110/118))

Van Rooy's target discussion instead emphasizes connected syntactic differences
and possible separate traditions. He cautions against repairing one form from
isolated pieces of the other. His reports of Cross, BHQ, Berlin and Salters
remain attributed reports, not separately consulted arguments.
([2012 study](https://hts.org.za/index.php/hts/rt/printerFriendly/1265/2541),
sections on 1:16–17 and conclusions)

A possible development toward the scroll form would combine reordering,
formulaic expansion and altered speaker grammar. The reverse could involve
regularizing the alphabet, reducing a repeated justice statement and restoring
a more consistently personified city. These are competing explanations, not
observed stages. A local copying/transposition event also remains possible;
it cannot by itself explain all the associated wording. Apparent coherence
supports testing a history, not certifying its direction.

The early Hebrew alternative deserves weight, but antiquity cannot identify
its exemplar or make every local feature earlier. Removing that modest
chronological preference leaves the same priority hold. Nor should the
commanding/watching contrast be selected solely for a preferred portrayal
of divine agency. The connected passage still needs its own transmission
and lexical arguments.
