## The newly consulted counterargument

CTAT retains kaf, relates bet to an interpretive al-tiqre tradition, and proposes contrasting returning wicked people with rightly acting people who are forgotten. The committee's six judgments favor kaf: five grade B and one grade C. These grades express qualified confidence, not percentages or manuscript votes. Its quoted Gittin reference prints 26b; our independently consulted target is 56b, so the locator is not silently adopted. CTAT also separates reconstructed reviser Greek from Syriac reports.

This is a real competing transmission and syntax argument, not proof that every bet copy arose by that route. Its position must be compared with Gentry's inferred Hebrew parent of Old Greek, rather than treating either authority as another ancient witness. The CTAT report of five Kennicott and thirteen De Rossi manuscripts is not eighteen verified independent copies: the earlier collations' overlap, correction states and identities remain unresolved.
