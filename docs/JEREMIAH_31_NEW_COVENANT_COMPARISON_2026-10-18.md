# Jeremiah 31 new covenant source comparison

## Judgment and translation consequence

Provisionally retain Jeremiah 31:31–34's Hebrew and current main English,
and retain Hebrews 8:8–12's distinct Greek wording. The divine response in
Jeremiah 31:32 is a consequential unresolved interpretation/source question:
Hebrew **בעלתי בם** supports husband or mastery language, while the consulted
Greek Jeremiah 38:32 and Hebrews 8:9 have **ἠμέλησα αὐτῶν**, disregard or
neglect. A proposed negative sense of the same Hebrew spelling remains open;
neither different Hebrew letters nor earliest-form priority is established.

POB's current Jeremiah note incorrectly says the Greek quoted in Hebrews
reflects the Hebrew dominion alternative. It does not. The Hebrews note already
distinguishes these senses. Correcting the Jeremiah disclosure is a justified
next application task, separate from changing its main translation. Its marker
also currently follows Egypt rather than the disputed divine response. This
comparison applies no canonical, reader, metadata or implementation change.
The [evidence packet](../sources/textual_restoration/comparisons/jeremiah31_new_covenant.2026-10-18.v1.json)
pins the inputs and records the precise consultation limits.

## The connected source forms

The actual WLC and current POB units were read with Jeremiah 3:12–18 and
31:35–37, plus Hebrews 8:6–13 and 10:15–18. The publisher's
[Rahlfs–Hanhart chapter 38](https://www.die-bibel.de/bibel/LXX/JER.38)
and Swete III (1905), printed 313–314 / PDF 337–338, provide independent
edition controls, not extra manuscripts. Complete Swete pages and its sigla
page PDF 24 were inspected visually.

| Feature | Hebrew Jeremiah | Selected Greek Jeremiah and Hebrews | Consequence |
|---|---|---|---|
| New covenant | 31:31 names Israel, Judah and a new covenant | Greek 38:31 and Hebrews 8:8 retain those identities and new-covenant language | Disregard wording does not itself remove Israel or Judah from the promise |
| Human breach | 31:32 says they broke the covenant | 38:32 and Hebrews 8:9 say they did not remain in it | Different expression, not automatically a recoverable different Hebrew verb |
| Divine response | בעלתי בם | ἠμέλησα αὐτῶν | Husband/master and disregard are not interchangeable glosses |
| Internal instruction | 31:33 has singular instruction, within them, written on their heart | 38:33 has plural laws, mind and hearts; Hebrews 8:10 has its own wording | Number and translation choices must not be collapsed into an identical quotation |
| Restored relationship | 31:33–34 promises God/people relationship, knowledge and forgiveness | Both Greek passages retain corresponding promises | A blanket claim of permanent rejection exceeds the inspected wording |

Swete's 38:32 selects διεθέμην where Hebrews has ἐποίησα. Its apparatus
reports ἐποίησα at Q* and the contracted κἀγώ at A; the key identifies Q as
Marchalianus and A as Alexandrinus. These are published edition reports, not
newly read manuscript hands or a proof of Christian alteration. The central
disregard verb is in both inspected selected Greek texts; no complete modern
apparatus or manuscript census was consulted.

Hebrews 8:13 explicitly draws its own old/new conclusion. Hebrews 10:16–17
selects and rearranges parts of the promise without repeating the disputed
divine-response clause. Those uses must be interpreted in their own contexts,
not used to rewrite Jeremiah's Hebrew. Neither the adjective new nor this
one verb decides every later theory of covenant continuity or replacement.

## Competing primary arguments

Connor Boyd, *A New Take on the New Covenant: A Reconsideration of the New
Covenant and Jeremiah 31:31–34 in the Masoretic Text, Septuagint, and Dead Sea
Scrolls* (PhD thesis, University of Edinburgh, December 2023),
[institutional text](https://era.ed.ac.uk/bitstreams/1f6c9583-dae8-47e2-9fb9-378b55eb68f5/download),
printed 37–43 / PDF 47–53, favors a negative lexical interpretation over a
different Hebrew source. His supports include older lexicography, Syriac and
context; his note 115 acknowledges the scarcity of Hebrew examples. These
seven complete pages and notes were read; PDF 48–53 were visually checked.
His separate *Biblical Research* 68 (2023): 6–27 article remains unconsulted,
as do the original lexicographic and Syriac sources behind these particular
claims. A citation to them is not fresh direct collation.

Adrian Schenker, *Das Neue am neuen Bund und das Alte am alten* (FRLANT 212;
Göttingen: Vandenhoeck & Ruprecht, 2006),
[BSB digital copy](https://www.digitale-sammlungen.de/de/view/bsb00044386),
instead favors a different Hebrew source and literary revision, with the
Greek form earlier. His section 2.3, printed 20–25 / scans 19–24, leaves the
precise lost Hebrew rejection verb unidentified. That complete section,
introduction 11–13 and title/imprint were inspected directly, not merely through
Boyd's account. Boyd's note 106 locates a quotation at Schenker 12; it is
actually on printed 22 / scan 21. The correction changes the locator, not the
existence of Schenker's argument. Neither full book nor full thesis was read.

## Independent controls and limits of the arguments

WLC Jeremiah 3:14 and 31:32 share the same qal perfect first-person form of
בעל and a following ב recipient, with second- versus third-person plural.
The [Greek 3:14](https://www.die-bibel.de/bibel/LXX/JER.3) instead has
κατακυριεύσω ὑμῶν, future mastery; Swete printed 229 / PDF 253 corroborates
this selected reading. A following ב alone therefore cannot demonstrate that
the negative sense is compulsory. This book-level comparison does not assume
one translator or require identical meaning in differing contexts.

The exact WLC lemma-1166 screen, including conjunction-prefixed lemma forms
but excluding noun Baal, finds 16 tokens in 14 verses. Their contexts provide
marriage/mastery controls, not a statistical proof against a rare sense.
The difficult 1 Chronicles 4:22 construction remains unresolved; Isaiah 62:5
is not silently changed from sons to builders. These are lexical controls,
not fourteen independent witnesses to Jeremiah's target clause.

At [Greek 4:17](https://www.die-bibel.de/bibel/LXX/JER.4), ἠμέλησας occurs
where WLC has מרתה, rebellion. The actor is Jerusalem, not God. This shows
why a Greek verb does not uniquely identify its Hebrew ancestor; it does not
attest מרה or any other proposed replacement at 31:32. Likewise Deuteronomy
32:20–21 provides a consequence construction after human provocation, so the
pronoun/conjunction pattern alone does not force an adversative though.

Greek disregard can suggest divine withdrawal, but total absence of a covenant
throughout the intervening centuries is a further historical/theological
inference. A later future promise of mastery at 3:14 need not logically deny
all prior relationship. Conversely, literary coherence with a continuing
relationship does not prove Hebrew priority. Test semantic possibilities and
transmission histories separately rather than choosing a theology first.

The pinned QDR biblical snapshot has partial passage indexing. Exact searches
for Jer 31:31–34 and Jer 3:14 return no indexed matches; there is consequently
no target hit whose supplied letters could decide this verb. This is not a
claim that every manuscript or quotation lacks the passage. The earlier
[Jeremiah 33 dossier](JEREMIAH_33_COVENANT_COMPARISON_2026-10-16.md)
is a contextual comparison, not a prior collation of the disputed verb here.
