# Joshua 10 camp formulas and the older Hebrew hypothesis

## Judgment for POB

Joshua 10:15 and 10:43, and the reference to a camp in 10:21, differ materially
between the current Masoretic Hebrew source and the selected Greek text.
Direct consultation of Kristin De Troyer's original argument supplies a new
test of the unresolved [Joshua translation-history question](JOSHUA_COUNTERMODEL_FOLLOWUP_2026-10-17.md).
The Greek contrast is confirmed; an earlier Hebrew form without these words
remains a hypothesis competing with selective Greek editing. Retain POB's
Hebrew and English pending the discriminating evidence below.

## The newly consulted argument

The Hebrew Bible Project publicly hosts De Troyer's
[study of Joshua 10](https://openscholar.huji.ac.il/sites/default/files/he_bible_project/files/k.d._troyer.pdf).
Its author-formatted PDF is headed *Textus* 26 (2016) and has 28 numbered
pages. Root reads the complete extracted argument, appendix and notes, then
visually checks the title, pages 15–18 and 23. This is not a new manuscript
edition or a claim to have checked every Hebrew/Greek table cell visually.
The [evidence record](../sources/textual_restoration/comparisons/joshua10_camp.2026-10-19.v1.json)
pins the unchanged original, inspected renders and current comparison inputs.

De Troyer argues that the shorter Hebrew source behind the earliest Greek was
expanded into the Masoretic form. She connects the return formulas with a
stronger emphasis on Joshua's companions, the camp and Gilgal. This is a
specific alternative to a Greek translator streamlining a layered Hebrew
narrative, not simply an appeal to shorter readings. Her page 23 nevertheless
qualifies the conclusions as tentative and distinguishes her linear-growth
model from Mazor's two recensions descending from a common source.

## Direct passage controls

Root inspects complete Swete I pages 440–443, including apparatus, and the
edition's witness key and hand conventions. These are PDF pages 472–475,
32 and 20–21. Swete's preface at xii and xv is also read in extraction:
its selected uncial witnesses are not a census or a complete reconstruction
of the earliest Greek. A denotes Alexandrinus, B Vaticanus; the main-text
and apparatus columns must not be counted as separate manuscripts.

| 10:15 | Joshua and Israel return to the camp at Gilgal | Swete's main text proceeds from 14 to 16. Its apparatus reports the verse in a marginal correction of B and explicitly records absence in B* and A. |
| 10:21 | The people return to the camp, to Joshua at Makkedah | Swete's text returns the people to Joshua at Makkedah without the camp phrase. |

De Troyer's page 17, notes 43–44, publishes preliminary apparatus material
communicated by Udo Quast, including marked additions of the return formulas.
This is substantive published evidence for her Greek reconstruction, but our
consultation is mediated through her article, not access to Quast's papers or
the complete modern edition. Her statement that the Schøyen Joshua codex
also lacks these readings is an attributed report: its leaves, plates and
2005 edition have not been examined in this pass. A later-hand addition in
Vaticanus must not be presented as its original-hand reading.

The accompanying formula is not simply absent throughout Greek Joshua 10.
Swete retains Israel accompanying Joshua at 29, 31, 34 and 36, including the
with-him expression. At 38 it retains Joshua and all Israel but lacks that
expression. These selected controls illustrate variation within the pattern;
they do not constitute a new all-occurrences collation. The two identical
return formulas are also correlated evidence, not two independent historical
proofs of the same editorial event.

## What changes and what remains undecided

The new argument makes a narrower test possible: distinguish the earliest
recoverable Greek at these units from the language and direction of the change.
Later Greek additions agreeing with Hebrew support a particular stage of
Greek transmission; they do not by themselves establish that the Hebrew
words were absent before the initial translation.

Our counterargument is that normal rendering of the camp noun elsewhere
does not exclude selective omission of a redundant or awkward clause here.
De Troyer herself acknowledges Greek levelling, added camp language and
clarification on pages 12–16. Conversely, those examples do not prove that
the translator deleted these particular verses. The explanations must meet
the same local evidence. Narrative coherence alone cannot identify the
language or date of a revision, and Gilgal's presence elsewhere in Greek is
not a refutation of her narrower claim about its increased prominence.

One descriptive error is kept out of our linguistic evidence: page 18 lists
10:7 and 10:9 as directional-he forms, but the checked WLC words end in
lamed, unlike the final-he forms in 10:6, 15 and 43. Also, 10:6 concerns the
delegation travelling to Joshua, not Israelites leaving Gilgal. These local
slips do not refute the proposed editorial history.

The actual contrary discussion in CTAT I, page 17, is a discriminating next
input because De Troyer expressly disputes it in note 46. A new bounded
check reaches its institutional DOI record's security challenge, not the
book; it is not read or bypassed. Next obtain that argument and readable
target apparatus/witness controls, then test selective Greek omission,
Hebrew expansion and branching Hebrew forms against the same units. Do not
repeat the closed access route or treat a second reading of this article as
new evidence.
