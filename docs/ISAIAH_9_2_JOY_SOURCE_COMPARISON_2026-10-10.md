# Isaiah 9 2 negative and increased joy comparison

## Outcome

Propose the recorded Masoretic reading לו, “to it,” as the provisional working
source for “you have increased its joy.” This would reverse POB's current
negative clause, not merely polish its English. The written לא, “not,” remains
a substantial alternative. Earliest wording is unresolved; no canonical record
is changed by this comparison.

The [contract](../sources/textual_restoration/comparisons/isaiah9_2_joy_contract.2026-10-10.v1.json)
pins source, context and candidates before their recorded assessment. Acquisition
is exploratory, not a held-out experiment. The
[assessment](../sources/textual_restoration/comparisons/isaiah9_2_joy_assessment.2026-10-10.v1.json)
stores the actual evidence, counterarguments, independent reviews and reopening
conditions. POB/WLC and the selected Greek label this verse 9:2; the selected
Latin and many English Bibles label the same clause 9:3.

## Written and read Hebrew

Pinned OSHB/WLC has written לא, tagged as a negative particle, and explicitly
records qere ל֖/וֹ, a preposition with third-person masculine singular suffix.
The immediately preceding singular nation supplies a coherent beneficiary:
“for it you increased joy,” naturally “you increased its joy.” Both are Hebrew
tradition, not Hebrew versus a modern English invention. Original-language
priority does not make written form automatically superior to reading tradition.

The paragraph moves from hunger/darkness at 8:19–22 toward relief and light at
8:23–9:1. Positive growth and joy then fit the harvest/spoil celebration and
the following explanations about broken oppression, destroyed war gear and
the given child. That contextual coherence is the affirmative reading's
strongest case. Its strongest objection is that the qere could repair an older,
difficult negative. Difficulty alone cannot establish either corruption or priority.

The negative has real written support, with versional support below. A contrast
between earlier growth without joy and later celebration is possible. But the
current unqualified clause supplies no “formerly,” “not yet,” relative connector
or referent change. Those interpretations require additional argument; the
negative is grammatically possible, not self-evidently nonsense. Root does not
adopt the [NET editors' assertion](https://classic.net.bible.org/passage.php?passage=Isa+9%3A3)
that it is obviously incorrect. Their separate first-colon conjecture replacing
nation/to-it with rejoicing is not the recorded qere and is not proposed here.

## Ancient Hebrew spelling and versional controls

The actual [1QIsaᵃ published transcription](https://lexicon.qumran-digital.org/transcriptions/1QIsa%5Ea%5E/2022-10-14/index.html)
spans VIII19–21. At VIII20 it has הגוי לוא הגדלתה השמחה, with the disputed
word outside supply brackets and without uncertainty or correction marks.
This is published attestation, not a new reading of pixels. The same scroll
uses לוא negatively at VIII3 and as a beneficiary at V15 and XXXIII10. Its
spelling therefore does not settle the target meaning. An early but
nondiscriminating form cannot decide the choice through chronological preference.

The actual [4Q57 transcription](https://lexicon.qumran-digital.org/transcriptions/4Q57/2025-08-25/index.html?v=2025-08-25)
begins fragment 1–2+49:1 within the following verse, WLC9:3. Neither its partial
letters nor its supplied context reaches the disputed clause. This is no target
coverage, not an attested omission. Qumran-Digital's published texts are
attributed to DFG465277421, based on Göttingen's Qumran-Wörterbuch and a Martin
Abegg predecessor, under [CC BY-SA4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-sa/4.0/).

The selected [Rahlfs/Hanhart2006 Greek](https://www.die-bibel.de/bibel/LXX/ISA.9)
portrays joy positively, but its opening diverges and has ἐν εὐφροσύνῃ σου,
“in your joy.” It is not the same beneficiary or complete syntax as the qere;
it cannot recover exact Hebrew לו. The selected
[Weber/Gryson2007 Latin](https://www.die-bibel.de/bibel/VUL/ISA.9) explicitly
has non magnificasti laetitiam, the negative interpretation. This is genuine
contrary evidence, not uniform versional support for the affirmative. Neither
selected edition is a single dated physical copy or full manuscript apparatus.

The Hebrew evidence assessor also read
[Parry2020](https://cdn.interpreterfoundation.org/jnlpdf/parry-v41-2020-pp55-74-PDF.pdf),
reporting that printed page65 treats this as pronoun orthography while page61
groups the scroll's spelling with the written negative and reports a conjecture.
Both observations are preserved; root did not independently consult that PDF.
The article supplies no unambiguous different target spelling in this check.

## Independent assessment and next application

Root compares negative then affirmative. One separate reviewer compares
affirmative then negative, with POB identities and canonical metadata withheld,
and supports a provisional affirmative source preference while retaining the
negative countercase. Familiar-verse knowledge is not fully blinded. A separate
Hebrew assessor verifies preservation, spelling controls and nearby coverage.
Their agreement does not constitute another manuscript or publication approval.

The next concrete action is a separate full-record candidate selecting the qere,
with an honest source description, written/read apparatus, negative alternative
at the clause's own note anchor, archived stale approvals and actual reader-export
verification. Source and English must move together; silently deleting “not”
against the current source would conceal the change. No whole-corpus superiority,
new decipherment or canon recommendation follows. Reopen priority for
discriminating direct Hebrew, locus-specific apparatus/versional arguments or
a stronger specific syntax/context explanation, not more preference votes.
