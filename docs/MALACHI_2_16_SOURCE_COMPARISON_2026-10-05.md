provisionally and prefer the human-subject conditional: **If he hates and

## Source evidence and competing arguments

The [previous preservation check](OT_SOURCE_COMPARISON_CONTINUATION_2026-10-04.md#malachi-2-16-gives-the-next-discriminating-question)
records published 4Q76 column 2 lines 4–7, Swete III PDF 121 / printed 97,
and NETS PDF 45 / printed 821. The explicit conditional opening survives in
the published Hebrew transcription, but the whole garment word does not.
Its changed person and covering wording belong to a distinct connected form.
Neither its age nor Greek conditional meaning licenses an if inserted into
MT and presented as a verbatim ancient text. Mur88/4Q78 do not cover this
verse in the inspected published controls; no omission or complete census follows.
Swete's B-based subjunctive and its reported Q imperative differ. The exact
Q hand remains held; NETS garments and Swete thoughts must not be conflated.

[Hugenberger's 1991 thesis](https://eprints.glos.ac.uk/3339/1/292355.pdf),
printed 69–73, argues for conditional `כי`, a human subject and the covering
clause as consequence. `שָׂנֵא` is construed as Qal perfect 3ms; `שַׁלַּח`
as an alternative-form Piel infinitive absolute replacing a finite verb;
`וְכִסָּה` as finite Piel perfect 3ms with the same subject. No repointing is
required by that argument. Its strongest objection remains recognition of
this function in this particular token. The ordinary infinitive-construct
analysis is not eliminated. English coordinates predicates without an explicit
Hebrew and and supplies her from the wife in 2:14–15. Present English expresses
a generic condition, not a newly attested tense. The injunctive guard yourselves
is a Niphal perfect in Hebrew, not morphologically imperative.

Root read the direct contrary arguments in [MJT 10.1 (2011)](https://www.biblicalstudies.org.uk/pdf/midwestern-journal-theology/vols/10-1.pdf).
Branch, printed 83–84, appeals to divine third-person speech at 1:9 and the
warning enclosing 2:15–16. His preferred first-person solution adopts Verhoef's
changed pointing and implicit pronoun. Watson, printed 89–92, offers an
unchanged God-hates construction: divorce is the object of hatred, then the
implicit subject of covering, with the divorcer as the referent of his.
That is a real alternative, not impossible because hate is third person.
It requires different implicit subjects/referents; POB's old the one who covers
does not solve that difficulty without nominalizing a finite predicate.

Watson's provisional opening analysis is not his final verdict. Printed 99–100
prefers human-subject options, inclines toward a Qumran-based reading, and
acknowledges the missing transmission explanation and possible clarification
of difficult MT. His Qumran English also normalizes the covering phrase:
it is not a diplomatic rendering of every printed word. Our decision adopts
neither that normalization nor his source-class priority ranking. Root read
printed 83–84, 87, 89–92 and 99–101 and visually inspected 83, 92 and 99;
the source assessor read Branch 75–84 and Watson's relevant argument separately.
The journal PDF has SHA256
`e7a89d65063eb4f322ed8795fb354f84edeebc83b3254be3896ffb7a69baac4b`.

The conditional command is also possible, with a third-to-second-person shift
and a further explanation of covering. Conditional human predicates give a
connected accusation without those shifts or repointing, which is the root's
provisional preference. The causal-warning objection is substantial but does
not exclude a conditional explanation of condemned conduct. Clarification in
4Q76/Greek versus deterioration in MT remains unresolved; older, smoother or
more difficult wording does not automatically decide priority. Full DJD XV,
BHQ and Göttingen apparatus, direct other-version collation and Fuller/Jones
originals remain unconsulted. The later Lumanze article was read for its local
discussion, but its inaccurate Greek glosses and morphological claims were
not imported as controls. Collins is known here through reports, not direct reading.
