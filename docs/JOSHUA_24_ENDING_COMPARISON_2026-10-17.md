# Joshua 24 ending and its relationship to Judges

## Result for POB

The consulted Greek and Hebrew forms have meaningfully different endings, not
just different verse numbers. The Greek places Israel's fidelity before Joshua's
death, adds a burial account for the flint knives, and continues beyond Eleazar's
death into Phinehas's succession, apostasy and Eglon's eighteen-year rule. Current
POB follows the shorter Hebrew ending. These alternatives warrant comparison
as connected literary forms; their attestation does not yet decide their priority.

No source, English, order, reader note or canon change is applied. In particular,
Greek narrative must not be inserted as supposedly recovered Hebrew. The
[evidence packet](../sources/textual_restoration/comparisons/joshua24_ending.2026-10-17.v1.json)
records the consulted sources, exact controls and review scope. This is
published-source comparison, not fresh decipherment or a discovery new to
scholarship.

## Compare content before verse labels

Root read the actual publisher's selected
[Greek chapter 24](https://www.die-bibel.de/bibel/LXX/JOS.24), while a source
reader checked Swete's actual printed text and apparatus. Root also read the
current WLC/POB target and connected passages. Edition selections and apparatus
reports are not additional physical manuscripts.

The table uses the publisher's RH 2006 labels. Swete I, fourth edition 1909
reprinted 1925, printed 473–474 / PDF 505–506, instead prints fidelity as **31**
before death **29**, burial **30** and knives **30a**. The same content must be
aligned across these labels, not counted as absent at another edition's number.

| Unit | Current WLC and POB Joshua | Selected RH Greek Joshua |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Dismissal | 24:28, each to his inheritance | 24:28, going each to his place |
| Fidelity during Joshua and the elders | 24:31, after death and burial; elders knew the deeds | 24:29, before death; elders saw the deeds |
| Joshua's death and burial | 24:29–30; age 110 | 24:30–31; age 110 |
| Flint knives | No corresponding burial unit | 24:31a, knives placed in Joshua's tomb, with circumcision and bringing-from-Egypt language |
| Joseph and Eleazar | 24:32–33; book ends with Eleazar's burial | 24:32–33, followed by additional narrative |
| Continuation | No corresponding final units | 24:33a–b: ark, Phinehas's succession/death/burial, departure, foreign worship and Eglon of Moab, eighteen years |

Hebrew Judges 2:6–9 has dismissal → fidelity → death → burial, agreeing in broad
sequence with Greek Joshua rather than Hebrew Joshua. Greek Judges must also
be distinguished by its A/B forms, not treated as one universally identical
text. These related accounts are not independent votes for one reconstructed
ancestor. “Known” and “seen” are real local differences, but neither verb alone
dates a literary layer or proves a deliberately altered eyewitness claim.

Swete's own key identifies A as Alexandrinus and B as Vaticanus. Its target
apparatus reports A's internal variants within the long units; this is not a
complete modern collation of all Greek families. Even the burial place differs:
Swete's main line has Galaad, its A report has Gaas, and RH selects Gaas. A shared
sequence does not imply identical wording throughout.

## Connected controls constrain the interpretation

The following controls test what the ending can support. They do not establish
that every difference arose in Hebrew or in one editorial operation. RH is the
selected Greek control unless Swete is specified.

| Claim to test | Actual connected control | Limit on the inference |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The knives addition is isolated | Selected Greek 21:42d also deposits the knives; Swete 5:2–9 supplies the circumcision context | Linked passages may reflect preservation or coordinated expansion; two references are not two independent manuscripts |
| The Greek removes Moses altogether | Greek 24:5 lacks the current Hebrew Moses/Aaron clause, but Moses remains in the book's wider narrative | A local difference is not wholesale replacement of the Mosaic story |
| Joshua displaces divine deliverance | Greek 24:5–7 and 24:17 still identify the Lord as deliverer | The striking 31a wording concerns Joshua's role; it does not remove divine agency |
| Shiloh replaces every Shechem reference | Selected Greek 24:1,25 has Shiloh; 24:32 still buries Joseph at Shechem | Distinct event locations, not a uniform substitution of all place names |
| The ending is a direct copy of Judges' first oppression | Hebrew Judges 3:8 has Cushan, Aram-naharaim and eight years; 3:12–14 has Eglon, Moab and eighteen years | The Greek ending anticipates a later Judges cycle; summary, relocation and a different seam remain alternatives |

The Greek continuation changes the conclusion's horizon: the reader reaches
apostasy and subjection within Joshua itself. It does not require that every
intermediate event in Judges was unknown or absent. Likewise the Hebrew fidelity
notice can function retrospectively after Joshua's burial; its position is not
grammatically impossible merely because another order is smoother.

Root and the Greek source reader also checked Swete printed 427–428 / PDF
459–460 and 465 / PDF 497. Its 5:4 allows uncircumcised people among the
Egypt-exiters, yet 5:6 retains disobedient warriors denied the promised land and
5:7 has their sons replace them. This is not wholesale removal of generational
disobedience. Its wilderness numeral is forty-two, not the current Hebrew forty;
neither is silently repaired here. At 21:42d the knives are deposited in the
city, not yet explicitly in Joshua's tomb. At the ending, the natural continuing
subject of circumcising and bringing-out is Joshua, but his name is not repeated
inside the exodus clause. A meaningful reading is distinguished from an explicit
proper-name assertion.

## The connected Qumran control is partial, not absent

A source reader checked the actual QDR primary rows of 4Q47, 4Q48, 4Q49,
4Q50 and 1Q6 separately from their biblical reference parallels. No Joshua 24
or Judges 2 target occurs in that checked set. This is a bounded published
coverage check, not an all-discovered-manuscript census.

Importantly, [4Q47 fragments 1–2](https://lexicon.qumran-digital.org/transcriptions/4Q47/2026-05-21/index.html?v=2026-05-21),
**lines 4–11**, are aligned with Joshua 5:2–7. Root checked these primary rows.
The relevant primary rows preserve uncertain portions of Egypt wording across
5:4–5, a small piece within 5:5, and “men of war” wording in 5:6. They support
parts of the Hebrew wording/sequence associated with the longer circumcision
explanation, not exclusively MT features at every surviving word. They do **not** preserve the full claims that the Egypt-exiters
were circumcised and the wilderness-born were not: those decisive clauses
remain editorial supplies. The forty-year/death/disobedience context likewise
must not be promoted wholesale from the reconstructed row.

This direct Hebrew control matters to any model linking the ending to an
earlier circumcision story. It neither establishes all of MT 5:4–6 as surviving
ancient ink nor makes the Greek sequence impossible. Published placements and
uncertain letters remain qualified, not fresh joins or decipherments. Similarly,
1Q6's tentatively aligned Judges 3:8 fragment does not preserve an eight-year
number; the modern verse reference cannot corroborate that numeral.

## Two scholarly models and what remains inference

Root read Mäkipelto's complete 2017 article, printed 217–242, including notes,
and visually checked its full synoptic table. He prefers Greek Joshua's sequence
and knives unit as earlier, with Hebrew Joshua reflecting relocation and
theologically motivated deletion. His argument links the knives, circumcision
and Moses/Aaron wording. He considers expansion and harmonization alternatives;
the final 33a–b units are expressly outside his main analysis (note 25).
[The institutional author offprint](https://helda.helsinki.fi/server/api/core/bitstreams/df2252bf-be20-4eef-964d-24aba8ef8d4b/content)
is consulted evidence, not new manuscript ink.

Root also read McKinion's complete 2025 article, printed 22–39, including its
bibliography, and visually checked the title and ending discussion. It emphasizes
parallel textual forms and their interpretive roles, especially Joshua's
Moses-like/prophetic portrayal and the transition to apostasy. It offers a
different interpretive lens, not a direct answer to every argument of the 2017
study. Its claims about communities preserving mutually illuminating authoritative
Hebrew forms remain historical explanations, not directly observed community
decisions. [The actual article](https://reference-global.com/download/article/10.2478/perc-2025-0002.pdf)
was acquired by ordinary download despite the separate web reader's failure.

Our judgment separates three questions. First, the different Greek wording and
order are available for comparison. Second, a Hebrew precursor is plausible for
some Greek narrative, but easy retroversion alone cannot prove its existence,
exact spelling or priority. Third, deciding between expansion and deletion
requires more than a possible ideological motive. Memorial interest could
explain addition of the knives; discomfort could explain deletion. Neither
scribe's intention is recovered by proposing it. Agreement with Judges can be
inherited or harmonizing. A narrative transition formula does not by itself
prove a later insertion.

Thus the articles establish serious competing explanations, not a mandate to
select the entire longer or shorter form as a package. The order, knives unit,
Moses/Aaron clause and final continuation need separate transmission arguments,
while their literary connections remain visible. A fluent hybrid would conceal
the very evidence the comparison is meant to explain.
