# Micah 6:8 lexical and version comparison

Micah 6:8 supplies a useful translation question, not a recovered Hebrew
variant. The retained POB phrase **“walk humbly”** has actual lexical support.
The selected Greek edition instead expresses readiness to walk with God.
Those interpretations deserve comparison, but neither the Greek nor a gap in
the desert manuscripts proves a different Hebrew source. Source and main
English remain unchanged. A qualified reader note is a candidate for a separate
review, not an approved application.

## Hebrew wording and grammar

The pinned POB record is `MIC.6.8`, with the target `הַצְנֵעַ לֶכֶת`.
Its complete record and source text are bound in the
[comparison receipt](../sources/textual_restoration/comparisons/micah6_8_lexical.2026-10-10.v1.json).
The verse contrasts escalating offerings in 6:6–7 with justice, loyal love,
and conduct with God. That context supports an ethical reading, but does not
alone decide modesty, humility, carefulness or readiness.

The [Open Scriptures Hebrew Bible](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/openscriptures/morphhb/65214d2bdf57f01feb4822c22c5b0d4de032891f/wlc/Mic.xml)
at commit `65214d2bdf57f01feb4822c22c5b0d4de032891f` labels the two target words
`HC/Vha` and `HVqc`: conjunction plus **Hiphil infinitive absolute**, followed
by **Qal infinitive construct**. The project's
[code definitions](https://hb.openscriptures.org/parsing/HebrewMorphologyCodes.html)
confirm those expansions. This is an editorial analysis of the pointed Hebrew,
not another ancient manuscript and not proof of a particular English sense.

The locally consulted *Enhanced Brown–Driver–Briggs Hebrew and English Lexicon*
labels the Micah expression Hiphil infinitive absolute and interprets it as
humble conduct. Its adjacent adjective entry points to Proverbs 11:2, where
modesty contrasts with pride. The title page says this enhanced edition is
based on the 1906 BDB, with added reference numbers. It is **not the original
1906 facsimile**: the actual entry is PDF page 2080, not an inspected printed
page 857. The entire entry page was visually read; no fresh HALOT entry was
consulted merely because the existing YAML names HALOT.

[Gesenius section 53a,k](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Gesenius%27_Hebrew_Grammar/53)
distinguishes the normal Hiphil construct and absolute formations while allowing
exceptions; 53f includes submissive conduct under this root.
[Section 113h,k](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Gesenius%27_Hebrew_Grammar/113._The_Infinitive_Absolute)
provides general adverbial patterns, not a target-specific proof: 113h concerns
connection with a finite verb, whereas the following Micah word is construct.
Thus the morphology supports testing a manner expression, without independently
settling the lexical dispute or every feature of the syntax.

## Greek rendering and its limits

Swete's *Old Testament in Greek*, volume III, third edition (1905), printed
page **35**, PDF page **59**, reads:

> ἕτοιμον εἶναι τοῦ πορεύεσθαι μετὰ Κυρίου θεοῦ σου

The approximate sense is **“to be ready to walk with the Lord your God.”**
The complete page, including surrounding 6:2–15 and the apparatus, was visually
inspected. The target's readiness expression is in the running text. This is a
selected published Greek reading, not a freshly read ancient manuscript or an
exhaustive Old Greek manuscript collation. The apparatus also records differences
elsewhere in verse 8; they are not silently imported into this target comparison.

Readiness may reflect the translator's interpretation of the same Hebrew root.
Without a locally justified translation-technique and retroversion argument,
it does **not** establish a uniquely different Hebrew consonantal sequence.
Neither the full Greek verse nor “ready” should replace the Hebrew-based English
just because the version is ancient. Latin and Syriac carefulness renderings
remain leads here, not independently inspected controls.

## Desert manuscript coverage

The pinned Qumran Digital biblical dataset has three Micah-labelled records:
4Q81, 4Q82 and Mur88. All return **zero `Mic 6:8` word tags**. The
[published Mur. 88 transcription](https://lexicon.qumran-digital.org/transcriptions/Mur._88/2026-05-21/index.html?v=2026-05-21)
displays partial 6:7 at column 14 line 39 and resumes at 6:11, column 15 line 7.
No target letters are displayed in that interval. This is a preservation and
coverage gap, **not an attested omission**, a supplied restoration of our target,
or a complete census of every known Micah witness. No manuscript pixels were
read in this check. BHQ fascicle 13's target apparatus/commentary and the
comprehensive Greek apparatus remain required before claiming broad collation.

## Decision and next contribution

Keep `הַצְנֵעַ לֶכֶת` and “walk humbly” provisionally. Humility/modesty has
positive support; readiness is a genuine selected Greek interpretation.
The strongest counterargument to replacing the English with “carefully” is
that the consulted Hebrew lexicon and related adjective support modesty, while
the modern prudence argument and other versional controls are not yet examined.
The strongest counterargument to declaring “humbly” uniquely optimal is the
ancient Greek interpretation plus the root's sparse biblical evidence.

A possible note could disclose the Greek readiness interpretation without
calling it an alternative recovered Hebrew text. Before application, freeze
the complete note and English alternatives, examine the modern argument and
target critical apparatus, and conduct a bounded independent critique. A pass
would approve only the scoped claim it checked, not prove originality.

The existing verse also has footnote markers attached to the wrong phrases.
That is a separate reader-quality defect, not evidence for changing the Hebrew;
it is recorded but not repaired by this lexical packet. Historical model scores
are not transferred to these findings. Canon, About, restoration images and
deployed-reader state remain unchanged. This case provides no evidence for
adding a book to a canon; textual meaning and community reception of books
remain separate research questions.
