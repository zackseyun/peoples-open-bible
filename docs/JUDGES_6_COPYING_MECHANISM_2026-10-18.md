# Judges 6 copying mechanism and expansion argument

## Judgment

A specific copying-loss mechanism is possible: the opening words of Judges
6:7 and 6:11 share the two letters וי. This strengthens the case for keeping
loss open, not for selecting it as the actual event. Literary expansion remains
the provisional working preference; historical priority is unresolved. No
Hebrew, main English or reader-note change follows.

## The original arguments

Hans Ausloos, [Literary Criticism and Textual Criticism in Judg 6:1–14 in Light of 4QJudgᵃ](https://scielo.org.za/pdf/ote/v27n2/03.pdf),
*Old Testament Essays* 27/2 (2014), 358–376, favors growth while explicitly
allowing a theoretical beginning-to-beginning eye skip on 371. His proposed
cue is only וי at verses 7 and 11, not matching complete words. Footnote 54
separates that possibility from Rofé's paragraph skip and Fernández Marcos's
blank-to-blank alternative. His other arguments distinguish final-form
coherence from original unity and caution that fragment size cannot establish
an otherwise unattested liturgical collection either.

Müller, Pakkala and ter Haar Romeny's [Evidence of Editing](https://www.sbl-site.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/11/EvidenceEditing_MullerPakkalaRomeny_SBL.pdf)
(2014), chapter 5, 59–68, argues that the repeated cry introduces a theological
expansion. Importantly, 67 note 19 acknowledges that this is not the typical
resumptive pattern: the repeated wording here introduces the added unit,
rather than occurring at its end. The smaller Greek cry difference discussed
on 67 is not another witness omitting the whole prophetic speech.

The chapter's rejection of a copying explanation on 64 must be qualified by
the concrete prefix possibility above. Its late-addition dating is an argument,
not a date directly supplied by the surviving copy. It cites BHQ 65*–66*, but
reading that citation is not consulting those apparatus pages. These original
studies analyze existing witnesses; neither adds another physical witness.

## Direct Hebrew countertest

The pinned WLC words are ויהי at 6:7 and ויבא at 6:11. Their longest common
initial sequence is וי. A conditional model copies 6:6, then those two letters
from 6:7, and resumes after the same two letters in 6:11. The resulting text
is exactly the consonantal 6:6 followed by 6:11, without 7–10. This demonstrates
mechanical compatibility, not a historical event or its probability.

The cue is not distinctive: within 7–11, the same prefix occurs in five written
tokens—ויהי, וישלח, ויאמר, ויבא and וישב. No exemplar layout is available to
explain a return specifically at 6:11 rather than another similar beginning.
Modern verse boundaries and the modeled concatenation are navigation aids,
not reconstructed ancient line breaks.

All nine primary Qumran-Digital lines were rechecked separately from parallel
rows. Line 5 preserves parts of the cry and YHWH, but supplies the angel's
arrival; line 6 preserves adjacent Joash/Abiezrite/Gideon material. Both an
earlier short form and a later skip can yield this published shorter sequence.
Matching its partly supplied junction therefore cannot distinguish those
histories or count as a newly read continuous junction.

The earlier repeated-cry test addressed an ending-based skip. This new
beginning-based mechanism is different; rejecting the former does not reject
the latter. Likewise, evidence that a speech was added at some stage does not
exclude an early addition followed by a later copying loss.
